# CPT Code 99202 Documentation & Billing Guide
**Office Visit  - New Patient  - Straightforward MDM**  
_Last Updated: February 2026_  

Status: **2025/2026 Medicare Fee Schedule Compliant** 

---

### Short Definition

Intermediate ophthalmological examination and evaluation for a new patient with initiation of diagnostic and treatment program

## Quick Reference

- **CPT Code:** 99202 
- **Patient Status:** New patient (no professional services from this provider or same specialty/subspecialty in the same group within the past 3 years).  
- **Complexity Level:** Straightforward Medical Decision Making (MDM).  
- **Category:** Office/Outpatient E/M Services. 
- **Typical Time (Total Time Method):** 15 -29 minutes on the date of the encounter.  
- **Primary Selection Method:** Either MDM **or** total time (not both).

---

## Short Description

CPT 99202 is an evaluation and management (E/M) code for an office or other outpatient visit with a **new** patient, requiring medically appropriate history/exam and **straightforward** MDM or 15 -29 minutes of total time on the date of the encounter. 

---

## Full Description

### Definition

99202 is the lowest-level new patient office/outpatient E/M code (99201 deleted in 2021) and is used when the visit involves minimal complexity.   

- **New Patient:** The patient has not received any professional services from the provider or another provider of the same specialty/subspecialty in the same group within the past three years. 
- **History/Exam:** Must be performed and documented as **medically appropriate**, but are no longer used for code selection under the 2021+ office E/M rules. 
- **Leveling:** Determined **only** by MDM or total time, whichever is used.   

### Clinical Context

Typical use for new patients with minor, uncomplicated issues and minimal risk.   

Examples by specialty (99202-appropriate scenarios):

- **[[Urology]]:** New patient with simple, uncomplicated UTI (no systemic symptoms, no high-risk comorbidities).  
- **[[Ophthalmology]]:** New patient with mild allergic conjunctivitis with no red flags (no vision loss, no severe pain, no corneal involvement).  
- **[[Otolaryngology]] (ENT):** New patient with acute otitis externa (swimmer’s ear) in one ear, no complications.  

(These are clinical pattern examples; always code to actual documented findings and MDM.)

- **99202 vs 99212:** Same basic MDM level, but 99202 is for **new** patients; 99212 is for **established** patients.
- **99202 vs 99203:** 99203 requires **low** MDM or ≥30 minutes; 99202 is for **straightforward** MDM or 15 -29 minutes. 
- **99201:** Deleted; use 99202 for the lowest-level new patient visits.

## 2024/2025 Documentation Guidelines (MDM-Based)

For office/outpatient E/M, the 1995/1997 “bullets” no longer determine the level; selection is based on **MDM** or **total time**. 

When using **MDM**, 99202 requires **straightforward** MDM. This is usually supported when **2 of 3** components meet straightforward criteria:

### MDM  - Straightforward Indicators for 99202

| MDM Component                              | Requirement (99202)                                      |
|-------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| Number & complexity of problems           | 1 self-limited or minor problem.            |
| Amount/complexity of data reviewed        | Minimal or none (e.g., review of a single prior note or simple test).  |
| Risk of complications/morbidity/mortality | Minimal risk from additional testing or treatment.  |

Typical documentation to support straightforward MDM:

- Clearly state the **single minor problem** and that it is uncomplicated.  
- Minimal data: e.g., “No labs ordered today” or “Reviewed prior urgent care visit note only.”  
- Low-risk management: OTC meds, simple prescription with low risk, self-care instructions, routine follow-up. 

---

## Time-Based Billing (Alternative Method)

You may instead select 99202 based on **total time** on the date of the encounter (face-to-face + non -face-to-face work on that date), when time is the controlling factor. 

- **Time range for 99202:** 15 -29 minutes total time. 
- Time may include:  
  - Reviewing records on the date of service  
  - Performing the history and exam  
  - Counseling/education  
  - Ordering tests or medications  
  - Documenting in the record  
  - Care coordination (same date) 

> Code by **either** MDM **or** time—whichever most accurately reflects the service— but not both.

## Sample Documentation Example (New Patient  - 99202)

```
CC: Eye redness and itching x 2 days.

HPI: 24-year-old NEW patient presents with bilateral eye itching and redness.
Symptoms began 2 days ago after mowing the lawn. Denies pain, photophobia,
discharge, or vision changes. No contact lens use. No prior similar episodes.

PE: Medically appropriate eye exam performed. Conjunctivae injected bilaterally,
no chemosis, no discharge. Corneas clear. Pupils PERRLA. EOMI. Visual acuity
grossly unchanged from baseline per patient.

Assessment:
1. Allergic conjunctivitis (H10.13)  - straightforward, self-limited problem.

Plan:
1. Over-the-counter antihistamine eye drops (e.g., olopatadine) BID.
2. Cool compresses to eyes prn for comfort.
3. Return or call if pain, vision changes, or purulent discharge develop; routine
   follow up if not improved in 3 -5 days.

MDM:
- Number/complexity: 1 self-limited/minor problem (allergic conjunctivitis).
- Data: No labs or imaging ordered; no external records reviewed.
- Risk: Minimal  - OTC medications and home care instructions only.

Time:
Total time on date of encounter: 18 minutes (chart review, history/exam,
patient counseling, and documentation).

CPT: 99202 (New patient, straightforward MDM; total time 15 -29 minutes).
```

### **Common Coding Pitfalls**

❌ **Using 99202 for established patients**: If the patient was seen by this provider (**or same specialty/subspecialty in the group**) within the past 3 years, use an established E/M code (e.g., [[99212]]) instead. 
❌ **Ignoring time when it supports a higher level**: If total time reaches 30 -44 minutes for a new patient, [[99203]] may be supported even if MDM appears straightforward, assuming documentation supports the time elements. 
❌ The “**specialty trap**”: If the patient saw a cardiologist in your group previously but is now seeing you in Urology (**different specialty/subspecialty**), the patient is new for your service. 
❌ **Under-describing MDM**: A sparse note can look like a “**sticky note**” visit; clearly document the problem, data, and risk to support even straightforward MDM.
