_____
>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[acar-]]
>#greek
>
>|Component|Origin|Meaning|
>|---|---|---|
>|**[[acar-]]** / **[[acaro-]]**|Greek _ἀκάρι_ (akari), from _ἀκάρεα_ (akarea)|"**mite**," "**scab**," "**tick**" — zoological/parasitological combining form|
>
> The word entered English in the **1840s** as *acarid* (**noun**), borrowed from Modern Latin ***acarus***, from Greek ***ἀκάρι*** (akari) — literally "**mite**" (originally denoting the specific mite causing scabies). The noun *acarocele* appeared in the **1900s**. The root *akari* ("**mite**") connects [[acar-]] to the entire **[[parasite and arthropod roots]]**: ***[[acaricide]]*** (**acar- + -cide $\rightarrow$ agent that kills mites**), ***[[acarophobia]]*** (**acar- + phobos $\rightarrow$ fear of mites or ticks**), and ***[[acarology]]*** (**acar- + logos $\rightarrow$ study of mites**). The **combining form** [[acar-]] is productive in parasitology, dermatology, and entomology: e.g., ***[[acaroid]]***, ***[[acaricidal]]***, ***[[acaricide]]***, and ***[[Dermanyssus]]***.
