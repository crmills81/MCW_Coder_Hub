>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[-rrhexis]]
>#greek

>|Component|Origin|Meaning|
>|---|---|---|
>|**[[rhex-]]** / **[[-rrhexis]]**|Greek _ῥήγνυμι_ (rhēgnymi), from _ῥῆξις_ (rhēxis)|"**bursting**," "**rupture**," "**tearing**" — Noun-forming compound suffix — "**bursting or rupture of (a tissue or organ)**"|
>
> The word entered English in the **1800s** as *rrhexis* (**noun**), borrowed from Modern Latin ***rrhexis***, from Greek ***ῥξις*** (rhēxis) — literally "**a bursting**" (from *rhēgnymi* "to burst, to break"). The double *r* in the Latinized form represents the Greek rough breathing (ῥ). The root *rhēgnymi* ("**to burst, to break**") connects [[-rrhexis]] to the entire **[[rupture and tearing suffixes]]**: ***[[vesical rrhexis]]*** (**vesic- + rrhexis $\rightarrow$ bursting of the bladder**), ***[[intestinal rrhexis]]*** (**entero- + rrhexis $\rightarrow$ bursting of the intestine**), and ***[[uterine rrhexis]]*** (**uter- + rrhexis $\rightarrow$ bursting of the uterus**). The **compound suffix** [[-rrhexis]] is productive in surgery, obstetrics, and pathology: e.g., ***[[vesical rrhexis]]***, ***[[intestinal rrhexis]]***, ***[[uterine rrhexis]]***, ***[[splenic rrhexis]]***, and ***[[gastric rrhexis]]***.
