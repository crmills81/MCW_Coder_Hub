>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[-tome]]
>**[[-tome]]** is a Greek-derived suffix and substantive noun element in medical nomenclature with two distinct applications: surgical instrumentation and neuroanatomical/embryological segmentation. Mechanically, it denotes an **instrument designed for cutting or slicing** specific biological tissues or specimens with calibrated precision, such as a *dermatome* (used in plastic and reconstructive surgery to harvest split-thickness skin grafts), an *osteotome* (a beveled wedge chisel used in orthopedics to sculpt or divide bone), or a *microtome* (a laboratory apparatus used in surgical pathology to cut tissue specimens into microscopic ribbons). Anatomically and embryologically, it designates a **discrete segment, section, or somite-derived division** of the body, most notably a *dermatome* (a cutaneous area innervated by sensory fibers of a single dorsal spinal nerve root), a *myotome* (a group of skeletal muscles innervated by motor axons of a single ventral spinal root), and a *sclerotome* (the embryonic somite component giving rise to vertebrae and rib cartilage). In 2026 clinical documentation and medical coding, distinguishing between the instrument and the neural mapping zone is essential: an instrument ending in `-tome` executes a surgical incision ([[ -tomy ]]) or resection ([[ -ectomy ]]), whereas an anatomical `-tome` serves as an indispensable diagnostic landmark for establishing medical necessity in radiculopathy ([[ M54.16 ]]), electromyography ([[ 95886 ]]), and spinal interventional procedures ([[ 64483 ]]).

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[-tome]]
>#greek
>
>|Component|Origin|Meaning|
>|---|---|---|
>|**[[tom-]]**|[Greek] _tomē_ (τομή) / _temnein_ (τέμνειν)|"**a cutting**," "**division**," "**section**," or "**slice**" — verbal root designating the physical action of incising or dividing.|
>|**[[-e]]**|[Greek/French] _-ē_ (-η) / _-e_|**Noun-forming suffix** — transforms the verbal action into a concrete instrument, mechanical tool, or physical anatomical segment.|
>
> The root element entered modern surgical and scientific vocabularies during the **18th and 19th centuries** alongside rapid advancements in microtechnique and specialized surgical tools (notably the invention of the laboratory *microtome* by Wilhelm His Sr. in 1870 and mechanical *dermatomes* by Earl Padgett in 1939). Concurrently, 19th-century comparative embryologists adopted *tomē* to describe the metameric division of paraxial mesoderm into segmented embryonic somites (*sclerotome*, *myotome*, *dermatome*). The element belongs to the foundational **[[tom-]]** linguistic family, connecting directly to **[[-tomy]]** (the operative act of incision), **[[-ectomy]]** (surgical excision or total extirpation), **[[-stomy]]** (surgical creation of an artificial stoma/opening), and diagnostic imaging terms such as *tomography* (sectional image reconstruction).

> [!danger]+ 🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **[[-tomy]]** — the operative procedure or surgical act of incising an organ or tissue (e.g., [[osteotomy]], [[craniotomy]]); the instrument (`-tome`) performs the `-tomy`.
> - **[[-ectomy]]** — surgical excision or total removal of an anatomical structure (e.g., [[appendectomy]], [[mastectomy]]); `-tome` instruments are frequently utilized during surgical resection.
> - **[[dermatome]]** — dual-meaning clinical term: (1) an oscillating or drum surgical instrument used to harvest split-thickness skin grafts; (2) a mapped area of skin innervated by a single dorsal spinal nerve root.
> - **[[myotome]]** — the collection of skeletal muscles innervated by motor axons from a single spinal nerve level; evaluated clinically through manual muscle testing (MMT) and needle electromyography (EMG).
> - **[[sclerotome]]** — embryonic mesodermal somite compartment that differentiates into the vertebrae, intervertebral discs, and rib cage cartilage.
> - **[[osteotome]]** — a beveled surgical cutting chisel driven by a mallet to divide, shape, or harvest bone tissue in orthopedic and maxillofacial procedures.
> - **[[microtome]]** — precision histology instrument used to section paraffin-wax-embedded specimens into 3–5 micrometer ribbons for slide preparation and microscopic diagnostic examination.
> - **[[keratome]]** — an angled micro-surgical blade used in ophthalmic surgery to create precision self-sealing clear corneal incisions during cataract extraction.
> - **[[neurotome]]** — specialized micro-surgical knife used in neurosurgery for the precise division or transection of nerve fibers.
> - **[[amyotrophy]]** — neurogenic muscle wasting resulting from lower motor neuron disease or denervation of a myotome (e.g., [[G12.21]]).
> - **[[radiculopathy]]** — compression or irritation of a spinal nerve root presenting with pain, paresthesias, or weakness along defined dermatomal and myotomal pathways (e.g., [[M54.16]]).

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** In 2026 inpatient profee and outpatient coding guidelines, terms ending in `[[-tome]]` demand careful verification between surgical instrumentation and anatomical nerve distribution. In operative surgery, documented use of a **dermatome** is primary technical evidence supporting split-thickness skin graft codes ([[15100]]–[[15121]]), distinguishing them from full-thickness excision and graft harvesting (CPT [[15200]]–[[15261]]) or simple debridement. When coding spinal interventions (e.g., CPT [[64483]]/[[64484]]), Medicare Part B and commercial payers require explicit medical necessity documentation correlating MRI findings of neural foraminal stenosis with clinical **dermatomal pain patterns** or **myotomal motor deficits** (ICD-10-CM [[M54.16]], [[M51.16]]). Undercoding alert: for electrodiagnostic testing (CPT [[95886]]), providers must document evaluation of at least 5 muscles innervated by at least 3 peripheral nerves or 4 spinal roots (myotomes) including paraspinal muscles to bill the complete code rather than the limited code ([[95885]]).
