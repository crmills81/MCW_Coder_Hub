>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[acou-]]
>#greek
>
>|Component|Origin|Meaning|
>|---|---|---|
>|**[[acou-]]** / **[[acous-]]** / **[[acu-]]**|Greek _ἀκούειν_ (akouein), from _ἀκοή_ (akoē)|"**hear**," "**hearing**," "**sound**" — sensory combining form denoting auditory perception|
>
> The word entered English in the **1620s** as *acoustic* (**adjective**), borrowed from French *acoustique*, from Latin ***acusticus***, from Greek ***ἀκουστικός*** (akoustikos) — literally "**pertaining to hearing**." The noun *acoustics* appeared in the **1700s**, and *acoumeter* in the **1850s**. The root *akouein* ("**to hear**") connects [[acou-]] to the entire **[[hearing and auditory roots]]**: ***[[acoustic neuroma]]*** (**acou- + stic + neuroma $\rightarrow$ tumor of the auditory nerve**), ***[[acoumetry]]*** (**acou- + [[-metry]] $\rightarrow$ measurement of hearing acuity**), and ***[[acouphenia]]*** (**acou- + phainesthai $\rightarrow$ phantom sound perception/tinnitus**). The **combining form** [[acou-]] is productive in otolaryngology, audiology, and neurology: e.g., ***[[acouesthesia]]***, ***[[acousticophobia]]***, ***[[acousma]]***, and ***[[acoumeter]]***.
