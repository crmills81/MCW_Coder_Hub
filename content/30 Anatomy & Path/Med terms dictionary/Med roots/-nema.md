>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[nema]]
>#greek
>
>|Component|Origin|Meaning|
>|---|---|---|
>|**[[nema]]** / **[[nemat-]]**|Greek _νῆμα_ (nêma), from _νήματος_ (nêmatos)|"**thread**," "**strand**," "**thread-like**" — anatomical/morphological combining form denoting thread-like or filamentous structures|
>
> The word entered English in the **1830s** as *nematode* (**noun**), borrowed from Modern Latin ***nematodeus***, from Greek ***νῆμα*** (nêma) — literally "**thread**" or "**thread-like**." The term *nematocyst* appeared in the **1900s**, and *nematosis* in the **1920s**. The root *nêma* ("**thread, strand**") connects [[nema]] to the entire **[[filament and thread roots]]**: ***[[nematode]]*** (**nema + -ode $\rightarrow$ thread-like animal/worm**), ***[[nematocyst]]*** (**nema + kystis $\rightarrow$ thread-like stinging capsule**), and ***[[nematosis]]*** (**nema + -osis $\rightarrow$ condition of thread-like growth or infection**). The **combining form** [[nema]] is productive in parasitology, entomology, and zoology: e.g., ***[[nematodiasis]]***, ***[[nematogenous]]***, ***[[nematocyst]]***, and ***[[filament]]*** (cf. Latin *filum*, cognate concept).
