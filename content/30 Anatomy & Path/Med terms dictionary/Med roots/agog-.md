>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[agog-]]
>(agent) leading, drawing forth
_____
>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[agog-]]
>#greek
>
>|Component|Origin|Meaning|
>|---|---|---|
>|**[[agog-]]** / **[[-agogue]]**|Greek _ἀγωγός_ (agogos), from _ἄγειν_ (agein)|"**leading**," "**drawing forth**," "**promoting the flow of**" — agentive combining form denoting induction of discharge|
>
> The word entered English in the **1600s** as *-agogue* (**noun-forming suffix**), borrowed from Late Latin *-agogus*, from Greek ***ἀγωγός*** (agogos) — literally "**leading**." The root *agein* ("**to lead**") connects [[agog-]] to the entire **[[directional and flow roots]]**: ***[[cholagogue]]*** (**[[chol-]] + agog- + [[-ue]] $\rightarrow$ agent leading bile flow**), ***[[emmenagogue]]*** (**emmen- + agog- + -ue $\rightarrow$ agent leading menstrual flow**), and ***[[galactagogue]]*** (**galact- + agog- + -ue $\rightarrow$ agent leading milk production**). The **agentive combining form** [[agog-]] is productive in pharmacology and physiology: e.g., ***[[sialagogue]]***, ***[[hydragogue]]***, and ***[[hemagogue]]***.
