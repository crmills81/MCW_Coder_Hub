>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[dysesthesia]]
>**[[Dysesthesia]]** is a**n unpleasant, abnormal sensation, most often of touch, that occurs spontaneously or is provoked by a stimulus**, and is typically described as **burning, wetness, itching, "pins and needles," or electric-shock sensations.** It differs from **[[paresthesia]],** which is an abnormal sensation that is not necessarily unpleasant (**such as simple tingling**), and from **[[hypoesthesia]]** (**reduced sensation**) and **[[anesthesia]]** (**absent sensation**). It is also related to **[[allodynia]]**, where a normally painless **stimulus** causes pain, and to **[[hyperesthesia]]**, where sensitivity is increased. The mechanism is a **[[lesion]]** or dysfunction anywhere along the **somatosensory** pathway, **peripheral nerve, spinal cord, or brain**, producing ectopic nerve firing and misinterpretation of input. It is almost always pathological (**diabetic [[polyneuropathy]], [[multiple sclerosis]], [[Guillain-Barré syndrome]], shingles, vitamin deficiency, alcohol- or drug-related nerve damage, spinal cord injury**), though transient forms occur with **nerve compression** ("**foot falling asleep**") or drug withdrawal. The forms you'll see in coding are skin sensation disturbance (**[[R20.8]]**), **burning mouth syndrome** (**[[K14.6]]**), **[[diabetic neuropathy]]** (E11.4-), **complex regional pain syndrome** (G90.5-), **meralgia paresthetica** (G57.1-), and **[[trigeminal]]** or **postherpetic neuralgia** (**[[G50.0]], [[B02.22]]**). It is most often confused with **[[paresthesia]]** (**[[R20.2]]**), but a **[[dysesthesia]]** is by definition unpleasant or painful, whereas a **[[paresthesia]]** may be neutral.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[dysesthesia]]
>#greek #new-latin
>
>|**Component**|**Origin**|**Meaning**|
>|:---:|---|---|
>|**[[dys-]]**|Greek _dys-_ (dis)|"**bad**," "**difficult**," "**abnormal**" — pejorative/negating prefix|
>|**[[esthes-]]**|Greek _aisthēsis_ (AIS-thay-sis, "sensation"), from _aisthanesthai_ (ais-than-ES-thai, "to perceive, to feel")|"**sensation**," "**feeling**," "**perception**"|
>|**[[-ia]]**|Greek _-ia_ (ee-ah)|Noun-forming suffix — "**condition or state of**"|
>
> The word entered English in the **19th century** (exact first-use date not verified) as *dysesthesia* (**noun**), borrowed from New Latin ***dysaesthesia***, from Greek ***dysaisthēsía*** — literally **"bad or abnormal sensation."** The British spelling *dysaesthesia* keeps the original diphthong, while the American spelling drops the "a." The adjective *dysesthetic* follows the same pattern as *aesthetic*. The root *aisthēsis* ("**sensation**") connects dysesthesia to the entire **[[-esthesia]] family**: ***anesthesia*** (**[[an-]] + esthesia → without sensation**), ***[[paresthesia]]*** (**[[para-]] + esthesia → abnormal sensation, beside normal**), ***[[hyperesthesia]]*** (**[[hyper-]] + esthesia → excessive sensation**), ***hypoesthesia*** (**[[hypo-]] + esthesia → decreased sensation**), and *kinesthesia* (**movement sensation**). The Greek prefix *[[dys-]]* is very productive in medical terminology, appearing in ***[[dyspnea]]***, ***[[dysphagia]]***, ***[[dysuria]]***, ***[[dysplasia]]***, and ***[[dyspepsia]]***.

> [!success]+ 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **Dysesthetic** _(adjective form — "dysesthetic pain," "dysesthetic burning," "dysesthetic sensation")_
> - **Abnormal sensation** _(lay and clinical term; common in neurology and PM&R notes before a definitive diagnosis)_
> - **Pins and needles** _(lay term; usually reflects paresthesia, but becomes dysesthesia when painful or unpleasant)_
> - **Burning sensation** _(clinical descriptor; common in [[neuropathy]], burning mouth, and small-fiber disease)_
> - **[[Paresthesia]]** _(related/partial form; abnormal sensation that is not necessarily unpleasant; [[R20.2]])_
> - **[[Cutaneous]] dysesthesia** _(anatomic subtype — abnormal skin sensation; coded [[R20.8]] or by underlying cause)_
> - **Scalp dysesthesia** _(anatomic subtype — burning, pain, or itching of the scalp without a visible rash; often with [[L29.8]] or by cause)_
> - **Oral dysesthesia / burning mouth syndrome** _(anatomic subtype — burning or dysesthetic sensation of the oral mucosa; [[K14.6]])_
> - **Occlusal dysesthesia ("phantom bite")** _(anatomic subtype — feeling that the bite is "out of place" despite normal findings; no specific ICD-10-CM code)_
> - **Neuropathic dysesthesia** _(etiologic subtype — due to peripheral nerve damage such as diabetic or alcoholic polyneuropathy; E11.4-, [[G62.1]])_
> - **Central dysesthesia** _(etiologic subtype — due to a lesion of the spinal cord or brain, such as [[multiple sclerosis]]; [[G35.D]])_
> - **Postherpetic dysesthesia** _(etiologic subtype — persistent burning or pain after shingles; B02.2-)_

> [!danger]+ 🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **[[Anesthesia]]** — absence of sensation; the opposite end of the sensory spectrum from dysesthesia's distorted or heightened sensation
> - **[[Esthesia]]** — shares the *[[esthes-]]* root; normal sensation or capacity to feel
> - **[[Paresthesia]]** — abnormal sensation, such as tingling or numbness, that is not necessarily painful; [[R20.2]]
> - **[[Hypoesthesia]]** — decreased sensitivity to stimuli; [[R20.1]]
> - **[[Hyperesthesia]]** — increased sensitivity to stimuli; [[R20.3]]
> - **[[Allodynia]]** — pain from a stimulus that is not normally painful, such as light touch; common in neuropathic pain and complex regional pain syndrome
> - **[[Neuropathy]]** — disease or dysfunction of peripheral nerves; the most common mechanism behind dysesthesia
> - **[[Neuropathic pain]]** — pain caused by a lesion or disease of the [[somatosensory]] nervous system, of which [[dysesthesia]] is a characteristic quality
> - **[[Demyelination]]** — loss of the myelin sheath, as in [[multiple scleros]]is, which can produce central dysesthesia
> - **[[Diabetic polyneuropathy]]** — the most frequent cause in practice ([[E10.42]], [[E11.42]])
> - **Complex regional pain syndrome** — burning pain with [[allodynia]] and dysesthesia after injury (G90.5-)
> - **[[Guillain-Barré syndrome]]** — acute inflammatory polyneuropathy with dysesthesia ([[G61.0]])
> - **Postherpetic neuralgia** — persistent pain after herpes zoster (B02.2-)
> - **Nerve conduction study / [[EMG]]** — key diagnostic tools for evaluating the underlying [[neuropathy]] (CPT [[95907]]-[[95913]], [[95885]]-[[95886]])

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** Code the underlying cause when it is documented, and use **[[R20.8]]** only when "**[[dysesthesia]]**" is the final diagnostic statement, because symptoms integral to a confirmed condition are not reported separately. For **[[diabetic neuropathy]]**, assign the E10/E11 combination code (**[[E11.42]] for [[polyneuropathy]])** rather than R20.- plus a separate diabetes code. For carpal tunnel and **meralgia paresthetica**, laterality is required and bilateral has its own code. Common undercodes are "**burning feet**," "**burning mouth**," and "**pins and needles after [[shingles]]**," which should prompt a query for **[[diabetic neuropathy]]**, **[[K14.6]]**, or **postherpetic [[neuralgia]]** (B02.2-). Complex regional pain syndrome needs the type and site documented (G90.51-, G90.52-). Add **[[-RT]] or [[-LT]]** where the CPT code is not laterality-specific, **[[-26]]** if you are billing only the interpretation of a nerve study, and **[[-25]]** for a separately identifiable E/M on the same day as a minor injection. Many payers want **NCS/EMG** supported by documented clinical findings, and nerve stimulator i-mplants need prior authorization and documented failed conservative therapy.
