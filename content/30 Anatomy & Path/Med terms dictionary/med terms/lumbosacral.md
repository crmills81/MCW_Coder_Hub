>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[lumbosacral]]
>**[[lumbosacral]]** is **an anatomic adjective meaning relating to both the lumbar spine** (**the five lower back vertebrae, L1-L5**) and **the sacrum** (**the triangular bone formed by the fused S1-S5 vertebrae**), most often the **lumbosacral junction where L5 meets the base of the sacrum**. It also describes the **lumbosacral plexus**, the network of nerves formed by the **lumbar plexus** (**L1-L4**) and **sacral plexus** (**L4-S4**) that supplies the **pelvis, hip, thigh, leg, and foot**. It differs from **[[lumbar]]** **(L1-L5 alone**), **[[sacral]]** (**S1-S5 alone**), and **[[sacroiliac]]** (**the joint between the sacrum and the ilium**). The region is the transition between the mobile lumbar spine and the fixed pelvis, so it carries heavy mechanical load and is vulnerable to **[[degeneration]], disc [[herniation]], [[listhesis]], and nerve root [[compression]]**. The **L5-S1 level** is also where the **spine's [[lordosis]]** meets the pelvis, so pelvic alignment affects surgical approach and nerve exposure. It is anatomic (**a normal region and a descriptor of normal structures**) and pathological only when a condition affects it. The conditions you'll code most are **intervertebral disc disorders with [[radiculopathy]]** (**[[M51.17]]**), **[[radiculopathy]]** (**[[M54.17]]**), **[[spondylolisthesis]]** (**[[M43.17]]**), **spinal [[stenosis]]** (**[[M48.07]]**), and **lumbosacral plexus disorders** (**[[G54.1]]**). It is often confused with **[[lumbar]]**, but ICD-10-CM and CPT treat the **lumbosacral** region as a separate coding site, so documentation of "**L5-S1**" matters.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[lumbosacral]]
>#latin
>
>|**Component**|**Origin**|**Meaning**|
>|:---:|---|---|
>|**[[lumb-]]**|Latin _lumbus_ (LUM-bus)|"**loin**," "**lower back**," "**area between the ribs and the pelvis**"|
>|**[[sacr-]]**|Latin _sacer_ (SAH-ker, "**sacred, holy**"), in the Late Latin phrase _os sacrum_ (os SAH-krum, "**sacred bone**"), translating Greek _hieron osteon_ (hee-eh-RON os-teh-ON)|"**sacred**," "**holy**" — name of the sacrum|
>|**[[-al]]**|Latin _-ālis_ (AH-lis)|Adjective-forming suffix — "**pertaining to**"|
>
> The word is a modern anatomic compound formed in English as *lumbosacral* (**adjective**), built from the combining form *[[lumbo-]]* (**lumbar**) and *sacral* (**pertaining to the sacrum**). The date of the first English use is not verified here. Literally it means **"pertaining to the loin and the sacred bone."** The Latin *os sacrum* is a translation of the Greek *hieron osteon*, found in the writings of Galen. Explanations for the "**sacred**" name include use of the **sacrum and coccyx of sacrificed animals in divination**, and the sacrum's protective role for the genitals. Dictionary.com dates English *sacrum* to the mid-18th century. The root *lumbus* connects lumbosacral to the **[[lumb-]] family**: ***lumbar*** (**[[lumb-]] + [[-ar]] → pertaining to the loin**), ***lumbago*** (**[[lumb-]] + [[-ago]] → lower back pain**), and ***[[lumbodynia]]*** (**[[lumbo-]] + [[-dynia]] → lower back pain**). The combining form *[[sacr-]]* is productive in medical terminology, appearing in ***[[sacroiliac]]***, ***[[sacrococcygeal]]***, ***[[sacralization]]***, ***sacrum***, and ***[[sacroiliitis]]***.

> [!success]+ 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **Lumbosacral region** _(adjective-plus-noun form; the coding site term in ICD-10-CM, as in "disc disorder, lumbosacral region")_
> - **Lumbosacral spine / LS spine** _(clinical shorthand in imaging and progress notes; L1 through S1, or the lowest lumbar and the sacrum)_
> - **L5-S1** _(level descriptor; the interspace between the fifth lumbar vertebra and the first sacral segment; maps to the lumbosacral region)_
> - **Lumbosacral junction** _(anatomic term for where the lumbar spine meets the sacrum; also called the lumbosacral joint)_
> - **Lumbosacral joint** _(the articulation between L5 and the base of the sacrum; stabilized by the iliolumbar ligament)_
> - **Lumbosacral plexus** _(neural subtype — combined lumbar plexus (L1-L4) and sacral plexus (L4-S4); [[G54.1]] for disorders)_
> - **Lumbosacral trunk** _(neural subtype — nerve bundle from L4-L5 that joins the sacral plexus)_
> - **Lumbosacral radiculopathy** _(clinical entity — nerve root dysfunction at L5-S1; [[M54.17]] or [[M51.17]])_
> - **Lumbosacral strain / sprain** _(injury subtype — ligament or muscle injury of the low back; [[S33.5XXA]], [[S39.012A]])_
> - **Lumbosacral disc disease** _(degenerative subtype — disc degeneration, herniation, or displacement at L5-S1; M51.37-, [[M51.27]])_
> - **Lumbosacral spondylolisthesis** _(structural subtype — forward slip of L5 on S1, often isthmic; [[M43.17]])_
> - **Lumbosacral transitional vertebra** _(congenital subtype — sacralization of L5 or lumbarization of S1; [[Q76.49]])_

> [!danger]+ 🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **[[Lumbar]]** — shares the *[[lumb-]]* root; relating to the five vertebrae between the thoracic spine and the sacrum
> - **[[Sacral]]** — shares the *[[sacr-]]* root; relating to the sacrum, the fused S1-S5 vertebrae forming the back of the pelvis
> - **[[Thoracolumbar]]** — the adjacent region above, where the thoracic and lumbar spine meet (T12-L1)
> - **[[Sacroiliac joint]]** — the joint between the sacrum and the ilium, a common separate source of low back and buttock pain
> - **[[Sacrococcygeal]]** — relating to the sacrum and coccyx; sacrococcygeal disorders are coded [[M53.3]]
> - **[[Sacralization]]** — fusion of the L5 vertebra to the sacrum (a transitional vertebra); the reverse is lumbarization of S1
> - **Cauda equina** — the bundle of lumbosacral nerve roots in the lower spinal canal; compression is a surgical emergency ([[G83.4]])
> - **[[sciatica]]** — pain along the sciatic nerve (L4-S3), often from lumbosacral nerve root compression (M54.3-, M54.4-)
> - **[[Radiculopathy]]** — nerve root dysfunction from compression or irritation, the most common lumbosacral problem ([[M54.17]], [[M51.17]])
> - **[[spondylolisthesis]]** — forward slip of one vertebra on another, most often at L5-S1 (M43.1-)
> - **[[Spondylolysis]]** — a defect in the pars interarticularis, a precursor to spondylolisthesis (M43.0-)
> - **[[Spinal stenosis]]** — canal or foraminal narrowing; lumbosacral form [[M48.07]]
> - **[[lordosis]]** — the lumbar curve that meets the sacrum at the lumbosacral junction (M40.4-, M40.5-)
> - **Lumbar puncture / Epidural injection** — procedures targeting this region
> - **MRI / X-ray** — key diagnostic tools; MRI shows disc and nerve root detail, standing X-rays show alignment and listhesis

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** The ICD-10-CM region is chosen by the documented level, so **L5-S1** goes to the **[[lumbosacral]]** code (**[[M51.17]], [[M54.17]], [[M43.17]]**), while **L1-L5 levels** go to lumbar. If the provider documents only "**low back**" or "**[[lumbar]]**," do not assume **lumbosacral**, and query for the level. For **[[M51.17]]**, **AAPC** lists **Excludes1** for **lumbar [[radiculitis]] NOS** (**[[M54.16]]**) and **[[sciatica]] NOS** (**[[M54.3]]**), so do not report them together. When a disc disorder with **[[radiculopathy]]** is documented, **[[M51.17]]** is preferred over **[[M54.17]]**, and the **M51.37- degeneration codes** need a sixth character for discogenic back pain and/or lower extremity pain, so query for pain type. Undercoding alerts for inpatient profee are "**[[sciatica]]**," "**[[foot drop]]**," and "**radicular pain**" with a documented **L5-S1 level**, which should prompt a query for **[[radiculopathy]]** and the cause (**disc, [[stenosis]], [[spondylolisthesis]]**). For CPT, **[[22586]]** is specific to the **L5-S1 interspace**, while **[[22558]]** reports other lumbar levels, so make sure the documented level matches the code. Transforaminal epidural injections (**[[64483]]**) are reported per level and include imaging guidance, so use **[[-50]]** for bilateral injections where payer rules allow and **[[-59]]** or **[[-XS]]** for separate levels or sessions. Add **[[-RT]]** or **[[-LT]]** for unilateral injections. Facet and epidural injections often need documented failed conservative therapy and payer prior authorization, and fusions need documented instability, **[[listhesis]]**, or failed **[[decompression]]**. For inpatient surgery, watch for **[[-22]]** (increased procedural services), **[[-62]]** (co-surgeons), **[[-80]]** or **[[-82]]** (assistant surgeons), and **[[-78]]** (unplanned return to the OR).
