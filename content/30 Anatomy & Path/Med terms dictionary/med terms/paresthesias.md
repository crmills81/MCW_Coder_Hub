>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[paresthesia]]
>**[[Paresthesia]]** is **an abnormal or inappropriate sensation of the skin, such as tingling, prickling, burning, creeping, or "pins and needles," that arises without an external stimulus and is not necessarily painful**. It differs from **[[dysesthesia]]**, which is by definition **unpleasant or painful**, from **[[hypoesthesia]]** (**reduced sensation**), and from **[[anesthesia]]** (**absent sensation**). The mechanism is **irritation, compression, or dysfunction of sensory nerve fibers or pathways**, causing abnormal nerve firing, and it can arise from the peripheral nerves, nerve roots, spinal cord, or brain. It can be physiological and transient (**a limb "falling asleep" from pressure on a nerve, [[hyperventilation]]**) or pathological and persistent (**[[carpal tunnel syndrome]], [[diabetic neuropathy]], B12 deficiency, [[multiple sclerosis]], [[stroke]]**). The forms you'll code most are **paresthesia of skin ([[R20.2]])**, **carpal tunnel syndrome** (G56.0-), **[[radiculopathy]]** **([[M54.12]], [[M54.16]]**), **diabetic polyneuropathy** (**[[E11.42]]**), and **meralgia paresthetica** (G57.1-). **[[Acroparesthesia]]** is classified to the **peripheral vascular** codes (**[[I73.89]]**) rather than **[[R20.2]]**. It is often confused with **[[dysesthesia]]** (**[[R20.8]]**) and with numbness, but **[[paresthesia]]** describes the abnormal spontaneous sensation itself and is a symptom, not a diagnosis.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[paresthesia]]
>#greek #new-latin
>
>|**Component**|**Origin**|**Meaning**|
>|:---:|---|---|
>|**[[para-]]**|Greek _para_ (PAH-rah)|"**beside**," "**alongside**," "**beyond**," "**abnormal**" — prefix of deviation|
>|**[[esthes-]]**|Greek _aisthēsis_ (AIS-thay-sis, "**sensation**"), from _aisthanesthai_ (ais-than-ES-thai, "**to perceive, to feel**")|"**sensation**," "**feeling**," "**perception**"|
>|**[[-ia]]**|Greek _-ia_ (ee-ah)|Noun-forming suffix — "**condition or state of**"|
>
> The word entered English in the **19th century** (exact first-use date not verified) as *paresthesia* (**noun**), borrowed from New Latin ***paraesthesia***, from Greek ***para-*** + ***aisthēsis*** — literally **"abnormal sensation, a sensation alongside the normal."** The British spelling *paraesthesia* (Collins, Cambridge) keeps the original diphthong, while the American spelling drops the "a." The adjective is *paresthetic* or *paraesthetic*. The root *aisthēsis* ("**sensation**") connects paresthesia to the entire **[[-esthesia]] family**: ***[[anesthesia]]*** (**[[an-]] + esthesia → without sensation**), ***[[dysesthesia]]*** (**[[dys-]] + esthesia → bad or abnormal sensation**), ***[[hyperesthesia]]*** (**[[hyper-]] + [[-esthesia]] → excessive sensation**), ***[[hypoesthesia]]*** (**[[hypo-]] + esthesia → decreased sensation**), and *synesthesia* (**[[syn-]] + esthesia → joined sensation**). The Greek prefix *[[para-]]* is productive in medical terminology, appearing in ***[[paralysis]]***, ***[[paraplegia]]***, ***[[parathyroid]]***, ***[[paranasal]]***, and ***[[paraspinal]]***.

> [!success]+ 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **Paresthetic** _(adjective form — "paresthetic sensations," "paresthetic meralgia," "paresthetic symptoms")_
> - **[[Paraesthesia]]** _(British spelling; the same term, used in UK sources and some journals)_
> - **Pins and needles** _(lay term; also called "tingling skin" or "prickling"; both indexed under [[R20.2]])_
> - **Numbness and tingling** _(lay and clinical shorthand in patient histories; code the documented diagnosis, or [[R20.2]] if only the symptom is documented)_
> - **[[Formication]]** _(clinical term for a crawling-insects sensation on the skin; indexed to [[R20.2]])_
> - **[[Acroparesthesia]]** _(partial form — tingling and numbness of the hands and feet, often at night; coded to [[I73.89]])_
> - **[[Dysesthesia]]** _(related form — abnormal sensation that is unpleasant or painful; [[R20.8]])_
> - **Transient [[paresthesia]]** _(physiological subtype — brief tingling after pressure on a nerve, such as a foot "falling asleep," or from hyperventilation)_
> - **Perioral paresthesia** _(anatomic subtype — tingling around the mouth, classically with hypocalcemia, hyperventilation, or local anesthetic)_
> - **Cervical radicular paresthesia** _(anatomic subtype — arm and hand tingling from nerve root compression; [[M54.12]])_
> - **Lumbar radicular paresthesia** _(anatomic subtype — leg and foot tingling from nerve root compression; [[M54.16]])_
> - **Median nerve paresthesia** _(etiologic subtype — thumb, index, and middle finger tingling from carpal tunnel syndrome; G56.0-)_

> [!danger]+ 🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **[[Anesthesia]]** — absence of sensation; the opposite end of the sensory spectrum from abnormal or heightened sensation ([[R20.0]])
> - **[[Esthesia]]** — shares the *[[esthes-]]* root; normal sensation or the capacity to feel
> - **[[Dysesthesia]]** — an unpleasant abnormal sensation, often burning or painful, arising from nerve damage ([[R20.8]])
> - **[[Hypoesthesia]]** — decreased sensitivity to stimuli ([[R20.1]])
> - **[[Hyperesthesia]]** — increased sensitivity to stimuli ([[R20.3]])
> - **[[Allodynia]]** — pain from a stimulus that is not normally painful, such as light touch
> - **[[Neuropathy]]** — disease or dysfunction of peripheral nerves, the most common cause of persistent paresthesia
> - **Entrapment neuropathy** — nerve compression at a tight site, as in carpal tunnel or cubital tunnel syndrome
> - **[[Radiculopathy]]** — nerve root dysfunction from compression or irritation (M54.1-, M51.1-, M50.1-)
> - **[[Carpal tunnel syndrome]]** — median nerve compression at the wrist; classic cause of hand paresthesia (G56.0-)
> - **Meralgia paresthetica** — lateral femoral cutaneous nerve compression causing thigh tingling and burning (G57.1-)
> - **Vitamin B12 deficiency** — metabolic cause of symmetric distal paresthesias ([[E53.8]], D51.-)
> - **[[Hypocalcemia]]** — electrolyte disorder causing perioral and fingertip paresthesia ([[E83.51]])
> - **Tinel sign / Phalen test** — bedside tests that reproduce paresthesia over a compressed nerve
> - **Nerve conduction study / EMG** — key diagnostic tools for localizing the cause (CPT [[95907]]-[[95913]], [[95885]]-[[95886]])

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** **[[R20.2]]** is a **symptom code and has no laterality or site character, so document the site in the note and code it only when [[paresthesia]] is the final diagnostic statement with no confirmed cause**. When the cause is established (**carpal tunnel, diabetic [[polyneuropathy]], [[radiculopathy]], [[stroke]]**), code the diagnosis instead, because symptoms integral to a confirmed condition are not reported separately. **[[R20.2]]** carries an **Excludes1 note** for **dissociative anesthesia and sensory loss** (**[[F44.6]]**) and for psychogenic disturbances, so do not report them together. **[[Acroparesthesia]]** does not go to **[[R20.2]]** and is classified to **[[I73.89]]** (index to confirm). Common undercodes are "**numbness and tingling in both feet**," "**burning feet**," and "**tingling after [[shingles]]**," which should prompt a query for **diabetic polyneuropathy ([[E11.42]])**, **vitamin deficiency, or postherpetic involvement**. If imaging or testing is ordered, link **[[R20.2]]** to the order only until a diagnosis is made, then update it. **[[Paresthesia]]** with **sudden onset, facial droop, or speech change should prompt a query for TIA or stroke ([[G45.9]], I63.-)**. For **CPT, NCS [[95907]]-[[95909]]** are chosen by the total number of studies, and **[[95885]] or [[95886]]** are add-on codes that are reported only with a nerve conduction study. Add **[[-RT]]** or **[[-LT]]** where the CPT code is not already laterality-specific, **[[-26]]** if you are billing only the interpretation, **[[-50]]** for bilateral injections or surgery where allowed, and **[[-25]]** for a separate **E/M** on the day of a minor injection. Many payers want the **NCS/EMG** supported by documented symptoms and exam findings, and decompression surgery often needs failed conservative care and electrodiagnostic confirmation.
