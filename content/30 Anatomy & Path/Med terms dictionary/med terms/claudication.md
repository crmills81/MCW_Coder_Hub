>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[claudication]]
>**[[claudication]]** is **exertional pain, cramping, aching, numbness, or fatigue in a limb** (**most often the calf, but also the thigh, hip, buttock, foot, or arm**) that is reproducibly brought on by activity and relieved by rest. It is a symptom, not a diagnosis, and the cause determines the code. The two big families are **vascular claudication** (**arterial insufficiency from [[peripheral artery disease]], where muscle demand outstrips blood supply, coded in [[atherosclerosis]] I70.21- and [[I73.9]]**) and **neurogenic claudication** (**also called [[pseudoclaudication]], from [[spinal stenosis]] compressing nerve roots, coded [[M48.062]]**). It is pathological by definition; a normal exercise limit or **muscle soreness** is not **[[claudication]]**. It is commonly confused with rest pain (**pain at rest from critical limb [[ischemia]], which is a more severe stage**), **[[neuropathy]]**, and **venous claudication from [[venous insufficiency]]**. The key difference is that **vascular claudication** stops quickly with standing rest and is reproducible at a set walking distance, while **neurogenic claudication** is relieved by sitting or leaning forward.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[claudication]]
>#latin
>
>|**Component**|**Origin**|**Meaning**|
>|:---:|---|---|
>|**[[claud-]]**|Latin _claudus_ (CLOW-dus)|"**lame**," "**limping**," "**halting**" — root of unknown earlier origin|
>|**[[claudic-]]**|Latin _claudicare_ (clow-dih-KAH-reh), from _claudus_|"**to limp**," "**to be lame**" — verb formed from the root|
>|**[[-ation]]**|Latin _-atio_ (AH-tee-oh)|Noun-forming suffix — "**act, process, or state of**"|
>
> The word entered English in the **1550s** as *claudication* (**noun**), borrowed from French *claudication* (13c.) or directly from Latin ***claudicationem*** (nominative ***claudicatio***), a noun of action from *claudicare* — literally **"a limping."** Merriam-Webster dates the first known use to the 15th century. The root *claudus* ("**lame**") also gave the adjective *claudicant* and the verb *claudicate*. Folk etymology links the name of the Roman emperor Claudius to the same root because he reportedly limped, and the story is often repeated in the medical literature. Jean Martin Charcot described intermittent **[[claudication]]** of the legs in 1858, which fixed the term's modern vascular meaning. The suffix *[[-ation]]* is very productive in medical terminology: ***[[amputation]]***, ***[[dilatation]]***, ***[[palpitation]]***, ***[[ligation]]***.

> [!success]+  🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **Claudicant / claudicating** _(adjective/participle forms — "claudicant gait," "claudicating patient," "claudicating at one block")_
> - **Intermittent claudication** _(the classic vascular form — exertional leg pain relieved by rest; [[I73.9]], I70.21-)_
> - **Limping** _(lay term and the literal meaning of the Latin root; not a synonym for a coded diagnosis — see gait codes R26.-)_
> - **Vascular / arterial claudication** _(due to arterial insufficiency from PAD; relief within minutes of standing still)_
> - **Neurogenic claudication ([[pseudoclaudication]])** _(due to lumbar spinal stenosis; relieved by sitting or flexing forward; [[M48.062]])_
> - **Venous claudication** _(bursting leg pain with exertion from iliofemoral venous outflow obstruction; relief is slow and needs elevation)_
> - **One-block / two-block claudication** _(clinical shorthand for the walking distance at which symptoms start)_
> - **Jaw claudication** _(pain with chewing; classic symptom of giant cell [[arteritis]])_
> - **Mesenteric claudication** _(abdominal angina — postprandial pain from chronic mesenteric ischemia; [[K55.1]])_
> - **Arm claudication** _(exertional upper-extremity pain from subclavian or brachial arterial disease)_

> [!danger]+  🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **Rest pain** — pain at rest, usually in the forefoot and worse when the leg is elevated; signals chronic limb-threatening ischemia, a later stage than claudication
> - **[[Claudicant]]** — shares the *[[claud-]]* root; describes a person or gait marked by limping
> - **[[Peripheral artery disease]] (PAD)** — narrowing of the arteries to the limbs, the leading cause of vascular claudication; [[I73.9]] or I70.2-
> - **[[Atherosclerosis]]** — plaque buildup in the arterial wall and the main mechanism behind PAD; I70.2-
> - **[[Ischemia]]** — inadequate blood supply for tissue demand; the mechanism of exertional muscle pain
> - **[[Spinal stenosis]]** — narrowing of the spinal canal; the cause of neurogenic claudication, coded M48.06-
> - **Chronic limb-threatening ischemia (CLTI)** — advanced PAD with rest pain, ulcer, or [[gangrene]]
> - **Buerger disease** — [[thromboangiitis]] obliterans, an inflammatory small and medium vessel disease; [[I73.1]]
> - **Giant cell arteritis** — vasculitis that can cause jaw claudication; M31.6-
> - **Ankle-brachial index (ABI)** — ratio of ankle to arm systolic pressure; first-line screening test, where under 0.90 supports PAD
> - **Treadmill test** — exercise testing that measures walking distance and post-exercise ABI

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** **[[claudication]]** is integral to **PAD**, so do not report a separate symptom code when it is already captured. **Intermittent claudication** in a patient with documented **[[atherosclerosis]]** goes to **I70.21-** with laterality (**right, left, bilateral**), not to **[[I73.9]]**, which is the fallback for **PAD** or **PVD** with no **[[atherosclerosis]]** or specificity documented. Sequence the specific I70.- code first. Add a code for the underlying condition when the guidelines direct it, such as diabetes with **[[E11.51]]**, and add tobacco use or dependence codes where documented. Query when the provider writes "**leg cramping**," "**PVD**," or "**calf pain with walking**" without naming **[[atherosclerosis]]**, laterality, or whether the artery is native or a bypass graft. **Neurogenic claudication** is only codable to **[[M48.062]]** when the provider documents "**neurogenic claudication**" with **lumbar stenosis**, so "**stenosis with leg pain**" is not enough. Check the **LCD** and payer policy for noninvasive vascular studies (**[[93922]]-[[93926]]**) and for **revascularization** (**[[37220]]-[[37231]]**) for diagnosis and medical necessity edits. **[[claudication]]** often must be documented as lifestyle-limiting, with failed conservative therapy, to support intervention. Pair modifiers such as **[[-RT]]/[[-LT]], [[-50]],** or **[[-59]]** with procedures per site, and do the same for the **[[37220]]**-series codes when an endovascular repair spans more than one vessel.
