>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[autoimmune]]
>An autoimmune disease arises from the adaptive immune system's anomalous reaction, mistakenly targeting self-tissues as foreign invaders, affecting over 80 conditions and nearly any body part, **with symptoms including fatigue, joint pain, and rashes that fluctuate in flares and remissions**
_____
>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[autoimmune]]
>#greek The term "**autoimmune**," coined around 1952, combines Greek "[[auto-]]" (self, from αὐτός/autós) and English "*immune*" (**from Latin immunis, exempt from public service or obligation**).
_____

> [!warning]+ RELATED TERMS
>
>- **Autoantibody**: Antibody targeting self-antigens.​
>- **Autoimmunity**: The state of immune response against self​
>- **Examples of diseases**: Type 1 diabetes (pancreas attack), multiple sclerosis (myelin sheath), systemic lupus erythematosus (multi-organ).​

> [!NOTE] # A Word from MedlinePlus:
> 
> ## Autoimmune diseases
> 
> An **autoimmune disease** occurs when [the body's immune system](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000821.htm) attacks and destroys healthy body tissue by mistake. There are more than 80 **autoimmune** diseases.
> 
> ## Causes
> 
> The blood cells in the body's immune system help protect against harmful substances. Examples include bacteria, viruses, [toxins](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002331.htm), [cancer](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/001289.htm) cells, and blood and tissue from outside the body. These substances contain antigens. The immune system produces [antibodies](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002223.htm) against these antigens that enable it to destroy these harmful substances.
> 
> When you have an **autoimmune** disease, your immune system does not distinguish between healthy tissue and potentially harmful antigens. As a result, your body sets off a reaction that damages or destroys normal tissues.
> 
> The exact cause of **autoimmune** diseases is unknown. One theory is that some microorganisms (such as bacteria or viruses) or medicines may trigger changes that confuse the immune system. This may happen more often in people who have genes that make them more prone to **autoimmune** diseases.
> 
> An **autoimmune** disease may result in:
> 
> - The destruction of body tissue
> - Abnormal growth of an organ
> - Changes in organ function
> 
> An **autoimmune** disease may affect one or more organ or tissue types. Areas often affected by **autoimmune** diseases include:
> 
> - Blood vessels
> - Connective tissues
> - [Endocrine](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002351.htm) glands such as the thyroid or pancreas
> - Joints
> - Muscles
> - Red blood cells
> - Skin
> 
> A person may have more than one **autoimmune** disease at the same time. Common **autoimmune** diseases include:
> 
> - [Addison disease](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000378.htm)
> - [Celiac disease - sprue](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000233.htm) (gluten-sensitive enteropathy)
> - [Dermatomyositis](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000839.htm)
> - [Graves disease](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000358.htm)
> - [Hashimoto thyroiditis](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000371.htm)
> - Inflammatory bowel disease ([Crohn disease](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000249.htm), [ulcerative colitis](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000250.htm))
> - [Multiple sclerosis](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000737.htm)
> - [Myasthenia gravis](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000712.htm)
> - [Pernicious anemia](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000569.htm)
> - [Reactive arthritis](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000440.htm)
> - [Rheumatoid arthritis](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000431.htm)
> - [Sjögren syndrome](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000456.htm)
> - [Systemic lupus erythematosus](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000435.htm) (lupus)
> - [Type I diabetes](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/000305.htm)
> 
> ## Symptoms
> 
> Symptoms will vary, based on the type and location of the faulty immune response. Common symptoms include:
> 
> - [Fatigue](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003088.htm)
> - [Fever](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003090.htm)
> - [General ill feeling](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003089.htm) (malaise)
> - [Joint pain](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003261.htm)
> - [Rash](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003220.htm)
> 
> ## Exams and Tests
> 
> Your health care provider will do a physical exam. Signs on an exam depend on the type of disease.
> 
> Tests that may be done to diagnose an **autoimmune** disease include:
> 
> - [Antinuclear antibody (ANA) tests](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003535.htm)
> - Autoantibody tests
> - [Complete blood count (CBC)](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003642.htm) with white blood cell differential (CBC with WBC differential)
> - [Comprehensive metabolic panel](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003468.htm)
> - [C-reactive protein (CRP)](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003356.htm)
> - [Erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR)](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003638.htm)
> - [Urinalysis](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/003579.htm)
> 
> ## Treatment
> 
> The goals of treatment are to:
> 
> - Control the **autoimmune** process
> - Maintain your body's ability to fight disease
> - Reduce symptoms
> 
> Treatments will depend on your disease and symptoms. Types of treatments include:
> 
> - Supplements to replace a substance that the body lacks, such as thyroid hormone, vitamin B12, or insulin, due to the **autoimmune** disease
> - Blood transfusions if blood is affected
> - Physical therapy to help with movement if the bones, joints, or muscles are affected
> 
> Many people take medicines to reduce the immune system's abnormal response. These are called **immunosuppressive** medicines. Examples include corticosteroids (such as prednisone) and nonsteroid medicines such as azathioprine, cyclophosphamide, mycophenolate, sirolimus, or tacrolimus. Targeted medicines such as tumor [[necrosis]] factor (TNF) blockers and Interleukin inhibitors can be used for some diseases.
> 
> ## Outlook (Prognosis)
> 
> The outcome depends on the disease. Most **autoimmune** diseases are [chronic](https://medlineplus.gov/ency/article/002312.htm), but many can be controlled with treatment.
> 
> Symptoms of **autoimmune** diseases can come and go. When symptoms get worse, it is called a flare-up.
> 
> ## Possible Complications
> 
> Complications depend on the disease. Medicines used to suppress the immune system can cause severe side effects, such as higher risk for infections.
> 
> ## When to Contact a Medical Professional
> 
> Contact your provider if you develop symptoms of an **autoimmune** disease.
> 
> ## Prevention
> 
> There is no known prevention for most **autoimmune** disorders.
