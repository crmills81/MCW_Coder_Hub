>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[lordosis]]
>**[[lordosis]]** is **the forward-facing (concave posterior) curve of the spine seen in the cervical and lumbar regions, which is a normal feature that helps absorb shock and maintain posture, or an exaggerated, abnormal increase in that curve ([[hyperlordosis]])**. Cleveland Clinic gives typical normal ranges of about 30 to 40 degrees in the cervical spine and 40 to 60 degrees in the lumbar spine. It differs from **[[kyphosis]]**, the outward (**convex posterior**) curve of the thoracic and sacral spine, and from **[[scoliosis]]**, a sideways curve with rotation. Its opposite is **[[flatback syndrome]]** or loss of **[[lordosis]]** (**[[hypolordosis]]**). The mechanism is a **sagittal-plane imbalance, usually from anterior pelvic tilt, weak abdominal and gluteal muscles with tight hip flexors**, increased abdominal weight, or a secondary effect of another spinal condition. It is physiological in the cervical and lumbar spine (**normal curve, pregnancy**), and pathological when exaggerated (**[[achondroplasia]], [[spondylolisthesis]], [[rickets]], [[muscular dystrophy]], [[cerebral palsy]], [[osteoporosis]], obesity, post-[[laminectomy]]**). The forms you'll code most are **postural lordosis** (**M40.4-**), lordosis, unspecified (**M40.5-**), **postsurgical lordosis** (**[[M96.4]]**), and **congenital spinal malformation** (**[[Q76.4]]**). It is often confused with **[[kyphosis]]** (**opposite direction, thoracic**) and lordotic posture, which is a functional, **correctable posture**, but true **lordosis** is documented as a curve abnormality on exam or imaging.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[lordosis]]
>#greek #new-latin
>
>|**Component**|**Origin**|**Meaning**|
>|:---:|---|---|
>|**[[lord-]]**|Greek _lordos_ (LOR-dos), "**bent backward**," of uncertain origin|"**bent backward**," "**curved inward**," "**arched**"|
>|**[[-osis]]**|Greek _-ōsis_ (OH-sis)|Noun-forming suffix — "**condition, process, or abnormal state**"|
>
> The word entered English in **1704** as *lordosis* (**noun**), borrowed from Modern (New) Latin ***lordosis***, from Greek ***lórdōsis*** — literally **"a bending back,"** from *lordós* ("**bent backward**") plus the suffix *-ōsis*. Etymonline records the Greek root as of uncertain origin, with possible cognates in Armenian, Celtic, and Germanic. The adjective *lordotic* followed in the same pattern as *kyphotic* and *scoliotic*. The root *lordos* connects lordosis to the entire **[[-osis]] spinal-curve family**: ***kyphosis*** (**kyphos "bent, hunchbacked" + [[-osis]] → outward curve of the spine**), ***[[scoliosis]]*** (**skolios "crooked" + [[-osis]] → sideways curve**), and *kyphoscoliosis* (**combined outward and sideways curve**). The suffix *[[-osis]]* is very productive in medical terminology, appearing in ***[[stenosis]]***, ***[[fibrosis]]***, ***[[sclerosis]]***, ***[[cirrhosis]]***, and ***[[necrosis]]***.

> [!success]+ 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **Lordotic** _(adjective form — "lordotic curve," "lordotic posture," "lordotic angle")_
> - **[[Hyperlordosis]]** _(clinical term for an exaggerated or excessive inward curve; used with lumbar and cervical sites)_
> - **Swayback** _(lay term for excessive lumbar lordosis, with a protruding abdomen and buttocks)_
> - **Saddle back / hollow back** _(historical and lay terms for the same exaggerated lumbar curve)_
> - **Increased lumbar lordosis** _(clinical descriptor used on exam and imaging reports; may be postural or structural)_
> - **[[Hypolordosis]]** _(opposite descriptor; loss or straightening of the normal curve; flatback syndrome is M40.3-)_
> - **Postural lordosis** _(etiologic subtype — correctable, from posture and muscle imbalance; M40.4-)_
> - **Congenital lordosis** _(etiologic subtype — present at birth, from a spinal malformation; [[Q76.4]])_
> - **Postsurgical lordosis** _(etiologic subtype — after laminectomy or other spinal surgery; [[M96.4]])_
> - **Neuromuscular lordosis** _(etiologic subtype — from muscular dystrophy or cerebral palsy; code the underlying disease)_
> - **Lumbar hyperlordosis** _(anatomic subtype — most common; [[M40.56]], [[M40.46]])_
> - **Cervical lordosis / loss of cervical lordosis** _(anatomic subtype — a radiology finding that is not codable alone; query the provider for a diagnosis)_

> [!danger]+ 🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **[[Kyphosis]]** — the opposite curve; outward (convex posterior) curvature of the thoracic spine, which can cause a compensatory lumbar lordosis (M40.0-, M40.1-, M40.2-)
> - **[[Scoliosis]]** — shares the spinal-curve *[[-osis]]* family; a lateral curve with vertebral rotation (M41.-)
> - **[[Flatback syndrome]]** — loss of the normal lumbar lordosis, with the trunk tilting forward (M40.3-)
> - **Sagittal balance** — the alignment of the head, trunk, and pelvis in the side view; lordosis is a key component
> - **Pelvic tilt** — anterior pelvic tilt increases lumbar lordosis, while posterior tilt decreases it
> - **[[spondylolisthesis]]** — forward slip of one vertebra on another, a known cause of increased lordosis (M43.1-)
> - **[[Achondroplasia]]** — genetic skeletal dysplasia commonly causing lumbar hyperlordosis ([[Q77.4]])
> - **[[Rickets]]** — vitamin D deficiency in children that can cause lordosis ([[E55.0]])
> - **[[Muscular dystrophy]]** — neuromuscular cause of hyperlordosis from weak trunk muscles (G71.0-)
> - **[[Cerebral palsy]]** — neuromuscular cause, with a higher incidence of severe hyperlordosis in spastic forms (G80.-)
> - **[[Osteoporosis]]** — bone loss that raises risk of vertebral fracture and curve change (M81.-)
> - **Cobb angle** — the standard radiographic measurement of spinal curves
> - **X-ray / MRI** — key diagnostic tools; a standing lateral X-ray measures the curve, while MRI evaluates associated disc and nerve findings

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** **Normal lordosis is never coded**. Only **an abnormal or exaggerated curve with documented clinical significance is reportable**, and the site is required (**cervical, thoracolumbar, lumbar, or lumbosacral**). Choose **M40.4-** when the provider documents "**postural**" **[[lordosis]]** and **M40.5-** for **lordosis** not otherwise specified, and check the **Excludes1 notes**, because congenital (**Q76.4-**) and postsurgical (**[[M96.4]]**) forms are not coded in **M40.-**. When **[[lordosis]]** is due to a documented disease such as **[[achondroplasia]], [[muscular dystrophy]], [[cerebral palsy]], or [[spondylolisthesis]]**, code the underlying disease and add the **lordosis code only** if it is separately documented and clinically significant. Radiology phrases like "**loss of cervical lordosis**," "**straightening of the lordosis**," or "**increased lumbar lordosis**" are findings, so query the provider for a diagnosis before coding them. Undercoding alerts for inpatient profee are "**swayback**," "**[[hyperlordosis]]**," and "**lumbar hyperlordosis**" with back pain, which should prompt a query for **M40.5-** or the underlying cause. Deformity correction surgery (**[[osteotomy]] [[22206]]-[[22216]], fusion [[22800]]-[[22812]], instrumentation add-ons**) needs documented levels, approach, and number of columns, and may need **[[-22]]** (increased procedural services), **[[-62]]** (co-surgeons), **[[-80]]** or **[[-82]]** (assistant surgeons), or **[[-78]]** for an unplanned return to the OR. Therapy codes **[[97140]]** and **[[97530]]** on the same day often need **[[-59]]** or **[[-XU]]** under NCCI, and many payers require **[[-GP]]** for services under a physical therapy plan of care and prior authorization for deformity surgery, with imaging (**standing lateral X-ray, Cobb angle, sagittal parameters**) supporting medical necessity.
