>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[chronic venous insufficiency]]
>**[[Chronic venous insufficiency]] (CVI)** is a **long-term failure of the lower-extremity venous system to return blood to the heart, resulting from valve incompetence (reflux), venous obstruction, or both**. It is the advanced end of **chronic venous disease** and corresponds to **CEAP clinical classes C3 through C6** (**[[edema]], skin changes, healed ulcer, active ulcer**), whereas **varicose veins** alone (C2) are not **CVI**. The mechanism is sustained **ambulatory venous [[hypertension]]**, which causes **capillary leak, inflammation, hemosiderin deposition, fibrosis of the skin and [[subcutaneous]] tissue ([[lipodermatosclerosis]]), and [[ulceration]]**. CVI is always pathological, and the usual causes are **primary valve degeneration (primary)** or **deep vein thrombosis** damage (**secondary, called postthrombotic syndrome**). Common coding presentations include venous insufficiency (**[[I87.2]]**), **chronic venous hypertension with ulcer** (**[[I87.311]]**), with **[[inflammation]]** (**[[I87.321]]**), with **[[ulcer]]** and **[[inflammation]]** (**[[I87.331]]**), and postthrombotic syndrome (**[[I87.001]]**). It is commonly confused with arterial insufficiency (**peripheral artery disease, I70.-**), which is a supply problem rather than a drainage problem, and with [[lymphedema]] ([[I89.0]]), which is a lymphatic failure and not a venous one.

> The phrase is a modern clinical compound. *Chronic* entered English in the **1500s** (**adjective**) via French *chronique*, from Greek *khronikos*, originally meaning "**of time**" and later applied to long-lasting disease. *Venous* is from Latin *venōsus* ("**full of veins**"), from *vena*. *Insufficiency* entered English in the **1400s** (**noun**) from Latin ***insufficientia*** — literally **"the state of not being enough."** The root *vena* ("**vein**") connects CVI to the **[[ven-]]** family: ***[[phlebitis]]*** (**[[phleb- ]]+ [[-itis]] → vein inflammation**), ***[[venipuncture]]*** (**vein + puncture → needle entry into a vein**), and *[[venule]]* (**small vein**). The negating prefix *[[in-]]* is productive in medical terminology and appears in ***[[incompetence]]***, ***[[insufficiency]]***, ***[[incontinence]]***, ***[[inflammation]]***, and ***[[insomnia]]***.

> [!success]+  🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **CVI** _(standard abbreviation; frequently used in vascular surgery, wound care, and dermatology notes)_
> - **Chronic venous disease (CVD)** _(umbrella term covering all CEAP classes C0-C6; CVI refers to the advanced C3-C6 stage)_
> - **Venous stasis** _(lay and clinical term; "venous stasis dermatitis," "venous stasis ulcer")_
> - **Venous reflux** _(backward blood flow through incompetent valves; the most common mechanism of CVI)_
> - **Venous hypertension** _(elevated pressure in leg veins; the indexing term for I87.3-)_
> - **Venous incompetence** _(valve failure; often used with the saphenofemoral junction or perforator veins)_
> - **Postthrombotic syndrome (PTS)** _(secondary CVI after DVT; I87.0-)_
> - **Stasis dermatitis** _(eczematous skin change from venous hypertension; with varicose veins I83.1-, without I[[87.2]])_
> - **Venous leg ulcer** _(CEAP C6; I87.31- or I83.0- plus L97.- for site and severity)_
> - **[[Lipodermatosclerosis]]** _(CEAP C4b; fibrosis and induration of the skin and subcutaneous fat of the lower leg)_
> - **Varicose veins** _(dilated, tortuous superficial veins; I83.9-, a frequent companion diagnosis)_
> - **Primary CVI** _(valve or wall degeneration without a clear cause)_
> - **Secondary CVI** _(due to DVT, trauma, or surgery)_

> [!danger]+  🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **Arterial insufficiency** — a supply problem rather than a drainage problem; reduced arterial inflow (peripheral artery disease) with pain, pallor, and non-healing ulcers
> - **Insufficiency** — shares the *[[-suffic-]]* root; failure of a structure to perform its function adequately (valvular, adrenal, renal)
> - **Varicose veins** — dilated, tortuous superficial veins from valve failure; CEAP C2, which is not CVI unless [[edema]], skin changes, or ulcer are present (I83.-)
> - **Postthrombotic syndrome** — secondary CVI from valve damage after DVT, coded I87.0-
> - **Venous reflux** — retrograde flow through incompetent valves; the pathophysiologic "R" in CEAP
> - **Venous obstruction** — blockage of outflow by thrombus or external compression; the "O" in CEAP (see [[compression]])
> - **Ambulatory venous pressure** — the pressure in the leg veins during walking, which stays elevated in CVI
> - **Calf muscle pump** — the calf muscles that squeeze blood upward against gravity; failure of this pump contributes to CVI
> - **Perforator veins** — connect superficial and deep systems, and incompetence here drives ulcers
> - **CEAP classification** — Clinical, Etiology, Anatomy, Pathophysiology staging system, C0 through C6
> - **[[Deep vein thrombosis]]** — the leading cause of secondary CVI; chronic forms coded I82.5-
> - **[[Lymphedema]]** — often coexists with CVI as [[phlebolymphedema]]; [[I89.0]]
> - **Venous duplex ultrasound** — the key diagnostic test to document reflux (more than 0.5 seconds) and obstruction
> - **Compression therapy** — the cornerstone of CVI treatment; stockings, wraps, and multi-layer systems

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** **[[I87.2]]** is a single code with no laterality, while **I87.3-** and **I87.0-** require laterality, so query when the note says only "**venous [[stasis]]**" or "**CVI with ulcer**." For **ulcer** codes (**[[I87.011]], [[I87.311]], [[I87.331]] and their laterality variants**), add a code from **L97.-** to show ulcer site and severity, and sequence the **I87** code first. Undercoding alert: "**[[venous]] stasis ulcer,**" "**venous [[hypertension]]**," and "**[[stasis]] dermatitis with ulcer**" in the physical exam often point to the more specific **I87.3-** or **I87.0- codes** rather than **[[I87.2]]**, but check the **ICD-10-CM Index** and the provider's documented cause before you change the code. A history of **DVT** with leg swelling should prompt a query for **postthrombotic syndrome**. When the provider documents both varicose veins and **CVI**, review the **Index and Tabular** carefully before assigning **I83.-** and **I87.-** together. For **endovenous ablation** (**[[36475]]-[[36483]]**), most payers require documented failure of conservative **compression therapy**, duplex-confirmed reflux, and symptoms. Many **Medicare** contractors have an **LCD** for varicose vein treatment, so check yours before submitting, and use modifiers **[[-RT]]/[[-LT]] or [[-50]]** as the payer requires. Verify all codes against your **FY 2027 ICD-10-CM** and **CPT 2027 encoder** before final entry.
