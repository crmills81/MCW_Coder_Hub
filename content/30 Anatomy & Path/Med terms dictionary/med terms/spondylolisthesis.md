>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[spondylolisthesis]]
>**[[spondylolisthesis]]** is **the slipping or displacement of one vertebra relative to the vertebra below it, most commonly the forward (anterior) slip of L5 on S1 or L4 on L5, though lateral and backward slips occur**. It differs from **[[spondylolysis]]**, a defect or stress fracture in the pars interarticularis without slippage, and from **[[retrolisthesis]]**, a backward slip, with **[[anterolisthesis]]** meaning the forward slip. The mechanism is loss of the bony or disc and facet restraints that hold the vertebrae in line, from a pars defect, **[[degeneration]]** of the **facet joints and disc, a congenital arch malformation, trauma, or surgery**. It is almost always pathological (**it is not a normal variant**), but it can be asymptomatic and found incidentally on imaging. The Wiltse classification divides it by cause into dysplastic **(I), isthmic (II, with a lytic fracture, elongated pars, or acute pars fracture), degenerative (III), traumatic (IV), pathologic (V), and iatrogenic (VI)**, while the Meyerding grading rates severity by percent slip from grade I (**under 25%**) to grade V (**complete slip, [[spondyloptosis]]**). The forms you'll code most are **[[spondylolisthesis]]** by site (**M43.1-, most often [[M43.16]] and [[M43.17]]**), **[[spondylolysis]]** (**M43.0-**), **congenital [[spondylolisthesis]]** (**[[Q76.2]]**), and **acute traumatic [[subluxation]]** (**[[S33.1XXA]]**). It is often confused with **[[spondylolysis]]** (**the defect, with no slip**) and **[[spondylosis]]** (**degenerative wear without slippage**), but **[[spondylolisthesis]]** requires documented vertebral translation.

>|**[[olisth-]]**|Greek _olisthēsis_ (oh-LIS-thay-sis, "**slipping**"), from _olisthánein_ (oh-lis-THAN-ine, "to slip")|"**to slip**," "**slippage**," "**sliding**"|
>|**[[-esis]]**|Greek _-ēsis_ (AY-sis)|Noun-forming suffix — "**act, process, or state of**" (as in "**slipping**")|

> The word entered English in the **19th century** (exact first-use date not verified) as *spondylolisthesis* (**noun**), borrowed from New Latin ***spondylolisthesis***, a compound of Greek ***spóndylos*** and ***olísthēsis*** — literally **"vertebra slipping."** A PubMed paper on the term states that it derives from the Greek *olisthánein* ("**to slip**"), and that the English words for slipping of vertebrae or joints are "olisthy" and "olisthe." Other sources give *oliothesis* or "**dislocation**" as the second element. The root *olisthánein* ("**to slip**") connects spondylolisthesis to the entire **[[-listhesis]] family**: ***[[anterolisthesis]]*** (**[[anter-]] + [[-listhesis]] → forward slip**), ***[[retrolisthesis]]*** (**[[retro-]] + listhesis → backward slip**), and ***[[laterolisthesis]]*** (**[[latero-]] + [[-listhesis]] → sideways slip**). The root *[[spondyl-]]* is productive in medical terminology, appearing in ***[[spondylolysis]]*** (**[[spondylo-]] + [[-lysis]] → vertebra dividing**), ***[[spondylosis]]***, ***[[spondylitis]]***, ***[[spondylodiscitis]]***, and ***[[spondyloarthropathy]]***.

> [!success]+ 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **[[Spondylolistheses]]** _(plural form; slips at more than one level)_
> - **[[Listhesis]]** _(shortened clinical and radiology term for vertebral slippage; also used as a suffix, as in "anterolisthesis")_
> - **Slipped vertebra** _(lay term; not to be confused with a "slipped disc," which is a herniated disc)_
> - **[[Anterolisthesis]]** _(directional subtype — forward slip of the upper vertebra on the one below; the usual meaning of spondylolisthesis)_
> - **[[Retrolisthesis]]** _(directional subtype — backward slip; indexed to spondylolisthesis in my recall, verify in the Alphabetic Index)_
> - **[[Pseudospondylolisthesis]]** _(degenerative form — slip with an intact pars; also called degenerative spondylolisthesis)_
> - **Degenerative spondylolisthesis** _(etiologic subtype — Wiltse type III; older adults, most often L4-L5; [[M43.16]])_
> - **Isthmic spondylolisthesis** _(etiologic subtype — Wiltse type II; pars defect, most often L5-S1; [[M43.17]])_
> - **Dysplastic / congenital spondylolisthesis** _(etiologic subtype — Wiltse type I; congenital malformation of the arch; [[Q76.2]])_
> - **Traumatic spondylolisthesis** _(etiologic subtype — Wiltse type IV; acute fracture of the posterior arch or dislocation; [[S33.1XXA]])_
> - **Pathologic spondylolisthesis** _(etiologic subtype — Wiltse type V; due to tumor, infection, or bone disease)_
> - **Iatrogenic spondylolisthesis** _(etiologic subtype — Wiltse type VI; after decompression or other spinal surgery)_

> [!danger]+ 🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **[[Spondylolysis]]** — shares the *[[spondyl-]]* root; a defect or stress fracture of the pars interarticularis, a frequent precursor of isthmic slips (M43.0-)
> - **[[Spondylosis]]** — degenerative changes of the spine (osteophytes, disc narrowing) without slippage (M47.-)
> - **[[Anterolisthesis]]** — forward slip of one vertebra on the one below
> - **[[Retrolisthesis]]** — backward slip of one vertebra on the one below
> - **Pars interarticularis** — the bony bridge between the superior and inferior facets; the defect site in isthmic forms
> - **Meyerding grade** — five-grade scale by percent of slip: I (0-25%), II (25-50%), III (50-75%), IV (75-100%), V (over 100%, spondyloptosis)
> - **[[Spondyloptosis]]** — complete forward displacement of a vertebra off the one below (Meyerding grade V)
> - **Wiltse classification** — etiologic system dividing slips into types I-VI (dysplastic, isthmic, degenerative, traumatic, pathologic, iatrogenic)
> - **Spinal instability** — abnormal motion between vertebrae, often seen on flexion-extension films (M53.2X-)
> - **Spinal [[stenosis]]** — canal or foraminal narrowing that commonly accompanies degenerative slips (M48.0-)
> - **[[Radiculopathy]]** — nerve root compression from foraminal narrowing, producing leg pain, numbness, or weakness (M54.1-)
> - **Neurogenic [[claudication]]** — leg pain and weakness on walking from stenosis, relieved by sitting ([[M48.062]])
> - **[[Sciatica]]** — leg pain along the sciatic nerve, a common symptom in lumbar slips (M54.3-)
> - **Cauda equina syndrome** — severe nerve root compression with bowel, bladder, or saddle numbness ([[G83.4]])
> - **[[Arthrodesis]]** — spinal fusion, the usual surgical treatment for symptomatic or unstable slips
> - **X-ray / MRI** — key diagnostic tools; standing lateral X-ray (with flexion-extension views) measures the slip and MRI shows nerve compression

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** **M43.1-** requires the site, so the documented level drives the code: **L4-L5 slip is lumbar** (**[[M43.16]]**), and **L5-S1 is lumbosacral** (**[[M43.17]]**). **ICD-10-CM** does not code the **Meyerding grade or Wiltse type**, but the type does matter: congenital forms go to **[[Q76.2]]**, acute traumatic forms to **[[S33.1XXA]]**, and **AAPC** shows **Excludes1 notes** under **[[M43.16]]** for both. **[[M43.0]]** (**[[spondylolysis]]**) has an **Excludes1 note** for **[[spondylolisthesis]]** (M43.1-) and for **congenital [[spondylolysis]]** (**[[Q76.2]]**), so do not report **[[spondylolysis]]** and **[[spondylolisthesis]]** together at the same site. Neural findings are separate diagnoses, so query for and code **[[radiculopathy]]**, **[[stenosis]]** with or without **neurogenic [[claudication]]**, and cauda equina when documented, and check the Excludes notes at each code. Common undercodes are "**grade I [[anterolisthesis]]**," "**pars defect**," "**slip**," and "**instability**" in an operative or imaging note, which should prompt a query for the diagnosis and level, since imaging text alone is not codable. For **CPT**, **[[fusion]]** codes are chosen by technique (**posterior or posterolateral, posterior interbody, combined, anterior interbody, or L5-S1 pre-sacral**) and by interspace, and instrumentation (**[[22840]]**-**[[22842]]**) and bone graft (**[[20930]]**-**[[20939]]**) are add-ons. **NCCI** does not allow **[[63030]]**, **[[63042]]**, or **[[63047]]** separately with **[[22633]]** at the same interspace, so confirm whether the **[[decompression]]** is part of the **[[fusion]]** approach. Use **[[-59]]** or **[[-XS]]** only when a **decompression** is at a different level, and **[[-22]]** for increased procedural services, **[[-62]]** for co-surgeons (**common with a spine team**), **[[-80]]** or **[[-82]]** for assistant surgeons, and **[[-78]]** for an unplanned return to the OR. **[[Fusion]]** usually needs payer prior authorization and documentation of failed non-operative care (**often 6 months**), progressive deficit, or instability on **[[flexion]]-extension films**.
