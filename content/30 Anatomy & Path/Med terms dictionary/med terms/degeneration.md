>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[degeneration]]
>**[[degeneration]]** is **the gradual, usually progressive loss of normal structure and function in a cell, tissue, or organ, in which the tissue changes toward a poorer or less specialized state**. It differs from **[[atrophy]]**, which is a decrease in size or cell number, and from **[[necrosis]]**, which is **cell death with [[inflammation]]**. Its opposite is **[[regeneration]]**, where tissue is restored. The mechanism is cumulative cellular injury: **mitochondrial and oxidative stress, protein misfolding, impaired repair, loss of blood or nerve supply, and matrix breakdown such as dehydrated collagen or proteoglycan loss in cartilage and discs.** It can be physiological (**age-related disc [[dehydration]], arcus senilis**) or pathological (**[[Alzheimer disease]], [[macular degeneration]], Wallerian [[degeneration]] after nerve injury**). The subtypes you'll code most are **intervertebral disc degeneration** (**M50.3-, M51.3-**), **macular degeneration** (**H35.3-**), **peripheral retinal** and **lattice degeneration** (**H35.4-**), **nervous system degeneration** (**G31.-**), and **degenerative joint disease/[[osteoarthritis]]** (**M15-M19**). It is often confused with **[[atrophy]]** (**shrinkage of a still-normal tissue**) and with **[[dystrophy]]** (**a usually inherited, defective-nutrition or defective-growth process**), but **[[degeneration]]** is defined by the deteriorating quality of the tissue itself.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[degeneration]]
>#latin
>
>|**Component**|**Origin**|**Meaning**|
>|:---:|---|---|
>|**[[de-]]**|Latin _dē_ (deh)|"**down from**," "**away from**," "**off**" — separative/privative prefix|
>|**[[gen-]]**|Latin _genus_ (GEH-noos), genitive _generis_, from PIE root *_gene-_ ("to give birth, beget")|"**birth**," "**race**," "**kind**," "**descent**"|
>|**[[-ation]]**|Latin _-ātiō_ (AH-tee-oh)|Noun-forming suffix — "**act, process, or result of**"|
>
> The word entered English in the **late 15th century** as *degeneration* (**noun**), from Late Latin ***dēgenerātiō*** (stem *dēgenerātiōn-*), from Latin ***dēgenerāre*** — literally from the phrase **"de genere," "away from one's kind/stock,"** meaning "**to be inferior to one's ancestors.**" The verb and adjective *degenerate* appeared in English in the 1540s and late 15th century respectively, and carried the sense of losing the qualities proper to the race or kind before being applied to tissues. The root *genus* ("**birth, kind**") connects degeneration to the entire **[[-gen]] family**: ***genesis*** (**[[gen-]] + [[-esis]] → origin, creation**), ***pathogen*** (**[[path-]] + [[-gen]] → disease producer**), and *regeneration* (**[[re-]] + generation → renewed production**). The Latin prefix *[[de-]]* is very productive in medical terminology, appearing in ***[[dehydration]]***, ***[[demyelination]]***, ***[[denervation]]***, ***[[decalcification]]***, and ***[[decompensation]]***.

> [!success]+ 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **[[Degenerative]]** _(adjective form — "degenerative joint disease," "degenerative disc disease," "degenerative myopia")_
> - **Deterioration** _(lay and clinical term; common in progress notes for declining function, especially chronic disease and geriatrics)_
> - **Degenerative changes** _(imaging/radiology phrasing — e.g., "mild degenerative changes of L4-L5"; this is a finding, so a provider must link it to a diagnosis before you code it)_
> - **Wear and tear** _(lay synonym for age-related joint and disc breakdown; clinically osteoarthritis or spondylosis)_
> - **Degenerative joint disease (DJD)** _(synonym for osteoarthritis, coded to M15-M19)_
> - **Degenerative disc disease (DDD)** _(intervertebral disc degeneration; M50.3-, M51.3-)_
> - **[[Neurodegeneration]]** _(progressive loss of neurons — Alzheimer, Parkinson, Lewy body, frontotemporal; G30-G31 range)_
> - **Wallerian degeneration** _(etiologic subtype — degeneration of the axon distal to a nerve injury or transection)_
> - **Fatty degeneration** _(etiologic/metabolic subtype — intracellular lipid accumulation, e.g., liver or myocardium)_
> - **Macular degeneration** _(anatomic subtype — central retina; AMD, dry/nonexudative and wet/exudative; H35.3-)_
> - **Lattice degeneration** _(anatomic subtype — peripheral retinal thinning, a risk factor for tears and detachment; H35.41-)_
> - **Vitreous degeneration** _(anatomic subtype — liquefaction/syneresis causing floaters; H43.81-)_
> - **Corneal degeneration** _(anatomic subtype — arcus senilis, nodular degeneration, band keratopathy; H18.4-)_

> [!danger]+ 🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **[[Regeneration]]** — the opposite of degeneration; restoration or regrowth of lost tissue through cell proliferation and repair
> - **[[Generation]]** — shares the *[[gen-]]* root; the production or formation of something new
> - **[[Atrophy]]** — decrease in size of a tissue or organ from reduced cell size or number; may follow chronic degeneration (e.g., optic atrophy, H47.2-)
> - **[[Dystrophy]]** — a defective-growth or defective-nutrition process, usually inherited (corneal, retinal, muscular); not simply age-related wear
> - **[[Necrosis]]** — irreversible cell death with inflammation; degeneration can progress toward necrosis if the injury is not reversed
> - **[[Apoptosis]]** — programmed cell death that underlies both physiological and pathological degeneration
> - **[[Demyelination]]** — loss of the myelin sheath, as in multiple sclerosis (G35.-), a degenerative-type process affecting conduction
> - **[[Osteoarthritis]]** — degenerative joint disease with cartilage loss and osteophytes (M15-M19)
> - **[[Spondylosis]]** — degenerative spinal osteoarthritis (M47.-)
> - **[[Alzheimer disease]]** — neurodegenerative disease with cortical atrophy and plaque/tangle deposition (G30.-)
> - **Age-related macular degeneration** — leading cause of central vision loss in older adults (H35.3-)
> - **Myocardial degeneration** — degenerative change in heart muscle ([[I51.5]])
> - **Hepatolenticular degeneration** — Wilson disease, an inherited copper-metabolism disorder ([[E83.01]])
> - **MRI / OCT** — key diagnostic tools: MRI for disc and brain degeneration, optical coherence tomography for macular degeneration

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** "**[[degeneration]]**" is not codable on its own. You need the site and **type, and for the spine, eye, and joints** you also need laterality or region. Disc degeneration with **[[myelopathy]]** (M51.0-) or **[[radiculopathy]]** (M51.1-) is coded to those codes instead of M51.3-, so check the **Excludes notes**. Sequence the underlying disease first when a manifestation code applies, such as **[[G32.0]]** or **[[G32.81]]**, which carry "**code first**" instructions. **Radiology** wording like "**degenerative changes**" can't be coded from the imaging report alone, so query the provider for a diagnosis. Common inpatient profee triggers are "**DJD**," "**DDD**," "**degenerative changes**," and "**[[dementia]], [[neurodegenerative]]**," which should prompt a query for type (**Alzheimer vs. Lewy body vs. frontotemporal**). Eye codes need **RT/LT/50 laterality** on the CPT side (**for example [[-RT]], [[-LT]], [[-50]]**), and a separately reported E/M on the same day as a minor procedure may need **[[-25]]**. **Intravitreal anti-VEGF drugs** and many spinal procedures need payer prior authorization, so confirm **exudative vs. nonexudative AMD**, stage, and the documented failed conservative therapy for disc surgery.
