>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[acromioclavicular]]
>**[[acromioclavicular]]** pertains to **the anatomical structures, specifically the diarthrodial joint and its supporting ligaments, located between the [[acromion]] of the [[scapula]] and the distal [[clavicle]]**. This joint functions as a strut to help connect the **upper extremity to the axial skeleton**, allowing for the transmission of forces and facilitating overhead arm movements. It is distinct from the **[[glenohumeral]] joint** (**the primary ball-and-socket shoulder joint**) and the **[[sternoclavicular]] joint** (**which connects the clavicle to the sternum**). Physiologically, it provides stability and a pivot point for shoulder elevation; pathologically, it is highly susceptible to traumatic separation (sprains or dislocations, coded to **S43.5-** and **S43.1-**) and **degenerative wear-and-tear** such as **[[osteoarthritis]]** (**M19.01-**). It is most commonly confused with the **[[coracoclavicular]] complex**, which refers to the nearby stabilizing ligaments (**conoid and trapezoid**) that are often concurrently torn in severe **acromioclavicular separations**, but do not form the articular joint space itself.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[acromioclavicular]]
>#latin #greek
>
>|**Component**|**Origin**|**Meaning**|
>|:---:|---|---|
>|**[[acromio-]]**|Greek _akromion_ (akromion), from _akros_ (highest) + _omos_ (shoulder)|"**acromion**," "**highest point of the shoulder**" — anatomical combining form|
>|**[[claviculo-]]**|Latin _clavicula_ (klah-VIK-yoo-lah), from _clavis_ (key)|"**clavicle**," "**collarbone**," "**little key**" — anatomical combining form|
>|**[[-ar]]**|Latin _-aris_ (ah-ris)|Adjective-forming suffix — "**pertaining to**"|
>
> The word entered English in the **1840s** as *acromioclavicular* (**adjective**), formed by combining the anatomical roots for the acromion and the clavicle to describe their articulation — literally **"pertaining to the acromion and the clavicle."** The root *clavicula* ("**little key**") connects acromioclavicular to the entire **[[clavicul-]]** family: ***[[sternoclavicular]]*** (**pertaining to the sternum and clavicle**), ***[[coracoclavicular]]*** (**pertaining to the coracoid process and clavicle**), and *[[supraclavicular]]* (**above the clavicle**). The combining form *[[acromio-]]* is highly productive in orthopedic medical terminology, appearing in terms like ***[[acromioplasty]]***, ***[[subacromial]]***, and ***[[acromiohumeral]]***.

> [!success]+ 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **[[acromioclavicular]]** _(adjective form — e.g., "acromioclavicular joint," "acromioclavicular ligament")_
> - **AC joint** _(lay and clinical acronym; heavily used in PM&R, orthopedics, and radiology)_
> - **AC articulation** _(clinical synonym used in anatomical and surgical documentation)_
> - **AC separation** _(related clinical entity — traumatic sprain or dislocation of the joint; S43.5- or S43.1-)_
> - **AC joint [[osteoarthritis]]** _(related clinical entity — degenerative joint disease of the AC joint; M19.01-)_
> - **Mumford procedure target** _(clinical descriptor synonym — the AC joint is the target of a distal clavicle excision)_
> - **Type I-VI AC injury** _(etiologic subtypes — Rockwood classification system for AC joint separations based on ligamentous tearing and displacement)_

> [!danger]+ 🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **[[Glenohumeral]]** — the primary shoulder joint; distinct from the AC joint, it is a major ball-and-socket joint (injections here are coded as major joints, whereas AC is intermediate).
> - **[[Sternoclavicular]]** — shares the *[[clavicul-]]* root; the joint at the opposite (medial) end of the clavicle connecting to the sternum.
> - **[[Coracoclavicular]]** — ligaments (conoid and trapezoid) that stabilize the clavicle to the coracoid process; rupture of these ligaments defines higher-grade AC separations.
> - **[[Acromioplasty]]** — surgical reshaping of the acromion, often performed concurrently with AC joint procedures for impingement syndrome.
> - **[[Osteoarthritis]]** — degenerative cellular mechanism term; progressive loss of articular cartilage, highly common in the AC joint due to repetitive overhead motion.
> - **Shoulder Impingement Syndrome** — complex syndrome (M75.4-) that overlaps with AC joint pathology; inferior osteophytes from the AC joint often impinge on the rotator cuff.
> - **Distal [[Claviculectomy]]** — primary surgical procedure associated with this term; excision of the distal end of the clavicle to relieve AC joint pain (Mumford procedure).

> 
> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** For **PM&R** and **orthopedic coders**, a critical distinction exists between the **AC joint** and the **glenohumeral joint for injection coding**. The AC joint is classified by CPT as an *intermediate* joint (**[[20605]]/[[20606]]**), whereas the **glenohumeral joint** is a *major* joint (**[[20610]]/[[20611]]**); miscoding this will result in claim denials or audit risks. When coding traumatic AC separations (**S43.1- or S43.5-**), ensure the 7th character (**A, D, or S**) accurately reflects the phase of care, and always code to the highest level of laterality. An undercoding alert frequently occurs with **arthroscopic distal [[claviculectomy]]** (**[[29824]]**) performed alongside other **shoulder arthroscopies** (**e.g., rotator cuff repair**); per **CMS/NCCI guidelines**, to bill **[[29824]]** separately, the operative report must explicitly document the removal of approximately 1 cm of the **distal clavicle** (**not merely shaving osteophytes**). If the documentation only states "**AC joint [[debridement]]**" without specifying the **1 cm excision**, query the provider or bundle it into the primary arthroscopy code.
