>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[myopathy]]
>**[[myopathy]]** is **a broad category of neuromuscular diseases in which the primary defect is within the muscle fiber itself, leading to muscular weakness, cramping, stiffness, or [[spasm]]**. It is clinically distinguished from **[[neuropathy]],** where **muscle weakness** is secondary to nerve damage or **[[denervation]]**. The underlying pathological mechanisms can be genetic (**such as mutations affecting muscle structural proteins**), inflammatory (**[[autoimmune]] attack on muscle fibers**), metabolic (**defects in glycogen or lipid metabolism**), or toxic (**drug-induced damage**). While some myopathies are congenital and present in infancy (**e.g., congenital myopathies, muscular dystrophies**), many are acquired and pathological (**e.g., statin-induced myopathy, [[polymyositis]]**). The clinically relevant subtypes most commonly encountered in coding include **toxic/drug-induced myopathy** (**[[G72.0]]**), inflammatory myopathies like **[[polymyositis]]** (**M33.2x**), and unspecified **[[myopathy]]** (**[[G72.9]]**). **[[myopathy]]** is commonly confused with **[[myalgia]]**; however, **[[myalgia]]** refers strictly to muscle pain, whereas **[[myopathy]]** indicates actual structural or functional disease of the muscle tissue, which may or may not be painful.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[myopathy]]
>#greek
>
>|Component|Origin|Meaning|
>|---|---|---|
>|**[[myo-]]** / **[[my-]]**|Greek _mys_ (moos)|"**muscle**," "**mouse**" — primary anatomical combining form|
>|**[[-pathy]]**|Greek _pathos_ (pah-thos), from _paschein_ (to suffer)|"**disease**," "**suffering**," "**feeling**" — Noun-forming suffix indicating a disease or disorder|
>|**[[-ic]]**|Greek _-ikos_ (ee-kos)|Adjective-forming suffix — "**pertaining to**"|
>
> The word entered English in the **1840s** as *myopathy* (**noun**), borrowed from French *myopathie*, from Modern Latin ***myopathia***, from Greek ***mys*** + ***pathos*** — literally **"muscle disease."** The adjective form *myopathic* appeared shortly thereafter to describe clinical findings (e.g., "**myopathic changes on EMG**"). The root *[[mys-]]* ("**muscle**") connects **[[myopathy]]** to the entire **-MYO FAMILY**: ***[[myocardium]]*** (**muscle + heart → heart muscle**), ***[[myositis]]*** (**muscle + inflammation → inflammation of muscle**), and *[[myalgia]]* (**muscle pain**). The suffix *[[-pathy]]* is highly productive in medical terminology, appearing in numerous disease states such as ***[[neuropathy]]***, ***[[cardiomyopathy]]***, and ***[[retinopathy]]***.

> [!success]+ 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **Myopathic** _(adjective form — e.g., "myopathic motor unit potentials," "myopathic gait")_
> - **Muscle disease** _(lay and clinical term; often used broadly in primary care or pediatrics)_
> - **[[Myositis]]** _(inflammatory form of the condition; characterized by muscle inflammation and weakness)_
> - **Primary muscle disorder** _(clinical synonym used in neurology/PMR to distinguish from neurogenic weakness)_
> - **[[Sarcopenia]]** _(age- or immobility-related form of muscle loss and weakness; [[M62.84]])_
> - **[[Cachexia]]** _(systemic wasting syndrome, often malignancy- or chronic disease-related, involving severe myopathy)_
> - **Toxic myopathy** _(define by cause — e.g., due to statins, corticosteroids, or alcohol)_
> - **Metabolic myopathy** _(define by cause — e.g., from glycogen storage diseases like Pompe disease)_
> - **Congenital myopathy** _(genetic/inherited form with ICD-10-CM code range — e.g., nemaline myopathy; G71.2x)_
> - **Endocrine myopathy** _(organ/systemic-specific form with ICD-10-CM code range — e.g., thyrotoxic myopathy; [[G73.7]])_
> - **Steroid myopathy** _(iatrogenic form with ICD-10-CM code range — e.g., prolonged corticosteroid use; [[G72.0]])_
> - **Critical illness myopathy** _(ICU-acquired form with ICD-10-CM code range — e.g., severe weakness post-ventilation; [[G72.81]])_

> [!danger]+ 🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **[[Neuropathy]]** — the opposite/counterpart of **myopathy**; disease of the peripheral nerves causing weakness via denervation, distinguished from myopathy by sensory loss and distal (rather than proximal) weakness.
> - **[[Myalgia]]** — shares the *[[myo-]]* root; muscle pain without necessarily involving structural muscle disease or weakness.
> - **[[Rhabdomyolysis]]** — severe, rapid breakdown of skeletal muscle tissue leading to myoglobinuria and potential acute kidney injury; [[M62.82]].
> - **[[Fibromyalgia]]** — complex chronic pain syndrome overlapping with myalgia and fatigue, but without the structural muscle fiber destruction seen in true myopathies.
> - **[[Denervation]]** — loss of nerve supply to a muscle, the physiological mechanism causing neurogenic muscle atrophy (as opposed to primary myopathic atrophy).
> - **[[Myotrophic]]** — adjective describing hormonal or nutritional inputs that sustain or promote muscle tissue growth.
> - **[[Apoptosis]]** — programmed cellular death underlying certain pathological forms of muscle wasting and genetic myopathies.
> - **[[Muscular Dystrophy]]** — genetic, progressive disease entity defined by this term; includes Duchenne and Becker types (G71.0-).
> - **[[Polymyositis]]** — inflammatory disease entity defined by this term; autoimmune attack on proximal musculature (M33.2x).
> - **[[Cardiomyopathy]]** — clinical entity defined by this term at a specific anatomic site; disease of the heart muscle tissue.
> - **[[Dermatomyositis]]** — inflammatory myopathy with characteristic skin manifestations (Gottron papules, heliotrope rash) (M33.0x-M33.1x).
> - **[[Electromyography]]** — primary diagnostic tool (EMG) for evaluating [[myopathy]] and distinguishing it from neuropathy.

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** For **inpatient profee coding**, always sequence the underlying cause first if the **[[myopathy]]** is secondary to another condition or a toxin. For example, if coding **drug-induced [[myopathy]]** (**[[G72.0]]**), you must first code the appropriate T-code (**e.g., [[T46.6X5A]] for adverse effect of statins**) to identify the drug. A common undercoding issue occurs when providers document "**generalized weakness**" or "**deconditioning**" when the clinical picture and CK levels actually support a specific **[[myopathy]]** or **[[rhabdomyolysis]]**; this should prompt a query for clarification. When billing **EMG** services (**[[95860]]-[[95864]]**) in the inpatient setting, remember to append modifier **[[-26]]** (Professional Component) if the equipment is owned by the hospital. Finally, ensure you are not billing standalone **EMG** codes (**[[95860]]-[[95864]]**) alongside **Nerve Conduction Studies (NCS)** on the same day; use the add-on codes (**[[95885]], [[95886]]**) when **EMG** and **NCS** are performed concurrently.
