>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[compression]]
>**[[compression]]** is **the act of pressing together or the state of being pressed, and clinically it means external or internal pressure on a nerve, vessel, organ, or bone that reduces its caliber, blood flow, conduction, or structural height**. It is distinct from **[[traction]]** (a pulling-apart force) and from **[[decompression]]** (**the relief or surgical release of that pressure**), and it is not the same as **[[stenosis]]**, which is a fixed narrowing of the lumen or canal itself. The mechanism is mechanical: sustained pressure causes **[[ischemia]]**, impaired axonal transport, **[[demyelination]], venous congestion**, or, in bone, trabecular failure and loss of vertebral body height. **[[compression]]** can be physiological (**therapeutic [[compression]] stockings, chest compressions during CPR**) or pathological (**a herniated disc, [[tumor]], [[hematoma]], or [[edema]] pressing on adjacent tissue**). Common coding presentations include unspecified cord compression (**[[G95.20]]**), other cord compression (**[[G95.29]]**), **compression** of brain (**[[G93.5]]**), compression of vein (**[[I87.1]]**), **[[carpal tunnel syndrome]]** (**[[G56.00]]**), compression fracture or collapsed vertebra (**[[M48.50XA]]**), and **cardiac [[tamponade]]** (**[[I31.4]]**). Do not confuse **compression** with **[[stenosis]]**, which is the narrowing itself (**for example, spinal [[stenosis]] M48.0-**). **[[compression]]** is the effect on the tissue and **[[stenosis]]** is the structural cause, and the two are coded separately.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[compression]]
>#latin
>
>|**Component**|**Origin**|**Meaning**|
>|:---:|---|---|
>|**[[com-]]** / **[[con-]]**|Latin _com-_ (kom), assimilated form of _cum_ ("**with**")|"**together**," "**with**," "**completely**" — a combining prefix that implies bringing things together|
>|**[[-press-]]**|Latin _pressus_ (PRES-us), past participle of _premere_ (PREM-er-eh)|"**to press**," "**to squeeze**," "**to push down**"|
>|**[[-ion]]**|Latin _-iō_ (genitive _-iōnis_) (ee-oh)|Noun-forming suffix — "**act, process, or result of**"|
>
> The word entered English in the **early 1400s** as *compression* (**noun**), borrowed from Old French *compression*, from Latin ***compressiō*** (accusative *compressiōnem*), the noun of action from *comprimere* ("**to press together**"). It is literally **"a pressing together."** The verb *compress* and the adjective *compressive* followed from the same Latin stem. The root *premere* ("**to press**") connects compression to a large family of words: ***[[depression]]*** (**[[de-]] "down" + pressus → pressing down**), ***[[expression]]*** (**[[ex-]] "out" + pressus → pressing out**), ***[[impression]]*** (**in- "into" + pressus → pressing into**), and *[[suppression]]* (**[[sub-]] "under" + pressus → pressing under**). The prefix *[[com-]]/[[con-]]* is productive in medical terminology, appearing in ***[[concentric]]***, ***[[congenital]]***, ***[[contraction]]***, ***[[convergence]]***, and ***[[confluent]]***.

> [!success]+  🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **[[Compressive]]** _(adjective form — "compressive [[neuropathy]]," "compressive myelopathy," "compressive optic neuropathy")_
> - **[[Entrapment]]** _(clinical synonym, used especially for peripheral nerves — "nerve entrapment," "entrapment neuropathy")_
> - **[[Impingement]]** _(a pinching of soft tissue between bony surfaces — shoulder impingement; often used for nerve root or tendon involvement)_
> - **Compressed / collapsed vertebra** _(compression fracture of a vertebral body; coded to M48.5- or M80.0-)_
> - **Cord compression** _(spinal cord compressed by [[tumor]], disc, [[hematoma]], or [[abscess]]; G95.2-)_
> - **Brain compression** _(mass effect with shift or [[herniation]]; [[G93.5]])_
> - **Mass effect** _(radiology and neurosurgery term for tissue [[displacement]] or compression by a lesion, hemorrhage, or edema)_
> - **[[Tamponade]]** _(compression of the heart by pericardial fluid; [[I31.4]])_
> - **Venous compression** _(external narrowing of a vein, such as May-Thurner syndrome; [[I87.1]])_
> - **Nerve root compression** _([[radiculopathy]]-type compression from disc or osteophyte; see M54.1- and M50-M51 series)_
> - **Carpal tunnel syndrome** _(median nerve compression at the wrist; G56.0-)_
> - **Thoracic outlet syndrome** _(compression of the brachial plexus or subclavian vessels; [[G54.0]])_

> [!danger]+  🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **[[Decompression]]** — the opposite of [[compression]]; relief of pressure, either surgical ([[laminectomy]], [[craniectomy]], carpal tunnel release) or physiologic (altitude or diving ascent)
> - **[[Traction]]** — a pulling-apart force, the mechanical counterpart of compression; used in orthopedics for fracture alignment and cervical spine care
> - **Pressure** — shares the *[[press-]]* root; the force per unit area that produces compression
> - **[[Stenosis]]** — a fixed narrowing of a canal, foramen, or lumen that often causes compression of the contents (spinal stenosis, M48.0-)
> - **[[Radiculopathy]]** — nerve root dysfunction, most often caused by compression from a herniated disc or osteophyte (M54.1-)
> - **[[Myelopathy]]** — spinal cord dysfunction due to compression; coded as [[G95.20]] or [[G95.29]], or by the underlying cause (for example M47.1-)
> - **[[Ischemia]]** — the mechanism by which sustained compression injures nerves and tissue, through loss of blood supply
> - **[[Neuropraxia]]** — a mild, reversible nerve conduction block from compression, with the axon intact
> - **Compartment syndrome** — raised pressure within a closed fascial space that compresses muscles, nerves, and vessels; a surgical emergency
> - **[[Herniation]]** — [[displacement]] of tissue through an opening, often the cause of compression (disc herniation, brain [[herniation]])
> - **[[Tamponade]]** — compression of the heart by fluid in the pericardial sac ([[I31.4]])
> - **[[Lymphedema]]** — chronic swelling treated with compression bandaging or garments ([[I89.0]])
> - **[[chronic venous insufficiency]]** — venous disease treated with compression therapy ([[I87.2]])
> - **[[Electromyography]]** and nerve conduction study — key diagnostic tools for localizing nerve compression, along with MRI and CT imaging for spinal and intracranial causes

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** For **nerve compression**, laterality is required for **G56.0-**, so query when the provider documents only "**carpal tunnel**." For **cord compression**, code the underlying cause first when it is known (**[[neoplasm]], [[abscess]], disc disease, trauma**), and add **[[G95.20]]** or **[[G95.29]]** only when the **cord compression** is documented as a distinct manifestation. **[[compression]]** fractures are a common undercoding area on inpatient profee claims, because "**compression deformity**," "**wedge deformity**," or **"loss of vertebral height**" on imaging can't be coded without provider documentation of the cause. Query for traumatic (**S22.-/S32.-**), **osteoporotic** (**M80.-**), or pathological from **[[neoplasm]]** (**M84.5-**) versus nonspecific collapsed vertebra (**M48.5-**). Multi-layer **[[compression]]** system codes (**[[29581]]-[[29584]]**) include the supplies, and **Medicare** contractors do not cover them for unskilled **[[lymphedema]]** bandaging, so documentation should support wound or skilled need. Always confirm the **7th character** and every code against the **FY 2027 ICD-10-CM** and **CPT 2027** code sets in your encoder before final entry, since I could not verify each code's **FY 2027** status individually.
