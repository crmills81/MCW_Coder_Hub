>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[periorbital edema]]
>**Periorbital edema** refers to **the accumulation of excess fluid in the tissues surrounding the eye**, particularly affecting the eyelids and the area immediately around the orbit. This condition can be unilateral or bilateral and ranges from mild puffiness to severe swelling that may impair vision. 
>**Types**: 
>  - **Periorbital Edema**: Swelling of tissues around the eye (**eyelids and surrounding skin**). 
>  - **Orbital Edema**: Swelling within the bony orbit itself (**more serious, often indicates orbital cellulitis or other intraorbital pathology**). 
>**Etiology**: Causes include: 
>  - **Allergic reactions**: Seasonal allergies, contact dermatitis, insect bites, medication reactions. 
>  - **Infections**: [[Conjunctivitis]], [[blepharitis]], [[preseptal]]/[[orbital cellulitis]], [[sinusitis]], upper respiratory infections. 
>  - **Trauma**: Blunt injury, surgical procedures, cosmetic injections. 
>  - **Systemic conditions**: [[Thyroid eye disease ]](**Graves**'), kidney disease (**[[nephrotic syndrome]]**), heart failure, liver disease. 
>  - **Lifestyle factors**: Sleep deprivation, excessive salt intake, alcohol consumption, crying. 
>  - **Medications**: Calcium channel blockers, corticosteroids, **NSAIDs**, certain diabetes medications.
>  - **Clinical Presentation**: Puffiness, tightness, difficulty opening eyes, possible discoloration (**if traumatic**), may be associated with pain, itching, or visual changes depending on cause. 
>  - **Treatment**: Depends on underlying cause; may include antihistamines, cold compresses, elevation, antibiotics (**if infectious**), corticosteroids, or treatment of systemic condition.
>

> [!danger]+ **Related Terms**
> - **[[Blepharitis]]:** Inflammation of the eyelid margins; can cause periorbital edema.
> - **[[Conjunctivitis]]:** Inflammation of the conjunctiva; often associated with periorbital swelling.
> - **Preseptal Cellulitis:** Infection of eyelid and periorbital tissues anterior to the orbital septum.
> - **Orbital Cellulitis:** Infection of tissues within the bony orbit (posterior to septum); medical emergency.
> - **Angioedema:** Deep tissue swelling often affecting periorbital area; can be allergic or hereditary.
> - **Chemosis:** Swelling of the conjunctiva itself (not periorbital tissues).
> - **[[proptosis]]/[[proptosis|exophthalmos]]:** Forward displacement of the eyeball; different from periorbital edema but may co-occur.
> - **Thyroid Eye Disease (Graves' Ophthalmopathy):** Autoimmune condition causing periorbital edema and proptosis.
> - **Nephrotic Syndrome:** Kidney disorder causing generalized edema, often first noticed as periorbital edema in mornings.
> - **[[Dacryocystitis]]:** Infection of the lacrimal sac; can cause medial periorbital swelling.
> - **[[stye|hordeolum]] ([[stye]]):** Localized infection causing focal periorbital swelling.
> - **[[chalazion]]:** Chronic [[granulomatous]] inflammation of meibomian gland; causes localized lid swelling.
> - **Contact Dermatitis:** Allergic or irritant reaction causing **periorbital edema**.
> - **[[Superior Vena Cava Syndrome]]:** Can cause bilateral **periorbital edema** due to venous obstruction.

> [!important]+  ETYMOLOGY of [[periorbital edema]]
> 
> #greek Derived from three Greek/Latin roots:
> 
> - **[[Peri-]]:** From Greek _peri_, meaning "**around**" or "**surrounding**."
> - **Orbit:** From Latin _orbita_, meaning "**track**" or "**course**" (**referring to the eye socket/bony cavity containing the eye**).
> - **Edema:** From Greek _oidēma_, meaning "**swelling**" (from _oidein_, to swell).
> - Literally translates to "**swelling around the eye socket**."
> - First used in medical literature in the early 20th century as anatomical terminology became standardized.
>

> **Important Coding Notes:**
> 
> - **Laterality:** Most eyelid edema codes require specification of right eye (1), left eye (2), or bilateral (3).
> - **Underlying Cause:** Always code the underlying condition causing the edema (allergy, infection, systemic disease, trauma) in addition to the edema code.
> - **Periorbital vs. Orbital:** Distinguish between periorbital edema (tissues around eye) and orbital edema/cellulitis (within bony orbit) as orbital conditions are more serious.
> - **Acute vs. Chronic:** Documentation should specify if edema is acute or chronic; affects treatment and coding.
> - **Unilateral vs. Bilateral:** Bilateral periorbital edema more commonly suggests systemic cause (kidney, heart, thyroid); unilateral more commonly suggests local cause (infection, trauma, allergy).
> - **7th Character:** Trauma codes (S00-S05) require 7th character for encounter type (A=initial, D=subsequent, S=sequela).
> - **E/M vs. Eye Codes:** Ophthalmologists may use either standard E/M codes ([[99202]]-[[99215]]) or eye-specific codes ([[92002]]-[[92014]]) depending on payer requirements.
> - **Imaging:** CT or MRI of orbits is appropriate if orbital cellulitis, trauma, or mass is suspected; not routinely needed for simple allergic edema.
