>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[sciatica]]
>**[[sciatica]]** is **a symptom, not a diagnosis, of pain, numbness, tingling, or weakness along the path of the sciatic nerve, typically running from the lower back or buttock through the back of the thigh and calf to the foot, and usually on one side**. The sciatic nerve is the largest nerve in the body, formed by the **L4-S3 nerve roots**, and it supplies the muscles behind the knee and lower leg and sensation to the back of the thigh, part of the lower leg, and the sole of the foot. It differs from **[[lumbago]]** (**low back pain only**), from **[[radiculopathy]]** (**the nerve root disorder that most often causes it**), from **sciatic [[neuropathy]]** (**a lesion of the nerve itself, G57.0-**), and from **piriformis syndrome** (**compression of the nerve at the buttock by the piriformis muscle**). The mechanism is **[[compression]]** or **[[inflammation]]** of the lumbar or sacral nerve roots, most often from a **herniated disc** or **bone spurs**, though **spinal stenosis**, **[[spondylolisthesis]]**, **pelvic fracture**, **tumor**, or **piriformis entrapment** can cause it. It is always pathological (**symptom of an underlying cause**), and in many cases no cause is found. The forms you'll code most are **[[sciatica]]** (**M54.3-**), **lumbago with sciatica** (**M54.4-**), **disc disorders with [[radiculopathy]]** (**[[M51.16]], [[M51.17]]**), **lesion of sciatic nerve** (**G57.0-**), and **[[radiculopathy]]** (**[[M54.16]], [[M54.17]]**). It is often confused with **[[radiculopathy]]**, and in coding the choice matters: sciatica due to a disc disorder goes **to M51.1-**, not **M54.3-**.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[sciatica]]
>#greek #latin #medieval-latin
>
>|**Component**|**Origin**|**Meaning**|
>|:---:|---|---|
>|**[[ischi-]]**|Greek _iskhion_ (is-kee-ON), "**hip joint**, socket in which the thigh-bone turns"; Greek _iskhias_ (is-KEE-as), genitive _iskhiados_, "**pain in the hips**"|"**hip**," "**hip socket**," "**ischium**"|
>|**[[-ic]]**|Greek _-ikos_ (ee-kos), via Latin _-icus_|Adjective-forming suffix — "**pertaining to**" (Greek _iskhiadikos_, Latin _ischiadicus_)|
>|**[[-a]]** (feminine noun ending)|Medieval Latin _sciatica_, from _sciatica passio_ ("**sciatic disease**")|Noun-forming feminine ending, with _passio_ understood — "**the sciatic (disease)**"|
>
> The word entered English around **1400** as *sciatica* (**noun**), borrowed from Medieval Latin ***sciatica***, in the phrase ***sciatica passio*** ("**sciatic disease**"), a corruption of Latin ***ischiadicus*** ("of pain in the hip"), from Greek ***iskhiadikós***, from ***iskhiás*** ("**pain in the hips**"). The odd "sc-" spelling is a Medieval Latin corruption of the Greek "ischi-," which is why the first syllable is pronounced like "see." The adjective *sciatic* (**Middle French *sciatique*, from Late Latin *sciaticus***) followed, and the sciatic nerve is named for the hip region where it passes. The root *iskhion* ("**hip socket**") connects sciatica to the entire **[[ischi-]] family**: ***ischium*** (**the hip bone's lower, posterior part**), ***ischial*** (**[[ischi-]] + [[-al]] → pertaining to the ischium**), and ***ischiadic*** (**an older form of sciatic**). The root *[[ischi-]]* is productive in medical terminology, appearing in ***[[ischium]]***, ***[[ischial tuberosity]]***, ***[[ischiofemoral]]***, ***[[ischiorectal]]***, and ***[[ischialgia]]***.

> [!success]+ 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **Sciatic** _(adjective form — "sciatic nerve," "sciatic notch," "sciatic pain," "sciatic scoliosis")_
> - **Sciatic pain / sciatic nerve pain** _(lay and clinical term for the leg pain in the nerve's distribution; most often unilateral)_
> - **Ischias** _(German-language term for sciatica, used in older and European literature)_
> - **Lumbar radiculopathy** _(clinical synonym when the nerve root is compressed; Spine-health notes it is commonly referred to as sciatica; [[M54.16]], [[M51.16]])_
> - **Radicular leg pain** _(clinical descriptor used in notes and imaging reports; the same pain pattern)_
> - **Lumbago with sciatica** _(related entity — low back pain plus leg pain; M54.4-)_
> - **Sciatic [[neuritis]] / [[neuralgia]]** _(older terms for inflammation or pain of the sciatic nerve; today indexed to sciatica or G57.0-)_
> - **Discogenic sciatica** _(etiologic subtype — due to a herniated or degenerated disc compressing a root; M51.1-, M51.36-, M51.37-)_
> - **Stenotic sciatica** _(etiologic subtype — due to spinal canal or foramen narrowing; M48.0-)_
> - **Piriformis syndrome (extraspinal sciatica)** _(etiologic subtype — entrapment of the sciatic nerve in the buttock; indexed to G57.0- in my recall, verify in Encoder Pro)_
> - **Traumatic sciatic nerve injury** _(etiologic subtype — due to pelvic or hip fracture, dislocation, or injection; S74.0-)_
> - **Sciatica of pregnancy** _(special subtype — from pressure of the gravid uterus; code the pregnancy-related condition and M54.3-)_

> [!danger]+ 🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **Sciatic nerve** — the large nerve formed by the L4-S3 roots; splits into the tibial and common peroneal nerves in the thigh
> - **[[Ischium]]** — shares the *[[ischi-]]* root; the lower, posterior part of the hip bone
> - **[[Radiculopathy]]** — nerve root dysfunction from compression or irritation, the most common mechanism of sciatica (M54.1-, M51.1-)
> - **[[Lumbago]]** — low back pain without leg involvement (M54.5-); combined with leg pain it becomes lumbago with sciatica (M54.4-)
> - **Sciatic [[neuropathy]]** — damage to the sciatic nerve itself rather than the roots (G57.0-)
> - **Piriformis syndrome** — compression of the sciatic nerve by the piriformis muscle in the buttock, causing sciatica-like pain
> - **Herniated disc** — displacement of disc material pressing on a nerve root; the most common cause (M51.2-, M51.1-)
> - **[[Spinal stenosis]]** — narrowing of the canal or foramina compressing nerve roots (M48.0-)
> - **[[Spondylolisthesis]]** — forward slip of one vertebra on another, another structural cause (M43.1-)
> - **[[Cauda equina syndrome]]** — compression of the lumbosacral nerve roots with bowel, bladder, or saddle numbness; a surgical emergency ([[G83.4]])
> - **[[Foot drop]]** — weakness of ankle dorsiflexion from L4-L5 or peroneal involvement; a red-flag motor sign
> - **Straight leg raise** — bedside test that reproduces sciatica by stretching the L5-S1 roots
> - **[[Paresthesia]]** — tingling or numbness that often accompanies sciatica ([[R20.2]])
> - **[[lumbosacral]]** — the L5-S1 region where the roots most commonly leave the spine and compress ([[M51.17]], [[M54.17]])
> - **MRI / EMG** — key diagnostic tools; MRI shows the compressive lesion and EMG/NCS confirms root or nerve involvement

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** **M54.3-** is a last-resort code for **sciatica** with no specific cause, with **Excludes1 notes** for **lesion** of **sciatic nerve** (**G57.0-**), **sciatica due to intervertebral disc disorder** (**M51.1-**), and **sciatica with lumbago** (**M54.4-**), and it also excludes **[[M51.361]]** and **[[M51.371]]** (**[[degeneration]] with lower extremity pain only**). **M54.4-** carries **Excludes1 notes** for lumbago with **[[sciatica]]** due to a disc disorder (**M51.1-**) and for **[[M51.362]]** and **[[M51.372]]** (**[[degeneration]] with back and lower extremity pain**). So when the provider documents sciatica from a herniated disc or documented **[[radiculopathy]]**, code **[[M51.16]]** or **[[M51.17]]** instead, and when imaging shows **[[degeneration]]** with leg pain, the **M51.36-/M51.37- six-character codes** apply. Laterality is required for both **M54.3-** and **M54.4-**, and there is no bilateral code in these categories, so I use right plus left when documented bilaterally (**check payer rules**). Common undercodes are "**[[sciatica]]**" with an MRI "**disc herniation**" or "**foraminal [[stenosis]]**" in the same record, which should prompt a query for **[[radiculopathy]]** and level. Red flags such as **saddle numbness**, **urinary retention**, **bilateral leg weakness**, or **foot drop** should prompt a query for cauda equina (**[[G83.4]]**), and "**weakness**" should prompt a motor-deficit query. For CPT, **[[64483]]** is reported per level with **[[64484]]** as the add-on, imaging guidance is included, and bilateral injections need **[[-50]]** where allowed, with **[[-RT]]** or **[[-LT]]** for unilateral and **[[-59]]** or **[[-XS]]** for separate sites or sessions. **Epidural injections** often need documented failed conservative therapy, a limit on sessions per year, and imaging or exam support, and **[[decompression]]** surgery needs documented failed non-operative care or progressive deficit. For inpatient surgery, watch for **[[-22]]** (increased procedural services), **[[-62]]** (co-surgeons), **[[-80]]** or **[[-82]]** (assistant surgeons), and **[[-78]]** for an unplanned return to the OR.
