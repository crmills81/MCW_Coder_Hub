>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[subluxation]]
>**[[subluxation]]** is a **partial or incomplete dislocation of a joint, in which the articulating surfaces have lost their normal anatomical alignment but still maintain partial contact with one another**. It is clinically distinguished from a **complete [[dislocation]] ([[luxation]])**, where there is a total loss of contact between the articular surfaces, and a **[[sprain]]**, which involves **ligamentous injury** without necessarily displacing the joint structures. The underlying mechanism can be traumatic (**due to acute ligamentous tearing or stretching**), congenital (**due to inherent capsular laxity**), or pathological (**arising from destructive joint diseases like rheumatoid [[arthritis]] or [[neuromuscular]] imbalances**). **Subluxations** can be physiological in highly mobile joints under extreme stress, but are typically pathological, presenting as **acute traumatic subluxations** (**e.g., cervical spine subluxation, S13.1-**) or **chronic/pathological subluxations** (**e.g., pathological subluxation of a joint, M24.3-**). Unlike a **complete [[dislocation]]** which requires immediate **[[reduction]]** to restore any joint congruency, a **subluxation** maintains partial articular contact but often presents with similar **pain**, **instability**, and **biomechanical dysfunction**, making precise radiological diagnosis critical.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[subluxation]]
>#latin
>
>|**Component**|**Origin**|**Meaning**|
>|:---:|---|---|
>|**[[sub-]]**|Latin _sub_ (sub)|"**under**," "**below**," "**less than**," "**partial**" — negating/diminishing prefix|
>|**[[luxat-]]**|Latin _luxatus_ (lux-AH-tus), from _luxare_ (lux-AH-ray)|"**to dislocate**," "**to put out of joint**," "**displaced**"|
>|**[[-ion]]**|Latin _-io_ / _-ionem_ (ee-OH-nem)|Noun-forming suffix — "**state, condition, or process of**"|
>
> The word entered English in the **1640s** as *subluxation* (**noun**), borrowed from Late Latin ***subluxationem***, from Latin ***[[sub-]]*** + ***luxare*** — literally **"the state of being partially dislocated."** The root *luxare* ("**to dislocate**") connects subluxation to the entire **[[luxat-]] root family**: ***luxation*** (**complete dislocation → total loss of joint contact**), ***luxate*** (**verb form → to dislocate**), and *eluxation* (**archaic term for [[dislocation]]**). The diminishing prefix *[[sub-]]* is highly productive in medical terminology, appearing in terms denoting partial or lesser states, such as ***[[subacute]]***, ***[[subcutaneous]]***, and ***[[subclinical]]***.

> [!success]+ 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **Subluxated** _(adjective form — e.g., "subluxated lens," "subluxated cervical spine," "subluxated TMJ")_
> - **Partial dislocation** _(lay and clinical term; especially common in orthopedic, emergency, and PM&R settings)_
> - **Incomplete dislocation** _(partial or incomplete form of the condition; synonymous with subluxation)_
> - **Subluxation complex** _(clinical synonym used in specific context — coded under M99.1- for chiropractic and osteopathic biomechanical lesions)_
> - **Pathological subluxation** _(age- or disease-related form; M24.3-, occurring without significant trauma due to underlying joint disease)_
> - **Recurrent subluxation** _(systemic or syndromic form — often due to inherent joint laxity, connective tissue disorders, or previous trauma; M24.4-)_
> - **Traumatic subluxation** _(define by cause — acute injury resulting from external force, coded in Chapter 19 / S-codes)_
> - **Congenital subluxation** _(define by cause — present at birth, such as congenital subluxation of the hip; Q65.3-)_
> - **Lens subluxation** _(organ/tissue-specific form with ICD-10-CM code range — H27.11-; partial displacement of the crystalline lens, seen in Marfan syndrome or trauma)_
> - **TMJ subluxation** _(organ/tissue-specific form with ICD-10-CM code range — S03.0- for acute traumatic, M26.5- for temporomandibular joint disorder)_
> - **Atlantoaxial subluxation** _(organ/tissue-specific form with ICD-10-CM code range — M43.3-; common in rheumatoid arthritis, affecting the C1-C2 articulation)_
> - **Nursemaid's elbow** _(organ/tissue-specific form with ICD-10-CM code range — radial head subluxation in pediatric patients; S53.03-)_

> [!danger]+ 🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **[[Dislocation]]** — the opposite/extreme of subluxation; complete loss of contact between articular surfaces requiring formal reduction.
> - **[[Luxation]]** — shares the *luxat-* root; synonymous with complete dislocation, representing the fully disordered version of the same displacement process.
> - **[[Sprain]]** — injury to ligaments surrounding a joint without displacement; often accompanies subluxation (e.g., S13.4- for cervical sprain).
> - **Hypermobility syndrome** — complex syndrome that overlaps with this term; excessive joint range of motion predisposing patients to recurrent subluxations and dislocations ([[M35.7]]).
> - **Ligamentous laxity** — the physiological mechanism or process — looseness or excessive flexibility of ligaments — and its primary role in causing recurrent or congenital subluxations.
> - **Subluxing** — adjective describing joints or tendons that frequently undergo partial dislocation or snapping (e.g., "subluxing patella").
> - **[[Ectopia]] lentis** — programmed or regulated cellular/structural defect underlying pathological forms of this condition in the eye; encompasses both subluxation and complete luxation of the lens.
> - **Marfan syndrome** — genetic disease whose name includes or is defined by this term; a connective tissue disorder highly associated with bilateral lens subluxation (Q87.4).
> - **Rheumatoid [[arthritis]]** — another clinical entity defined by this term; an [[autoimmune]] inflammatory disease that frequently causes destructive cervical (atlantoaxial) subluxation (M05.-).
> - **Ehlers-Danlos syndrome** — another clinical entity defined by this term at a specific anatomic site; a collagen disorder causing widespread, multi-joint recurrent subluxations (Q79.6-).
> - **Temporomandibular joint disorder (TMD)** — another clinical entity; often involves anterior [[subluxation]] of the articular disc during jaw opening (M26.5-).
> - **[[Spondylolisthesis]]** — another clinical entity in a specific specialty — forward subluxation of one vertebral body over another, heavily treated in PM&R and spine surgery (M43.1-).
> - **[[Radiography]] (X-ray)** — primary or key diagnostic tool for evaluating joint alignment, measuring joint space, and confirming the presence and degree of subluxation.

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** For **inpatient profee coding** under **Noridian MAC (JE/JF)**, strict attention must be paid to the etiology of the **[[subluxation]]**. **Traumatic subluxations** (**S-codes**) require a **7th character** (**A for initial encounter, D for subsequent, S for sequela**) and must be sequenced according to the primary reason for the admission. Do not confuse **acute traumatic subluxations** with pathological (**M24.3-**) or recurrent (**M24.4-**) **subluxations**, which are chronic conditions coded from **Chapter 13**. An undercoding alert exists for **[[ophthalmology]]**: when a patient has a subluxated lens requiring **[[cataract]] extraction**, ensure the operative report is queried for the use of **capsular tension rings**, **iris hooks**, or **trypan blue**, which justifies the **complex cataract code** (**[[66982]]**) rather than the standard code (**[[66984]]**). For **PM&R** and **manipulative therapies**, **Noridian** requires clear documentation of **somatic dysfunction** (**M99.0-**) or **[[subluxation]]** complex (**M99.1-**) linked to the specific body regions treated to support OMT (**[[98925]]-[[98929]]**) or CMT (**[[98940]]-[[98943]]**) codes.
