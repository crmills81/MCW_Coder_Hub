>[!Note] DEFINITION of leukocyte
>**[[leukocyte]]** is **any of the nucleated cells of the blood and lymphatic system that collectively make up the body's cellular immune defense**, distinguishing them from the **anucleate [[erythrocyte]] (which transports oxygen) and the [[thrombocyte]] (platelet, which mediates clotting)**. All **[[leukocytes]]** arise from pluripotent hematopoietic stem cells in the bone marrow through the process of hematopoiesis and are broadly divided into granulocytes (**[[neutrophils]], eosinophils, basophils, containing visible cytoplasmic granules**) and agranulocytes (**lymphocytes and monocytes, lacking prominent granules**). Leukocyte counts and behavior can be physiological, as with the transient rise seen after exercise or stress, or pathological, as with infection-driven elevation or bone marrow suppression. Clinically significant abnormalities are coded as either an elevated count, or **[[leukocytosis]]** (D72.82-), or a decreased count, or **leukopenia** (D72.81), with neutrophil-specific depletion coded separately as **[[neutropenia]]** (D70). **Leukocyte** is often confused with **[[leukocytosis]]** and **leukopenia**: **[[leukocyte]]** refers to the cell itself, while **[[leukocytosis]]** and **leukopenia** describe abnormal quantities of that cell in circulation.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[leukocyte]]
>#greek
>
>|Component|Origin|Meaning|
>|---|---|---|
>|**[[leuko-]]**|Greek _λευκός_ (leukós)|"**white**," "**clear**, **bright**" — descriptive combining form referring to the pale, colorless appearance of these cells compared to red blood cells|
>|**[[-cyte]]**|Greek _κύτος_ (kýtos), from Greek _kytos_ "a hollow, vessel, receptacle"|"**cell**," "**hollow vessel**" — noun-forming suffix used throughout modern biology and medicine to denote a cell type|
>
> The word entered English in the **1860s** as *leucocyte* (**noun**), borrowed from French *leucocyte*, coined from Greek *leukós* ("**white**") + *kytos* ("**hollow vessel, cell**"), literally **"white cell."** The American spelling *leukocyte* later became standard in U.S. clinical usage, though *leucocyte* persists in British English. The root *kytos* ("**cell**") connects leukocyte to the entire **[[-cyte]] root family**: ***[[erythrocyte]]*** (red cell → oxygen-carrying blood cell), ***[[thrombocyte]]*** (clot cell → platelet), and ***[[phagocyte]]*** (eating cell → a cell that engulfs pathogens or debris). The combining form *[[leuko-]]* is highly productive in medical terminology and appears in numerous other terms: ***[[leukoplakia]]***, ***[[leukorrhea]]***, ***[[leukemia]]***, ***[[leukoderma]]***.

> [!success]+ ### 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
>
> - **Leukocytic** _(adjective form — used in "leukocytic infiltration," "leukocytic response," "leukocytic esterase reaction")_
> - **White blood cell (WBC)** _(lay and clinical synonym — used interchangeably with "leukocyte" in nearly all clinical documentation and lab reporting)_
> - **[[Leucocyte]]** _(British/alternate spelling variant — identical meaning, used in UK and international literature)_
> - **Granulocyte** _(structural subtype — leukocyte containing visible cytoplasmic granules; includes neutrophils, eosinophils, and basophils)_
> - **Agranulocyte** _(structural subtype — leukocyte lacking prominent cytoplasmic granules; includes lymphocytes and monocytes)_
> - **Phagocyte** _(functional subtype — leukocyte specialized for engulfing pathogens and cellular debris; primarily neutrophils and monocytes/macrophages)_

_____

> [!danger]+ ### 🔗 RELATED TERMS
>
> - **[[Erythrocyte]]** — the opposite blood cell lineage from leukocytes; an anucleate, hemoglobin-filled cell responsible for oxygen transport rather than immune defense
> - **[[Thrombocyte]]** — platelet; a non-nucleated cell fragment responsible for hemostasis and clotting, distinct from the immune function of leukocytes
> - **[[Leukocytosis]]** — abnormally elevated leukocyte count; coded under D72.82-
> - **[[Leukopenia]]** — abnormally decreased leukocyte count; coded under D72.81-
> - **[[Neutropenia]]** — specific depletion of the neutrophil subtype of leukocyte; coded under D70
> - **[[Leukemia]]** — malignant clonal proliferation of leukocyte precursors in the bone marrow, distinguished from reactive leukocytosis by clonality and blast presence
> - **[[pyuria]]** — the presence of leukocytes in urine, typically indicating urinary tract infection or inflammation; coded as **[[R82.81]]**
> - **Leukocyte esterase** — an enzyme produced by leukocytes and detected on urine dipstick testing as an indirect marker of pyuria
> - **[[Hematopoiesis]]** — the bone marrow process by which pluripotent stem cells differentiate into leukocytes, erythrocytes, and thrombocytes
> - **Differential count** — a laboratory test enumerating the relative percentages of each leukocyte subtype (neutrophils, lymphocytes, monocytes, eosinophils, basophils) within the total white blood cell count

>
> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** These **[[leukocyte]]**-count codes carry no laterality requirement since they describe systemic blood findings rather than an anatomic site. Sequencing logic follows the abnormal-finding rule: if a definitive underlying cause is documented (**e.g., [[pneumonia]], [[sepsis]], chemotherapy-induced [[neutropenia]]**), code the underlying condition first and the leukocyte abnormality as a secondary or manifestation code only if it affects treatment; use the **D72/D70/R82 codes** as primary only when no cause is identified. A frequently undercoded entry is **[[D72.825]] (Bandemia)** — the documentation trigger phrase "**left shift**" or "**bands**" on a CBC differential should prompt a coder query to confirm whether bandemia is separately reportable. Payer edits commonly bundle **[[85025]]** with **[[85004]]/[[85027]]**, so never bill the automated **CBC-with-diff code** alongside a standalone differential on the same date without modifier justification. When **[[leukocytosis]]** appears in the context of a possible hematologic **[[malignancy]]**, specificity is critical — confirm whether the documentation supports a r**eactive leukemoid reaction** (**[[D72.823]]**) versus an actual **[[leukemia]]** diagnosis (**C91–C95 category**) before finalizing the code, since misclassifying a malignancy as a benign reactive process is a high-risk audit finding.
