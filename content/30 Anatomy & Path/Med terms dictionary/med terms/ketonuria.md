>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[ketonuria]]
>**[[ketonuria]]** is **the presence of detectable ketone bodies** (**acetoacetic acid, beta-hydroxybutyrate**, and **acetone**) **in the urine**. It differs from **ketonemia** (**elevated ketones in the blood**) and from **[[ketoacidosis]]**, which is the **pathological, acid-base-disturbing accumulation of ketones** severe enough to **lower blood pH**. **Ketone bodies** form in the liver when **fatty acids** are broken down for fuel because glucose is unavailable or unusable, and once plasma levels rise past a renal threshold they spill into the urine. This can be physiological (**prolonged fasting, strenuous exercise, a ketogenic diet, or normal pregnancy**) or pathological (**poorly controlled diabetes mellitus, starvation, alcoholism, or eclampsia**). In coding, isolated **[[ketonuria]]** without a known cause is captured as **acetonuria ([[R82.4]])**, while **[[ketonuria]]** occurring as part of **[[diabetic ketoacidosis]]** is captured under the relevant diabetes-with-**[[ketoacidosis]]** code (e.g., **[[E10.10]]**, **[[E11.10]]**) rather than **[[R82.4]]** alone.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[ketonuria]]
>#german #greek
>
>|Component|Origin|Meaning|
>|---|---|---|
>|**[[keton-]]**|German _Keton_ (1848), coined by chemist Leopold Gmelin from German _Aketon_, itself from French _acétone_|"**ketone**" — the class of organic compounds whose excess in the body produces this condition|
>|**[[-uria]]**|Greek _ouron_ (οὖρον, "**urine**")|Noun-forming suffix — "**condition of the urine**," "**presence of a substance in the urine**"|
>
> The term *ketone* entered chemical usage in **1851** in English, borrowed from German _keton_, which Gmelin devised in 1848 as "an arbitrary variation of *acetone*, to make a distinction." Attaching the Greek root *[[-uria]]* ("**urine**") produced *ketonuria*, following the pattern of the entire **[[-uria]] family**: ***[[glycosuria]]*** (**glucose in the urine**), ***[[proteinuria]]*** (**protein in the urine**), ***hematuria*** (**blood in the urine**), and ***[[ureterolithiasis|urolithiasis]]***-adjacent terms built on the same Greek *ouron* root. The synonym *acetonuria* uses the parent compound name *acetone* directly rather than the class name *ketone*, which is why ICD-10-CM lists the entity under **Acetonuria (R82.4)** with "**[[ketonuria]]**" as an inclusion term.

> [!success]+ ### 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
>
> - **Acetonuria** _(the official ICD-10-CM index term; used interchangeably with ketonuria)_
> - **Ketoaciduria** _(lay/clinical term emphasizing the acidic ketone bodies excreted, especially in diabetic ketoacidosis)_
> - **Urine ketones / urine ketone bodies** _(lab-report phrasing for the dipstick or quantitative result)_
> - **Diabetic ketosis** _(etiologic subtype — ketonuria arising from poorly controlled or type 1 diabetes; coded under [[E10.10]]/[[E11.10]] when ketoacidosis is present, not R82.4)_
> - **Starvation ketosis** _(etiologic subtype — from prolonged fasting or very-low-carbohydrate/ketogenic dieting)_

_____

> [!danger]+ ### 🔗 RELATED TERMS
>
> - **[[ketosis]]** — the metabolic state of elevated ketone body production; ketonuria is its detectable urinary manifestation.
> - **[[ketoacidosis]]** — shares the *keton-* root; the pathological, decompensated form of ketosis causing metabolic acidosis, distinct from simple ketonuria because it lowers blood pH.
> - **Ketonemia** — elevated ketone bodies in the *blood* rather than urine; often precedes or accompanies ketonuria.
> - **Glycosuria** — glucose in the urine; commonly coexists with ketonuria in uncontrolled diabetes and is tested on the same dipstick panel.
> - **Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)** — the most clinically significant cause of pathological **ketonuria**; documented type 1 or type 2 diabetes with ketoacidosis is coded under **[[E10.10]]**, **[[E10.11]]**, **[[E11.10]]**, or **[[E11.11]]** rather than **[[R82.4]]**.
> - **[[Ketogenesis]]** — the hepatic biochemical process (fatty-acid oxidation to acetyl-CoA and ketone bodies) underlying both physiological and pathological ketonuria.
> - **[[urinalysis]]** — the primary diagnostic tool that detects ketonuria via dipstick reagent testing.

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** Report isolated **[[ketonuria]]** with **[[R82.4]]** only when no underlying cause has been established; per **ICD-10-CM guidelines**, **R-codes** should not be used as a principal diagnosis once a definitive underlying diagnosis is documented. When the documentation links **[[ketonuria]]** to **[[diabetic ketoacidosis]]**, sequence the **[[diabetes]]**-with-**[[ketoacidosis]]** code (**[[E10.10]]**/**[[E10.11]]**/**[[E11.10]]**/**[[E11.11]]**) first — do **not** additionally report **[[R82.4]]**, since the **[[ketoacidosis]]** code already captures the **[[ketonuria]]**. A common undercoding trap on profee claims is billing only the **urinalysis CPT code** (**81000–81003**) without capturing a positive ketone finding as a coded diagnosis when it is clinically significant; if "**ketones present**" or "**trace/moderate/large ketones**" appears in the urinalysis result with no stated cause, query the provider for the underlying etiology before defaulting to **R82.4**.
