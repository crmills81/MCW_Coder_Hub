>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[temporomandibular]]
>**[[temporomandibular]]** is **an anatomical adjective describing structures, conditions, or procedures related to both the [[temporal bone]] of the skull and the [[mandible]] (lower jaw)**. Clinically, it almost exclusively refers to the **temporomandibular joint (TMJ)**, a unique bi-articular synovial joint that allows for the complex hinge and gliding movements necessary for **[[mastication]]** and speech. The joint's underlying physiological mechanism relies on an **articular disc** (**[[meniscus]]**) situated between the **mandibular condyle** and the **mandibular fossa** of the **temporal bone**, which cushions the joint and facilitates smooth translation. While physiological **[[temporomandibular]]** **function** is asymptomatic and smooth, pathological alterations often lead to **Temporomandibular** **Disorders** (**TMD**), encompassing a range of structural and functional issues such as **internal derangement**, **myofascial pain**, and **[[osteoarthritis]]** (**M26.6-**). It is commonly distinguished from **[[maxillofacial]]**, which refers to the **upper jaw** (**maxilla**) and face, whereas **[[temporomandibular]]** strictly isolates the **functional articulation** of the lower jaw to the cranial base.

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[temporomandibular]]
>#latin
>
>|**Component**|**Origin**|**Meaning**|
>|:---:|---|---|
>|**[[tempor-]]**|Latin _tempus_ (tempor-)|"**temple**," "**time**" — anatomical root referring to the temporal bone (historically associated with the temples where gray hair first appears, marking time)|
>|**[[mandibul-]]**|Latin _mandibula_ (mandibula), from _mandere_ (to chew)|"**lower jaw**," "**jawbone**," "**to chew**"|
>|**[[-ar]]**|Latin _-aris_ (-aris)|Adjective-forming suffix — "**pertaining to**"|
>
> The word entered English in the **1870s** as *temporomandibular* (**adjective**), formed by combining the Latin anatomical roots for the temple and the jaw. The root *mandibul-* ("**lower jaw**") connects **[[temporomandibular]]** to the entire **[[-mandibul-]]** family: ***[[mandibulectomy]]*** (**excision of the mandible → surgical removal of the lower jaw**), ***[[craniomandibular]]*** (**pertaining to skull and jaw → relating to the skull and mandible**), and *[[submandibular]]* (**below the jaw**). The combining form *[[tempor-]]* is highly productive in anatomical medical terminology, appearing in terms like ***[[temporoparietal]]***, ***[[temporalis]]***, and ***[[temporosphenoidal]]***.

> [!success]+ 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **[[temporomandibular]]** _(adjective form — commonly seen in clinical collocations like "temporomandibular joint," "temporomandibular disorder")_
> - **TMJ** _(lay and clinical acronym; refers to the joint itself, though often colloquially misused by patients to mean the disorder)_
> - **TMD** _(Temporomandibular disorder; the clinical acronym for the pathological condition affecting the joint and muscles of [[mastication]])_
> - **[[Craniomandibular]] [[articulation]]** _(clinical synonym used in specific anatomical or osteopathic contexts)_
> - **Myofascial pain dysfunction syndrome** _(muscular-origin subtype; often related to bruxism or stress; [[M26.69]])_
> - **Internal derangement of the TMJ** _(structural subtype; displacement of the articular disc, often presenting with clicking or locking; M26.63-)_
> - **TMJ [[Ankylosis]]** _(stiffness or fixation subtype; fusion of the joint due to trauma, infection, or severe arthritis; M26.61-)_
> - **TMJ [[Arthralgia]]** _(symptom-based subtype; joint pain without specified structural derangement; M26.62-)_

> [!danger]+ 🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **[[Maxillofacial]]** — pertaining to the maxilla (upper jaw) and face; distinguishes upper jaw/midface structures from the lower jaw articulation of the temporomandibular joint.
> - **[[Mandibular]]** — shares the *mandibul-* root; pertaining solely to the lower jawbone itself, rather than its articulation with the skull.
> - **[[Bruxism]]** — the involuntary grinding or clenching of teeth ([[F45.8]] or [[G24.3]]); a primary mechanical trigger and comorbid condition leading to temporomandibular joint disorders.
> - **[[Malocclusion]]** — imperfect positioning of the teeth when the jaws are closed; a common anatomical contributor to temporomandibular dysfunction.
> - **[[Arthrocentesis]]** — joint aspiration/injection; a common minimally invasive therapeutic mechanism used to flush the TMJ and inject corticosteroids or hyaluronic acid.
> - **[[Synovial]]** — adjective describing the fluid-filled joint capsule type that characterizes the TMJ, making it susceptible to inflammatory arthritides.
> - **[[Osteoarthritis]]** — degenerative joint disease; when affecting the TMJ ([[M26.69]]), it involves the breakdown of the articular cartilage and underlying bone.
> - **[[Trismus]]** — restricted range of motion of the jaw ([[lockjaw]]) ([[R40.0]]); a severe clinical manifestation of temporomandibular joint infection, trauma, or tetanus.
> - **[[Arthroscopy]]** — primary minimally invasive diagnostic and therapeutic surgical procedure for evaluating and treating internal derangements of the temporomandibular joint.

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** When coding **temporomandibular joint disorders** (**M26.6- series**) for **inpatient** or **profee claims**, laterality (**right, left, bilateral**) is strictly required; unspecified codes (**[[M26.60]]**) will frequently trigger denials under **Noridian MAC policies**. For surgical interventions, remember that **diagnostic [[arthroscopy]]** (**[[29800]]**) is inherently included in **surgical arthroscopy** (**[[29804]]**) and should not be billed separately if performed during the same operative session on the same joint. A common undercoding issue occurs with **[[arthrocentesis]]** (**[[20605]]/[[20606]]**); ensure documentation explicitly states if ultrasound guidance was used with permanent recording, as **[[20606]]** captures higher **RVUs**. Additionally, **TMJ** surgeries (**like [[21243]] for prosthetic replacement**) are heavily scrutinized for medical necessity; documentation must clearly demonstrate failure of conservative therapies (**e.g., bite guards, NSAIDs, physical therapy**) and severe functional impairment (**e.g., inability to masticate solid foods**) to meet prior authorization criteria.
