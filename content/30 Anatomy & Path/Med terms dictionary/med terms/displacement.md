>[!Note] DEFINITION of [[displacement]]
>**[[displacement]]** is **the removal or shifting of a structure, bone fragment, organ, tissue, or implanted device from its normal anatomic position.** It is a positional change, so it differs from **[[dislocation]]** (**complete loss of contact between the two articular surfaces of a joint**), **[[subluxation]]** (**partial loss of contact**), and **[[prolapse]]** (**descent of an organ through or toward an opening**). Its opposite is **[[reduction]],** where the part is returned to its normal position. The mechanism is usually mechanical force (**trauma that shifts fracture fragments**), degenerative or structural failure (**a disc nucleus pushing through a torn annulus**), gravity and weak support (**[[ptosis]]**), or loss of fixation (**a [[stent]], [[catheter]], or lens migrating**). It can be physiological (**a mobile kidney shifting with respiration, or normal organ [[displacement]] in pregnancy**) or pathological (**displaced [[fracture]], disc displacement, midline shift from mass effect**). The forms you'll code most are displaced fractures (**S-codes, e.g., [[S72.141A]]**), **intervertebral disc displacement** (**M50.2-, M51.2-**), and **device displacement** (**T83.-, [[T85.22XA]]**). It is often confused with **[[dislocation]]**, which is a joint-specific term, and with **[[angulation]]** or **[[malalignment]]**, which describe the direction or degree of a displaced fragment rather than the fact of **[[displacement]].** Outside medicine, "**[[displacement]]**" is also the psychological defense mechanism of redirecting an emotion to a safer target, and the physics term for a change in position or volume of fluid moved. Neither of those has an **ICD-10-CM code.**

>[!info]+ ETYMOLOGY of [[displacement]]
>#latin #french #greek
>
>|**Component**|**Origin**|**Meaning**|
>|:---:|---|---|
>|**[[dis-]]**|Latin _dis-_ (dis), via Old French _des-_ (deh)|"**apart**," "**away**," "**undo**" — separative/reversing prefix|
>|**[[plat-]]** (place)|Old French _place_ (plahs), from Latin _platea_ (PLAH-teh-ah), from Greek _plateia_ (plah-TAY-ah, "broad street"), from _platys_ (plah-TOOS)|"**broad**," "**flat**," "**open space**," "**location**"|
>|**[[-ment]]**|Latin _-mentum_ (MEN-toom), via Old French _-ment_|Noun-forming suffix — "**result, means, or act of**"|
>
> The word entered English in the **early 17th century** as *displacement* (**noun**), from the verb *displace* (**16th century**), borrowed from Old French ***desplacier*** ("**to remove from a place**"), from ***des-*** + ***place*** ("a place"), from Latin ***platea*** ("broad street, open space"), from Greek ***plateia (hodos)*** — literally **"broad way."** The verb *displace* appeared in English in the **1530s**, and the sense of a body part moved out of position developed in the medical literature later. The root *platys* ("**broad, flat**") connects displacement to the **[[plat-]] family**: ***platysma*** (**flat sheet → broad neck muscle**), ***platelet*** (**small flat plate → [[thrombocyte]]**), and *platyhelminth* (**flat worm → flatworm**). The separative prefix *[[dis-]]* is productive in medical terminology, appearing in ***[[dislocation]]***, ***[[dissection]]***, ***[[disarticulation]]***, ***[[dissociation]]***, and ***[[disinfection]]***.

> [!success]+ 🔀 ALIASES / ALTERNATE TERMS
> 
> - **[[Displaced]]** _(adjective form — "displaced fracture," "displaced intracapsular fracture," "displaced nasogastric tube")_
> - **Malposition** _(clinical term for a structure or device sitting in the wrong place; used for catheters, tubes, leads, and lenses)_
> - **Dislodgement** _(device-specific term for a tube, catheter, or line coming out of its intended position; the same event is coded as displacement)_
> - **Migration** _(gradual movement of a device or foreign body from its original site; stents, IUDs, and orthopedic hardware)_
> - **[[Herniation]]** _(clinical descriptor for disc material or an organ pushing through its normal boundary; indexed to disc displacement, M50.2-, M51.2-)_
> - **Midline shift** _(neuroimaging descriptor for displacement of brain structures from the midline due to mass effect; code the underlying cause, e.g., [[G93.5]] compression of brain)_
> - **Translation** _(radiology/orthopedic term for sideways shift of one fragment or vertebra relative to another)_
> - **[[Ectopia]]** _(congenital displacement of an organ or tissue; e.g., ectopic testis Q53.-, ectopia lentis [[Q12.1]])_
> - **Cervical disc displacement** _(anatomic subtype — M50.2-)_
> - **Thoracic/lumbar disc displacement** _(anatomic subtype — [[M51.24]], [[M51.25]], [[M51.26]], [[M51.27]])_
> - **Displaced fracture** _(anatomic subtype — fragments separated or out of alignment; 7th character coding in S-codes)_
> - **Device displacement** _(complication subtype — T83.- genitourinary devices, [[T85.22XA]] intraocular lens)_

> [!danger]+ 🔗 RELATED TERMS
> 
> - **[[Reduction]]** — the opposite of displacement; manipulation (closed) or surgery (open) that restores a displaced structure to normal position
> - **[[Placement]]** — shares the *[[plat-]]* root; the act of putting something in its intended position
> - **[[Dislocation]]** — complete separation of joint surfaces; a joint-specific form of displacement
> - **[[subluxation]]** — partial displacement of joint surfaces where some contact remains
> - **Nondisplaced fracture** — a fracture with the fragments still aligned; this is the contrast term for coding the "displaced" default
> - **[[Angulation]]** — the angle between displaced fracture fragments
> - **[[Prolapse]]** — downward displacement of an organ through or toward a body opening (e.g., pelvic organ prolapse)
> - **[[Ptosis]]** — drooping or downward displacement of an organ or structure, e.g., [[nephroptosis]] ([[N28.83]]), [[blepharoptosis]]
> - **Mass effect** — displacement of normal tissue by an expanding lesion such as a [[tumor]], [[hematoma]], or [[abscess]]
> - **Herniated nucleus pulposus** — displacement of disc nucleus material through the annulus (M50.2-, M51.2-)
> - **Ureteral stent** — an indwelling device that can migrate; [[displacement]] is coded to [[T83.122A]]
> - **Intraocular lens** — an implant that can decenter or dislocate; displacement is coded to [[T85.22XA]]
> - **[[Reduction]] / [[Fixation]]** — the standard treatments for displaced fractures (CPT fracture families)
> - **X-ray / CT / MRI** — key diagnostic tools for displaced fractures, disc displacement, and device position

> > ⚠️ **Coding Note:** "**[[Displaced]]**" and "**[[nondisplaced]]**" drive fracture code selection. Per the **ICD-10-CM guidelines**, a fracture not specified as **displaced** or **nondisplaced** defaults to displaced, and one not specified as open or closed defaults to closed. Laterality and 7th character are required (**A initial active treatment, D subsequent routine healing, G delayed healing, K [[nonunion]], P [[malunion]], S sequela**). For inpatient profee, the surgeon's initial-encounter claim uses A even if the patient was seen earlier in the ED. **Disc [[displacement]]** with **[[myelopathy]]** (M51.0-) or **[[radiculopathy]]** (M51.1-) is coded to those codes instead of M51.2-. A common undercode is a device complication: phrases like "**stent migrated**," "**Foley fell out**," or "**lens decentered**" should prompt T83.- or **[[T85.22XA]]** instead of a routine encounter code. Fracture care **CPT** codes carry global periods, so watch for **[[-54]], [[-55]]**, and **[[-56]]** when care is split. Unplanned return to the OR for a displaced device or hardware during the global period may need **[[-78]]**, while a staged or related procedure may need **[[-58]]**. Add **[[-RT]]** or **[[-LT]]** where the **CPT code** is not already laterality-specific.
