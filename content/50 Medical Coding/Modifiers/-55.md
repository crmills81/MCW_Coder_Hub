# Modifier -55: Postoperative Management [[odontology]]

Since **Modifier [[-54]]** (**Surgical Care Only**) and **Modifier -55** (**Postoperative Management Only**) are two sides of the same coin, having them both in your vault will help you cross-reference them accurately when you're coding for Urology or Ophthalmology cases where care is often split.

---

## The Basics of Modifier -55

### Meaning

**Modifier -55** is defined as **Postoperative Management Only**. It is used when one physician performed the surgery, but _you_ (**or the physician you are coding for**) are providing the follow-up care during the global period.

### Common Use Cases

- **The Returning Patient:** A patient has a complex procedure at a teaching hospital (like the Medical College of Wisconsin) but returns to their local specialist for the 10-day or 90-day post-op follow-up.
    
- **Transfer of Care:** A surgeon becomes unavailable (illness, vacation) and formally transfers the postoperative management to a colleague or partner.
    

### Key Rules to Remember

- **Dates Matter:** When billing -55, you must include the **Date of Surgery** in the electronic claim (usually Item 15 or 19 on a CMS-1500) and the date you assumed care.
    
- **The "Days" Calculation:** Reimbursement is often pro-rated based on how many days of the global period you actually covered. If the global period is 90 days and you took over on day 30, you only bill for the remaining 60 days.
    
- **Global Period:** This modifier is only necessary for procedures with a **10-day or 90-day** global period. Minor procedures with a "**000**" global period don't require it.

|**Feature**|**Modifier -54**|**Modifier -55**|
|---|---|---|
|**Role**|The Surgeon | The Follow-up Physician|
|**Timing**|Day of Surgery | After Surgery (Global Period)|
|**Claim Requirement**|Must document transfer of care | Must include Date of Surgery on claim|
|**Common Specialties**|Specialized Surgery centers| Local Clinics / Specialty follow-up|

>[!tip ] 
>**Pro-fee Tip:** In [[Ophthalmology]], this is extremely common with "Co-management" of cataract surgeries. The surgeon bills -54, and the referring optometrist or ophthalmologist bills -55 for the post-op care.
