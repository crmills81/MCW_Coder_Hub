**Your question**: I have a chart that I'm coding. Pt had the CPT 35800 done on 2/1/26 but then due to bleeding around the trach site, the ENT doctor was called back to the patient on 2/2/26 to have a flexible fiber optic tracheoscopy inserted and cuff reinflated. Can I bill out the CPT [[31575]] for 2/2/26 after coding CT [[35800]] BEFORE the patient needed a new trach the very next day? If so what modifier do I use? If not, why?

**Microsoft Copilot**: Let’s walk through it cleanly and defensibly.

---

## Short Answer

**Yes, CPT 31575 _may_ be separately reportable on 2/2/26 — but only if documentation supports that it was a distinct, medically necessary diagnostic service.**  
If it is billable, **modifier ‑58** is the most appropriate modifier **in most cases**, not ‑59.

---

## Step‑by‑Step Analysis

### 1. Relationship Between CPT 35800 and 31575

- **CPT 35800** = exploration for postoperative hemorrhage, thrombosis, or infection.
- **CPT 31575** = diagnostic flexible fiberoptic laryngoscopy/tracheoscopy.

These are:

- **Different CPT families**
- **Different anatomic focus**
- **Different intent** (surgical control of bleeding vs diagnostic airway evaluation)

They are **not inherently bundled** by CPT or NCCI.

---

### 2. Timing Matters — This Was the Next Day

The scope was performed:

- **On a different date of service (2/2/26)**
- **After the initial surgical intervention**
- **Due to a new or ongoing clinical issue (bleeding around the trach site)**

That alone removes same‑session bundling concerns.

---

### 3. Was the Scope “Routine Post‑Op Care”?

This is the **key question**.

**31575 is NOT billable if:**

- The scope was merely to check placement
- It was part of routine post‑op airway management
- It was incidental to managing the trach without diagnostic intent

**31575 _IS_ billable if documentation shows:**

- Evaluation of bleeding source
- Assessment of airway integrity
- Visualization to determine cause of complication
- Medical decision‑making based on findings

Diagnostic intent is critical 

---

## Modifier Selection — What to Use (and What NOT to Use)

### ✅ **Modifier ‑58 (Staged or Related Procedure)**

This is the **best fit** when:

- The patient is in the **post‑operative period**
- The service is **related to the original condition**
- The procedure was **planned or medically necessary due to evolving complications**

Modifier ‑58:

- **Allows payment**
- **Resets the global period**
- Signals this was **not routine post‑op care**

This aligns with CPT guidance for post‑op diagnostic procedures.

---

### 🚫 Why NOT Modifier ‑59?

Modifier [[‑59]] is for:

- **Same‑day**
- **Same session**
- **Bundled procedures**

This was:

- A **different date**
- A **post‑op evaluation**
- Not an NCCI edit issue

Using [[‑59]] here would be **incorrect and audit‑vulnerable** [AAPC](https://www.aapc.com/blog/25335-a-quick-guide-to-separate-procedures/).

---

### 🚫 Why NOT Modifier ‑78?

Modifier [[‑78]] applies to:

- **Return to the OR**
- **Unplanned surgical procedures**

A bedside or procedural scope **does not qualify as a return to the OR**.

---

## Documentation Must Include

To safely bill **31575‑58**, the note should clearly state:

- Reason ENT was called back
- Diagnostic purpose of the scope
- Findings (bleeding source, cuff status, airway condition)
- Medical decision‑making based on findings
- That this was **not routine trach care**

---

## Final Coding Recommendation

|Date|CPT|Modifier|Billable?|Rationale|
|---|---|---|---|---|
|2/1/26|35800|—|Yes|Primary surgical intervention|
|2/2/26|31575|**‑58**|**Yes, if documented**|Post‑op diagnostic evaluation due to complication|

---

This is where auditors and payers _love_ to deny — so let’s be very explicit about **what flips CPT 31575 from billable to non‑billable** in your scenario.

I’ll break this into **clear failure points** so you can audit the note against each one.

---

## 1️⃣ It Becomes Non‑Billable If the Scope Was **Routine Post‑Op Airway Management**

**31575 is NOT payable** when the scope is used simply to:

- Check trach position
- Confirm patency
- Assist with cuff reinflation
- Perform routine airway surveillance

If the documentation reads like:

- “Flexible scope used to visualize trach and reinflate cuff”
- “Scope passed to assess airway and adjust cuff”
- “Routine post‑op airway check”

➡️ **That is considered inherent post‑operative care** following a trach‑related complication and is **bundled into the global** of 35800.

**Key audit phrase that kills it:**

> “Used to assist with management”  
> instead of  
> “Used to evaluate cause of bleeding / airway compromise”

---

## 2️⃣ It’s Non‑Billable If There Is **No Diagnostic Intent**

CPT 31575 is a **diagnostic code**, not a therapeutic one.

It becomes non‑billable if:

- No diagnostic question is stated
- No pathology is being evaluated
- No findings are documented beyond “visualized”

**Missing elements that trigger denial:**

- No stated reason _why_ visualization was required
- No findings (bleeding source, edema, erosion, displacement)
- No medical decision‑making tied to the scope

If the scope didn’t **change or guide care**, payers will say:

> “This was incidental to post‑op management.”

---

## 3️⃣ It’s Non‑Billable If the Bleeding Is Considered **Expected Post‑Op Course**

If documentation implies:

- Mild oozing
- Expected post‑trach bleeding
- Normal post‑surgical findings

Then the scope is considered:

- Monitoring
- Observation
- Routine follow‑up

➡️ **Expected sequelae = not separately payable**

To bill 31575, the bleeding must be framed as:

- Concerning
- Unanticipated
- Requiring diagnostic evaluation

---

## 4️⃣ It’s Non‑Billable If the Scope Was **Solely to Fix a Problem**

This is subtle but important.

If the scope was used **only** to:

- Reinflate the cuff
- Adjust the trach
- Confirm resolution after intervention

Then it’s **therapeutic assistance**, not diagnostic.

**Diagnostic first → intervention second = billable**  
**Intervention only = bundled**

---

## 5️⃣ It’s Non‑Billable If Documentation Suggests It Was Part of the Original Procedure’s Aftercare**

If the note ties the scope directly back to:

- “Post‑op check from yesterday’s surgery”
- “Follow‑up from exploration”
- “Continued management of surgical site”

Then payers will argue:

> “This is routine post‑operative management within the global period.”

Even though it’s a different date, **global rules still apply**.

---

## 6️⃣ It’s Non‑Billable If the Wrong Modifier Is Used

Even when the service _could_ be billable, it will deny if:

- Modifier ‑59 is used instead of ‑58
- No modifier is appended at all
- Modifier ‑78 is incorrectly applied

Payers expect **‑58** when:

- Diagnostic evaluation occurs during the post‑op period
- It’s related to the original condition
- It’s medically necessary due to complication

Wrong modifier = automatic denial.

---

## Quick Audit Checklist (Use This on the Note)

31575 is **NOT billable** if **any** of the following are true:

- No diagnostic question stated
- Scope used only to assist cuff reinflation
- Findings not documented
- Bleeding described as expected or minimal
- Scope described as routine post‑op care
- No medical decision‑making tied to scope
- Modifier ‑58 not supported or not used

---

## Bottom Line

**The scope lives or dies on documentation intent.**  
Same patient, same timeline — one sentence can flip this from payable to bundled.
