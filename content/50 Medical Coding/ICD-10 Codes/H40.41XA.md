# H40.41XA — Glaucoma Secondary to Eye Inflammation, Right Eye, Initial Encounter

## Code Overview

**H40.41XA** is a billable ICD-10-CM diagnosis code representing **glaucoma secondary to eye inflammation, right eye, stage unspecified, initial encounter**. This code falls under the broader category of secondary glaucomas -- conditions in which elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) and subsequent optic nerve damage arise as a direct consequence of intraocular inflammation ([[uveitis]], iritis, [[cyclitis]], etc.) rather than as a primary structural or hereditary process.

>[!tip]
> **Note on the 7th Character "A":** The "A" in H40.41XA is the 7th character denoting **initial encounter** -- meaning the patient is receiving **active treatment** for the condition for the first time. This is distinct from subsequent encounters (D) or sequela (S). In ophthalmology, "initial encounter" typically applies to the first episode of active management of the inflammatory glaucoma.

>[!note]
> **Note on the "X" placeholder:** The 6th character "X" in H40.41X_ is a dummy/placeholder required by ICD-10-CM to allow for the 7th character extension. It carries no clinical meaning on its own.

## Clinical Description

Glaucoma secondary to eye inflammation occurs when intraocular inflammation damages or obstructs the eye's aqueous humor drainage pathway, leading to elevated IOP and progressive optic nerve damage. The inflammatory process may involve the trabecular meshwork directly (trabeculitis), or it may cause peripheral anterior synechiae (PAS) — adhesions that physically block the drainage angle. Inflammatory cells, proteins, and debris can also clog the trabecular meshwork, impeding outflow.

**Common underlying inflammatory etiologies include:**

- [[uveitis]] (anterior, intermediate, posterior, or panuveitis)
    
- Iritis / [[Iridocyclitis]] (including HLA-B27-associated disease)
    
- Fuchs heterochromic [[iridocyclitis]]
    
- Posner-Schlossman syndrome (glaucomatocyclitic crisis)
    
- Sarcoidosis-related uveitis
    
- Herpes simplex or herpes zoster-related anterior uveitis
    
- Juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA)-associated uveitis
    
- Post-surgical or post-traumatic intraocular inflammation
    

The resulting secondary open-angle or closed-angle glaucoma can be steroid-induced as a complication of treatment (which would then require a separate code, H40.6_) or directly inflammation-mediated. Careful documentation distinguishing inflammatory glaucoma from steroid-response glaucoma is critical for accurate code selection.

## Stage Guidance (6th Character)

>[!note]
> **Important:** When staging is known, it should always be specified. Avoid "stage unspecified" (X0) when clinical documentation supports a more specific stage. Payers, particularly Medicare, may deny claims coded to unspecified stages when sufficient documentation exists to support a specific stage assignment.

|6th Character|Stage|Clinical Meaning|
|---|---|---|
|**0**|Stage Unspecified|Staging not determinable or not documented|
|**1**|Mild|Early changes; visual field defect not encroaching within 5° of fixation|
|**2**|Moderate|Visual field defect not within 5° of fixation in at least one hemifield|
|**3**|Severe|Visual field defect within 5° of fixation in both hemifields, or loss of central fixation|
|**4**|Indeterminate|Cannot be assessed (unreliable fields, media opacity, patient unable to perform)|

## Includes / Excludes Notes

## Includes (H40.4 Category)

- Glaucoma associated with ocular inflammation
    
- Glaucoma resulting from uveitic trabecular damage
    
- Inflammatory open-angle glaucoma
    
- Glaucoma due to iridocyclitis
    

**Code Also:** Underlying condition causing the inflammation (e.g., uveitis, iritis, Fuchs heterochromic iridocyclitis)

|Code|Description|

|[[Q15.0]]|Congenital glaucoma|

## Excludes2 (Not Included Here — May Code Additionally If Present)

_None listed at the H40.4 subcategory level_

## Instructional Notes

- **Code first** any associated condition when the underlying cause is documented and indexed as such
    
- **Use additional code** for adverse effect if glaucoma is due to a drug (see H40.6_ for steroid-induced glaucoma)
    
- Do **not** confuse with H40.6_ (glaucoma secondary to drugs) — steroid-induced glaucoma from treatment of the underlying uveitis is a separate and distinct condition

## HCC (Hierarchical Condition Category) Mapping

**H40.41XA does NOT map to a CMS-HCC** under the standard CMS-HCC Risk Adjustment Model (V28).

Glaucoma codes as a category (H40._) are **not HCC-mapped** under CMS-HCC risk adjustment. This means the code does not directly generate a Risk Adjustment Factor (RAF) score and will not impact capitation payments under Medicare Advantage risk adjustment models.

|HCC Model|HCC Assignment|RAF Impact|
|---|---|---|
|CMS-HCC Model V28|Not assigned|No RAF|
|RxHCC Model|Not assigned|No RAF|
|HHS-HCC (Marketplace)|Not assigned|No RAF|

>[!tip]
> **Coding Tip:** While H40.41XA itself does not drive HCC capture, the **underlying inflammatory condition** (e.g., sarcoidosis D86._, rheumatoid arthritis M05._-M06._, juvenile idiopathic arthritis M08._) may very well map to an HCC. Always code the underlying etiology to capture the full disease burden and potential RAF impact.

## MS-DRG Mapping (Inpatient)

When H40.41XA is assigned as the **principal diagnosis** in an inpatient encounter, it maps to:

|MS-DRG|Description|Trigger|
|---|---|---|
|**124**|Other Disorders of the Eye **with MCC**|Principal Dx = H40.41XA + MCC present|
|**125**|Other Disorders of the Eye **without MCC**|Principal Dx = H40.41XA, no MCC|

>[!note]
> **Note:** Glaucoma is rarely a standalone reason for inpatient admission. In the inpatient setting, H40.41XA would most commonly appear as a **secondary diagnosis** rather than principal diagnosis. The underlying uveitis, iritis, or systemic inflammatory condition typically drives the inpatient admission and serves as the PDx.

**MDC:** MDC 02 - Diseases and Disorders of the Eye

**CC/MCC Consideration:**

- H40.41XA itself is **not classified as a CC or MCC** in the MS-DRG system
    
- The underlying inflammatory condition may qualify as a CC or MCC depending on specificity

## Coding Examples

## Example 1 — New Patient, Initial Evaluation, Stage Not Yet Determined

**Clinical Scenario:** A 38-year-old male with known HLA-B27-positive ankylosing spondylitis presents to ophthalmology as a new patient with acute right eye pain, redness, and photophobia. Gonioscopy reveals an open angle with IOP of 38 mmHg OD. Slit lamp shows cells and flare in the anterior chamber with posterior synechiae. Diagnosis: acute iritis with secondary glaucoma, right eye, stage not yet determinable.

**ICD-10-CM Codes:**

- `H40.41XA` — Glaucoma secondary to eye inflammation, right eye, stage unspecified, initial encounter
    
- `H20.011` — Primary iritis, right eye _(code also: underlying condition)_
    
- `M45.0` — Ankylosing spondylitis of multiple sites in spine _(underlying systemic etiology)_
    

**CPT Codes:**

- `92004` — Comprehensive ophthalmological examination, new patient
    
- `92020` — Gonioscopy

## Example 2 — Established Patient, Moderate Stage, Laser Treatment

**Clinical Scenario:** A 52-year-old female with a history of Fuchs heterochromic iridocyclitis of the right eye presents for follow-up. Visual fields show moderate glaucomatous defect. IOP is 28 mmHg despite topical therapy. Provider performs SLT of the right eye.

**ICD-10-CM Codes:**

- `H40.41X2` — Glaucoma secondary to eye inflammation, right eye, moderate stage _(Note: no 7th character "A" needed here as this is follow-up — use "D" for subsequent encounter if applicable: H40.41X2 — this subcategory does not carry the A/D/S extension; H40.41XA uses the X placeholder + A for encounter)_
    
- `H20.811` — Fuchs heterochromic cyclitis, right eye _(code also: underlying condition)_
    

**CPT Codes:**

- `92014` — Comprehensive ophthalmological exam, established patient
    
- `66855` — Laser trabeculoplasty (SLT)
    

>[!note]
> **Coder Note:** The 7th character A/D/S applies specifically to the **H40.41X_** form of the code. For the stage-specific forms (H40.41X0-H40.41X4), verify whether the 7th character extension applies in your encoder, as some editions represent the stage as the final character without an additional encounter extension.

## Example 3 — Inpatient Admission for Surgical Management, Severe Stage

**Clinical Scenario:** A 67-year-old patient with a history of sarcoidosis and recurrent uveitis presents for inpatient surgical management of medically refractory inflammatory glaucoma, right eye, severe stage, with planned Ahmed valve placement.

**Principal Diagnosis:** `H20.039` — Unspecified anterior uveitis _(or specific sarcoid uveitis code)_

**Secondary Diagnoses:**

- `H40.41X3` — Glaucoma secondary to eye inflammation, right eye, severe stage
    
- `D86.89` — Sarcoidosis of other sites _(systemic etiology)_
    

**ICD-10-PCS / CPT Procedure:**

- `66180` — Aqueous shunt to extraocular equatorial plate reservoir with graft (Ahmed Glaucoma Valve)
    

>[!tip]
**MS-DRG:** 124 or 125 depending on MCC status — driven by the principal uveitis/sarcoidosis diagnosis, not glaucoma

## Key Coding Pitfalls & Tips

- **Do not use H40.41XA for steroid-induced glaucoma.** If the glaucoma is a response to corticosteroid treatment of the underlying uveitis, use **H40.6_** (glaucoma secondary to drugs) with the appropriate adverse effect code from Table of Drugs and Chemicals.
    
- **Always code the underlying condition.** ICD-10-CM instructs "code also underlying condition" at the H40.4 subcategory level. Coding H40.41XA without the etiologic inflammatory diagnosis is incomplete.
    
- **Avoid unspecified stage (X0) when documentation supports staging.** Most commercial payers and Medicare will downcode or deny claims with unspecified staging when clinical notes contain gonioscopy and visual field data sufficient to support a specific stage.
    
- **Differentiate initial vs. subsequent encounter.** Use "A" for active treatment visits, "D" for routine monitoring/follow-up once the acute inflammatory episode has resolved or stabilized.
    
- **Bilateral disease:** If both eyes are affected, use **H40.43X_** (bilateral) rather than coding H40.41_ and H40.42_ separately, unless the stages differ between eyes — in that case, code each eye separately.
    
- **Gonioscopy documentation is critical.** Payers auditing glaucoma claims expect gonioscopy findings documented to support angle assessment and secondary glaucoma differentiation.
