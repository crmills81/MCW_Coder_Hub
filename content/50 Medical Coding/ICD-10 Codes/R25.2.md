# ICD-10 CM R25.2 — Cramp and Spasm

> [!info] Code Snapshot **Code Type:** ICD-10-CM Diagnosis Code  
> **Chapter:** 18 — Symptoms, Signs and Abnormal Clinical and Laboratory Findings (R00–R99)  
> **Block:** R25–R29 — Symptoms and Signs Involving the Nervous and Musculoskeletal Systems  
> **Billable:** ✅ Yes  
> **HCC Mapped:** ❌ No  
> **CC/MCC Status:** ⬜ Non-CC  
> **POA Required:** ✅ Yes  
> **Valid as Principal Dx:** ✅ Yes

---

## 🩺 Clinical Description

**ICD-10 CM [[R25.2]]** is a billable ICD-10-CM diagnosis code representing **cramp and spasm** — a sudden, involuntary, and often painful contraction of a muscle or group of muscles without a confirmed etiologic or anatomically specific diagnosis documented at the time of coding.

This is a **Chapter 18 symptom code**, which means it is used when:

- The underlying cause of the cramp or spasm has **not yet been confirmed** by the treating provider
- The symptom is being **evaluated, documented, and/or managed independently** without a more specific definitive diagnosis
- Documentation uses nonspecific language such as _"muscle cramp," "muscle spasm," "cramps,"_ or _"spasm NOS"_ without pinpointing a specific muscle group with a more specific code available
- An **outpatient encounter** uses a probable or suspected diagnosis without enough clinical certainty to assign a definitive code

Per **ICD-10-CM Official Coding Guidelines Section I.C.18:** when a symptom is **integral to** a confirmed definitive diagnosis, code the definitive diagnosis and omit the symptom code. Use [[R25.2]] only when the symptom is either the primary finding or not routinely associated with an identified condition and is documented and managed separately.

> [!warning] 
> Symptom-Code Rule Reminder Coders should **not default to [[R25.2]]** when documentation contains a more anatomically or etiologically specific term for the spasm. Specificity should always be maximized. Common misassignments include laryngeal spasm (→ [[J38.5]]) and blepharospasm (→ [[G24.5]]).

## 🚫 Excludes

### Excludes1 — _Cannot be coded simultaneously with [[R25.2]]_

These codes represent mutually exclusive conditions. If the documented term matches an Excludes1 code, [[R25.2]] **must not** be assigned.<sup>ICD-10-CM FY2026 Tabular</sup>

|Code|Description|Rationale|
|---|---|---|
|[[R29.0]]|Tetany / Carpopedal spasm|Carpopedal spasm is a specific manifestation of tetany (hypocalcemia, respiratory alkalosis, etc.) and carries its own code. It is not interchangeable with generic cramp/spasm.|

---

### Excludes2 — _Not included here; code additionally if clinically applicable_

These conditions have their own specific codes and should be assigned **instead of** (or in addition to) [[R25.2]] when they are supported by documentation. Specificity is always preferred.<sup>ICD-10-CM FY2026 Official Guidelines</sup>

| Code        | Description                                             | Specialty            |
| ----------- | ------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------- |
| **[[F48.8]]**   | Other somatoform disorders (includes writer's cramp)    | Psychiatry/Neurology |
| **[[G24.5]]**   | Blepharospasm                                           | **Ophthalmology**    |
| **[[G51.3]]**   | Clonic hemifacial spasm                                 | **ENT / Neurology**  |
| **[[J38.5]]**   | Laryngeal spasm                                         | **Otolaryngology**   |
| **[[M62.830]]** | Muscle spasm, unspecified site (musculoskeletal origin) | Orthopedics/PM&R     |

> [!caution] Specialty-Specific Coding Alerts
> 
> **Ophthalmology:** The term _blepharospasm_ in provider documentation directs you to [[G24.5]], not [[R25.2]]. These are not interchangeable. If documentation only states _"eyelid spasm, etiology unclear,"_ and blepharospasm is neither confirmed nor excluded, [[R25.2]] may be appropriate in an outpatient setting until the diagnosis is confirmed.
> 
> **Otolaryngology:** _Laryngeal spasm_ and _laryngospasm_ are specifically coded to [[J38.5]]. Do not assign [[R25.2]] for laryngeal-origin spasm when the documentation is clear.
> 
> **Urology:** Bladder spasm, ureteral spasm, and urethral spasm are **not** coded to [[R25.2]]. These require more specific genitourinary codes depending on context (e.g., postprocedural complications, bladder disorders, urethral dysfunction). Query the provider for specificity.

| **Medicare Advantage Impact**    | None        |

> [!note] HCC Explanation 
> [[R25.2]] is a **Chapter 18 symptom/sign code** and carries **no HCC mapping** under any version of the CMS-HCC model, including v28 (effective for payment year 2024 onward). It will **not** increase a patient's Risk Adjustment Factor or affect Medicare Advantage capitated payments.
> 
> If the cramp or spasm is a **manifestation of an underlying chronic condition** — such as multiple sclerosis ([[G35]]), ALS ([[G12.21]]), chronic kidney disease ([[N18.x]]), or diabetes — that underlying condition should be coded in addition to or instead of [[R25.2]]. Many of those conditions **do** carry HCC weight, making their accurate coding critical for risk capture.
> 
> **CDI Opportunity:** When [[R25.2]] appears repeatedly in an inpatient chart, it may signal an underlying condition that warrants a clinical documentation improvement (CDI) query to identify and capture a more specific diagnosis.

> [!caution] Inpatient Coding Guidance 
> As a **Non-CC**, [[R25.2]] will **not** upgrade or change a DRG assignment when listed as a secondary diagnosis. However, it should still be coded when all of the following apply:
> 
> 1. The cramp/spasm is **documented by the provider** (attending, consultant, or treating clinician)
> 2. It represents an **additional condition** that was evaluated, monitored, or treated during the inpatient stay
> 3. It is **clinically significant** — it affected nursing care, physician management, or length of stay
> 
> Per **UHDDS (Uniform Hospital Discharge Data Set)** guidelines, all conditions that meet the definition of an additional diagnosis should be reported, regardless of CC/MCC status. Non-CC codes contribute to **case mix accuracy, payer auditing, quality metrics (e.g., PSI, HAC),** and **clinical documentation integrity.**

## 📝 Coding Examples

### Example 1 — Urology: Post-Surgical Muscle Cramps with Electrolyte Etiology

**Clinical Scenario:** A 61-year-old male is admitted for robotic-assisted laparoscopic radical prostatectomy for prostate cancer. On post-op day 2, the patient develops bilateral calf cramping. Labs show serum magnesium of 1.2 mEq/L. The hospitalist documents "painful bilateral lower extremity muscle cramps due to hypomagnesemia" and initiates IV magnesium replacement.

| Sequence     | Code       | Description                    | Rationale                                                                                                                                                                               |
| ------------ | ---------- | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Principal Dx | **[[C61]]**    | **Malignant neoplasm of prostate** | Reason for admission; drives DRG                                                                                                                                                        |
| Secondary Dx | **[[E83.42]]** | **Hypomagnesemia**                 | Confirmed etiology of the cramping; evaluate if CC or MCC                                                                                                                               |
| Secondary Dx | **[[R25.2]]**  | **Cramp and spasm**                | May be coded as a symptom since it is separately documented and treated; however, per guidelines, omit if routinely associated with E83.42 and provider does not document it separately |

> [!note] Guideline Note 
> Per ICD-10-CM Official Guidelines Section I.C.18.b: when a sign or symptom is integral to a confirmed condition, assign only the confirmed condition code. Since cramping **is** a known manifestation of [[hypomagnesemia]], [[R25.2]] may be redundant here. Code it only if the provider documents it as a separately significant finding warranting independent management documentation. When in doubt, **query the provider** or follow your facility's clinical documentation policy.

---

### Example 2 — Ophthalmology: Unconfirmed Eyelid Spasm, Outpatient

**Clinical Scenario:** A 67-year-old female presents to an ophthalmologist with a 3-week history of involuntary, repetitive eyelid closure bilaterally. The ophthalmologist performs a comprehensive examination. Documentation states: _"bilateral eyelid spasm, etiology undetermined — blepharospasm suspected but not yet confirmed pending additional workup."_

| Code      | Description                                              | Rationale                                                                                                                                         |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **[[99214]]** | Office/outpatient E/M, moderate complexity (established) | E/M service provided                                                                                                                              |
| **[[R25.2]]** | Cramp and spasm                                          | ✅ Correct — outpatient visit; blepharospasm is **suspected but not confirmed.** In outpatient, code the **symptom**, not the suspected diagnosis. |

> [!warning] Outpatient vs. Inpatient Rule In **outpatient** settings (including physician offices and clinic visits), code only **confirmed** diagnoses. When a diagnosis is described as _"probable," "suspected," "likely,"_ or _"rule out,"_ code the **symptom or sign** instead — in this case, [[R25.2]].
> 
> In **inpatient** settings, you **may** code a diagnosis documented as probable or suspected at the time of discharge as if confirmed (per UHDDS and ICD-10-CM Guideline Section II.H).
> 
> Once blepharospasm is confirmed, the appropriate code would be [[G24.5]].

---

### Example 3 — Otolaryngology: Laryngeal Spasm During Endoscopy

**Clinical Scenario:** A 52-year-old male presents with intermittent episodes of throat tightening, stridor, and inspiratory choking. The otolaryngologist performs a flexible laryngoscopy in-office. Documentation: _"Laryngeal spasm confirmed on laryngoscopy — etiology evaluation ongoing."_

| Code          | Description                                                      | Rationale                                                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **[[31575]]** | Laryngoscopy, flexible; diagnostic                               | Procedure performed in-office                                                                                                                                               |
| **[[-25]]**   | Modifier — not applicable here; procedure is the primary service |                                                                                                                                                                             |
| **[[J38.5]]** | Laryngeal spasm                                                  | ✅ Correct — documentation is **specific** to laryngeal spasm. Do NOT use [[R25.2]]. [[J38.5]] is the appropriate code per the specificity hierarchy and Excludes2 guidance. |

> [!caution] Do Not Use [[R25.2]] Here 
> When the provider clearly documents _"laryngeal spasm,"_ the Excludes2 guidance directs you to [[J38.5]]. Using [[R25.2]] in this scenario would represent a **coding error** — it is both less specific and inconsistent with tabular guidance.

---

### Example 4 — Inpatient Multi-specialty: Idiopathic Muscle Cramps, No Etiology Established

**Clinical Scenario:** A 74-year-old female is admitted for acute uncomplicated pyelonephritis and treated with IV antibiotics. On hospital day 3, she develops severe bilateral quadriceps cramping. Nephrology, hospitalist, and physical therapy are all consulted. Electrolytes are within normal limits. All workup is unremarkable. The attending documents at discharge: _"Idiopathic bilateral quadriceps muscle cramps — evaluated and treated with supportive care and stretching protocol; etiology not established."_

| Sequence     | Code      | Description          | Notes                                                                                                |
| ------------ | --------- | -------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Principal Dx | **[[N10]]**   | **Acute pyelonephritis** | Reason for admission; drives DRG                                                                     |
| Secondary Dx | **[[R25.2]]** | **Cramp and spasm**      | ✅ Appropriate — no more specific code available; documented, evaluated, and treated during admission |

**MS-DRG Impact:**  
[[R25.2]] is a **Non-CC** and will not shift the DRG assignment. The DRG is driven by [[N10]] and any other secondary diagnoses. However, [[R25.2]] is correctly coded because it was clinically significant, evaluated by multiple clinicians, and managed during the stay.

**POA Indicator:** **N** (developed after admission — documented on day 3, not at admission)

> [!tip] Inpatient CDI Opportunity 
> If recurrent or severe cramping is documented without etiology in an inpatient chart, consider a CDI query to explore:
> 
> - Peripheral vascular insufficiency
> - Uremic cramping (**if CKD or AKI is present**)
> - Electrolyte micronutrient deficiencies (**zinc, potassium, magnesium**)
> - Medication-induced cramp (**diuretics, statins, calcium channel blockers**)
> 
> Any of these, if confirmed, could yield a more specific and potentially higher-weighted code than [[R25.2]].

## 💡 Coder Tips & Clinical Pearls

**1. Symptom vs. Definitive Diagnosis**  
[[R25.2]] should only remain as the coded diagnosis when no more specific or etiologic code is supported by documentation. The moment a provider confirms a cause, move to that confirmed diagnosis and reassess whether [[R25.2]] is still needed.

**2. Inpatient Query Triggers**  
When [[R25.2]] is the only code accounting for a documented clinical issue in an inpatient chart, it is a signal to query. Electrolyte abnormalities, CKD, peripheral arterial disease, neurological conditions, and several medications can all cause cramps/spasms — many of these have HCC mapping or CC/MCC status.

**3. Urology Pearl — Don't Use R25.2 for GU Spasm**  
Bladder spasm, ureteral spasm, and urethral spasm are **not coded to [[R25.2]]**. These require specific GU codes based on the documented anatomical site, etiology, and clinical context. Common alternatives include codes within N32.-, N28.-, or postprocedural complication categories.

**4. Ophthalmology Pearl — R25.2 is a Bridge Code**  
In ophthalmology, [[R25.2]] is often a **temporary bridge code** used while the workup for blepharospasm ([[G24.5]]) or hemifacial spasm ([[G51.3]]) is ongoing. Once confirmed, update to the definitive code. This is especially relevant for Botulinum toxin injection visits ([[64612]]), which require a confirmed diagnosis for payer reimbursement.

**5. ENT Pearl — Laryngospasm Has Its Own Code**  
When documentation is specific to the larynx, always use [[J38.5]] over [[R25.2]]. Payers may deny [[64617]] (chemodenervation of larynx) if paired with [[R25.2]] instead of [[J38.5]] or a confirmed neurological diagnosis.

**6. Outpatient Scenarios — Symptom Coding is Correct**  
In ambulatory/outpatient settings, when the provider does not confirm a specific etiology, [[R25.2]] is the **correct** code choice. Do not speculate or code a suspected diagnosis as confirmed in the outpatient setting.

**7. HCC Watch — Underlying Conditions Matter**  
Although [[R25.2]] carries no HCC weight, the conditions that **cause** cramp and spasm frequently do. Accurate coding of the etiologic condition (CKD, MS, diabetes, ALS) is critical for RAF score accuracy and appropriate risk adjustment.

**8. Modifier Awareness**

- Modifier [[-25]] — used when a significant, separately identifiable E/M service is performed on the same day as a procedure (e.g., E/M at same visit as [[31575]] or [[64612]])
- Modifier [[-59]] — used to indicate a distinct procedural service that is not bundled with another on the same claim; use X-modifiers (-XE, -XS, -XP, -XU) when payer requires greater specificity
