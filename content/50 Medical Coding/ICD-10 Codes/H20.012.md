# ICD-10-CM H20.012 – Primary Iridocyclitis, Left Eye

> ⚠️ **Note:** H20.012 is an **ICD-10-CM diagnosis code**, not a CPT procedure code. Fields such as wRVU, global period, and assistant payable do not apply to diagnosis codes. This note includes associated CPT procedure codes and billing information in the Associated CPT Procedures section.

>[!note]
> 🔗 **Laterality Cross-Reference:** H20.012 (left eye) is the direct laterality counterpart to **H20.011** (right eye). All clinical definitions, tabular Includes, Excludes 1 and 2, HCC associations, and MS-DRG mappings are identical between the two — only laterality modifiers and code digits differ. When both eyes are affected simultaneously, use **H20.013** (bilateral) rather than coding H20.011 + H20.012 together.

---

## Short Definition

**Primary iridocyclitis of the left eye** — an acute or subacute inflammatory condition of the iris (iritis) and ciliary body (cyclitis) of the **left eye** with **no identifiable underlying systemic, infectious, or traumatic cause** confirmed at the time of diagnosis (idiopathic/primary).

---

## Long / Clinical Definition

Iridocyclitis is **inflammation of the anterior uveal tract**, simultaneously involving the **iris** and **ciliary body** of the left eye. When inflammation is restricted to the iris it is termed iritis; when restricted to the ciliary body it is termed cyclitis; when both are involved simultaneously — which is the clinical norm — the combined term **iridocyclitis** is used, fully synonymous with **anterior uveitis**.

The designation **"primary"** indicates:
1. No confirmed underlying systemic, infectious, autoimmune, or traumatic etiology has been identified **at the time of the encounter**, OR
2. A complete and appropriate workup has been performed and no underlying cause was found (truly idiopathic)

>[!note]💡Left Eye Clinical Note:
>In clinical uveitis practice, acute anterior uveitis caused by HLA-B27–associated spondyloarthropathy has a strong tendency to be **unilateral and alternating** — affecting one eye at a time, sometimes alternating between right and left eyes across separate episodes. When a patient who previously had H20.011 (right eye) now presents with a left eye episode, code **H20.012** (left eye) — not bilateral, unless both eyes are simultaneously active.

### Primary vs. Secondary: The Foundational Distinction

| Classification | Code | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary / Idiopathic** | H20.012 | No confirmed etiology — use during initial workup or when workup is negative |
| **Recurrent Acute** | H20.022 | Documented pattern of two or more distinct acute episodes with remission between them |
| **Secondary – Infectious** | H20.032 | Confirmed microbial cause (HSV, VZV, syphilis, TB, etc.) — see Excludes 1 |
| **Secondary – Noninfectious** | H20.042 | Confirmed systemic autoimmune cause (AS, JIA, IBD, psoriatic arthritis, etc.) |
| **Chronic** | H20.12 | Duration of active inflammation ≥ 3 months (SUN criteria) |
| **Hypopyon** | H20.052 | Layered white blood cells in inferior anterior chamber — most severe acute tier |

### Clinical Presentation

| Feature                | Typical Finding                                                                 |
| ---------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Onset**                  | Acute or subacute; often sudden; may awaken patient overnight                   |
| **Laterality**             | Unilateral left eye (H20.012) — if bilateral, use H20.013                       |
| **Pain**                   | Deep aching periocular or frontal pain; worsened by accommodation               |
| **Photophobia**            | Prominent — ciliary muscle spasm; pathognomonic                                 |
| **Vision**                 | Variable blurring; reduced contrast sensitivity; cells in visual axis           |
| **Conjunctival Injection** | Circumcorneal (ciliary flush / perilimbal injection) — not diffuse              |
| **Tearing**                | Reflex epiphora                                                                 |
| **Pupil**                  | Miotic; sluggish; irregular if posterior synechiae form                         |
| **Slit Lamp**              | Cells and flare in anterior chamber; keratic precipitates (KPs); may see fibrin |
| **IOP**                    | Variable — may be elevated (trabeculitis) or reduced (ciliary body suppression) |
| **Vitreous**               | Anterior vitreous cells may be present in severe cases (spillover)              |

### SUN Grading: Anterior Chamber Cells

| Grade | Cells per 1mm × 1mm Slit Field | Severity |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | < 1 | Inactive |
| 0.5+ | 1–5 | Trace |
| 1+ | 6–15 | Mild |
| 2+ | 16–25 | Moderate |
| 3+ | 26–50 | Marked |
| 4+ | > 50 | Severe |

### KP Morphology as Clinical Differentiator

| KP Type               | Appearance                                    | Clinical Suggestion                              |
| --------------------- | --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **Fine, stellate KPs**    | Small, dusted on inferior corneal endothelium | Nongranulomatous — HLA-B27, idiopathic, herpetic |
| **Medium, pigmented KPs** | Medium-sized, slightly larger                 | Herpetic, Fuchs' cyclitis                        |
| **Mutton-fat KPs**        | Large, greasy, coalesced on inferior cornea   | Granulomatous — sarcoidosis, TB, VKH, syphilis   |

>[!note]
> ⚠️ **Granulomatous Uveitis Alert:** If mutton-fat KPs are documented on the left eye, the uveitis is granulomatous in nature and a specific etiology (sarcoidosis → D86.83; TB → A18.54; VKH → H20.821) is more likely. H20.012 (primary) may be appropriate during workup, but expect a code update once the granulomatous etiology is confirmed.

## Area of the Body

- **Primary Structure:** Iris and ciliary body of the **left eye** — anterior uveal tract
- **Secondary Structures at Risk:**
  - Anterior chamber (aqueous humor — cells, flare, hypopyon)
  - Corneal endothelium (keratic precipitates — KPs)
  - Lens (posterior synechiae → iris-lens adhesion → cataract)
  - Trabecular meshwork (trabeculitis → secondary IOP elevation)
  - Vitreous (anterior spillover in severe cases)
  - Macula (cystoid macular edema — most vision-threatening complication)
  - Optic nerve (inflammatory disc edema in severe cases)
- **Vascular Supply:** Major arterial circle of the iris (long posterior and anterior ciliary arteries — branches of the ophthalmic artery from the internal carotid)
- **Innervation:** CN III (parasympathetic via ciliary ganglion → sphincter pupillae); sympathetic fibers (→ dilator pupillae)
- **Laterality:** Left eye exclusively — right eye = H20.011; both eyes simultaneously = H20.013
- **Uveal Tract Layers (Anterior to Posterior):**
  - Anterior uvea: Iris + Ciliary body (H20.012 involves these)
  - Posterior uvea: Choroid (inflammation here = H30.x)
  - All layers: Panuveitis (H44.11x)

Per the ICD-10-CM FY2026 Tabular List, the following clinical terms are included under the H20.0 category and apply to H20.012:

- Acute anterior uveitis, left eye (primary)
- Acute cyclitis, left eye (primary)

### Excludes 1 (at H20.0 Category Level — Cannot Code Simultaneously)

>[!note]
> ⚠️ **Excludes 1 is non-negotiable.** These codes are mutually exclusive with H20.012. If any of these confirmed etiologies are documented, the appropriate etiology-specific code replaces H20.012 entirely — they cannot coexist on the same claim for the same eye condition. The most common clinical scenarios requiring vigilance:
> - Patient on immunosuppression with suspected CMV/HSV uveitis → aqueous PCR results positive → transition to B00.51 (HSV) or appropriate CMV code
> - Sarcoid workup returns ACE elevated + CXR bilateral hilar adenopathy + biopsy-confirmed → D86.83 replaces H20.012
> - RPR/FTA positive → A52.71 replaces H20.012

>[!note]
> 💡 **HLA-B27 and Laterality Pattern:** In HLA-B27–associated anterior uveitis (the most common cause of recurrent primary iridocyclitis in adults), episodes characteristically alternate between eyes rather than occurring bilaterally. A patient who had H20.011 (right eye) 8 months ago presenting now with left eye acute iridocyclitis should be coded H20.012 — and the underlying spondyloarthropathy (M45.x = HCC-40) should be coded every visit where it is documented and managed.
>
> 💡 **RAF Optimization Note:** When H20.012 is coded for a Medicare Advantage patient, perform a rapid HCC audit:
> - Is AS, psoriatic arthritis, JIA, or IBD documented? → HCC-40 or HCC-35
> - Is the patient on methotrexate, biologics, or systemic immunosuppression? → Document indication → HCC-40 likely
> - Is CKD or DM present? → HCC-328, HCC-37
> These conditions almost universally coexist in the chronic uveitis patient population and each carry independent RAF weight.

## Coding Examples / Scenarios

---

### Scenario 1 – First-Episode Acute Iritis, Left Eye, No Known Etiology (Urgent Ophthalmology)

**Clinical Situation:** A 31-year-old female presents urgently with sudden left eye pain, photophobia, and blurred vision noticed on waking. Slit-lamp examination: 3+ cells, 2+ flare in the left anterior chamber; fine stellate KPs on inferior corneal endothelium; left pupil miotic and sluggish. IOP: Left eye 22 mmHg (elevated, likely trabeculitis), Right eye 14 mmHg. No known systemic disease. Workup ordered: HLA-B27, ANA, ACE, RPR, QuantiFERON-TB, CXR, CBC/CMP. Diagnosis: Acute anterior uveitis, left eye.

**ICD-10-CM:**
- `H20.012` – Primary iridocyclitis, left eye *(first-listed — first episode; no etiology confirmed; workup pending)*

**CPT:**
- `92004-LT` – Ophthalmological services, new patient, comprehensive *(or 92002 if intermediate exam)*
- `92020-LT` – Gonioscopy, left eye *(angle assessment — important given elevated IOP)*

> ✅ H20.012 is correct as first-listed. The -LT modifier is required to indicate the left eye. Once workup results return, reassess whether code should be updated to H20.042 (confirmed systemic), H20.032 (confirmed infectious), or H20.022 (recurrent).

---

### Scenario 2 – Alternating Uveitis: Right Eye History, Now Left Eye Episode (HLA-B27 Positive)

**Clinical Situation:** A 38-year-old HLA-B27 positive male with a known history of ankylosing spondylitis (M45.9) presents with a left eye acute iridocyclitis episode. His prior episodes have been in the right eye (3 prior right eye episodes). This is his first left eye episode. AS is well-documented and managed by rheumatology. Ophthalmologist documents "acute anterior uveitis, left eye, secondary to ankylosing spondylitis."

**ICD-10-CM:**
- `H20.042` – Secondary noninfectious iridocyclitis, left eye *(NOT H20.012 — the systemic etiology is established)*
- `M45.9` – Ankylosing spondylitis of unspecified sites *(underlying cause — HCC-40)*

> 💡 **Do not use H20.012 here.** Once AS is a documented and confirmed diagnosis, the uveitis is secondary noninfectious (H20.042), not primary (H20.012). This is one of the most common uveitis coding errors in ophthalmology.

> 💡 **Alternating Eyes — Code Distinctly.** Prior right eye episodes coded as H20.021 (recurrent acute, right eye — established recurrence). This left eye episode, being a first left eye occurrence of AS-associated uveitis in a patient with established AS, codes as H20.042 (secondary noninfectious, left eye). If recurrence in the left eye is later established, upgrade to H20.022. Each eye's history is tracked independently in the ICD-10-CM laterality system.

---

### Scenario 3 – Code Transition: Primary → Recurrent Acute Left Eye

**Clinical Situation:** A 27-year-old female was first coded as H20.012 (primary iridocyclitis, left eye) 10 months ago. She now presents with her third acute episode of left eye iridocyclitis. Each episode resolved fully with topical steroids. Ophthalmologist documents "recurrent acute anterior uveitis, left eye — third episode."

**Updated ICD-10-CM:**
- `H20.022` – Recurrent acute iridocyclitis, left eye *(upgrade from H20.012 — recurrent pattern now documented with multiple discrete episodes)*

> ⚠️ **Code Upgrade Required:** Once a recurrent pattern is clinically documented (typically two or more distinct episodes with intervening remission), the code should be transitioned from H20.012 (primary first episode) to **H20.022** (recurrent acute, left eye). Using H20.012 for a recurrent presentation with documented prior episodes is inaccurate and fails to capture the clinical severity and management implications of recurrent disease.

---

### Scenario 4 – JIA-Associated Uveitis: Pediatric, Left Eye Chronic Iridocyclitis

**Clinical Situation:** A 7-year-old girl with oligoarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis (JIA) presents for routine ophthalmologic screening. No symptoms reported. Slit-lamp reveals 1+ cells, 1+ flare in the left anterior chamber. She has been in an ongoing low-grade inflammatory state for 5 months. ANA positive. She is managed on topical prednisolone and methotrexate.

**ICD-10-CM:**
- `H20.12` – Chronic iridocyclitis, left eye *(NOT H20.012 — duration is 5 months, exceeding the 3-month chronic threshold per SUN criteria)*
- `M08.40` – Pauciarticular juvenile idiopathic arthritis, unspecified site *(underlying systemic disease — HCC-40)*
- `Z79.3` – Long-term (current) use of systemic steroids *(if applicable)*
- `Z79.899` – Long-term use of other medications *(methotrexate)*

> 💡 JIA-associated uveitis in children is characteristically **insidious, asymptomatic, and chronic**. These children often have NO photophobia, NO redness, NO pain — and yet have active slit-lamp findings. They require routine scheduled screening even without symptoms. When duration exceeds 3 months, use **H20.12** (chronic) rather than H20.012 (acute/primary).

---

### Scenario 5 – Anterior Chamber Paracentesis for Infectious Workup, Left Eye

**Clinical Situation:** A 55-year-old organ transplant recipient (on tacrolimus + mycophenolate) presents with persistent left eye uveitis refractory to topical steroids over 6 weeks. Herpetic or CMV uveitis is suspected given the immunosuppressed state. Anterior chamber paracentesis (aqueous humor tap) is performed for multiplex PCR.

**CPT:**
- `65800-LT` – Paracentesis of anterior chamber, diagnostic, left eye

**ICD-10-CM:**
- `H20.012` – Primary iridocyclitis, left eye *(appropriate — no infectious etiology confirmed yet)*
- `Z94.0` – Kidney transplant status *(immunosuppressed — organ transplant)*
- `Z79.899` – Long-term use of other medications *(tacrolimus, mycophenolate)*

**Post-Results Update — if PCR returns CMV-positive:**
> Transition to appropriate CMV code (B25.89 or as tabular directs for CMV anterior uveitis). **Discontinue H20.012** — CMV-confirmed uveitis has its own etiology-specific code. H20.012 was the appropriate bridge code during workup; it should not be retained once a specific etiology is confirmed.

---

### Scenario 6 – Intravitreal Dexamethasone Implant for Uveitic CME, Left Eye

**Clinical Situation:** A 49-year-old male with known idiopathic recurrent anterior uveitis, left eye (H20.022), presents with decreased vision (20/60 left eye). OCT macula, left eye reveals cystoid macular edema (CME) with central subfield thickness of 410μm. Ozurdex (dexamethasone intravitreal implant 0.7mg) is placed, left eye.

**CPT:**
- `92014-LT` – Established patient comprehensive ophthalmological visit
- `92134-LT` – OCT posterior segment/macula, left eye
- `67028-LT` – Intravitreal injection of pharmacologic agent, left eye
- `C9257` – Dexamethasone intravitreal implant, 0.7 mg (Ozurdex)

**ICD-10-CM:**
- `H20.022` – Recurrent acute iridocyclitis, left eye *(primary diagnosis — established recurrent uveitis)*
- `H35.812` – Cystoid macular edema *(or most appropriate CME code for left eye — uveitic CME)* following cataract surgery — ⚠️ verify most appropriate CME code for inflammatory etiology; H35.892 (Other specified retinal disorders, left eye) may be more accurate for uveitic CME not caused by surgery*

> ⚠️ CPT 67028 (-LT) and the HCPCS C9257 drug code are billed together. The 67028 captures the injection service; C9257 captures the drug cost. Both should carry -LT for the left eye. Units on C9257 should reflect the number of implants placed (typically 1 per injection session).

### Scenario 7 – Emergency Department Presentation: Acute Left Eye Iritis

**Clinical Situation:** A 24-year-old male presents to the ED with sudden left eye pain and photophobia for 6 hours. Emergency physician performs slit-lamp exam; confirms anterior chamber cells/flare left eye. No systemic workup performed. Prescribes prednisolone acetate 1% eye drops and cyclopentolate. Referral to ophthalmology.

**ICD-10-CM (ED Claim):**
- `H20.012` – Primary iridocyclitis, left eye *(first-listed — acute iritis = iridocyclitis = H20.012 for left eye)*

**CPT (ED):**
- `99283` or `99284` – Emergency department E/M (moderate to moderate-high complexity)

> ✅ H20.012 applies equally in the ED setting. "Acute iritis, left eye," "acute cyclitis, left eye," and "acute anterior uveitis, left eye" are all coded to H20.012. The -LT modifier is not typically required on E/M codes — it is applied to procedure codes. The diagnosis code H20.012 itself communicates left eye laterality.

---

### Scenario 8 – Bilateral Presentation: Both Eyes Active Simultaneously (Code Selection)

**Clinical Situation:** A 35-year-old female with known sarcoidosis (D86.0 — pulmonary; D86.83 — sarcoidosis of eye) presents with simultaneous bilateral anterior uveitis. Both eyes are actively inflamed with 2+ cells bilaterally.

**ICD-10-CM:**
- `D86.83` – Sarcoidosis with iridocyclitis *(NOT H20.012 — Excludes 1 applies; sarcoid uveitis has its own specific code; applies to both eyes)*

> ⚠️ This scenario demonstrates that bilateral simultaneous active iridocyclitis does **not** always require H20.013 (bilateral). When the etiology is sarcoidosis, the entire H20.0x code family is replaced by D86.83, which captures both the sarcoidosis and the ocular manifestation in a single code. H20.012 and H20.013 would both be inappropriate here.

---

### Scenario 9 – Hypopyon Left Eye: Code Override

**Clinical Situation:** A 42-year-old with HLA-B27 positive reactive arthritis presents with severe left eye anterior uveitis. Slit-lamp reveals a visible 2mm hypopyon layered in the inferior anterior chamber. IOP is 30 mmHg left eye. Urgent treatment initiated.

**ICD-10-CM:**
- `H20.052` – Hypopyon, left eye *(NOT H20.012 — hypopyon is documented; use the more specific and more severe code)*
- `H40.422` – Glaucoma secondary to eye inflammation, left eye *(additional — IOP 30 mmHg indicates inflammatory glaucoma)*
- `M02.30` – Reiter's syndrome (reactive arthritis), unspecified site *(if documented as etiology — code the underlying reactive arthritis)*

> ⚠️ When hypopyon is documented, H20.05x overrides H20.012 for that eye. Do not report both H20.052 and H20.012 for the same eye on the same claim — hypopyon is the more clinically severe and specific descriptor and takes precedence. The two are not dual-coded together.

## Documentation Requirements

For accurate coding, audit support, and medical necessity, documentation should include:

- [ ] **Laterality explicitly stated:** Left eye, right eye, or bilateral — in the assessment and plan
- [ ] **Acuity/Duration:** Acute, subacute, or chronic (≥ 3 months = H20.12, not H20.012)
- [ ] **Episode number:** First episode vs. recurrent — if recurrent, document prior episode history (recurrence → H20.022)
- [ ] **AC cell and flare grade:** Using SUN scale (0–4+) — supports medical necessity for ongoing treatment and repeat OCT/testing
- [ ] **KP characteristics:** Fine stellate (nongranulomatous) vs. mutton-fat (granulomatous — triggers specific workup)
- [ ] **Hypopyon documentation:** If present, document explicitly — changes code to H20.052
- [ ] **Posterior synechiae:** If present and documented — H21.522 (left eye) may be added
- [ ] **IOP both eyes:** Required every visit — trabeculitis/secondary glaucoma detection
- [ ] **Dilated fundus exam:** Document vitreous cells, CME, disc status — posterior spillover changes clinical category
- [ ] **Etiologic workup status:** What has been ordered, what has been resulted, and whether a systemic diagnosis has been confirmed — determines if H20.012 should be transitioned to H20.042, H20.032, or Excludes 1 code
- [ ] **Systemic review of systems:** Axial/SI joint pain (AS), skin changes (psoriasis), bowel symptoms (IBD), joint swelling (JIA), pulmonary symptoms (sarcoidosis), STI history (syphilis/gonorrhea), TB exposure, herpes history, immunosuppression
- [ ] **Treatment plan:** Topical steroids (type, frequency, taper), cycloplegic, systemic therapy decision — supports medical necessity
- [ ] **Medication list:** Current systemic medications (especially immunosuppressants, biologics, corticosteroids) → support for Z79.x long-term use codes

## Coding Tips & Pitfalls

> 💡 **H20.012 is the correct code for any of these synonyms — left eye:** "Acute iritis, left eye," "acute cyclitis, left eye," "acute anterior uveitis, left eye," "subacute iridocyclitis, left eye," "nongranulomatous uveitis, left eye (primary)." The ICD-10-CM Alphabetic Index cross-references all of these to the H20.0x family with laterality selection.

> 💡 **Never use H20.012 + H20.011 when both eyes are simultaneously affected.** When both eyes are active in the same encounter, use **H20.013** (primary iridocyclitis, bilateral). Do not dual-code the right and left eye codes as separate diagnoses for the same bilateral episode — use the bilateral code.

> 💡 **Do not default to H20.019 (unspecified eye).** Laterality is always clinically determinable in an ophthalmologic evaluation — visual acuity, APD, slit-lamp, and IOP are all documented per eye. If a provider documents "anterior uveitis" without laterality, query before defaulting to the unspecified code. H20.019 may trigger payer edits when paired with laterality-specific procedures (e.g., 67028-LT).

> 💡 **Steroid IOP response is a hidden pitfall.** Patients with left eye iridocyclitis treated with topical prednisolone acetate 1% may develop steroid-induced IOP elevation (a steroid response, not inflammatory glaucoma). Document and code this accurately:
> - Elevated IOP from steroid drops → **H40.062** (Open-angle glaucoma, stage unspecified, left eye) if glaucoma criteria met, or note elevated IOP without glaucoma code
> - Elevated IOP from active trabeculitis (inflammation itself) → **H40.422** (Secondary glaucoma from inflammation, left eye)
> These are clinically different and code differently. The distinction matters for both clinical management and coding accuracy.

> 💡 **The OCT is your most important diagnostic tool in active uveitis — code it with -LT and -KX.** CME is the most vision-threatening non-glaucoma complication of anterior uveitis, and OCT (92134) is the standard of care for monitoring it. Bill 92134-LT for every visit where CME monitoring is clinically indicated. Most MAC LCDs cover H20.012 as a medically necessary indication. Apply modifier -KX when your MAC requires attestation of documented medical necessity criteria.

> 💡 **Transition codes proactively — don't wait for an audit to catch it.** The four code-upgrade triggers for H20.012 are: (1) confirmed systemic etiology → H20.042; (2) confirmed infectious etiology → Excludes 1 code; (3) documented recurrence → H20.022; (4) duration ≥ 3 months → H20.12. Build these decision checkpoints into the chart review workflow at each encounter.
