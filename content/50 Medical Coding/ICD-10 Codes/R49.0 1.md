**R49.0 is a single, non-decomposable code** - no laterality, severity, or etiology subcodes. It encompasses all dysphonic presentations until a more specific diagnosis is established.

## Coding Examples

---

**Example 1: Acute Hoarseness in Primary Care (R49.0 as Principal)**

**Scenario:**

- 35-year-old teacher with 1-week history of progressive hoarseness after prolonged vocal use.
    
- No URI symptoms, no dysphagia, nonsmoker.
    
- Exam: Mild laryngeal edema, no masses.
    
- Plan: Voice rest, hydration, follow-up.
    

**ICD-10-CM:**

- **R49.0** - Dysphonia (principal dx).
    

**CPT:**

- 99213 - Established patient office visit.
    

**Rationale:**

- Hoarseness is chief complaint; no specific pathology yet → R49.0 appropriate.
    

---

**Example 2: Chronic Hoarseness in ENT with Laryngoscopy Findings**

**Scenario:**

- 55-year-old smoker, 3-month hoarseness.
    
- Laryngoscopy: Bilateral vocal fold nodules.
    
- Documentation: “Dysphonia due to vocal cord nodules.”
    

**ICD-10-CM:**

- **J38.2** - Nodules of vocal cords (primary, specific pathology).
    
- **R49.0** - Dysphonia (secondary, symptom).
    

**CPT:**

- 99214 + 31575 (laryngoscopy).
    

**Rationale:**

- Code specific pathology (nodules) as primary; R49.0 as symptom.
    

---

**Example 3: Spasmodic Dysphonia Symptoms (R49.0 Until Diagnosed)**

**Scenario:**

- 48-year-old woman with intermittent voice breaks, strained phonation.
    
- ENT exam/laryngoscopy: No structural lesion.
    
- SLP assessment: “Dysphonia, likely spasmodic.”
    
- Plan: Voice therapy, Botox referral.
    

**ICD-10-CM:**

- **R49.0** - Dysphonia.
    

**CPT:**

- 92524 - Behavioral voice analysis.
    

**Rationale:**

- Symptom-based until G24.5 (spasmodic dysphonia) confirmed.
    

---

**Example 4: Post-Extubation Dysphonia**

**Scenario:**

- 65-year-old post-intubation for respiratory failure.
    
- Day 3: Persistent hoarseness, weak voice.
    
- Laryngoscopy: Mild edema, no paralysis.
    
- Documentation: “Post-extubation dysphonia.”
    

**ICD-10-CM:**

- **R49.0** - Dysphonia.
    
- J96.01 - Acute respiratory failure with hypoxia (principal).
    

**Rationale:**

- R49.0 captures voice symptom; intubation sequelae implied but not separately coded unless paralysis (J38.0).
    

---

**Example 5: Hoarseness with Acute URI (Code Both)**

**Scenario:**

- 28-year-old with sore throat, cough, hoarseness x 4 days.
    
- ENT: Laryngitis with dysphonia.
    

**ICD-10-CM:**

- **J02.9** - Acute pharyngitis, unspecified.
    
- **R49.0** - Dysphonia.
    

**Rationale:**

- Excludes2 allows both acute infection + voice symptom.

## **2. Full Description**

R49.9 is used when a patient has **abnormalities in voice quality, pitch, loudness, or resonance**, but the provider **does not specify the exact type** (e.g., dysphonia, aphonia, hypernasality).  
It falls under **Chapter 18 - Symptoms, signs, and abnormal clinical and laboratory findings**, specifically **R47-R49: Symptoms and signs involving speech and voice**.

This code is appropriate when:

- The provider documents **voice change**, **abnormal voice**, or **resonance disorder**,
- But **no specific etiology or subtype** is identified.

## **5. Clinical Background**

Voice and resonance disorders may arise from:

- Laryngitis
- Laryngeal polyps
- Vocal cord dysfunction
- Laryngeal or throat carcinoma
- Neurologic disorders (Parkinson’s, MS, stroke)
- Hypothyroidism
- Tracheostomy or airway injury  
    [icd10data.com](https://www.icd10data.com/ICD10CM/Codes/R00-R99/R47-R49/R49-/R49.9)

R49.9 is used when the **provider does not specify** which of these is suspected or confirmed.

---

## **6. Coding Guidance for R49.9**

### **When to Use**

- Provider documents **“voice change”**, **“abnormal voice”**, **“resonance issue”**, **“vocal dysfunction”** without further detail.
- No clear etiology is documented.
- No more specific R49.x code applies.

### **When NOT to Use**

Use a more specific code if documented:

- **R49.0 - Dysphonia (hoarseness)**
- **R49.1 - Aphonia (loss of voice)**
- **R49.2 - Hypernasality / Hyponasality**
- **R49.8 - Other voice and resonance disorders**  
    [icd10coded.com](https://icd10coded.com/cm/R49.9/)

## **13. Documentation Tips for Providers**

To avoid unspecified coding, encourage documentation of:

- **Specific voice abnormality** (hoarseness, breathy, strained, aphonic, hypernasal, etc.)
- **Suspected cause** (infection, neurologic, structural, functional)
- **Duration** (acute, chronic)
- **Impact on speech or swallowing**
- **Associated symptoms** (pain, dysphagia, cough, dyspnea)

---

## **14. Coder Notes**

- R49.9 is a **symptom code** — use only when no more specific diagnosis is documented.
- If the provider documents a **cause**, code the cause instead or in addition.
- For chronic or recurrent voice issues, query if a specific diagnosis exists (e.g., vocal cord paralysis, nodules, laryngitis).
