# 🧬 ICD-10 CM R33.9 - Retention of Urine, Unspecified

## Short Description

**ICD-10 CM [[R33.9]]** is used for **urinary retention when the patient is unable to empty the bladder adequately or cannot urinate, and the specific cause or type of retention is not documented or not specified.**

---

## Full Description & Clinical Context

**ICD-10 CM [[R33.9]]** - **Retention of urine**, unspecified describes a condition where urine remains in the bladder due to impaired emptying (**partial or complete**), but the documentation does not specify the cause or a more specific type of retention.

**Clinical manifestations that often accompany urinary retention include:**
- Inability to urinate or markedly reduced urinary output despite urge. 
- Distended, tender bladder on exam and lower abdominal discomfort or suprapubic pain. 
- Frequent, unsuccessful attempts to void, passing only small amounts of urine. 
- Weak, intermittent, or dribbling urinary stream when voiding occurs.
- Significant post‑void residual (PVR) on bladder scan or ultrasound.

**ICD-10 CM [[R33.9]]** is used when retention is clearly present, but the provider has not documented a specific etiology (**e.g., drug‑induced, neurogenic, post‑operative, obstructive**) or a more precise R33 subcode.

## Code Details

- **Code set:** ICD‑10‑CM  
- **Full code:** **<font color="#00b050">R33.9</font>**  
- **Official title:** Retention of urine, unspecified 
- **Category:** R33 - Retention of urine 
- **Code type:** Billable/specific diagnosis code. 

**Category R33 - Retention of urine:**  
- [[R33.0]] - Drug‑induced retention of urine  
- [[R33.8]] - Other retention of urine  
- **R33.9 - Retention of urine, unspecified**  

**Excludes1 (R33 category):**  
- Psychogenic retention of urine → [[F45.8]] 

**ICD-10 CM [[R33.9]]** is a **symptom code**, not an etiology; it often appears with conditions like BPH, neurogenic bladder, urethral stricture, pelvic organ prolapse, or post‑operative states, but those underlying conditions are not captured in the **[[R33.9]]** code itself unless coded separately.

## R33.9 vs R33.0 vs R33.8 vs R39.14 (Critical Distinctions)

### Within R33 (Retention of urine)

| Code   | Description                     | When Used / Key Feature                            |
|--------|---------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------|
| [[R33.0]]  | Drug‑induced retention of urine | Retention clearly attributed to medication effect. |
| [[R33.8]]  | Other retention of urine        | Specific type/description documented, not drug‑induced. |
| **R33.9** | **Retention of urine, unspecified** | **Retention present, cause or type not specified.** |

**Coding principle:**  
- Use **R33.0** when provider links retention to medication (e.g., opioids, anticholinergics).  
- Use **R33.8** when a specific, non‑drug cause/type is described (e.g., “overflow retention” or “chronic retention”) but no dedicated code exists.  
- Use **R33.9** when the documentation only states **“urinary retention” or “retention of urine”** without further specification.

### R33.x (objective retention) vs R39.14 (subjective feeling)

| Code     | Concept                                   | Documentation Phrase (Typical)                           |
|----------|-------------------------------------------|----------------------------------------------------------|
| **[[R33.8]] / R33.9** | **Objective urinary retention / incomplete emptying** | “Incomplete bladder emptying,” “urinary retention,” elevated PVR, documented retention.[web:286][web:287][web:289] |
| **[[R39.14]]** | Feeling of incomplete bladder emptying | “Feeling of incomplete bladder emptying,” “sensation that bladder doesn’t empty.” (subjective LUTS, not proven retention). |

Guidance from coding discussions stresses that **“incomplete bladder emptying” as a finding indexes to R33.9 (retention of urine, unspecified),** whereas **“feeling of incomplete bladder emptying” is coded to [[R39.14]].**

## When to Use R33.9

Use **R33.9** when ALL of the following are true:

1. **Urinary retention is present and documented**, for example:
   - “Urinary retention,” “retention of urine,” “incomplete bladder emptying,” or “post‑void residual 600 mL consistent with retention.”  
1. **The cause or type of retention is not specified** (no clear link to a drug, no specific type such as neurogenic, post‑operative, obstructive, etc.).  
2. No more specific R33 subcode is supported (e.g., not clearly drug‑induced → R33.0).  
3. Psychogenic retention is not documented (that would be [[F45.8]]).  

Common scenarios:
- ED visit: patient unable to void, bladder scan shows large volume, Foley catheter placed, diagnosis simply “urinary retention.”  
- Outpatient urology: “Chronic urinary retention” documented without further specification of etiology or drug linkage.  
- Post‑void residual ultrasound ordered with indication “urinary retention” or “retention of urine.” 

---

## When NOT to Use R33.9

Do **NOT** use R33.9 when:

- **Medication clearly responsible** (e.g., opioids, anticholinergics, antidepressants) → use **R33.0 (drug‑induced retention of urine).**  
- **Retention type is specified and falls under “other retention”** (e.g., specific chronic type, overflow retention, neurogenic pattern) without a dedicated etiology code → consider **R33.8.**  
- **Only subjective “feeling” is documented** (e.g., “feeling of incomplete emptying”) without objective evidence of retention → **R39.14**, not R33.x. 
- Documentation describes **psychogenic urinary retention** → **F45.8**, per Excludes1. 
- Provider documents a **definite etiologic diagnosis** (e.g., BPH with LUTS, neurogenic bladder) and does not document retention, in which case you code the underlying condition alone.

## Clinical Features & Evaluation (Supportive for Documentation)

Typical findings in **urinary retention** include:

- Inability to void or passing only a few drops at a time.  
- Palpable or percussible bladder distention with suprapubic pain or pressure.  
- Repeated voiding attempts with minimal output.  
- Weak or intermittent urinary stream when voiding is possible.  
- Large **post‑void residual (PVR)** on bladder scan or ultrasound.  
- Possible recurrent UTIs due to incomplete emptying.  

Common causes (which may NOT be specified when R33.9 is used):
- BPH or bladder outlet obstruction.  
- Neurogenic bladder (spinal cord injury, MS, diabetic neuropathy).  
- Post‑operative or post‑anesthesia urinary retention.  
- Medications (opioids, anticholinergics, TCAs, etc.).  

---

## Documentation Requirements (CDI‑Focused)

**Minimum documentation to support R33.9:**

- An explicit diagnosis or impression of **“urinary retention,” “retention of urine,” “incomplete bladder emptying,” or equivalent**, based on clinical exam and/or PVR studies.  
- Evidence that patient is **objectively retaining urine** (e.g., bladder scan volume, catheter output after failed void, distended bladder on exam) - even if this is not required in the wording, it is strong clinical support.  
- No specified etiology or drug cause in the documentation (otherwise, choose a more specific R33 code or underlying condition).

**Best‑practice documentation elements:**

- Whether retention is **acute vs chronic**.  
- Measured **PVR volume** (e.g., “PVR 800 mL by bladder scan”). 
- Presence/absence of pain, hematuria, infection.  
- Precipitating factors (recent surgery, new medications, constipation, neurologic disease).  
- Interventions (e.g., straight cath, Foley, intermittent catheterization).

## HCC Information

- **R33.9 does NOT map to a CMS‑HCC** in standard HCC models. 
- It is primarily a **symptom code** that may be associated with underlying conditions (BPH, neurogenic bladder, spinal cord disease) that themselves might be HCC‑relevant, but R33.9 alone is not risk‑adjusted.

## RVU / wRVU Information

- **ICD‑10‑CM codes, including R33.9, do not have RVUs or wRVUs.**  
- RVUs attach to **CPT/HCPCS** procedure and service codes; R33.9 supports **medical necessity and complexity** for services like bladder scans, catheterization, urodynamic testing, and E/M visits.

## Clinical Examples

### ✅ Example 1 - Acute Urinary Retention, Cause Not Specified

> 72‑year‑old male in ED with inability to urinate for 12 hours, severe suprapubic pain, and palpable bladder distention. Bladder scan shows 950 mL; Foley catheter placed with 1,000 mL urine returned. Provider documents: “Acute urinary retention - etiology unclear, rule out BPH vs medication effect.”

- **Code:** R33.9 (Retention of urine, unspecified)  
- Consider additional codes once etiology is clarified (e.g., [[N40.1]] for BPH).

### ✅ Example 2 - Incomplete Bladder Emptying with Large PVR

> 65‑year‑old male: “difficulty emptying bladder,” weak stream, requires straining. PVR by ultrasound is 450 mL. Provider impression: “Urinary retention / incomplete bladder emptying.”

- **Code:** R33.9 (Retention of urine, unspecified - incomplete bladder emptying).  
- Underlying cause (BPH vs neurogenic) to be worked up and coded when known.

### ✅ Example 3 - Post‑Op Retention, No Etiology Specified

> Post‑knee‑replacement day 1, patient unable to void; bladder scan PVR 700 mL, straight cath performed. Provider documents: “Post‑operative urinary retention.”

- **Code:** R33.9, unless the provider clearly links the retention to a drug (then [[R33.0]]) or a more specific cause is documented.

### ❌ Example 4 - WRONG: Subjective Feeling Only

> “Patient reports feeling of incomplete bladder emptying, but PVR is 20 mL and exam shows no retention.”

- **NOT R33.9.**  
- **Correct:** [[R39.14]] (Feeling of incomplete bladder emptying) - subjective symptom only.

---

## Common Documentation Errors to Avoid

1. **Using R33.9 for purely subjective complaints** (e.g., “feeling of incomplete emptying” without objective evidence or provider diagnosis of retention) - should be R39.14.  
2. **Failing to specify drug‑induced retention** when clearly linked to medication; in those cases, R33.0 is more accurate.  
3. **Not documenting PVR or exam findings** that support retention when available (these strengthen the diagnosis).  
4. **Coding psychogenic urinary retention** with R33.9 instead of F45.8, per Excludes1.  

---

## Compliance Checklist

Before assigning **R33.9**, verify:

- [ ] Provider documents **“urinary retention,” “retention of urine,” or “incomplete bladder emptying”** as a diagnosis.  
- [ ] There is objective support (PVR, catheter output, exam findings) when feasible.  
- [ ] The cause or specific type of retention is **not** identified (otherwise consider R33.0 or R33.8, or an etiologic diagnosis).  
- [ ] Documentation does **not** describe psychogenic retention ([[F45.8]]).  
- [ ] The complaint is objective retention, **not just a “feeling”** (for which [[R39.14]] is used).
