# 🦠 ICD-10  CM T80.211A — Bloodstream Infection Due to Central Venous Catheter, Initial Encounter

> [!success] Billable Code Confirmed
>
> **ICD-10-CM [[T80.211A]]** is a valid, billable **8-character ICD-10-CM diagnosis code** (**T80.211 + 7th character A**) for FY2026. Characters 1–3 (T80) identify the category as complications following **infusion**, **transfusion**, and **therapeutic injection**; character 4 (.2) specifies infections; character 5 (1) specifies **central venous [[catheter]]**; character 6 (1) designates bloodstream infection specifically (**as opposed to local infection — T80.212X**); the 7th character A designates initial encounter (**active treatment phase**). All characters are required.

> [!warning] Non-Billable Parent Codes — Never Submit These
>
> - ❌ `T80` — 3-character category — does not specify type of complication
> - ❌ `T80.2` — 5-character subcategory — specifies infection type only, not site
> - ❌ `T80.21` — 6-character subcategory — specifies CVC, but not bloodstream vs. local vs. other infection
> - ❌ `T80.211` — 7-character code — **requires 7th character; not billable without A/D/S**
>
> Always submit **[[T80.211A]]** (initial encounter — active treatment), **[[T80.211D]]** (subsequent encounter), or **[[T80.211S]]** (sequela) depending on the encounter phase.

> [!important] 7th Character — Active Treatment Governs "A," Not First Visit
>
> **Per ICD-10-CM Official Guidelines**, the 7th character **A (initial encounter)** is assigned throughout the **entire active treatment phase** — not just the first visit. A patient readmitted 2 weeks after **CLABSI** diagnosis who is still on antibiotics targeting the **CVC** infection still receives **[[T80.211A]]**. The 7th character **D (subsequent encounter)** is used for routine follow-up after active treatment has concluded. The 7th character **S (sequela)** is assigned when a late effect of the CVC infection is the focus of care. In most inpatient scenarios involving active **CLABSI** management, **[[T80.211A]]** is the correct 7th character.

> [!danger] HAC Status — Hospital-Acquired Condition Alert
>
> **[[T80.211A]]** — **CLABSI** — is designated as a **Hospital-Acquired Condition (HAC)** under the **CMS HAC** Reduction Program (**HAC Category: Central Line-Associated Bloodstream Infection**). When **T80.211A** is present on admission (**POA**) = "N" (**not present on admission**), it may **disqualify the case from receiving MCC/CC DRG weight** for that secondary diagnosis and can trigger a payment reduction under the **HAC** Reduction Program. **POA** indicator accuracy is critical: if the **CVC** was placed prior to admission and the **CLABSI** was present on admission, **POA = "Y"** preserves **MCC/CC** credit. If **CLABSI** developed after admission, **POA = "N"** triggers **HAC** penalties. Always verify **POA** status with clinical documentation.

## 🔍 Code Description

**ICD-10-CM [[T80.211A]]** classifies a **bloodstream infection attributable to a central venous catheter (CVC)**, captured during the initial/active treatment encounter. This entity — known clinically as **CLABSI (Central Line-Associated Bloodstream Infection)** or **CRBSI (Catheter-Related Bloodstream Infection)** — represents the systemic invasion of a microorganism into the bloodstream via the intravascular segment of a central venous **[[catheter]]**, its hub, or the insertion site tract. Common causative organisms include coagulase-negative staphylococci (**e.g., S. epidermidis — [[B95.7]]), MRSA ([[B95.62]]), Klebsiella pneumoniae ([[B96.1]]), Candida species ([[B37.7]]), and gram-negative bacilli including E. coli ([[B96.20]])**.

Pathophysiologically, **CLABSI** arises via three mechanisms: (1) **extraluminal migration** of skin flora along the outer **catheter** surface at the insertion site — most common for short-term **CVCs**; (2) **intraluminal contamination** of the hub or infusate — predominant for long-term catheters; (3) **hematogenous seeding** from a distant infection source. The infection can manifest clinically as **[[bacteremia]]/[[fungemia]]** with or without systemic sepsis response — when sepsis criteria are met, a concurrent sepsis code (A41.x) must be assigned in addition to **[[T80.211A]].**

> [!tip] Bloodstream (T80.211A) vs. Local (T80.212A) vs. Other (T80.218A) CVC Infection
> Code selection within the T80.21x family depends on the **extent and type of infection** documented by the provider:
> - **[[T80.211A]] — Bloodstream infection (CLABSI):** Positive blood culture(s) with clinical signs of systemic infection attributable to the CVC — the pathogen is in the bloodstream
> - **[[T80.212A]] — Local infection:** Exit site infection, tunnel infection, or port-pocket infection without documented bloodstream involvement — cellulitis, purulent drainage at insertion site, but negative blood cultures
> - **[[T80.218A]] — Other CVC infection:** Catheter-related infection not fitting the bloodstream or local categories (e.g., septic thrombophlebitis)
> - **[[T80.219A]] — Unspecified CVC infection:** Use only when the type of CVC infection is genuinely not documented — never default here when blood culture results and clinical documentation support a specific category

## ✅ Includes

The following clinical terms and scenarios map to **[[T80.211A]]** during initial/active treatment:

- **CLABSI** — Central line-associated bloodstream infection (**CDC/NHSN definition**)
- **CRBSI** — **[[Catheter]]**-related bloodstream infection with confirmed **CVC** source
- **Bacteremia** due to **central venous catheter** (confirmed or clinically attributed)
- **Fungemia** due to **central venous catheter** (e.g., C**andida bloodstream infection via CVC**)
- **[[Septicemia]]** attributable to **central venous catheter**
- Bloodstream infection from tunneled catheter (**Hickman, Broviac**), **PICC** line, non-tunneled triple-lumen **CVC**, or port-a-cath when manifesting as bloodstream infection
- **CVC**-associated **bacteremia**/**septicemia**, initial active treatment

> [!danger] Most Critical Code Pairing — T80.211A + Sepsis Code
> The most significant coding action when **[[T80.211A]]** is assigned is to **always evaluate for concurrent sepsis documentation**. When the provider has documented **sepsis** in the context of **CLABSI**, both the **[[sepsis]]** code (A41.x) and **[[T80.211A]]** must be assigned — the **sepsis** code sequences as principal in most inpatient scenarios (**it is the condition meeting criteria for admission**), with **[[T80.211A]]** identifying the source. Omitting the sepsis code when **sepsis** is documented is both a clinical documentation failure and a significant **DRG** weight miss — the **Septicemia DRG family** (**DRG 870/871/872**) carries substantially higher weight than the **Other Infectious Diseases DRG family** (**DRG 867/868/869**).

## 📋 Clinical Overview

### 7th Character Selection — A vs. D vs. S

| 7th Character | Code | Use When |
|---|---|---|
| **A — Initial Encounter** | **[[T80.211A]]** | Patient is receiving **active treatment** for the CLABSI — IV antibiotics, antifungals, catheter management decisions, infectious disease consultation — regardless of whether this is the first, second, or tenth visit for this episode |
| **D — Subsequent Encounter** | **[[T80.211D]]** | Patient is seen during **routine follow-up** after active treatment has concluded — monitoring, wound check after line removal site healed, surveillance cultures |
| **S — Sequela** | **[[T80.211S]]** | Patient presents with a **late effect** of the prior CLABSI as the focus of care — e.g., endocarditis developing as a sequela, septic emboli, osteomyelitis resulting from prior bacteremia |

> [!important] Active Treatment = 7th Character A
> In the inpatient setting, essentially every admission for **CLABSI** management will use **[[T80.211A]]** — IV antimicrobial therapy, **CVC** removal and replacement decision-making, and ID consultation all constitute "**active treatment**." The 7th character does not track the number of visits; it tracks the phase of care. Even if the patient was started on outpatient IV antibiotics and then admitted for deterioration, they are still in the active treatment phase — **[[T80.211A]]** applies.

### CLABSI vs. CRBSI — Clinical Definitions and Coding Impact

The **CDC/NHSN** uses **CLABSI** (**Central Line-Associated Bloodstream Infection**) as a surveillance definition, while clinicians often use **CRBSI** (**Catheter-Related Bloodstream Infection**) as a more stringent diagnostic definition. Both map to **[[T80.211A]]** — the **ICD-10-CM code** does not distinguish between these surveillance vs. clinical definitions. The provider's documentation of a bloodstream infection attributable to the **CVC** is sufficient.

| Feature | CLABSI (CDC/NHSN) | CRBSI (Clinical Diagnosis) |
|---|---|---|
| **Definition** | Laboratory-confirmed **BSI** in patient with central line ≥2 days with no other identifiable source | BSI with quantitative cultures or differential time to positivity confirming CVC as source |
| **Confirmation Required** | No — surveillance definition, no DTP required | Yes — more rigorous, requires catheter tip culture or DTP |
| **ICD-10-CM Code** | **[[T80.211A]]** | **[[T80.211A]]** |
| **HAC Reporting** | **NHSN CLABSI** rate reported to CMS | Clinical **CRBSI** diagnosis drives provider notes |
| **Coding Guidance** | Provider diagnosis governs — do **not** query **NHSN** **CLABSI** data for code assignment | Assign based on provider's clinical diagnosis documentation |

> [!tip] Coding Manifestations — Required and Optional Additional Codes
> Per the T80.2 **Use Additional Code** instruction, the following are required or strongly recommended when documented:
> - **[[B95.62]]** — **MRSA** as causative organism (**MCC — drives DRG 867 with MCC**)
> - **[[B95.61]]** — **MSSA** as causative organism
> - **[[B37.7]]** — Candidal **septicemia** (**when fungemia is the CLABSI organism**)
> - **[[A41.89]]** — Other specified **sepsis** (**when sepsis is documented and organism is specified**)
> - **[[A41.9]]** — **Sepsis**, unspecified organism (**when sepsis is documented but organism is not specified**)
> - **[[R65.21]]** — Severe **sepsis** with **[[septic shock]]** (**MCC — when septic shock complicates CLABSI**)
> - **[[Z79.2]]** — Long-term (current) use of antibiotics (**when prolonged antibiotic course is documented**)

> [!caution] HCC Opportunity — Code Concurrent Sepsis
> When a patient with a **Medicare Advantage plan** is admitted for **CLABSI**-associated sepsis, the **sepsis code** (A41.x) maps to **HCC 2** (**Septicemia, Sepsis, Systemic Inflammatory Response Syndrome/Shock**) under **CMS-HCC v28** — one of the highest-weighted **HCCs** with a substantial **RAF** coefficient. **[[T80.211A]]** itself carries no **HCC** weight, but the clinical context almost always presents an **HCC** capture opportunity through the concurrent **sepsis** diagnosis. Capture all documented concurrent conditions (**septic shock, acute kidney injury, respiratory failure**) as these may also carry **HCC** weight.

> [!warning] Sequencing: CLABSI + Sepsis = Septicemia DRG Family (Higher Weight)
> When **CLABSI** causes **sepsis**, **A41.x** (**sepsis**) typically sequences as the **principal diagnosis** (the condition that drove the admission), with **[[T80.211A]]** as a secondary code identifying the source. This shifts the **DRG** from the Other Infectious/Parasitic Diseases family (**DRG 867/868/869**) to the **Septicemia/Severe Sepsis family (DRG 870/871/872)** — a substantially higher-weighted **DRG** family. Correct sequencing in **CLABSI** + **sepsis** cases is therefore not only a clinical accuracy requirement but a significant **DRG** weight driver. Additionally, [**[T80.211A]]** as a **secondary diagnosis** (when the principal is a non-infectious primary admission) functions as an **MCC** in many **DRG** groupings — activating the highest **CC/MCC t**ier. **HAC POA = "N"** status negates this **MCC** credit — document and report **POA** accurately.

### Scenario 1 — Inpatient: CLABSI with Sepsis Due to MRSA

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 58-year-old male with end-stage renal disease on hemodialysis is admitted from the dialysis center with fever (39.8°C), rigors, and hypotension. He has a tunneled hemodialysis catheter (right internal jugular). Blood cultures drawn from both the catheter hub and a peripheral vein grow MRSA. ID consultation is obtained. The attending documents: "MRSA bacteremia/sepsis due to infected tunneled hemodialysis catheter — plan for catheter removal and IV vancomycin 6 weeks per ID." The patient is hemodynamically stabilized in the ICU.

**Principal Diagnosis:**
- [[A41.02]] — Sepsis due to methicillin-resistant Staphylococcus aureus *(reason for admission — MCC; sequences as principal)*

**Secondary Diagnoses:**
- [[T80.211A]] — Bloodstream infection due to central venous catheter, initial encounter *(CLABSI source — MCC as secondary)*
- [[B95.62]] — MRSA as the cause of diseases classified elsewhere *(required organism code per Use Additional Code instruction)*
- [[N18.6]] — End-stage renal disease *(underlying comorbidity — CC)*
- [[Z99.2]] — Dependence on renal dialysis *(dialysis status)*

**MS-DRG Assignment:** [[A41.02]] as principal (MCC itself) with [[T80.211A]] (MCC) and [[B95.62]] as secondary codes groups to **DRG 870** (Septicemia or Severe Sepsis without MV >96 Hours — the MCC tier) — highest weight tier of the Septicemia DRG family.

### Scenario 2 — Inpatient: CLABSI Without Sepsis (Local + Bloodstream, Post-Chemo Port)

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 45-year-old female with active breast cancer undergoing chemotherapy presents to the ED with fever and redness/purulent drainage at her Port-a-Cath site. Blood cultures drawn peripherally are positive for coagulase-negative staphylococcus (S. epidermidis). The oncologist documents: "Bloodstream infection and local port site infection due to Port-a-Cath (central venous port) — no sepsis criteria met. Plan for port removal and IV antibiotics." The infection is confirmed to involve the port's intravascular catheter component and the port pocket.

**Principal Diagnosis:**
- [[T80.211A]] — Bloodstream infection due to central venous catheter, initial encounter *(systemic component — most significant infection)*

**Secondary Diagnoses:**
- [[T80.212A]] — Local infection due to central venous catheter, initial encounter *(port pocket/exit site infection — separately coded per documentation of both components)*
- [[B95.7]] — Other staphylococcus as the cause of diseases classified elsewhere *(S. epidermidis / CoNS organism)*
- [[C50.911]] — Malignant neoplasm of unspecified site of right female breast *(active malignancy — MCC; drives DRG to 867)*

**MS-DRG Assignment:** [[T80.211A]] as principal with [[C50.911]] as MCC groups to **DRG 867** (Other Infectious and Parasitic Diseases Diagnoses with MCC).

### Scenario 3 — HAC POA Scenario: CLABSI Developing Post-Admission

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 72-year-old male is admitted for elective colectomy for colon cancer. A subclavian triple-lumen CVC is placed on post-operative day 1. On post-operative day 6, the patient develops fever and blood cultures grow Candida albicans. ID is consulted and documents: "Candida bloodstream infection — likely central line source (CVC in place 5 days). CLABSI."

**POA (Present on Admission) Indicator:**
- **[[T80.211A]]** — POA = **N** (Not Present on Admission) — the CLABSI developed **after** admission; the CVC was placed post-admission

**Coding Impact of POA = N:**
- [[T80.211A]] carries MCC status when coded as a secondary diagnosis — however, because POA = "N," the **CMS HAC policy** disqualifies this secondary diagnosis from receiving MCC DRG credit, preventing the case from grouping to the MCC tier
- Additionally, this case triggers NHSN CLABSI reporting and CMS HAC Reduction Program scoring, which may reduce the hospital's overall HAC score

**Secondary Diagnoses:**
- [[T80.211A]] — Bloodstream infection due to CVC, initial encounter *(POA = N — HAC; MCC credit disqualified per HAC rules)*
- [[B37.7]] — Candidal septicemia *(required organism code; Candida fungemia)*
- [[A41.89]] — Other specified sepsis *(if sepsis criteria are met and documented by attending — MCC; sequences as principal if it drove transfer to ICU)*

> [!danger] HAC Documentation Requirement
> When [[T80.211A]] has POA = "N," the hospital's quality team, infection control, and coding department should all be notified. CLABSI is subject to public reporting, CMS HAC Reduction Program penalties, and is tracked by The Joint Commission. Accurate POA assignment is both a billing compliance requirement and a patient safety quality measure.

## ⚠️ Coding Pitfalls and Tips

|     | Pitfall or Tip |
| --- | -------------- |
| ❌  | **Omitting the causative organism code (B95–B97).** The T80.2 subcategory carries a "Use additional code" instruction to identify the organism — this is a mandatory instructional note, not optional. When the blood culture organism is documented, the appropriate B95.x, B96.x, B97.x, or **[[B37.7]]** code must be assigned. Omitting the organism code is both a coding guideline violation and a DRG accuracy issue — **MRSA** (**[[B95.62]]**) is an MCC-level code that elevates DRG grouping independently. |
| ❌  | **Failing to code concurrent sepsis when documented.** When the provider documents sepsis in the context of CLABSI, both the sepsis code (A41.x) and **[[T80.211A]]** must be assigned. Many coders assign only **[[T80.211A]]** when **CLABSI** is the focus — but documented **sepsis** must be coded separately. The sepsis code sequences as principal in most inpatient scenarios, and the DRG weight difference between **DRG 867** (**CLABSI** as principal) and **DRG 870/871** (**[[sepsis]] as principal with CLABSI as source**) is substantial. |
| ❌  | **Using [[T80.211A]] for implanted port infections when T85.7x applies.** When the infection is documented as arising specifically from the **port reservoir/pocket** of a totally implanted port (Port-a-Cath), rather than the intravascular CVC component, **T85.79x** (infection due to other internal prosthetic devices) may be the more anatomically precise code. Provider documentation of whether the infection involves the **intravascular catheter** (T80.211x) vs. the implanted port device itself (T85.79x) governs code selection — query when unclear. |
| ✅  | **Always assign POA correctly and flag for HAC review.** **CLABSI** is one of CMS's tracked Hospital-Acquired Conditions. When **[[T80.211A]]** has **POA = "N,"** the hospital compliance and quality teams must be notified. **POA = "Y"** (if the infection was truly present at admission) preserves **MCC/CC** credit. Inaccurate POA assignment in either direction — labeling a pre-existing infection as HAC or failing to flag a true **HAC** — creates both compliance and payment risk. |
| ✅  | **Code both bloodstream ([[T80.211A]]) AND local infection ([[T80.212A]]) when both are documented.** When the provider documents both a local **CVC** site infection (e**xit site, tunnel, or port pocket**) AND a bloodstream infection from the same catheter, assign both **[[T80.211A]]** and **[[T80.212A]]** — these are distinct manifestations of CVC infection that are separately codeable when both are present and documented. |
