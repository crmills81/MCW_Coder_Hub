# 🩸ICD-10 CM I65.23 - Occlusion and Stenosis of Bilateral Carotid Arteries

> [!note] **Quick Reference**
> **Code:** [[I65.23]]
> **Type:** ICD-10-CM | **Status:** ✅ Billable & Valid FY2026
> **Full Description:** Occlusion and stenosis of **bilateral** carotid arteries, **not resulting in cerebral infarction**
> **Combination Code:** ❌ No
> **HCC Mapping:** ❌ NOT an HCC-generating code in V24 or V28
> **MDC:** MDC 01 - Diseases and Disorders of the Nervous System

> [!warning] **⚠️ Critical Coding Distinction**
> [[I65.23]] is ONLY valid when the bilateral carotid occlusion/stenosis has **NOT resulted in a cerebral infarction**. If carotid stenosis or occlusion has caused a cerebral infarction, do **NOT** assign [[I65.23]]. Instead, use the appropriate I63.2- combination code:
> - Right carotid infarction → [[I63.231]]
> - Left carotid infarction → [[I63.232]]
> - Bilateral carotid infarction → [[I63.233]]
>
> The Excludes1 note at category [[I65]] makes this mutually exclusive — these code sets cannot be used together for the same carotid disease event.

---

## 📋 Clinical Overview

**ICD-10 CM [[I65.23]]** identifies **bilateral occlusion and/or stenosis of the carotid arteries** — affecting both the right and left carotid systems — **in the absence of a resulting cerebral infarction**. The condition may be asymptomatic (**discovered on screening or incidental imaging**) or symptomatic (**producing transient ischemic attacks, amaurosis fugax, or other anterior circulation warning signs**) without having yet caused permanent infarction.

This code belongs to category [[I65]], which covers the full spectrum of **precerebral artery obstruction that has not (yet) caused an infarction**. "Precerebral" means the affected arteries are located **proximal to the brain** — in the neck — as opposed to the intracranial cerebral arteries covered under [[I66]]. The carotid arteries are the primary precerebral vessels, and bilateral stenosis carries particularly high risk for anterior circulation stroke given the absence of redundant unilateral blood supply.

This is **not a combination code** — it does not incorporate any downstream event. The bilateral nature distinguishes it from:
- [[I65.21]] - Right carotid only
- [[I65.22]] - Left carotid only
- [[I65.29]] - Unspecified laterality

## 🫀 Anatomy & Pathophysiology

### Carotid Artery System — Bilateral Context

The **carotid arteries** are the dominant precerebral vessels supplying the anterior cerebral circulation.

| Artery | Origin | Primary Territory |
|--------|--------|-------------------|
| **Right Common Carotid** | Brachiocephalic trunk | Bifurcates into right ICA + right ECA |
| **Right Internal Carotid (ICA)** | Right CCA bifurcation | Right cerebral hemisphere, right eye (via ophthalmic artery) |
| **Left Common Carotid** | Aortic arch (directly) | Bifurcates into left ICA + left ECA |
| **Left Internal Carotid (ICA)** | Left CCA bifurcation | Left cerebral hemisphere, left eye |

In **bilateral carotid disease**, both carotid systems carry stenotic or occlusive lesions. This is clinically more serious than unilateral disease because:
1. **Diminished collateral reserve:** The Circle of Willis may not fully compensate for bilateral high-grade stenosis
2. **Increased embolic burden:** Bilateral ulcerated plaques double artery-to-artery embolic risk
3. **Hemodynamic compromise:** Bilateral significant stenosis can reduce total cerebral perfusion pressure, particularly during hypotensive conditions

### Pathologic Mechanisms Included Under [[I65.23]]:

| Included Condition | Clinical Meaning |
|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Embolism** of precerebral artery | Local embolic occlusion of bilateral carotids, without infarction |
| **Narrowing** of precerebral artery | Atherosclerotic or fibromuscular narrowing of bilateral carotids |
| **Obstruction** (complete or partial) | Partial or total luminal blockage of bilateral carotids |
| **Thrombosis** of precerebral artery | In-situ thrombus at bilateral carotids, without downstream infarction |

### Degrees of Stenosis — Clinical Significance:

| Degree | % Luminal Narrowing | Clinical Category | Management Implication |
|--------|--------------------|--------------------|----------------------|
| Mild | < 50% | Minimal hemodynamic risk | Medical management, risk factor control |
| Moderate | 50-69% | Clinically significant | Close monitoring; symptom-based intervention |
| Severe | 70-99% | High-risk for stroke | CEA or stenting strongly considered |
| Near-occlusion | > 99% | Critical | Urgent evaluation; paradoxically lower embolic risk than severe |
| Total occlusion | 100% | Complete | CEA generally not indicated for occluded side |

### Common Etiologies:
- **Atherosclerosis** (most common) — lipid-laden plaque at the carotid bifurcation
- **Fibromuscular dysplasia (FMD)** — particularly in young and middle-aged women
- **Radiation-induced stenosis** — following neck irradiation
- **Inflammatory/vasculitic** — Takayasu arteritis, giant cell arteritis
- **Spontaneous dissection** — with or without trauma
- **Post-procedural** — recurrent stenosis following prior CEA

## 🧬 Clinical Presentations Associated with [[I65.23]]

### Asymptomatic:
- Carotid bruit on auscultation → further workup reveals bilateral stenosis
- Incidental finding on neck CTA, MRA, or carotid ultrasound
- Found during cardiovascular risk workup (high-risk coronary disease, peripheral arterial disease)

### Symptomatic (without resulting infarction — code G45.- separately when applicable):
- **Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA)** — code [[G45.9]] separately; [[I65.23]] is the underlying etiology
- **Amaurosis fugax** — transient monocular blindness ([[G45.3]]) — classic carotid TIA symptom
- **Hemianopia or hemiparesis** — transient, resolving fully within 24 hours
- **Aphasia or dysarthria** — transient speech disturbance
- **Bilateral hemispheric TIAs** — hemisymptoms alternating sides, suggesting bilateral disease

> [!important] **Coding TIA + Carotid Stenosis**
> If the patient has a TIA (G45.-) AND bilateral carotid stenosis ([[I65.23]]), both codes may be assigned. Code the TIA as the principal diagnosis when it is the reason for admission, and [[I65.23]] as a secondary diagnosis. They are from different code categories (G45 vs. I65) and can coexist.

> [!note] **Why "Includes" Matters Here**
> Unlike the I63.3- series (where mechanism specificity is required), [[I65.23]] does NOT require the provider to specify whether the mechanism is thrombosis, embolism, or narrowing. "Bilateral carotid stenosis" or "bilateral carotid occlusion" without a specified mechanism is appropriately captured here — as long as no cerebral infarction has resulted.

## 🚫 Excludes

### Excludes1 *(Mutually exclusive — CANNOT be coded simultaneously with [[I65.23]] for the same event)*

| Code | Description | Clinical Reason |
|------|-------------|----------------|
| [[G45.2]] | Multiple and bilateral precerebral artery syndromes | G45.- covers TIA; I65.23 covers the structural disease — different code categories CAN be coded together, but read carefully |
| G45.- range | Transient cerebral ischemic attack / insufficiency of precerebral artery, NOS | The Excludes1 at I65 states "insufficiency, NOS, of precerebral artery (G45.-)" — TIA codes must come from G45.-; the stenosis is separately coded from I65.23 |
| [[I63.0]]-[[I63.2]] | Insufficiency of precerebral arteries **causing cerebral infarction** | **Most critical:** If bilateral carotid stenosis caused a stroke → use I63.2- codes; [[I65.23]] must NOT be assigned |

> [!tip] **Excludes1 Nuance: I65.23 + G45.-**
> The Excludes1 at category I65 means you cannot use both I65.- AND G45.- to describe the SAME CONDITION. However, you CAN code:
> - **G45.9** (the acute TIA episode) as principal diagnosis
> - **[[I65.23]]** (the bilateral carotid stenosis causing the TIA) as secondary diagnosis
> These represent two different concepts: the acute event vs. the underlying anatomy. This is a valid and recommended coding pair.

## 🏥 MS-DRG Assignment

[[I65.23]] maps to the **Nonspecific CVA and Precerebral Occlusion without Infarction** DRG family under **MDC 01** (confirmed in CMS MS-DRG v37.0 definitions manual, consistent through v41.1).

| MS-DRG | Title | Criteria |
|--------|-------|----------|
| **067** | Nonspecific CVA and Precerebral Occlusion without Infarction **WITH MCC** | At least one qualifying MCC as secondary diagnosis |
| **068** | Nonspecific CVA and Precerebral Occlusion without Infarction **WITHOUT MCC** | No qualifying MCC present |

> [!important] **DRG 067/068 Apply to Medical Admissions Only**
> These DRGs apply when [[I65.23]] is the **principal diagnosis** AND no qualifying O.R. procedure is performed. When CEA or carotid stenting is performed during the same admission, the case exits the medical DRG partition and enters the **surgical DRG partition**, significantly changing DRG assignment and reimbursement.

### Surgical DRG Considerations (When O.R. Procedures Are Performed):

| Procedure | CPT Context | DRG Impact |
|-----------|-------------|------------|
| Carotid Endarterectomy (CEA) | CPT 35301 | Exits DRG 067/068 → enters surgical DRG partition |
| Carotid Stenting | CPT 37215/37216 | Exits DRG 067/068 → enters endovascular surgical DRG |
| Diagnostic angiography only | Not an O.R. procedure | Remains DRG 067/068 |

### Common MCCs That May Elevate to DRG 067:
- Acute respiratory failure
- Sepsis (any type)
- Coma
- Acute kidney injury / ESRD
- Severe malnutrition
- Concurrent major cardiac event (STEMI, decompensated CHF)

> [!tip] **Profee Coding Note**
> As an inpatient profee coder, the DRG is a facility determination. However, the procedures you document for the surgeon (CEA, stenting) will redirect the facility from DRG 067/068 to a higher-reimbursement surgical DRG — making accurate procedure capture critically important for facility revenue integrity and your profee documentation accuracy.

## 📊 HCC Risk Adjustment

> [!warning] **[[I65.23]] Does NOT Map to Any HCC Category**
> This is frequently misunderstood. [[I65.23]] — carotid stenosis/occlusion **without infarction** — does **NOT** independently generate a risk adjustment credit in either CMS-HCC V24 or V28.

| Model | HCC | Status |
|-------|-----|--------|
| **CMS-HCC V24** | None | ❌ Not in the V24 HCC code mapping file |
| **CMS-HCC V28** | None | ❌ Not in the V28 HCC code mapping file |

### Why This Matters for Risk Adjustment:

1. **No direct RAF contribution:** Coding [[I65.23]] alone does not increase a Medicare Advantage patient's risk score.
2. **Contrast with stroke codes:** Once a cerebral infarction occurs (I63.2- codes), the patient becomes HCC 96 (V24) / HCC 224 (V28) — with significant RAF weight — underscoring why the distinction between [[I65.23]] and I63.2- matters for both clinical accuracy and risk integrity.
3. **Clinical value remains high:** While [[I65.23]] does not generate HCC credit, it is essential for:
   - Supporting medical necessity for diagnostic studies (carotid duplex, CTA, MRA)
   - Justifying CEA or carotid stenting procedures
   - Quality measure and STAR rating reporting
   - Accurate severity-of-illness documentation
4. **Associated conditions DO generate HCC:** Concurrent conditions like diabetes ([[E11.9]]), atherosclerosis, heart failure, and chronic kidney disease that accompany bilateral carotid disease WILL generate HCC credit — always capture all documented comorbidities.

## 📝 Coding Guidelines & Clinical Notes

### Official Guideline Highlights (ICD-10-CM FY2026):

1. **The Key Rule — No Infarction:** [[I65.23]] is ONLY valid when the bilateral carotid disease has NOT caused a cerebral infarction. The moment infarction is documented as resulting from carotid stenosis, code must change to I63.2- combination codes.

2. **Bilateral Means Both Arteries:** [[I65.23]] is appropriate ONLY when both right AND left carotid systems are affected. If only one side has disease, use [[I65.21]] (right) or [[I65.22]] (left).

3. **Mechanism Is Not Required:** Unlike the I63.3- series, [[I65.23]] does NOT require documentation of specific mechanism. "Carotid stenosis" or "carotid occlusion" without further specification is appropriately captured here — as long as no infarction has resulted.

4. **Carotid Disease Without Specified Occlusion or Stenosis:** If the provider documents only "bilateral carotid artery disease" without specifying occlusion or stenosis, the correct code may be [[I67.2]] (Cerebral atherosclerosis). Query the provider if the nature of disease is unclear — [[I65.23]] should reflect confirmed stenosis or occlusion.

5. **TIA + Carotid Stenosis — Both Codes Apply:**
   - Principal DX: G45.- (what brought the patient in)
   - Secondary DX: [[I65.23]] (the underlying bilateral carotid stenosis)
   - Highly recommended coding pair for neurovascular workup and CEA medical necessity

6. **Asymptomatic Carotid Stenosis:** If bilateral carotid stenosis is found incidentally AND documented AND affects patient management, [[I65.23]] CAN be coded as a secondary diagnosis. Do not code conditions mentioned in passing without clinical significance.

7. **Post-CEA Status:** After endarterectomy, code appropriately for any residual or recurrent stenosis. If stenosis is resolved, it should not continue to be coded.

## 🩺 Coding Examples / Clinical Scenarios

---

### 📌 Example 1 - Symptomatic Bilateral Carotid Stenosis + TIA (DRG 068)

**Clinical Scenario:**
A 71-year-old male with hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and 40 pack-year smoking history presents after two episodes of right arm numbness and slurred speech resolving within 15 minutes. MRI brain is negative for infarction. Carotid duplex reveals 65% right ICA stenosis and 55% left ICA stenosis. Neurologist documents: *"Bilateral carotid stenosis with TIA. No infarction on MRI."* No surgical procedure performed.

**Codes Assigned:**
- [[G45.9]] - Transient cerebral ischemic attack, unspecified *(Principal Dx)*
- [[I65.23]] - Occlusion and stenosis of bilateral carotid arteries *(Secondary — underlying anatomy)*
- [[I10]] - Essential hypertension *(Secondary)*
- [[E78.5]] - Hyperlipidemia, unspecified *(Secondary)*
- [[F17.210]] - Nicotine dependence, cigarettes *(Secondary)*

**MS-DRG:** DRG 068 - Nonspecific CVA and Precerebral Occlusion without Infarction without MCC

> **Coder Note:** TIA ([[G45.9]]) is the principal diagnosis — it is the reason for admission. [[I65.23]] is the secondary diagnosis explaining the anatomic etiology.

---

### 📌 Example 2 - Bilateral Carotid Stenosis with MCC (DRG 067)

**Clinical Scenario:**
A 78-year-old female with ESRD on dialysis and atrial fibrillation is admitted with amaurosis fugax of the left eye. MRI confirms no infarction. CTA neck demonstrates 80% right ICA stenosis and 70% left ICA stenosis. Physician documents: *"Bilateral high-grade carotid stenosis presenting with amaurosis fugax. ESRD. AFib."*

**Codes Assigned:**
- [[G45.3]] - Amaurosis fugax *(Principal Dx)*
- [[I65.23]] - Bilateral carotid stenosis *(Secondary)*
- [[N18.6]] - End-stage renal disease *(MCC)*
- [[I48.11]] - Long-standing persistent atrial fibrillation *(Secondary)*

**MS-DRG:** DRG 067 - WITH MCC (ESRD qualifies as MCC)

---

### 📌 Example 3 - Bilateral Carotid Stenosis with CEA Performed (Surgical DRG)

**Clinical Scenario:**
A 68-year-old male is admitted for elective right carotid endarterectomy for documented 75% right ICA stenosis. Left ICA has 60% stenosis (asymptomatic). Surgeon documents: *"Right CEA for symptomatic 75% right carotid stenosis. Known bilateral carotid disease."*

**Codes Assigned:**
- [[I65.21]] - Right carotid stenosis *(Principal Dx — the reason for surgery)*
- [[I65.23]] - Bilateral carotid stenosis *(Secondary — left side still stenosed; bilateral disease burden)*

**Procedure:** CPT 35301 (profee) / ICD-10-PCS code (facility)

**MS-DRG:** Surgical DRG — exits DRG 067/068 into surgical partition

> **Coder Note:** When the RIGHT carotid is the surgical target, [[I65.21]] is the principal diagnosis. [[I65.23]] is secondary to represent the bilateral disease burden. Do NOT use [[I65.23]] as principal when only one side is being operated on.

---

### 📌 Example 4 - Asymptomatic Bilateral Stenosis Found During CABG Admission

**Clinical Scenario:**
A 62-year-old patient is admitted for CABG. Pre-op vascular assessment reveals moderate bilateral carotid stenosis (50% right, 45% left) on CTA. No neurological symptoms. Cardiologist documents: *"Bilateral carotid stenosis, moderate, asymptomatic — follow up with vascular surgery post-discharge."*

**Codes Assigned:**
- Principal Dx: Coronary artery disease *(reason for CABG)*
- Secondary Dx: [[I65.23]] - Bilateral carotid stenosis *(documented, affects pre-op management)*

> **Coder Note:** [[I65.23]] is appropriate as secondary because it was documented AND affected management (carotid evaluation is part of pre-op CABG planning protocol). Do not omit because it appears "incidental."

---

### 📌 Example 5 - Incorrect Coding: Carotid Stenosis WITH Resulting Infarction

**Clinical Scenario:**
A 75-year-old female is admitted with acute left-sided weakness and dysarthria. MRI confirms right hemisphere infarction. Neurologist documents: *"Acute cerebral infarction due to bilateral carotid artery stenosis."*

**Incorrect Code:**
- ❌ [[I65.23]] — NOT appropriate; infarction resulted from the stenosis

**Correct Code:**
- ✅ [[I63.233]] - Cerebral infarction due to unspecified occlusion or stenosis of bilateral carotid arteries *(combination code — captures both the infarction AND the bilateral carotid disease)*

> **Coder Note:** Once infarction is documented as caused by bilateral carotid stenosis, [[I65.23]] must NOT be used. [[I63.233]] absorbs the carotid stenosis entirely into the combination code.

### 📌 Example 6 - Outpatient Surveillance Visit (Profee/Outpatient)

**Clinical Scenario:**
A 70-year-old male established with vascular surgery for bilateral carotid stenosis (right 60%, left 55%) is seen for semi-annual duplex surveillance. Stable, asymptomatic, on antiplatelet and statin. Provider documents: *"Bilateral carotid stenosis, stable, medical management continued."*

**Codes Assigned:**
- [[I65.23]] - Bilateral carotid stenosis *(Principal Dx for this outpatient visit)*
- [[I10]] - Hypertension *(Secondary)*
- [[E78.5]] - Hyperlipidemia *(Secondary)*

**CPT for surveillance imaging:** 93880 (bilateral complete duplex scan of extracranial arteries)

---

## ⚠️ Common Coding Errors & Pitfalls

| ❌ Error | ✅ Correct Practice |
|---------|-------------------|
| Using [[I65.23]] when carotid stenosis has caused a cerebral infarction | Use I63.2- combination code ([[I63.233]] for bilateral causative stenosis) — Excludes1 prohibits [[I65.23]] |
| Coding [[I65.23]] AND [[I63.233]] together for the same event | These are mutually exclusive per Excludes1; use only one |
| Using [[I65.29]] (unspecified) when bilateral is documented | [[I65.23]] is correct for bilateral — [[I65.29]] is for truly unknown laterality |
| Assigning [[I65.23]] when only one carotid is diseased | Use [[I65.21]] (right) or [[I65.22]] (left) for unilateral disease |
| Assuming [[I65.23]] generates HCC credit | ❌ It does NOT map to any HCC in V24 or V28 |
| Not coding [[I65.23]] as secondary when documented and clinically relevant | Always code it when documented and affecting management — undercoding is a compliance issue |
| Omitting G45.- TIA code and only coding [[I65.23]] when TIA is the reason for admission | Code both: G45.- (TIA episode, principal) AND [[I65.23]] (structural anatomy, secondary) |
| Using [[I67.2]] when stenosis/occlusion is confirmed | [[I65.23]] is the specific correct code when bilateral carotid stenosis is confirmed by imaging |

---

## 📄 Documentation Requirements Checklist

- [ ] **Bilateral involvement:** Both RIGHT AND LEFT carotid arteries documented as affected
- [ ] **No resulting infarction:** MRI/CT negative for infarction OR infarction from unrelated cause confirmed
- [ ] **Stenosis or occlusion confirmed:** Imaging (duplex, CTA, MRA, or angiography) confirming bilateral stenosis/occlusion
- [ ] **Degree of stenosis:** Percentage from each side to support medical necessity for interventions
- [ ] **Symptomatic vs. asymptomatic:** Documented to support surgical decision-making and medical necessity
- [ ] **Clinical management impact:** If stenosis affects management → document to justify secondary coding
- [ ] **Risk factors documented:** Hypertension, diabetes, hyperlipidemia, tobacco — code separately
- [ ] **TIA or amaurosis fugax:** If present, documented separately with G45.- codes for complete coding

---

## 🔑 Key Takeaways for Inpatient Profee Coders

- [[I65.23]] = **bilateral carotid stenosis/occlusion** — both sides, **NO resulting infarction**
- **The moment infarction occurs** from bilateral carotid disease → use **[[I63.233]]** (combination code); [[I65.23]] must NOT be used
- [[I65.23]] does **NOT generate HCC credit** in V24 or V28 — understand this for risk adjustment strategy discussions with MA plans
- **DRG 067/068** (Nonspecific CVA and Precerebral Occlusion without Infarction) apply to medical admissions with this as principal Dx
- When **CEA or carotid stenting** is performed → exits DRG 067/068 into the surgical DRG partition
- **TIA + [[I65.23]]** is a valid, recommended coding pair — G45.- is principal, [[I65.23]] is secondary (different code categories)
- **Asymptomatic** bilateral carotid stenosis → code only when documented AND affecting management
- **Do NOT use [[I65.29]]** (unspecified) when bilateral is clearly documented — [[I65.23]] is the right code and provides required specificity
