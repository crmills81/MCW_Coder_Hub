# 🧬 ICD-10 CM G89.11 — Acute Pain Due to Trauma

> [!success] Billable Code Confirmed
> **ICD-10-CM [[G89.11]]** is a 5-character code: chapter 6 letter G, category 89 (pain NEC), subcategory 1 (acute pain), and a final digit 1 for trauma. It is valid for HIPAA claims in FY2026 and requires no 7th character or further specificity.<sup>1,3</sup>

> [!warning] Non-Billable Parent Codes
> **G89** — **Pain**, not elsewhere classified is a category header and cannot be reported on a claim.<sup>2</sup>
> **G89.1** — **Acute pain**, not elsewhere classified is a subcategory header that requires a 5th character to identify **trauma, post-thoracotomy, or other postprocedural origin**.<sup>2</sup>

> [!important] Clinical Context
> **Provider documentation** must link the acute pain to an injury or trauma to select this code. **Pain** that is not documented **as acute or chronic does not qualify for any G89 code.**<sup>1</sup>

> [!danger] Code Classification
> This is a **diagnosis code (ICD-10-CM)**, not a procedure code. It is a **pain-type code** that supplements or, in limited circumstances, replaces a site-specific pain code, and it is not a definitive diagnosis for the injury itself.<sup>1</sup>

---

## 🔍 Code Description

**ICD-10 CM [[G89.11]]** captures **acute pain** that the provider attributes to a recent injury or trauma. It sits in **subcategory G89.1** beside **[[G89.12]]** (**acute post-[[thoracotomy]] pain**) and **[[G89.18]]** (**other acute postprocedural pain**), and the Index path is Pain, acute, due to trauma.<sup>2,3</sup> Per the **FY2026 Guidelines**, **G89 codes** may be used with codes from other chapters to add detail about acute or chronic pain.<sup>1</sup>

Do **not** assign a **G89 code** when the underlying definitive diagnosis is known, unless the reason for the encounter is pain control or **pain management** rather than treatment of the underlying condition.<sup>1</sup> When **pain management** is the stated reason for the encounter, **[[G89.11]]** may be sequenced first, followed by the injury or site-specific code such as **[[M54.2]]**.<sup>1,7</sup> Pain that is not documented as acute or chronic defaults to **[[R52]]**, not G89.<sup>2</sup>

> [!example] Acute vs. Chronic Trauma Pain
> Choosing **[[G89.11]]** over **[[G89.21]]** depends on provider documentation of **acute versus chronic**. There is no defined time frame for when pain becomes chronic, so query the provider if the documentation is unclear.<sup>1</sup>

> The **G89 Excludes notes** are listed at the category level, so they apply to **[[G89.11]]** even though they do not print under the **5-character code** in the tabular.<sup>2</sup>

## ✅ Includes

- **Acute pain due to injury:** Index and approximate-synonym wording that maps to **[[G89.11]]**.<sup>3</sup>
- **Acute pain due to trauma:** Provider documentation of pain linked to a recent **[[fracture]], [[contusion]], sprain, or other injury**.
- **Pain control encounters for traumatic injury:** Encounters where **pain management** is the stated reason, for example a nerve block for acute **[[fracture]]** pain.<sup>1</sup>

---

## ❌ Excludes

### Excludes 1

- **[[R52]]:** **Pain NOS and generalized pain NOS** are mutually exclusive with **G89 codes**. If the pain type is not specified, **[[R52]]** applies instead.<sup>2</sup>
- **[[F45.41]]:** Pain disorder exclusively related to psychological factors cannot be reported with **G89 codes**.<sup>2</sup>

> [!danger]
> The most common **Excludes 1 error** is reporting **[[G89.11]]** and **[[R52]]** together when the provider documents only "**pain**." **[[G89.11]]** requires explicit documentation of **acute pain** due to trauma, so a nonspecific pain note should go to **[[R52]]** alone.

### Excludes 2

- **[[G50.1]]:** Atypical facial pain is classified separately. Both can be reported if the patient has acute traumatic pain and a separately documented atypical facial pain condition.<sup>2</sup>
- **[[M54.9]]:** Localized pain of unspecified type is coded by site. For acute traumatic back pain, **[[G89.11]]** can be reported with the site-specific code to add acuity and cause.<sup>1,2</sup>
- **G44.-:** Headache syndromes are excluded from **G89** and coded separately. Both may be reported when documentation supports a distinct headache syndrome.<sup>2</sup>

## 📋 Clinical Overview

### Acute Traumatic vs. Chronic Traumatic vs. Postprocedural Pain

**Clinicians** distinguish pain by timing and cause, and the **G89 subcategories** mirror that. **[[G89.11]]** reflects pain from a recent injury and is expected to resolve as the tissue heals. The comparison codes cover pain that persists or that stems from a procedure rather than an injury.

| **Feature** | **[[G89.11]]** | **Related [[G89.21]]** | **Related [[G89.18]]** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Onset** | Recent, acute pain caused by injury or trauma. | Persistent pain that continues after the injury, documented as chronic. | Pain after a procedure or surgery, other than thoracotomy. |
| **Cause** | Trauma such as a fall, fracture, crash, or contusion. | Trauma, with provider documentation of chronicity. | Surgery or another procedure, not an injury. |
| **Use rule** | Report when acute pain is documented and linked to trauma. | Report when chronic pain is documented and linked to trauma. | Report when postprocedural pain is documented as beyond routine expected postoperative pain. |

> [!important]
> A **CDI trigger** is documentation that says only "**pain**" with a known injury. **Query** the provider for acuity and for whether pain control is the reason for the encounter, because that decides whether **[[G89.11]]** can be principal or first-listed.<sup>1</sup>

### Manifestations & Symptom Burden

- **Localized pain:** Sharp or aching pain at the injury site, coded by site with **[[M25.561]]** or **[[M79.601]]**-type codes.
- **Reduced function:** Pain that limits movement or weight bearing, which supports medical necessity for blocks or consults.
- **Sleep disturbance and distress:** Acute pain can disrupt sleep and mood. Related psychological factors may be added with **[[F45.42]]** when documented.<sup>2</sup>
- **Escalating analgesic need:** Opioid or regional anesthesia requirements often drive inpatient pain service involvement.

> [!tip]
> **ICD-10 CM [[G89.11]]** is not a manifestation code and has no **etiology/manifestation** convention. Report the injury code and the site-specific pain code separately when documented.<sup>1</sup>

- **[[64483]]:** Transforaminal epidural injection, lumbar or sacral, single level, with imaging guidance. Check the ESI LCD criteria first, because acute traumatic pain alone is not a listed indication.<sup>5</sup>

### NCCI Bundling Considerations

**Imaging guidance** is included in **[[64483]]**, so do not bill it separately. A **same-day E/M** with a block needs **[[-25]]** and documentation that it was significant and separately identifiable. Check **current NCCI edits** before using **[[-59]]**.

## 💊 Coding Scenarios and Examples

### Example 1
> **Clinical Scenario:**
> The pain service is consulted for a patient awaiting hip surgery after a fall with a closed left femoral neck fracture. The stated reason for the consult is acute pain control, and the anesthesiologist performs a single-injection femoral nerve block on the left.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[64447]] [[-LT]] | Single-injection femoral nerve block, left side. |
| PDx | [[G89.11]] | The encounter is specifically for pain management of acute traumatic pain. |
| SDx | [[S72.002A]] | Reports the underlying closed left femoral neck fracture, initial encounter. |

> [!tip]
> Sequence [[G89.11]] first because pain control is the reason for the encounter, followed by the fracture code.<sup>1</sup>
> CDI note: confirm the provider documents "acute pain due to fracture" and the pain management intent.

### Example 2
> **Clinical Scenario:**
> A patient with a closed right proximal humerus fracture from a fall is seen by the pain service for an initial consult. The provider performs a right interscalene brachial plexus block for acute pain.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[64415]] [[-RT]] | Single-injection brachial plexus block, right side. |
| CPT 2 | [[99223]] [[-25]] | Separately identifiable high-level initial E/M on the same day as the block. |
| PDx | [[G89.11]] | Pain control is the focus of the pain service encounter. |
| SDx | [[S42.201A]] | Reports the closed right humerus fracture, initial encounter. |

> [!tip]
> The E/M needs documentation beyond the usual pre-procedure evaluation to support [[-25]].
> CDI note: document the fracture side so the block laterality and S-code agree.

### Example 3
> **Clinical Scenario:**
> On hospital day 3, the pain service follows up on a patient with closed right-sided multiple rib fractures. Acute pain remains the focus, and the provider adjusts the multimodal regimen.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[99233]] | Subsequent hospital care, high level, for pain management. |
| PDx | [[G89.11]] | Documented acute traumatic pain is the reason for the pain service visit. |
| SDx | [[S22.41XA]] | Reports the multiple right rib fractures, initial encounter. |

> [!tip]
> The 7th character stays "A" while the patient is receiving active treatment.
> CDI note: if the pain service documents no pain-specific plan, the visit may not support [[G89.11]] as first-listed.

## ⚠️ Coding Pitfalls and Tips

- **Pitfall 1**: Assigning **[[G89.11]]** when the injury is known and the encounter is for treating it; **Tips**: Report **[[G89.11]]** only when pain control is the stated reason for the encounter, or as an additional code to add detail.<sup>1</sup>
- **Pitfall 2**: Reporting **[[G89.11]]** with **[[R52]]**; **Tips**: These are **Excludes 1.** If the pain type is not specified as acute or chronic, use [[R52]] alone.<sup>2</sup>
- **Pitfall 3**: Using **[[G89.11]]** for routine postoperative pain; **Tips**: Routine or expected **postoperative pain** is not coded to **G89**. Use **[[G89.18]]** only when the provider documents **postprocedural pain** that is not routine.<sup>1</sup>
- **Pitfall 4**: Confusing acute and chronic trauma pain; **Tips**: Choose **G89.11** or **[[G89.21]]** from the provider's documentation, since there is no set timeframe for chronic pain.<sup>1</sup>
- **Pitfall 5**: Billing a block with an E/M and no **[[-25]]**; **Tips**: Document a significant, separately identifiable E/M, and apply **[[-25]]** only when supported.
- **Pitfall 6**: Assuming an **LCD covers** any pain diagnosis for epidural injections; **Tips**: **[[G89.11]]** alone does not meet the **ESI LCD** covered indications, so check your **MAC's current LCD** and article before billing.<sup>5</sup>
