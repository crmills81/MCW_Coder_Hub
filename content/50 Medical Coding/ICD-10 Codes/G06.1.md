# 🧬 ICD-10 CM G06.1 — Intraspinal Abscess and Granuloma

> [!success] Billable Code Confirmed
> **ICD-10 CM [[G06.1]]** is a complete code: **G06** is the **3-character category** and the fourth character "**1**" (**intraspinal**) is the final level, so there is nothing more to add. It is valid for **HIPAA** claims in **FY2026** and had no change that year.<sup>3</sup>

> [!warning] Non-Billable Parent Codes
> **G06** (I**ntracranial and intraspinal abscess and granuloma**) is a category header and is not reportable on its own. It requires the fourth character to say intracranial (.**0**), intraspinal (.**1**), or **extradural/subdural** unspecified (.**2**).<sup>1,2</sup>

> [!important] Clinical Context
> The location of the **[[abscess]]** drives the code: inside the spinal canal (**epidural, extradural, subdural, or cord**) is **[[G06.1]]**, and inside the skull is **[[G06.0]]**. **Spinal epidural abscess** is a neurosurgical emergency because **cord compression** can cause permanent deficits.<sup>8</sup>

> [!danger] Code Classification
> This is an **ICD-10-CM diagnosis code**. It is not a procedure code, and it is not a **manifestation code** (no "**code first**" rule), so it can be sequenced as the principal or first-listed diagnosis.

---

## 🔍 Code Description

**ICD-10 CM G06.1** reports an **[[abscess]]** (**pus collection**) or **[[granuloma]]** (**inflammatory mass**) inside the spinal canal, including the **[[epidural]]**, **[[extradural]]**, and **[[subdural]]** spaces and the cord itself. The category has a "**use additional code (B95-B97)**" note, so the organism (for example **[[B95.61]]** **MSSA** or **[[B95.62]]** **MRSA**) is reported with it when documented.<sup>1,2</sup>

The usual presentation is **back pain, fever, and progressive neurologic deficit**, and the **[[abscess]]** often spreads from **vertebral [[osteomyelitis]]** (**[[M46.26]]**) or **[[discitis]]**. **[[compression]]** of the cord is captured separately with **[[G95.20]]** when documented. Intracranial disease goes to **[[G06.0]]**, and a location documented only as "**extradural and subdural abscess, unspecified**" goes to **[[G06.2]]**.<sup>1,8</sup>

> [!example] Intraspinal vs. Unspecified Extradural/Subdural
> If the provider documents "**spinal epidural abscess**," go to **[[G06.1]]**, not **[[G06.2]]**. **[[G06.2]]** is for a documented **[[extradural]]** or **[[subdural]]** **[[abscess]]** with no spinal or cranial site stated, so query the provider if that is what you have.

> [!tip]
> Do **not** report **[[G06.1]]** and a skin or soft-tissue **[[abscess]]** code for the same **[[lesion]]**. The Alphabetic Index sends "**Abscess, spinal cord**" and "**Abscess, epidural, spinal**" to **[[G06.1]]**.<sup>5</sup>

## ✅ Includes

- **Abscess (embolic) of spinal cord [any part]**: Covers the cord itself, at any level.<sup>2</sup>
- **Intraspinal epidural abscess or granuloma**: This is the classic spinal epidural abscess (SEA).<sup>2</sup>
- **Intraspinal extradural abscess or granuloma**: This is an alternate wording for the epidural space.<sup>2</sup>
- **Intraspinal subdural abscess or granuloma**: This is a rarer location, but it still falls under **[[G06.1]]**.<sup>2</sup>

---

## ❌ Excludes

### Excludes 1

- **ICD-10 CM [[A17.81]]** (**[[Tuberculoma]] of meninges**): **Tuberculous intraspinal [[abscess]]** and **[[granuloma]]** is excluded from **[[G06.1]]**. These two cannot be reported together, because the **[[tuberculous]]** form is classified in the infectious chapter.<sup>1,2</sup>

> [!danger]
> The common **error** is reporting **[[G06.1]]** with **[[A17.81]]** when a **spinal abscess** is documented as **[[tuberculous]]**. **Pott disease of the spine** or a **TB abscess** goes to **[[A17.81]]** or the appropriate **A18.0- code**, and the provider documentation decides which.

### Excludes 2

- **Chapter-level Excludes2 (G00-G99):** The note at the top of the **G chapte**r lists **infectious** and **parasitic diseases** (**A00-B99**), **neoplasms** (**C00-D49**), and **injury** and **poisoning** (**S00-T88**), among others. It is a **chapter-level note** and not a note under **[[G06.1]].** It means both codes can be reported together when both conditions are present, for example an infectious organism code from **B95-B97** alongside **[[G06.1]]**.<sup>14</sup>

## 📋 Clinical Overview

### Intraspinal vs. Intracranial vs. Unspecified Abscess

The three **G06 codes** split on where the abscess sits. Choosing the right one depends on the provider naming the anatomic site, and the **CDI query** is usually about the site, not the organism.

| **Feature** | **[[G06.1]]** | **Related [[G06.0]]** | **Related [[G06.2]]** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Site** | Inside the spinal canal: epidural, extradural, subdural, or cord. | Inside the skull: brain, cerebellum, or intracranial epidural/subdural. | Extradural or subdural abscess with no spinal or cranial site stated. |
| **Typical presentation** | Back pain, fever, and progressive weakness or sensory loss. | Headache, fever, focal deficit, and seizures. | Varies, and the documentation is incomplete. |
| **Coding use** | Use for any documented spinal epidural, extradural, subdural, or cord abscess. | Use for brain abscess and for otogenic abscess. | Use only when the site really cannot be specified. |

> [!important]
> **CDI trigger**: a record that says "**epidural [[abscess]]**" without a spinal or cranial site should be queried so it does not default to **[[G06.2]]**. Also query for the organism, since it is needed for the **B95-B97 additional code**, and for any **cord compression** or neurologic deficit.

### Manifestations & Symptom Burden

- **Back pain:** Focal spinal pain with tenderness over the involved level is the most common first sign.
- **Fever:** Often present, though it can be absent early on.
- **Motor or sensory deficit:** Weakness, numbness, or paralysis below the lesion level, which signals cord or root compression.
- **Bladder and bowel dysfunction:** Retention or incontinence, which may be coded separately (for example **[[G83.4]]** for cauda equina syndrome).
- **Spinal cord compression:** Reported with **[[G95.20]]** when documented.

> [!tip]
> Symptoms that are integral to the **[[abscess]]** (**fever, back pain**) are not coded separately. Deficits such as **[[paraplegia]], neurogenic bladder, or cord [[compression]]** are coded when the provider documents them.

**ICD-10 CM [[G06.1]]** appears on the principal diagnosis list for **DRGs 094-096.**<sup>4</sup> If the patient has an operative procedure (f**or example [[laminectomy]] with evacuation**), the case groups to a **surgical DRG** according to the **ICD-10-PCS code** and not to **094-096**. Sequencing is straightforward when the **[[abscess]]** is the reason for admission: sequence **[[G06.1]]** first, then the organism code (**B95-B97**) as a secondary diagnosis. Confirm the **CC/MCC status** of **[[G06.1]]** and the secondary diagnoses in **Appendix C** of the **FY2026 v43 Definitions Manual**.<sup>4</sup>

**NCD/LCD information:**
- **NCD:** I found no **NCD** specific to **[[G06.1]]** or to spinal abscess drainage. **Medicare** also has no **NCD** for lumbar spine surgery.<sup>7</sup>
- **LCD:** If a **[[fusion]]** is done at the same time as the **[[decompression]]**, check the **MAC's** **spinal fusion LCD**, for example the **Lumbar Spinal Fusion LCD (L37848)**.<sup>6</sup> Infection is a typical indication in these policies, but coverage criteria vary by **MAC**, so check the one that applies to your claim.
- **Billing and coding articles:** For **current ICD-10 and CPT lists**, search the CMS Medicare Coverage Database for the billing and coding article tied to the LCD.<sup>6</sup>

### NCCI Bundling Considerations

The **[[laminectomy]]** codes already include the **exposure** and **[[decompression]]**, so a separate **decompression** code at the same level generally bundles. Check the **current NCCI procedure-to-procedure edits** before adding **[[-59]]** to any spinal procedure pair, and report **[[-59]]** only when sites or sessions are truly distinct.

## 🔬 ICD-10-PCS Crosswalk

- **009U3ZZ**: **Drainage of Spinal Canal, Percutaneous Approach**. Use for needle or [[catheter]] aspiration of the [[abscess]] without a drainage device.
- **00CU0ZZ**: **Extirpation of Matter from Spinal Canal, Open Approach**. This is the common code for open evacuation of abscess material.
- **00CT0ZZ**: **Extirpation of Matter from Spinal Meninges, Open Approach**. Use when the abscess is documented in the [[meninges]].

## 💊 Coding Scenarios and Examples

<br>

### Example 1
> **Clinical Scenario:**
> A 58-year-old man with diabetes is admitted with fever, low back pain, and leg weakness. MRI shows a lumbar epidural abscess. The neurosurgeon performs an L3-L4 laminectomy with evacuation of the abscess, and cultures grow MSSA.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[63267]] | Laminectomy for evacuation of lumbar extradural intraspinal lesion other than neoplasm. |
| PDx | [[G06.1]] | Documented lumbar epidural abscess is an intraspinal abscess. |
| Dx 2 | [[B95.61]] | The organism is reported as an additional code per the G06 note. |

> [!tip]
> Sequence [[G06.1]] first and the organism code second. Query the provider if the organism is only "positive cultures" without a stated diagnosis.

### Example 2
> **Clinical Scenario:**
> A neurosurgeon sees a patient with a thoracic epidural abscess in the ED. During that initial visit the surgeon decides to operate, and a T6-T8 laminectomy for evacuation is done later that day.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[99223]]-[[-57]] | Initial hospital E/M that resulted in the decision for surgery on a 90-day global procedure. |
| CPT 2 | [[63266]] | Laminectomy for evacuation of thoracic extradural intraspinal lesion other than neoplasm. |
| PDx | [[G06.1]] | Thoracic epidural abscess is an intraspinal abscess. |

> [!tip]
> The [[-57]] goes on the E/M, not the procedure. Link [[G06.1]] to both lines.

### Example 3
> **Clinical Scenario:**
> A patient had a lumbar laminectomy for epidural abscess five days ago. The wound shows persistent purulence and MRI shows residual abscess, so the surgeon returns the patient to the OR for repeat evacuation.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[63267]]-[[-78]] | Unplanned return to the OR for a related complication during the global period. |
| PDx | [[G06.1]] | The residual abscess is still the intraspinal abscess. |

> [!tip]
> A [[-78]] procedure only pays the intraoperative portion, with no new global period. Make sure the documentation shows this was unplanned, or use [[-58]] if it was a planned stage.

## ⚠️ Coding Pitfalls and Tips

- **Pitfall 1**: Reporting **[[G06.2]]** for a **spinal epidural [[abscess]]**; **Tips**: The Index sends "**[[Abscess]], epidural, spinal**" to **[[G06.1]]**, so use **[[G06.2]]** only when no site is documented.
- **Pitfall 2**: Forgetting the organism code; **Tips**: The **G06** note says to use an additional code from **B95-B97**, for example **[[B95.61]]** or **[[B95.62]]**, when the organism is documented.
- **Pitfall 3**: Reporting **[[G06.1]]** together with **[[A17.81]]**; **Tips**: This is an **Excludes1 pair**, so a **[[tuberculous]] [[abscess]]** is coded only to **[[A17.81]]**.
- **Pitfall 4**: Putting **[[-57]]** on the surgery instead of the E/M; **Tips**: **[[-57]]** goes on the E/M that led to the decision for surgery, and the **[[laminectomy]]** is billed without it.
- **Pitfall 5**: Assuming a **DRG** of **094-096** for surgical cases; **Tips**: With an operative procedure the case groups by the **ICD-10-PCS code**, so check the grouper rather than assuming.
- **Pitfall 6**: Using the wrong **[[laminectomy]]** code for an **intradural [[abscess]]**; **Tips**: Choose **[[63265]]-[[63267]]** for extradural and **[[63270]]-[[63272]]** for intradural based on the operative note.
