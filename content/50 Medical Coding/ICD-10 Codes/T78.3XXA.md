# T78.3XXA – Angioneurotic Edema, Initial Encounter

## Short Description

**T78.3XXA** describes **angioneurotic edema (angioedema), initial encounter**, when the patient is receiving **active treatment** for a new or ongoing episode of angioedema.[web:384][web:388][web:389] This includes rapid, transient swelling of deeper layers of skin or mucosa, most commonly involving the face, lips, tongue, or airway.[web:388]

**Key points:** This is a **T-code with required 7th character** (**A** for initial encounter) and is used while the patient is under **active management** (ED visit, inpatient admission, acute evaluation), not just follow-up.[web:388][web:389]

---

## Full Description & Clinical Context

**Angioneurotic edema (angioedema)** is localized, non-pitting edema caused by increased vascular permeability in the deep dermis, subcutaneous, or submucosal tissues.[web:388] It typically appears as sudden swelling, often asymmetric, and may affect:[web:388]
- Lips, tongue, face, eyelids
- Larynx and upper airway (can be life-threatening)
- Gastrointestinal tract (intestinal angioedema with abdominal pain)[web:388]

**T78.3XXA applies when:**[web:384][web:388]
- Angioedema is the **primary clinical issue** being evaluated/treated
- The encounter represents **active treatment** (ED visit, acute inpatient, urgent clinic)
- Etiology may be **allergic, idiopathic, hereditary, or drug-related (e.g., ACE inhibitor)**[web:388]
- The 7th character **A** (initial encounter) accurately reflects current treatment phase[web:388][web:389]

**Clinical significance:**[web:388]
- May be isolated or associated with **urticaria** and/or anaphylaxis
- Angioedema of tongue/larynx can rapidly compromise airway
- Hereditary angioedema involves C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency and may recur
- ACE-inhibitor–induced angioedema is a key drug-related cause (often facial/tongue swelling)[web:388]

## Angioedema Types & Etiologies (Clinical Overview)

Common etiologic categories that may be seen with T78.3XXA:[web:388][web:386]

- **Allergic angioedema** (IgE-mediated)
  - Foods (nuts, shellfish, eggs)
  - Medications (penicillin, NSAIDs, contrast, vaccines)
  - Insect stings

- **Drug-induced angioedema** (non–IgE, bradykinin-mediated)
  - **ACE inhibitor–induced angioedema** (e.g., lisinopril)
  - ARBs less commonly

- **Idiopathic angioedema**
  - No clear trigger; may be recurrent

- **Hereditary angioedema (HAE)**[web:388]
  - Types I and II: C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency or dysfunction
  - Type III: Often estrogen-dependent, factor XII-related

- **Acquired C1 inhibitor deficiency**
  - Lymphoproliferative disorders
  - Autoantibodies against C1 inhibitor

**Clinical manifestations:**[web:388]
- Deep swelling, often without pruritus
- May occur with or without urticaria
- Abdominal pain, vomiting, diarrhea in intestinal angioedema
- Stridor, hoarseness, dyspnea when airway involved (emergency)

| Code | Description | When to Use |
|------|-------------|------------|

## When to Use T78.3XXA

Use **T78.3XXA** when **ALL** of the following apply:[web:384][web:388]

1. **Angioedema is present and documented:**
   - Facial, lip, tongue, periorbital swelling
   - Laryngeal/airway swelling
   - Intestinal angioedema (GI pain with imaging/clinical evidence)

2. **Encounter is for active treatment:**[web:388][web:389]
   - Emergency department visit for acute swelling
   - Inpatient admission for airway monitoring or treatment
   - Urgent care visit for new or worsening angioedema

3. **The angioedema represents an adverse effect or reaction:**[web:388]
   - Due to drug, food, allergen, hereditary deficiency, etc.

4. **This is the "initial encounter" for this episode:**[web:388][web:389]
   - Patient is in the **acute treatment phase** for this episode, regardless of how many clinicians are involved

**Key guideline:** "Initial encounter" is about **active treatment**, not necessarily the first provider the patient sees.[web:388][web:389]

---

## When NOT to Use T78.3XXA

Do **NOT** use T78.3XXA when:

| Scenario | Use Instead | Rationale |
|----------|------------|-----------|
| Routine follow-up for resolved angioedema | **T78.3XXD** | Subsequent encounter (healing phase)[web:390][web:393] |
| Long-term sequelae (e.g., chronic airway scarring) | **T78.3XXS** | Sequela, late effects[web:390][web:393] |
| Only urticaria (hives) without deeper swelling | **L50.-** | Urticaria, not angioedema[web:384] |
| Chronic hereditary angioedema outside acute episode | HAE-specific codes (e.g., D84.1– if applicable) + Z-codes | Use disease-specific or history codes if no acute episode |
| Mild allergic rhinitis, no angioedema | J30.- or other allergy codes | No angioedema present |

## Documentation Requirements for T78.3XXA

**Minimum documentation needed:**[web:384][web:388][web:390]

✅ **MUST include:**

1. **Explicit diagnosis of angioedema/angioneurotic edema:**
   - "Angioedema" or "angioneurotic edema" documented by provider

2. **Anatomic sites involved:**[web:388]
   - Lips, tongue, larynx, face, extremities, bowel

3. **Temporal/encounter status:**
   - Document that this is an **acute episode** under active treatment
   - ED visit, new inpatient admission for angioedema, etc.

4. **Suspected cause if known:**[web:388]
   - Food, drug, insect sting, hereditary, ACE inhibitor, NSAID, etc.
   - Allows addition of adverse effect, toxic effect, or disease codes

5. **Severity & airway status:**
   - Stridor, hoarseness, drooling, respiratory distress
   - Need for epinephrine, intubation, ICU

✅ **SHOULD document (best practice):**[web:388]
- Onset and duration of symptoms
- Associated symptoms (urticaria, hypotension, wheezing, GI pain)
- Medication exposures (ACE inhibitors, NSAIDs, antibiotics)
- Family history of angioedema (for hereditary forms)
- Response to treatment (epinephrine, antihistamines, steroids, C1 inhibitor)

## HCC Information

- **T78.3XXA does NOT map to a CMS-HCC** directly.[web:396]
- As a T-code for acute adverse effect, it is generally a **non-HCC acute condition**.
- Some **underlying causes** or **complications** might map to HCCs (e.g., certain immunodeficiencies, chronic lung disease if airway compromise documented), but T78.3XXA itself does not.[web:396]

**Coding impact:**
- Still clinically important for risk stratification and DRG severity when associated with airway compromise or ICU care.

## RVU / wRVU Information

- **ICD-10-CM diagnosis codes (including T78.3XXA) do NOT have RVUs/wRVUs**.
- RVUs apply to **CPT/HCPCS** procedure and E/M codes.
- T78.3XXA supports medical necessity and diagnosis for:
  - ED E/M codes (e.g., 99283–99285)
  - Critical care codes (99291–99292) when airway is threatened
  - Procedures such as intubation, laryngoscopy, epinephrine administration

## Clinical Examples: When to Use T78.3XXA

### ✅ Example 1 – ACE Inhibitor–Induced Tongue Swelling
```text
SCENARIO:
62-year-old male presents to ED with sudden onset tongue and lip swelling.

History:
- On lisinopril for 3 years
- No prior similar episodes
- No new foods, insect stings, or exposures

Exam:
- Significant tongue and lower lip angioedema
- Mild stridor, speaking in short sentences
- No urticaria

Treatment:
- Discontinue lisinopril immediately
- Epinephrine IM, IV steroids, antihistamines
- Airway observation in ICU

Assessment:
- ACE inhibitor–induced angioedema (angioneurotic edema)

CODES:
- **T78.3XXA** – Angioneurotic edema, initial encounter ✓
- **T46.4X5A** – Adverse effect of angiotensin-converting-enzyme inhibitor, initial encounter
- **I10** – Essential hypertension (underlying condition)

RATIONALE:
- Acute angioedema episode, initial/active treatment
- Drug-related cause documented, adverse-effect code added
```

### ✅ Example 2 – Allergic Food-Induced Facial Angioedema with Urticaria
```text
SCENARIO:
25-year-old female presents to ED after eating shrimp.

History:
- Within 30 minutes, developed facial swelling and hives
- Itchy rash over trunk
- No prior severe reactions

Exam:
- Periorbital and lip angioedema
- Diffuse urticaria
- No wheezing or hypotension

Treatment:
- IM epinephrine
- Oral antihistamines and steroids
- Observation in ED for 4 hours

Assessment:
- IgE-mediated allergic angioedema due to shellfish

CODES:
- **T78.3XXA** – Angioneurotic edema, initial encounter ✓
- **L50.0** – Allergic urticaria
- **T78.1XXA** – Other adverse food reaction, not elsewhere classified, initial encounter (if payer requires)
- External cause code for exposure to shellfish (if required)

RATIONALE:
- Acute angioedema with urticaria due to ingested food
- T78.3XXA captures the angioedema component.
```

### ✅ Example 3 – Hereditary Angioedema Flare
```text
SCENARIO:
18-year-old male with known hereditary angioedema presents with abdominal pain and vomiting.

History:
- Known C1 esterase inhibitor deficiency
- Multiple prior abdominal angioedema attacks
- No urticaria, no pruritus

Exam:
- Diffuse abdominal tenderness, no guarding
- CT scan: Bowel wall edema consistent with angioedema

Treatment:
- C1 esterase inhibitor concentrate infusion
- IV fluids
- Symptom monitoring

Assessment:
- Intestinal angioedema due to hereditary angioedema

CODES:
- **T78.3XXA** – Angioneurotic edema, initial encounter ✓
- Hereditary angioedema code (e.g., D84.1x, per most current ICD listing)

RATIONALE:
- Acute episode of angioedema with active treatment
- T78.3XXA documents the acute angioedema; hereditary disorder coded separately.
```

### ❌ Example 4 – WRONG: Subsequent Encounter Coded as Initial
```text
SCENARIO:
Same patient returns 1 week later only for follow-up, no active swelling.

WRONG: T78.3XXA (initial encounter)
CORRECT: T78.3XXD (subsequent encounter)

RATIONALE:
- No active treatment of angioedema episode
- Visit is for follow-up/aftercare.
```

## Documentation Pitfalls & Compliance Tips

### Common Errors to Avoid

1. **Missing the 7th character:**
   - T78.3 by itself is NOT billable – must use T78.3XXA, T78.3XXD, or T78.3XXS.[web:388][web:389]

2. **Using T78.3XXA for chronic or historical angioedema only:**
   - T78.3XXA is for **active episodes**, not just "history of".
   - Use Z-codes for history if no current episode.

3. **Coding urticaria only as angioedema:**
   - If only hives present → L50.-, not T78.3XXA.[web:384]

4. **Ignoring adverse effect coding rules for drug-induced angioedema:**
   - Must code the **adverse effect** with appropriate T36–T50 code plus T78.3XXA for the clinical manifestation when required.[web:388][web:389]

### Best-Practice Documentation Template (Provider-Friendly)

```text
Diagnosis:
- Angioneurotic edema (angioedema) involving [site: lips/tongue/face/larynx/bowel]

History:
- Onset: [time from trigger]
- Trigger/exposure: [food, drug, insect sting, unknown]
- Prior similar episodes: [yes/no]
- Associated symptoms: [urticaria, dyspnea, wheezing, abdominal pain, hypotension]

Exam:
- Extent/location of swelling
- Airway status: [patent/stridor/hoarseness/respiratory distress]
- Vitals: [BP, HR, RR, O2 sat]

Assessment:
- [Allergic/drug-induced/hereditary/idiopathic] angioedema
- Risk of airway compromise: [low/moderate/high]

Treatment:
- Medications given: [epinephrine, antihistamines, steroids, C1 inhibitor]
- Airway interventions: [none/intubation/ICU monitoring]
- Disposition: [discharge/observation/admission]

Plan:
- Avoidance of trigger
- Prescription for epinephrine autoinjector (if indicated)
- Allergy/immunology referral

Coding note:
- This encounter represents active treatment of angioedema → use T78.3XXA.
```

**Key Concept:** **T78.3XXA** is the code for **angioneurotic edema (angioedema), initial encounter** – used when a patient is receiving **active treatment** for an acute episode of angioedema, often in the ED or inpatient setting. It requires a 7th character (**A**) and should be carefully distinguished from subsequent encounters (**D**) and sequela (**S**). Always document the presence, location, suspected cause, and severity of angioedema, and code any underlying allergy, adverse drug effect, or hereditary angioedema separately.
