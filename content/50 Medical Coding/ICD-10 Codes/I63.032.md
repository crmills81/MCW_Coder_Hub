# 🧬 ICD-10-CM I63.032 — Cerebral Infarction Due to Thrombosis of Left Carotid Artery

> [!success] Billable Code Confirmed
>
> **ICD-10-CM [[I63.032]]** is a valid, billable **6-character ICD-10-CM
> code** for FY2026. All six characters are present: `I63` (**category**) +
> `.0` (**due to thrombosis of precerebral arteries**) + `3` (**carotid
> artery**) + `2` (**left**). No 7th character is required.

> [!warning] Non-Billable Parent Codes — Never Submit These
>
> - ❌ `I63.03` — 5-character header — missing laterality character
> - ❌ `I63.0` — 4-character header — missing vessel and laterality
>
> Always submit **[[I63.032]]** (all 6 characters) when cerebral infarction
> due to thrombosis of the left carotid artery is documented.

> [!important] ⭐ HCC-Mapped Code — High RAF Value
>
> **[[I63.032]]** maps to **HCC 85 (Ischemic or Unspecified Stroke)** under
> CMS-HCC v28 — same high-value HCC assignment as [[I63.031]] (right
> carotid). Every **I63.032** encounter in a Medicare Advantage patient carries
> direct RAF weight. Document and code all associated neurological deficits,
> systemic comorbidities, and qualifying conditions — they build the complete
> clinical picture and drive DRG tier.

> [!danger] ⚠️ Left Hemisphere = Dominant Hemisphere — Aphasia Is the Hallmark
>
> This is the single most critical clinical distinction between [[I63.032]]
> (left carotid) and [[I63.031]] (right carotid). The **left hemisphere is
> dominant for language** in approximately **95% of right-handed patients**
> and **~70% of left-handed patients**. Thrombosis of the LEFT carotid
> produces right-sided motor and sensory deficits PLUS language deficits:
> - **Aphasia ([[R47.01]])** — expressive, receptive, or global depending on
>   infarct extent — is the hallmark deficit of left carotid strokes
> - Right carotid ([[I63.031]]) → left neglect, spatial inattention, rarely aphasia
> - Left carotid (**I63.032**) → **aphasia**, right hemiplegia, right sensory loss
>
> **ICD-10 CM [[R47.01]] carries CC status** — always code aphasia when documented.
> Its absence in the coding record when a left carotid stroke is coded
> is a CDI red flag.

> [!info] Mechanism Specificity — Thrombosis, Not Embolism
>
> **ICD-10 CM [[I63.032]]** is specific to a **thrombotic** mechanism — in-situ clot
> formation or superimposed thrombus on an atherosclerotic plaque within
> the left carotid artery. If the physician documents **embolic** occlusion
> of the left carotid artery causing infarction, the correct code is
> **ICD-10 CM [[I63.132]]** (cerebral infarction due to embolism of left carotid
> artery), NOT [[I63.032]]. Mechanism must be documented — query when absent.

## 🔍 Code Description

**ICD-10 CM [[I63.032]]** classifies **cerebral infarction caused by in-situ
thrombosis of the left carotid artery** — an ischemic stroke in the
anterior circulation resulting from clot formation within the extracranial
or proximal intracranial segment of the left carotid artery, causing
downstream ischemia and infarction of left-hemisphere brain tissue.

Because the **left hemisphere is dominant for language** in the vast
majority of patients, strokes in the left carotid territory carry a
distinctly different neurological profile than right-sided strokes.
Left carotid territory infarction classically produces **contralateral
right-sided** motor and sensory deficits — reflecting the crossed anatomy
of the corticospinal tracts — combined with **ipsilateral language
dysfunction** (aphasia), which arises from involvement of Broca's area
(anterior/inferior frontal lobe), Wernicke's area (posterior superior
temporal lobe), or both.

The left carotid territory infarct is often described as a **"catastrophic"
stroke** when total left MCA territory is involved — global aphasia, right
hemiplegia, and right hemisensory loss occurring together. Even partial
left carotid territory infarcts carry significant disability burden given
the critical language, motor, and cognitive functions of the left hemisphere.

> [!danger] TIA vs. Cerebral Infarction — Highest-Stakes Excludes 1
> The same critical rule from [[I63.031]] applies here: when MRI DWI
> confirms a diffusion restriction (infarction) in the left carotid
> territory, the correct code is **[[I63.032]]** — NOT [[G45.9]] (TIA).
> For left carotid territory, the aphasia documentation in the record
> is a powerful confirmatory marker — aphasia does not occur in TIA
> with a truly normal brain and a normal DWI scan. Document the imaging
> correlation explicitly in the record and query any physician who
> documents "TIA" when DWI is positive.

## 📋 Clinical Overview

### Pathophysiology — Left Carotid Artery Thrombosis

Left carotid artery thrombosis follows the same three mechanisms as
right carotid thrombosis (see [[I63.031]]) — but the downstream
consequences are governed by the dominance of the left hemisphere:

1. **Atherosclerotic plaque rupture at the left carotid bifurcation** — the
   most common mechanism; plaque rupture triggers platelet aggregation and
   in-situ thrombus formation, causing local occlusion or distal propagation
   into the left ICA, left MCA, or left ACA

2. **Left carotid artery dissection** — intimal tear creating a false
   lumen; can occur spontaneously (connective tissue disorders, FMD) or
   following trauma (chiropractic manipulation, motor vehicle accident,
   sports injury); cervical dissection is a leading cause of stroke in
   patients under 50

3. **Hypercoagulable state** — in-situ thrombosis on a relatively normal
   vessel wall; antiphospholipid syndrome, malignancy, and thrombophilia
   are key causes in younger patients

### Left Hemisphere — Dominant Hemisphere Anatomy

Understanding why left carotid strokes produce aphasia requires
understanding left hemisphere language architecture:

| Cortical Area | Location | Function | Infarct Deficit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Broca's area** | Left inferior frontal gyrus (L. MCA superior division) | Speech production, motor planning of language | **Expressive (Broca's) aphasia** — understands, cannot speak fluently |
| **Wernicke's area** | Left posterior superior temporal gyrus (L. MCA inferior division) | Language comprehension | **Receptive (Wernicke's) aphasia** — fluent but incomprehensible speech, poor comprehension |
| **Arcuate fasciculus** | White matter connecting Broca's + Wernicke's | Connects production and comprehension | **Conduction aphasia** — poor repetition |
| **Total left MCA territory** | Entire left hemisphere | All language functions | **Global aphasia** — neither speaks nor understands |
| **Left ACA territory** | Left medial frontal lobe | Motivation, initiation | Transcortical motor aphasia — follows commands, cannot initiate |

### Neurological Deficit Profile — Left Carotid Territory

Left carotid territory infarction produces **contralateral right-sided**
motor/sensory deficits AND **ipsilateral language dysfunction** (aphasia):

| Deficit | Side | Code | CC/MCC Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hemiplegia/hemiparesis | **Right** (contralateral) | [[G81.01]] flaccid right dominant, [[G81.11]] spastic | **MCC/CC** — code when documented |
| Hemisensory loss | **Right** | [[R20.2]] or deficit captured in G81.x1 | CC potential |
| **Aphasia** | Language — not side-specific | **[[R47.01]]** | **CC — HALLMARK of left carotid stroke** |
| Dysarthria | — | [[R47.1]] | CC potential |
| Dysphagia | — | [[R13.12]] oropharyngeal phase | **CC — always code when documented** |
| Apraxia | Right limb | [[R48.2]] | Document |
| Alexia/Agraphia | — | [[R48.0]] dyslexia, [[R48.8]] | Document when assessed |
| Right homonymous hemianopia | Right visual field | [[H53.461]] | Document |
| Gaze deviation | Left (eyes look toward lesion) | Captured in neurological exam documentation | Document |
| **Spatial neglect** | Typically **NOT** right neglect — neglect is a right hemisphere dominant deficit | Right carotid feature → left neglect | Do NOT assume neglect in left carotid stroke |

> [!important] Right Hemiplegia + Aphasia = Left Carotid Until Proven Otherwise
> The combination of **right-sided weakness AND aphasia** is pathognomonic
> for **left hemisphere** pathology. In the clinical setting, this
> presentation points directly to left carotid or left MCA territory.
> Conversely, if a case coded as [[I63.032]] (left carotid) has
> documentation of **left-sided** weakness and **right neglect** — that
> is a clinical mismatch that should trigger a coding query. Left carotid
> = right-sided deficits, full stop.

### Aphasia Subtypes and Documentation

> [!tip] Document the Aphasia Subtype When Available — Same Code, Richer Record
> All aphasia types map to **[[R47.01]]** — there is no ICD-10-CM
> distinction between Broca's, Wernicke's, global, and conduction aphasia.
> However, the clinical documentation of aphasia subtype is important for:
> - SLP treatment planning and medical necessity
> - Rehabilitation coding and functional status
> - Quality reporting
> - Legal and disability documentation
>
> When the SLP or neurologist documents a specific aphasia type in the
> record, code [[R47.01]] and ensure the subtype is captured in the
> physician documentation — even if the code doesn't distinguish it.

### NIHSS — Code It Every Time

> [!tip] [[R29.700]]-[[R29.744]] — NIHSS Is a Tabular "*Use Additional Code*" Instruction
> Same requirement as [[I63.031]] — the ICD-10-CM tabular instruction at
> I63 mandates **"Use additional code, if applicable, to identify the
> NIHSS score (R29.7-)"**. Left hemisphere strokes with aphasia often
> score higher on the NIHSS than right hemisphere strokes of similar
> infarct volume — language items contribute significantly to the scale.
>
> | NIHSS Range | Stroke Severity | Code Range |

> | 16-20 | Moderately severe | [[R29.716]]-[[R29.720]] |

> and DRG tier, not the HCC assignment.

> [!caution] I69.3xx — Annual Sequela Code Recapture
> Once a patient has had a left carotid territory infarction, their
> chronic language and motor deficits are coded using **I69.3xx** sequela
> codes in every subsequent outpatient encounter and non-acute admission:
>
> - [[50 Medical Coding/ICD-10 Codes/I69.320]] — Aphasia following cerebral infarction

>   right dominant side
> - [[I69.391]] — Dysphagia following cerebral infarction
>
> These I69.3xx codes also map to **HCC 85** — ensuring RAF capture
> persists year over year even after the acute stroke episode ends.
> This is one of the highest-yield chronic condition recapture
> opportunities in Medicare Advantage coding programs.

> [!caution] CEA and Carotid Stenting — Left vs. Right Laterality Modifiers
> When [[35301]] (CEA) or [[37215]]/[[37216]] (carotid stenting) is performed
> on the LEFT [[carotid]] artery, append modifier **[[-LT]]** to identify the
> operative side. Left carotid CEA adds additional surgical complexity
> considerations due to proximity to the recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN)
> on the left side — postoperative hoarseness should be documented and
> coded as a potential complication if it occurs.

> [!important] Left CEA and Recurrent Laryngeal Nerve Risk
> The **left recurrent laryngeal nerve (RLN)** loops under the aortic arch
> before ascending in the tracheoesophageal groove — placing it at
> greater anatomical risk during left carotid exposure than right-sided
> CEA (**where the RLN loops at the subclavian**). Postoperative hoarseness
> after left CEA should prompt documentation of potential RLN injury
> ([[J38.00]] vocal cord paralysis unspecified, or [[J38.01]] unilateral)
> — this is a separately codeable complication that affects the care episode.

### tPA Administration

> [!note] IV tPA Is a Pharmaceutical — Not a Standalone CPT Code
> Same principle as [[I63.031]] — IV alteplase is captured as a
> pharmaceutical charge in facility billing, not a standalone CPT in the
> physician fee schedule. The tPA fact is flagged in ICD-10-CM via
> [[Z92.82]] (if given at outside facility) and in ICD-10-PCS via the
> Administration section code. DRG routing activates Path A (DRG 61-63)
> when tPA is administered and a precerebral occlusion code like I63.032
> is the principal diagnosis.

## 💊 Coding Scenarios and Examples

---

### Scenario 1 — Classic Left Carotid Thrombosis, Aphasia + Right Hemiplegia, IV tPA (Inpatient)

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 71-year-old right-handed male with known left
> carotid stenosis (70% on prior duplex) presents to the ED with sudden
> onset right arm weakness and inability to speak at 9:00 AM. NIHSS 17
> on arrival. CT head negative for hemorrhage. CT angiography confirms
> left ICA occlusion at the bifurcation. IV tPA administered at 9:45 AM.
> MRI DWI confirms left MCA territory infarction — left frontal and
> parietal cortex. Admitted to neuro ICU. SLP documents **global aphasia**
> — no verbal output, no command following. PT/OT document **right flaccid
> [[hemiplegia]]**. [[Dysphagia]] documented, NGT placed. Vascular surgery
> recommends left CEA once neurologically stable.

**Principal Diagnosis:**
- [[I63.032]] — Cerebral infarction due to thrombosis of left carotid
  artery

**Additional Diagnoses (CC/MCC — drive DRG tier):**
- [[G81.01]] — Flaccid hemiplegia affecting right dominant side **← MCC**
- [[R47.01]] — Aphasia **← CC** (global aphasia documented)
- [[R13.12]] — Dysphagia, oropharyngeal phase **← CC**
- [[R29.717]] — NIHSS score 17 *(use additional code per tabular)*
- [[I65.22]] — Occlusion/stenosis left carotid, prior *(risk factor context)*
- [[I10]] — Essential hypertension

**ICD-10-PCS:**
- **3E05317** — Introduction of thrombolytic into peripheral vein, percutaneous
  *(IV tPA)*

**MS-DRG Routing:**
- tPA administered + precerebral occlusion (I63.032) + MCC ([[G81.01]]) →
  **DRG 61** *(highest tier — with thrombolytic, with MCC)*

> [!tip] Three Separate Codeable Deficits Here
> **G81.01 (MCC) + R47.01 (CC) + R13.12 (CC)** = a fully documented,
> three-deficit left carotid stroke. This case belongs in DRG 61 — any
> lower grouping signals missing deficit documentation. CDI should
> proactively query for these three deficits in every left carotid
> admission before the record closes.

### Scenario 2 — Left Carotid Dissection with Thrombosis, Younger Patient (Inpatient)

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 38-year-old left-handed female presents with
> sudden left-sided neck pain followed by right arm numbness and word-finding
> difficulty. She was in a rear-end motor vehicle accident 2 days prior.
> MRI confirms left hemisphere infarction. MRA neck shows left ICA dissection
> with intramural hematoma. Mechanism documented: left carotid dissection
> with superimposed thrombosis → cerebral infarction. NIHSS 6. Anticoagulated
> with heparin bridge to warfarin.

> [!warning] Left-Handed Patient — Dominant Hemisphere May Vary
> In **left-handed patients**, language lateralization is more variable —
> approximately 30% of left-handed individuals have right hemisphere
> language dominance. In this case, the physician documented word-finding
> difficulty (**language deficit**), which — regardless of handedness — maps
> to [[R47.01]] (**aphasia**) when documented. The code assignment follows
> physician documentation of the deficit, not assumed dominance.

**Principal Diagnosis:**
- [[I63.032]] — Cerebral infarction due to thrombosis of left carotid
  artery

**Additional Diagnoses:**
- [[I77.72]] — Dissection of left carotid artery *(documented mechanism)*
- [[R47.01]] — Aphasia *(word-finding difficulty = aphasia when documented)*
- [[R29.706]] — NIHSS score 6
- [[V49.50XA]] — Driver in traffic accident, unspecified motor vehicle *(if
  applicable — injury context for mechanism)*

### Scenario 3 — Left Carotid Thrombosis, tPA Given at Outside Hospital (Transfer)

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 66-year-old female receives IV tPA at a
> community hospital 90 minutes after symptom onset and is transferred
> to a comprehensive stroke center. Right-sided weakness and expressive
> aphasia documented. On arrival, NIHSS is 11. MRI confirms left MCA
> territory infarction consistent with left [[carotid]] [[thrombosis]]. Left ICA
> occlusion confirmed on CTA. No additional intervention performed at
> the receiving facility.

**Principal Diagnosis:**
- [[I63.032]] — Cerebral infarction due to thrombosis of left carotid artery

**Additional Diagnoses:**
- [[G81.11]] — [[spastic hemiplegia]] affecting right dominant side
  *(right-sided weakness — may be flaccid acute vs. spastic per
  documentation; query if unclear)*
- [[R47.01]] — [[Aphasia]] *(expressive aphasia documented)* **← CC**
- [[R29.711]] — NIHSS score 11
- [[Z92.82]] — Status post tPA administration in last 24 hours prior to
  admission **← flags tPA for DRG routing even though given at OSH**

**MS-DRG Routing:**
- [[Z92.82]] activates DRG Path A consideration → **DRG 62** (with CC —
  R47.01) or **DRG 61** (with MCC — if [[hemiplegia]] confirmed as MCC-level)

> [!important] Z92.82 Is Your DRG Routing Key for Transfer Cases
> Same principle as [[I63.031]] — **[[Z92.82]]** is non-negotiable when
> tPA was given at an outside facility. It routes the receiving hospital's
> case to DRG 61-63 (**thrombolytic path**) instead of DRG 64-66. Without
> Z92.82, the receiving facility loses the thrombolytic DRG routing
> entirely despite the patient having received tPA — one of the most
> financially impactful missed codes in inpatient stroke coding.

### Scenario 4 — Documentation Says "Left MCA Stroke" — Query for I63.032 vs. I63.312

> **Clinical Vignette:** A coder reviews a left hemisphere stroke case.
> The discharge summary reads: "Left MCA territory cerebral infarction,
> thrombotic mechanism." CTA neck is documented as showing left ICA
> near-[[occlusion]]. The coder is deciding between **[[I63.032]]** (left
> carotid thrombosis → precerebral artery) and **[[I63.312]]** (left MCA
> thrombosis → cerebral artery).

**Analysis:**
- **[[I63.032]]** is correct when the thrombosis is in the **left carotid
  artery** (extracranial precerebral vessel) and the infarction is
  downstream in the MCA territory
- **[[I63.312]]** is correct when the thrombosis originates within the
  **left middle cerebral artery itself** (**intracranial**)
- The discharge summary says "left ICA near-occlusion" on CTA — this
  supports **[[I63.032]]** (left precerebral carotid artery thrombosis)
- If the CTA had shown a normal ICA with clot in the left MCA itself,
  [[I63.312]] would be correct

**Coding Action:**
- Assign [[I63.032]] based on the CTA documentation of left ICA
  near-occlusion with thrombotic mechanism
- If ambiguous between left ICA vs. left MCA as the primary thrombosis
  site, submit a CDI query before finalizing the code

> [!tip] Precerebral (Carotid) vs. Cerebral (MCA) Thrombosis — How to Tell
> The distinction between I63.03x (**precerebral carotid**) and I63.31x
> (**cerebral MCA**) is an imaging and anatomic determination:
> - **Carotid thrombosis** (I63.03x): clot in the ICA or CCA,
>   typically visualized on CTA neck/MRA neck, at or near the carotid
>   bifurcation or along the cervical ICA course
> - **MCA thrombosis** (I63.31x): clot within the left M1 or M2 segment
>   of the **MCA**, visualized on CTA head/MRA head
> - In acute [[stroke]], both can cause left hemisphere infarction — the
>   vessel where the primary [[thrombus]] resides determines the code

## ⚠️ Coding Pitfalls and Tips

| | Pitfall or Tip |
|--|----------------|
| ❌ | **Never submit I63.03 (5-character) as a billable code** — [[I63.032]] (6 characters) is required; the parent code will reject |
| ❌ | **Do not assign [[I63.032]] for embolic mechanism** — left carotid embolism maps to [[I63.132]]; mechanism drives the code, not just the vessel |
| ❌ | **Do not assign [[I63.032]] for left MCA/intracranial thrombosis** — thrombosis in the left MCA itself maps to [[I63.312]]; carotid (precerebral) vs. MCA (cerebral) is the key distinction |
| ❌ | **Do not code [[I63.032]] simultaneously with [[G45.x]] (TIA)** — mutually exclusive; DWI-positive imaging upgrades the diagnosis to I63.032 |
| ❌ | **Do not leave aphasia uncoded in a left carotid stroke** — [[R47.01]] is the hallmark deficit; its absence is a CDI red flag and a missed CC |
| ❌ | **Do not code left-sided neglect/inattention as the primary finding** — neglect is a right hemisphere (right carotid, I63.031) feature; left carotid → right-sided deficits; left neglect in a chart coded I63.032 is a clinical mismatch requiring query |
| ❌ | **Do not forget [[Z92.82]] in transfer cases** — tPA given at an outside facility within 24 hours activates DRG 61-63 routing; without Z92.82 the case defaults to DRG 64-66 at the receiving facility |
| ✅ | **Code aphasia every single time it is documented** — [[R47.01]] is a CC, it maps to HCC (via I69.320 in sequela), and it accurately reflects the catastrophic nature of dominant hemisphere infarction |
| ✅ | **Code the NIHSS score** — tabular "use additional code" instruction at I63; left carotid strokes with aphasia often have higher NIHSS; document and code it |
| ✅ | **Query for hemiplegia laterality** — right flaccid hemiplegia ([[G81.01]]) is MCC status; right spastic ([[G81.11]]) is CC; hemiparesis NOS is lower tier; always query for the correct specificity |
| ✅ | **Capture left carotid dissection separately** — when the mechanism is dissection + thrombosis, code both [[I63.032]] (infarction) AND [[I77.72]] (dissection, left carotid) |
| ✅ | **Sweep for post-stroke depression** — [[F32.x]] or [[F06.31]] in aphasia patients is frequently missed; review SLP, psychology, and nursing notes; depression in a non-communicative aphasia patient may be documented in behavioral observation notes |
| ✅ | **Annual sequela code recapture** — in outpatient and subsequent admissions, replace I63.032 with [[50 Medical Coding/ICD-10 Codes/I69.320]] (aphasia), [[I69.351]] (right hemiplegia), [[I69.391]] (dysphagia) to maintain HCC 85 capture year over year |
| ✅ | **Left CEA modifier -LT** — when CEA ([[35301]]) is performed on the left carotid, modifier [[-LT]] is required; watch for RLN injury documentation post-operatively |
