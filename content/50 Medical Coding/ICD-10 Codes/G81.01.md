# 🧬 ICD-10 CM G81.01 — Flaccid Hemiplegia Affecting Right Dominant Side

> [!success] Billable Code Confirmed
> **ICD-10 CM [[G81.01]]** is a complete, **six-character ICD-10-CM code** that specifies **flaccid-type hemiplegia**, laterality (**right**), and dominance (**dominant side**), leaving no unspecified elements, which is why it is fully billable in **FY2026**.^2

> [!warning] Non-Billable Parent Codes
> **G81** (**[[Hemiplegia]] and [[hemiparesis]]**) is a **non-billable category** header because it does not specify the type of **[[paralysis]]**, side affected, or dominance. **G81.0** (**Flaccid hemiplegia**) is likewise **non-billable** on its own since it omits the required laterality and dominance sixth character. Both require an additional character before they can be reported on a claim.

> [!important] Clinical Context
> The "**dominant side**" designation reflects hand dominance, not the side of the brain lesion, so a **right-handed** patient with **left-brain injury** causing right-sided weakness is coded as dominant-side involvement.

> [!danger] Code Classification
> **ICD-10 CM [[G81.01]]** is a diagnosis code describing a neurologic sign/symptom-level **deficit** and is not a procedure code.

---

## 🔍 Code Description
**[[Flaccid hemiplegia]]** affecting **the right dominant side describes complete or near-complete loss of muscle tone and voluntary movement on the right side of the body** in a patient whose right side is also their dominant (**typically right-handed**) side. Unlike **[[G81.11]]** **spastic [[hemiplegia]]**, which presents with increased muscle tone and **[[hyperreflexia]]**, **flaccid [[hemiplegia]]** is characterized by decreased tone, diminished or absent **reflexes**, and **limp musculature**, often seen in the acute phase immediately following a **central nervous system** insult before **[[spasticity]]** develops. This code category sits within the broader **[[G81]]** **[[hemiplegia]]** and **[[hemiparesis]]** group, itself nested under the paralytic syndromes block of **Chapter 6** of **ICD-10-CM.**

**Correct code selection** depends heavily on documentation of both the type of **[[paralysis]]** (**flaccid versus spastic**) and whether the affected side matches the patient's hand dominance, since **ICD-10-CM** does not allow coders to assume dominance based on side alone. When the underlying cause is a **[[cerebrovascular accident]]** and the **[[hemiplegia]]** represents a late effect, coders must instead use the appropriate **I69.05**-series sequela code with its own laterality/dominance seventh character, rather than **[[G81.01]]**, per **Excludes1 instructions**. Documentation gaps regarding dominance are one of the most frequent physician queries generated by this code family.

> [!example] Dominance Documentation Drives Specificity
> **Payers** and **quality programs** increasingly deny or downcode claims using **[[G81.00]]** (unspecified side) when the medical record clearly documents right-sided weakness, so querying for dominance status upgrades the claim from an unspecified code to **[[G81.01]]** or **[[G81.03]]**.

> [!tip]
> Always confirm **hand dominance** directly from documentation (**occupational therapy notes are a reliable source**) rather than defaulting to right-hand dominance, since incorrect assumptions can misassign the sixth character.

## ✅ Includes
- **Flaccid [[paralysis]]** limited to the right arm and leg in a right-hand-dominant patient, with no facial involvement required for this code family
- Longstanding **[[flaccid hemiplegia]]** of the right dominant side when the underlying acute cause has resolved but is not documented as a **[[stroke]]** sequela
- **[[Flaccid hemiplegia]]** identified during inpatient **[[rehabilitation]]** assessment as an admitting or secondary diagnosis, distinct from **[[G81.11]]** spastic presentations

---

## ❌ Excludes

### Excludes 1
- **G80.-** (**[[Cerebral palsy]]**) is excluded because **[[cerebral palsy]]** codes are reserved for congenital, non-progressive motor disorders originating in early brain development, whereas **[[G81.01]]** applies to acquired conditions in any patient regardless of age.
- **I69.05-, I69.15-, I69.25-, I69.35-, I69.85-, I69.95-** (**[[Hemiplegia]] and [[hemiparesis]] following cerebrovascular disease**) are excluded because these late-effect codes must be used whenever the **[[flaccid hemiplegia]]** is a documented residual of a prior **stroke** or **intracerebral hemorrhage**.

> [!danger]
> The most common **Excludes1 error** is coding **[[G81.01]]** for a patient with a documented history of **stroke** instead of sequencing the appropriate I69.05- family code, which results in incomplete capture of the **stroke** etiology and can trigger payer edits.

### Excludes 2
No **Excludes2 notes** are published for **[[G81.01]]** in the **FY2026** **ICD-10-CM tabular list**, meaning there are no conditions specifically permitted to be coded simultaneously with this code under an **Excludes2 instruction.**

## 📋 Clinical Overview

### Flaccid vs. Spastic Hemiplegia
Distinguishing flaccid from **spastic hemiplegia** is essential because they represent different points in the natural history of a neurologic injury and carry different code assignments. **Flaccid hemiplegia** typically appears acutely and reflects "**spinal shock**" or early cortical injury, while **[[spastic hemiplegia]]** often develops weeks later as **reflex** arcs reorganize.

| Feature | **[[G81.01]]** | **[[G81.11]] (Spastic, right dominant)** | **[[G81.00]] (Flaccid, unspecified side)** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Muscle tone** | Decreased/absent tone with limp musculature on the right dominant side. | Increased tone, [[hyperreflexia]], and clonus on the right dominant side. | Decreased/absent tone, side of body not documented. |
| **Timing** | Often early post-injury before [[spasticity]] develops. | Often subacute to chronic, weeks to months post-injury. | Same flaccid presentation, but laterality unspecified. |
| **Coding specificity** | Fully specified, billable at highest granularity. | Fully specified, billable at highest granularity. | Billable but nonspecific; should prompt a physician query for side/dominance. |

> [!important]
> A **CDI trigger** should fire any time "**[[hemiplegia]]**" or "**[[hemiparesis]]**" appears in documentation without a stated tone quality (**flaccid**/**spastic**) or dominance status, since both elements are required to move off unspecified codes.

### Manifestations & Symptom Burden
- **Absent** or **markedly reduced deep tendon reflexes** on the affected right side.
- **Complete** or **near-complete loss of voluntary motor control** in the right arm and leg.
- **Muscle [[atrophy]]** over time from disuse if flaccidity is prolonged.
- **Impaired fine motor skills** affecting activities of daily living on the dominant hand side.
- **Increased fall risk** and need for assistive devices during inpatient **rehabilitation** stays.

> [!tip]
> **Manifestation codes** for **[[atrophy]]**, **[[contracture]]**, or **gait abnormality** secondary to the **[[hemiplegia]]** should be coded separately when documented, as **[[G81.01]]** alone does not capture downstream functional complications.

## 💰 HCC Risk Adjustment
**ICD-10 CM [[G81.01]]** maps to **CMS-HCC v28 category 253** (**Hemiplegia/Hemiparesis**), with a community **RAF weight** of approximately 0.387.^1 Because **[[hemiplegia]]** is **a chronic condition requiring ongoing management**, it must be documented and coded at every encounter where it is evaluated or treated to remain on the patient's active risk profile for that payment year. Coders should watch for documentation that only references history of stroke without restating the current hemiplegic status, since this omission causes the **HCC** to drop off the risk score.

---

## 🏥 MS-DRG Assignment
**ICD-10 CM [[G81.01]]** groups within **MDC 01 (Nervous System)** and can act as a **CC** contributor when reported as a secondary diagnosis alongside a qualifying principal diagnosis, influencing assignment among **DRG 057, DRG 058, and DRG 059 (Degenerative Nervous System Disorders with MCC, with CC, and without CC/MCC respectively)**. Sequencing rules require that if the **flaccid hemiplegia** is the acute reason for admission, the underlying etiology, if known, should generally be sequenced first, with **[[G81.01]]** as an additional diagnosis. Accurate **CC** capture depends on complete documentation of the flaccid quality and dominant-side laterality, since defaulting to an unspecified code may fail to trigger **CC/MCC logic** in the grouper.

## 🛠️ Commonly Associated CPT Codes
- **[[97110]]** (Therapeutic exercise) - commonly billed for right-sided flaccid hemiplegia patients undergoing strengthening and range-of-motion therapy during inpatient rehab stays.
- **[[97112]]** (Neuromuscular reeducation) - used when therapy targets balance, coordination, and proprioceptive retraining of the flaccid right limb.
- **[[97140]]** (Manual therapy techniques) - billed when a therapist performs joint mobilization or soft tissue work to prevent contracture in the flaccid extremity.
- **[[95886]]** (Needle EMG, extremity muscles) - ordered to evaluate the extent and pattern of denervation contributing to the flaccid presentation.
- **[[99223]]/[[99233]]** (Initial/subsequent inpatient hospital care) - reported by the treating physician managing the patient during an inpatient stay where [[G81.01]] is an active secondary diagnosis.

### NCCI Bundling Considerations
Therapeutic exercise (**[[97110]]**) and **neuromuscular** reeducation (**[[97112]]**) are not bundled under **NCCI edits** and may be billed together on the same date when distinct, medically necessary services are documented separately. However, manual therapy (**[[97140]]**) can be bundled with other timed therapy codes depending on payer-specific policy, so time-based documentation supporting each unit billed is essential to avoid denials.

## 🔬 ICD-10-PCS Crosswalk
**ICD-10 CM [[G81.01]]** is a diagnosis code and does not directly crosswalk to a procedure code, but related inpatient rehabilitation encounters may generate **PCS** codes such as **F07Z6ZZ** (Motor Treatment using Therapeutic Exercise, one body part) when reported in a rehabilitation-focused facility using **PCS coding**, or standard physical medicine PCS values under section F for functional mobility assessment and treatment activities associated with managing the flaccid limb.

## 💊 Coding Scenarios and Examples

**Scenario 1:** A 58-year-old right-hand-dominant male is admitted for pneumonia and has a documented history of flaccid right-sided hemiplegia following a traumatic brain injury two years prior, currently managed with ongoing physical therapy. 

**Correct coding**: principal diagnosis for pneumonia, secondary diagnosis [[G81.01]]. Sequencing places the acute reason for admission first, with the chronic hemiplegia as a secondary condition actively being managed, supporting HCC capture.

**Scenario 2:** A patient presents with new-onset right arm and leg weakness with decreased tone, and imaging confirms an acute ischemic stroke in the left MCA territory; the patient is right-hand dominant. 

**Correct coding**: acute stroke code from the I63.- series is sequenced first, and [[G81.01]] is not used here because the acute hemiplegia is inherent to the current cerebrovascular event, not yet a coded late effect.

**Scenario 3:** A follow-up outpatient visit six months after a stroke documents "right-sided flaccid hemiplegia, dominant side, due to old CVA." 

**Correct coding:** the appropriate sequela code from the **I69.05-** family with the correct seventh character for dominant side is used instead of [[G81.01]], per the Excludes1 instruction, since this represents a late effect of cerebrovascular disease.

---

## ⚠️ Coding Pitfalls and Tips
- Do **not** default to **[[G81.00]]** (unspecified side) when documentation supports laterality; query the provider to confirm **right or left involvement** before finalizing the code.
- Never assign **[[G81.01]]** when the **[[hemiplegia]]** is documented as a late effect of **stroke**; use the matching **I69.05-** sequela code family instead.
- Confirm hand dominance directly from the chart rather than assuming right-hand dominance by default, since this error can misassign **[[G81.01]]** versus **[[G81.03]]**.
- Remember that **flaccid** and **[[spastic hemiplegia]]** are mutually exclusive tone descriptions within the same encounter; do not code **[[G81.01]]** and **[[G81.11]]** together for the same limb at the same time.
- Recapture **chronic [[hemiplegia]]** diagnoses like **[[G81.01]]** annually when actively monitored or treated to maintain accurate **HCC risk scores** under **CMS-HCC v28**.
