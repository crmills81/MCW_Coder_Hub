# 🧬 ICD-10 CM I73.89 — Other Specified Peripheral Vascular Diseases

> [!success] Billable Code Confirmed  
> **ICD-10 CM [[I73.89]]** is a **6-character code** (**I73 category, .8 subcategory, 9th-position-style final character "9" for "other specified"**) and is the most specific code available, so it is valid for claim submission.<sup>1,2</sup>

> [!warning] Non-Billable Parent Codes  
> **I73** (**Other peripheral vascular diseases**) is a category header and cannot be reported. **I73.8** (**Other specified peripheral vascular diseases**) is a subcategory header that requires a **6th character** to choose **[[erythromelalgia]]** or other specified disease.<sup>1,3</sup>

> [!important] Clinical Context  
> Use this **code** when the provider documents a specific **peripheral vascular disease** that has no unique code, such as **[[acrocyanosis]]** or **[[erythrocyanosis]]**. **[[Erythromelalgia]]** has its own code, **[[I73.81]]**, and **Raynaud's** has **[[I73.00]]**/**[[I73.01]]**.<sup>2,3</sup>

> [!danger] Code Classification  
> This is an **ICD-10-CM diagnosis code** (**Chapter 9, Circulatory System**). It is not a **procedure code** and is reported on the **professional claim** and the **inpatient facility** claim as a diagnosis only.

---

## 🔍 Code Description  
**ICD-10 CM [[I73.89]]** captures "**other specified**" **peripheral vascular diseases**, which are **[[vasomotor]]** and **small-vessel disorders** of the **arteries, arterioles and capillaries** outside the heart and brain. The **inclusion** terms are **[[acrocyanosis]]**, **[[erythrocyanosis]]**, **simple [[acroparesthesia]]** (**Schultze's type**) and **vasomotor acroparesthesia** (**Nothnagel's type**).<sup>2,4</sup> These typically cause cold, bluish or red **discoloration** and tingling in the hands and feet.

The **code** sits under **I73.8** alongside **[[I73.81]]** (**[[erythromelalgia]]**). It is separate from unspecified **PVD** (**[[I73.9]]**) because the provider has named a specific condition. It is also distinct from **[[atherosclerosis]]** of the extremities (**[[I70.209]]** and related codes), which is classified in **category I70**.<sup>1,3</sup>

> [!example] Specificity vs. I73.9  
> **Payers** and **audits** favor the named condition over "**PVD, unspecified**." If the provider documents **[[acrocyanosis]]**, **[[I73.89]]** is correct, and **[[I73.9]]** should be reserved for undifferentiated "**PVD**" documentation.<sup>3</sup>

> [!tip] 
> **Query** the provider when documentation says "**PVD**" but the clinical picture is **atherosclerotic disease**. That would move the code to **category I70**, not **I73**.

## ✅ Includes  
- **[[Acrocyanosis]]**: Persistent bluish discoloration of the hands and feet from small-vessel [[vasospasm]].<sup>2</sup>
- **[[Erythrocyanosis]]**: Red-blue discoloration, usually of the lower legs, thighs and buttocks.<sup>2</sup>
- **Simple [[acroparesthesia]] [Schultze's type]**: Tingling and numbness in the extremities without a fixed [[vascular]] [[lesion]].<sup>2</sup>
- **Vasomotor acroparesthesia [Nothnagel's type]**: Vasomotor-driven extremity [[paresthesia]].<sup>2</sup>

---

## ❌ Excludes  

### Excludes 1  
> - **E08-E13 with .51-.52**: **Diabetic (peripheral) angiopathy**. **Peripheral angiopathy** in a diabetic patient is classified to the diabetes category, so the two **cannot** be reported together for the same condition.<sup>2,3</sup>

> [!danger] 
> The most common error is reporting **[[I73.89]]** or **[[I73.9]]** along with a **diabetic angiopathy code**. When the provider links the **peripheral vascular disease** to **[[diabetes]]**, use the **E08-E13 .51/.52 code only**.

### Excludes 2  
> - **[[T69.1]]** **Chilblains**, **T33-T34** **Frostbite**, **T69.0-** **Immersion hand or foot**: These **cold-injury conditions** can coexist with a **vasomotor disorder**, so both may be coded when documented.<sup>2,3</sup>
> - **[[G45.9]]** S**pasm of cerebral artery**: This is a neurologic vascular event and can be coded with **[[I73.89]]** when both are separately documented.<sup>3</sup>

## 📋 Clinical Overview  

### Vasomotor Disorder vs. Occlusive or Unspecified PVD  
The key decision is whether the provider has named a **specific vasomotor condition** (**[[I73.89]]**), a **Raynaud's phenomenon** (**I73.0-**), or only "**PVD**" (**[[I73.9]]**). **Atherosclerotic occlusive disease** belongs to **category I70** instead.

| **Feature** | **[[I73.89]]** | **Related [[I73.00]]** | **Related [[I73.9]]** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Typical documentation** | Acrocyanosis, erythrocyanosis or acroparesthesia named by the provider. | Raynaud's syndrome or phenomenon without gangrene. | "PVD" or "peripheral vascular disease" with no further detail. |
| **Mechanism** | Vasospasm and small-vessel dysfunction without a fixed obstruction. | Episodic digital vasospasm with triphasic color change. | Not specified. |
| **Specificity** | Specific "other" code, no further digits. | More specific with a gangrene split (.00/.01). | Least specific, a frequent query target. |

> [!important] 
> **CDI trigger**: "**PVD**" with documented **[[cyanosis]]** or **[[paresthesia]]** needs clarification. Ask whether the provider means **[[acrocyanosis]], Raynaud's or [[atherosclerosis]]**, because each lands in a different code family.

### Manifestations & Symptom Burden  
- **Color change**: Bluish or red discoloration of fingers, toes or lower legs.<sup>4</sup>
- **Cold sensitivity**: Symptoms are triggered or worsened by cold or stress.<sup>4</sup>
- **Paresthesia**: Numbness or tingling without significant skin discoloration.<sup>4</sup>
- **Pain or swelling**: Discomfort or edema in the affected extremity.<sup>4</sup>

> [!tip] 
> **Symptom codes** (**for example [[R23.0]] [[cyanosis]]**) are not coded separately when the symptom is integral to the confirmed **[[I73.89]]** diagnosis. Check **Excludes1 notes** at **[[R20.2]]** and **[[R23.0]]** before adding them.<sup>3</sup>

As a principal diagnosis, **[[I73.89]]** groups to **MS-DRGs 299-301**, and the tier depends on secondary diagnoses.<sup>5,6</sup> It does not act as a **CC** or **MCC** when secondary. Confirm the **principal diagnosis** is truly the reason for admission, since **vasomotor disorders** are rarely the sole inpatient driver.
- **NCD/LCD**: No specific **NCD** applies to this diagnosis. Check your **MAC's LCD** for **noninvasive vascular studies** (**CPT [[93922]]-[[93931]]**) for covered **ICD-10-CM list**s.

## 🛠️ Commonly Associated CPT Codes  
- **[[93922]]**: Limited bilateral noninvasive physiologic studies of upper or lower extremity arteries. Often used to evaluate suspected small-vessel or vasomotor disease.
- **[[93923]]**: Complete bilateral noninvasive physiologic studies of upper or lower extremity arteries, 3 or more levels. Requires documentation of the levels tested.
- **[[93925]]**: Duplex scan of lower extremity arteries, complete bilateral study. Do not report with [[93922]]/[[93923]] for the same extremity on the same day.
- **[[93930]]**: Duplex scan of upper extremity arteries, complete bilateral study. Useful for upper-extremity acrocyanosis work-ups.
- **[[99213]]**: Established patient office/outpatient E/M, low medical decision making. Common for follow-up visits.
- **[[99214]]**: Established patient office/outpatient E/M, moderate medical decision making. Supported when the work-up involves multiple data points or prescription management.
- **[[64820]]**: Sympathectomy of digital arteries, each digit. Rare, reserved for refractory vasospastic disease.

**Noninvasive physiologic studies** (**[[93922]]/[[93923]]**) and **duplex scans** (**[[93925]]/[[93930]]**) have **NCCI edits** when reported for the same extremity on the same date. Check **current NCCI PTP edits** before applying **[[-59]]**.<sup>7</sup>

## 🔬 ICD-10-PCS Crosswalk  
**ICD-10 CM [[I73.8]]9** is a **diagnosis**, so there is **no direct one-to-one ICD-10-PCS crosswalk**. **Noninvasive vascular studies** and **office-based work-ups** are generally not reported with **PCS** in the **inpatient setting**. If a surgical sympathectomy is performed **inpatient**, the **PCS code** is built from the **Peripheral Nervous System Excision/Destruction table** based on the exact sympathetic nerve and approach documented in the operative note. I did not list specific **PCS codes** here because I could not verify them. Confirm in your 2026 PCS tables or encoder.

## 💊 Coding Scenarios and Examples  

<br>

### Example 1
> **Clinical Scenario:**  
> A 34-year-old woman is seen in the vascular clinic for chronic bluish discoloration of both hands that worsens in cold weather. The provider diagnoses acrocyanosis and orders a limited bilateral upper extremity arterial study, which shows normal large-vessel flow.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[93922]] | Limited bilateral noninvasive physiologic study of the upper extremities. |
| PDx | [[I73.89]] | Acrocyanosis is an inclusion term at [[I73.89]]. |

> [!tip] 
> The diagnosis supports medical necessity for the study; check your MAC's LCD for covered diagnoses.
> CDI: Make sure the provider writes "acrocyanosis," not just "cyanosis" or "PVD."

### Example 2
> **Clinical Scenario:**  
> A 52-year-old man with erythrocyanosis of the lower legs is seen in follow-up. The provider performs a moderate-complexity visit and a complete bilateral lower extremity arterial duplex on the same day.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[99214]]-[[-25]] | Significant, separately identifiable E/M on the same day as the diagnostic study. |
| CPT 2 | [[93925]] | Complete bilateral lower extremity arterial duplex. |
| PDx | [[I73.89]] | Erythrocyanosis is an inclusion term at [[I73.89]]. |

> [!tip] 
> Link [[I73.89]] to both the E/M and the duplex study.
> CDI: Confirm the duplex is not for suspected atherosclerosis, which would change the diagnosis.

### Example 3
> **Clinical Scenario:**  
> A patient is admitted for cellulitis. The H&P notes chronic vasomotor acroparesthesia (Nothnagel's type) of the hands, which is monitored but not treated.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[99223]] | Initial hospital inpatient care, high medical decision making, for the professional claim. |
| PDx | [[L03.90]] | Cellulitis is the reason for admission and is sequenced first. |

> [!tip] 
> ICD-10 CM [[I73.89]] is reported as a secondary diagnosis only if it meets reporting criteria (evaluated, monitored or treated).
> CDI: It is a non-CC, so it will not change the DRG tier.

## ⚠️ Coding Pitfalls and Tips  
- **Pitfall 1**: Reporting **[[I73.89]]** with **diabetic angiopathy**. **Tips**: **Excludes1** applies; use the **E08-E13 .51/.52 code only**.
- **Pitfall 2**: Using **[[I73.9]]** when a specific condition like **[[acrocyanosis]]** is documented. **Tips**: Pick **[[I73.89]]** when the provider names the condition.
- **Pitfall 3**: Coding **Raynaud's** as **[[I73.89]]**. **Tips**: **Raynaud's** goes to **[[I73.00]]**/**[[I73.01]]**.
- **Pitfall 4**: Coding **[[erythromelalgia]]** as **[[I73.89]]**. **Tips**: Use **[[I73.81]]**.
- **Pitfall 5**: Assuming an **HCC** or **CC**. **Tips**: It is neither, so do not expect **RAF** or **DRG impact**.
- **Pitfall 6**: Stopping at the **I73.8- header.** **Tips**: **I73.8-** is not billable, so add the **sixth** character.
