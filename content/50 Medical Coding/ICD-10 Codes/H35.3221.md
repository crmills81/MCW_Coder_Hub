# 🧬 ICD-10-CM H35.3221 — Exudative AMD, Left Eye, Active CNV

> [!success] Billable Code — No Warning Needed
> **ICD-10 CM [[H35.3221]]** is a valid, billable, **fully specified** 7-character ICD-10-CM code for FY2025. [web:138] All seven characters are present and meaningful: `H35` (category) + `.3` (degeneration of macula) + `2` (exudative) + `2` (left eye) + `1` (active choroidal neovascularization). This is the **most clinically urgent code in the entire AMD family** — active CNV is an ophthalmologic emergency driving immediate anti-VEGF treatment.

> [!danger] Active CNV = Ophthalmologic Urgency
> **ICD-10 CM [[H35.3221]]** represents **active, leaking choroidal new blood vessels** in the left eye. Without prompt anti-VEGF treatment, active CNV causes **irreversible central vision loss within weeks**. Any patient with new [[metamorphopsia]] (Amsler grid distortion), sudden central blur, or acute visual decline in the left eye requires **same-day or next-day** dilated examination, OCT, and evaluation for anti-VEGF initiation. This is not a routine follow-up diagnosis — it is the clinical equivalent of a STEMI for the retina.

> [!warning] Non-Billable Parent Codes
> - ❌ `H35.322` — 6-character header — missing stage character
> - ❌ `H35.32` — 5-character header — missing laterality AND stage
> Always submit **[[H35.3221]]** (all 7 characters) for active wet AMD, left eye.

## 🔍 Code Description

**ICD-10 CM [[H35.3221]]** classifies **exudative (wet) age-related macular degeneration of the left eye with active choroidal [[neovascularization]]** — the left-eye equivalent of [[H35.3211]], representing the stage of wet AMD where **CNV vessels are actively leaking fluid, blood, or lipid exudates** into the subretinal or intraretinal space. 

Active CNV is confirmed by the presence of **subretinal fluid (SRF), intraretinal fluid (IRF), sub-RPE fluid, subretinal hyperreflective material (SHRM), or new subretinal hemorrhage** on OCT — and/or **active leakage** on fluorescein angiography (FA) or OCT angiography (OCT-A). The diagnosis of "active CNV" is not a chronic stable state — it changes with treatment response. A patient may move from  **[[H35.3221]]** (**active**) to [[H35.3222]] (**inactive**) as anti-VEGF treatment takes effect, and may revert to  **[[H35.3221]]** when fluid reactivates between injection intervals. The code must reflect the **current activity status at each specific encounter**, not a historical status.

### What "Active" Means on OCT — The Evidence Base for H35.3221

Active CNV is diagnosed when **at least one** of the following is present on OCT or imaging: 

| Finding | OCT Description | Clinical Significance |
|---|---|---|
| **Subretinal fluid (SRF)** | Hyporeflective space between photoreceptors and RPE | Fluid leaking from CNV into subretinal space — most common finding |
| **Intraretinal fluid (IRF)** | Cystoid hyporeflective spaces within retinal layers | Fluid within retina — often associated with type 3 RAP lesions |
| **Sub-RPE fluid** | Fluid beneath the RPE — pigment epithelial detachment (PED) | Common in type 1 (occult) CNV — often fibrovascular PED |
| **Subretinal hyperreflective material (SHRM)** | Hyperreflective material in subretinal space | CNV membrane itself or hemorrhage/fibrin |
| **New subretinal hemorrhage** | Hyperreflective or hyporeflective blood in subretinal space | Bleeding from fragile CNV vessels — urgent |
| **Active leakage on FA** | Pooling dye on fluorescein angiography | Gold standard for CNV activity confirmation |
| **Flow signal on OCT-A** | New or enlarged flow void/signal in outer retina | CNV vessel detection without dye |

> [!tip] SRF vs. IRF — Clinical and Prognostic Significance
> The **type of fluid** in active CNV has prognostic and treatment implications that are increasingly documented by retina specialists — and may appear in medical record documentation worth capturing:
> - **SRF only** — generally more benign; some patients with small amounts of stable SRF may have CNV that is relatively "tolerant" of modest fluid accumulation under T&E protocols; some retina physicians accept small SRF as an acceptable endpoint under extended dosing
> - **IRF** — associated with worse visual prognosis; most retina physicians treat to complete IRF resolution and do not accept persistent IRF as a stable endpoint
> - **Both SRF + IRF** — worst prognosis for VA outcomes; aggressive treatment protocol
>
> These distinctions don't change the code (all are still  **[[H35.3221]]** — active CNV), but understanding them helps you recognize active vs. inactive CNV language in documentation and appreciate why the physician is treating aggressively even when vision appears preserved.

## 📋 Clinical Overview — Active CNV in Context

### The CNV Activation Event

Active CNV in the left eye ( **[[H35.3221]]**) most commonly develops by one of two mechanisms:<sup>2</sup>

1. **De novo conversion** — left eye previously had intermediate or advanced dry AMD ([[H35.3122]]/[[H35.3124]]) and converts to wet AMD; often heralded by sudden metamorphopsia
2. **Reactivation** — previously treated left-eye wet AMD ([[H35.3222]] inactive) flares; CNV re-activates with recurrent fluid between injection intervals

The distinction matters for clinical documentation and helps confirm code assignment:
- New conversion = first assignment of  **[[H35.3221]]** in the left eye; prior code was H35.312x (dry)
- Reactivation = code moves from [[H35.3222]] back to  **[[H35.3221]]** at that encounter

### Symptoms of Active CNV — Left Eye

| Symptom | Details |
|---|---|
| **Metamorphopsia** | Straight lines appear wavy or distorted — Amsler grid distortion; the most specific early symptom |
| **Central scotoma** | Dark or blank spot in central vision left eye |
| **Acute VA decline** | Measurable drop from prior visit — even 1-2 Snellen lines warrants OCT |
| **Micropsia** | Objects appear smaller than normal — fluid distorting photoreceptor spacing |
| **Color desaturation** | Colors appear less vivid in the central field left eye |
| **Asymptomatic** | Rare but possible — especially with Type 1 occult CNV; detected on surveillance OCT |

> [!caution] Asymptomatic Active CNV on OCT — Still Codes H35.3221
> A patient under active T&E monitoring may present asymptomatic but have **new subretinal fluid on OCT** at a scheduled follow-up visit. This is still **active CNV** regardless of symptom status — the OCT findings drive the code, not patient-reported symptoms. If the physician injects based on OCT fluid detection alone, assign  **[[H35.3221]]**. Do not assign [[H35.3222]] simply because the patient says they "feel fine."

### The Fellow Eye Risk — Bilateral Vigilance

When  **[[H35.3221]]** is active in the left eye, the **right eye must be simultaneously evaluated**:

- If the right eye has **any AMD** (dry or wet), document and code its current status separately
- If right eye develops active CNV → assign **[[H35.3211]]** alongside  **[[H35.3221]]** (bilateral active CNV — two separate laterality codes unless bilateral code [[H35.3231]] better applies)
- Fellow eye conversion risk in a patient with one wet AMD eye is approximately **12% per year** 
- Both eyes' AMD codes must reflect their **independent current status** — never use the same code for both eyes when staging differs

> Beovu carries an FDA black box warning for **retinal vasculitis** and **retinal vascular occlusion**, including in the fellow eye. ### JZ / JW Modifier — Mandatory Since July 1, 2023

> [!danger] Missing JZ or JW = Claim Denial
> Since **July 1, 2023**, CMS requires one of these modifiers on all separately billed drug J-codes: [web:130]
>
> | Modifier | Meaning | When to Use |
> |---|---|---|
> | **-JZ** | Zero units discarded — full dose administered | Standard — when entire vial used for one patient |
> | **-JW** | Drug discarded — report waste | When a partial dose is used and remaining drug is discarded |
>
> **Example correct billing for Vabysmo left eye, no waste:**
> `J2777-LT-JZ × 60 units`
>
> **Example correct billing for Lucentis left eye, partial dose discarded:**
> `J2778-LT-JW` (for wasted portion, separate line)
>
> **Practical tip for Crystal:** The -LT modifier and -JZ modifier are both required on left-eye injection drug claims. Build them into your charge entry template so they don't get missed. A single missing -JZ on a high-cost anti-VEGF claim can trigger a full audit.

## 🔄 The Active → Inactive → Active Cycle — Dynamic Code Assignment

One of the most important features of wet AMD coding is that **[[H35.3221]] and [[H35.3222]] are not permanent assignments** — they change visit-to-visit based on OCT findings:

```
NEW DIAGNOSIS ──► H35.3221 (Active CNV, loading × 3 injections)
                      │
              OCT: Fluid resolved
                      │
                      ▼
               H35.3222 (Inactive CNV — extend interval)
                      │
              OCT: Fluid returns at next visit
                      │
                      ▼
               H35.3221 (Active CNV — reactivation — inject again)
                      │
              OCT: Fluid resolved again — extend further
                      │
                      ▼
               H35.3222 (Inactive — now extended to q12 weeks)
                      │
           Disease eventually "burns out"
                      │
                      ▼
               H35.3223 (Inactive scar — disciform end stage)
```

> [!tip] The Code Changes at Every Visit — Check OCT Every Time
> This dynamic cycle means that a patient who had [[H35.3222]] (inactive) at their last visit may present as  **[[H35.3221]]** (active) today — and vice versa. **The OCT report at each visit is the definitive authority.** Never carry forward the prior visit's code without confirming the current fluid status. This is one of the most common wet AMD coding errors: coding [[H35.3222]] on a visit where the OCT shows recurrent fluid because the coder assumed the prior inactive status persisted.

## 🔭 Emerging Treatments — What's Coming for H35.3221 (FY2026+)

> [!info] The Anti-VEGF Landscape Is Actively Evolving — 2025-2026 Updates
> The standard-of-care monthly/bimonthly intravitreal injection paradigm is being challenged by sustained-delivery and gene therapy approaches that may dramatically reduce injection burden for patients with  **[[H35.3221]]**.  These are not yet standard of care but are relevant for CDI and documentation awareness as they enter the clinical mainstream:

| Treatment | Mechanism | Status (2025-2026) | Potential Coding Impact |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ADVM-022 (ixo-vec)** | AAV2.7m8 intravitreal gene therapy — continuous aflibercept expression | Phase 3 ARTEMIS trial enrolling  | Single injection → sustained CNV suppression;  **[[H35.3221]]** active until sustained suppression confirmed; then [[H35.3222]] |
| **ABBV-RGX-314** | Suprachoroidal AAV gene therapy — anti-VEGF transgene | Phase 2/3 — 80% reduction in supplemental anti-VEGF injections  | New HCPCS codes when approved; delivery CPT different from [[67028]] |
| **AXT107** | Suprachoroidal integrin peptide — anti-VEGF-A/C + Tie2 | Phase 1/2 DISCOVER trial | Novel delivery route — suprachoroidal injection may require different CPT |
| **Port Delivery System (Susvimo)** | Ranibizumab ocular implant — continuous intravitreal delivery | FDA approved — wet AMD and DME | Implantation CPT [[67299]] or [[67027]]; refill J-code; different claim structure than injection |
| **Bispecific/trispecific antibodies** | Dual or triple pathway inhibition beyond VEGF+Ang-2 | Multiple Phase 2 trials  | New J-codes upon FDA approval |

> [!tip] Gene Therapy Will Not Eliminate H35.3221 — It Will Change the Injection Frequency
> If/when AAV gene therapy for wet AMD reaches approval, patients may still be coded as  **[[H35.3221]]** (active CNV) during the period between gene therapy administration and confirmed CNV suppression. [[H35.3222]] (inactive) would be assigned when sustained suppression is achieved and no fluid is present. The diagnosis codes themselves are unlikely to change — but the CPT/HCPCS codes for the new delivery modalities will require monitoring as new permanent codes are assigned.

## 💊 Coding Scenarios

---

### Scenario 1 — New Wet AMD Conversion, Left Eye (Outpatient)

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 76-year-old female with known intermediate dry AMD, left eye ([[H35.3122]]) presents urgently — she noticed wavy lines when reading with her left eye starting 2 days ago. VA: 20/200 OS (was 20/30 six weeks ago). OCT left eye: large subretinal fluid collection with CNV membrane. FA: active type 2 classic CNV with leakage. Right eye: intermediate dry AMD, stable — VA 20/25 OD. Impression: "New wet AMD conversion, left eye — active CNV — initiating Vabysmo loading — urgent." Vabysmo 6mg administered today.

**CPT / HCPCS:**
- [[92004]]-LT *(or [[92014]] if established)* — Comprehensive exam
- [[92235]] — Fluorescein angiography *(CNV characterization — standard at initial wet diagnosis)*
- [[92134]] — OCT posterior segment *(SRF confirmation, CNV measurement)*
- [[67028]]-LT — Intravitreal injection, left eye *(Loading dose #1)*
- J2777-LT-JZ × 60 units — Vabysmo 6mg, no waste

**ICD-10-CM:**
-  **[[H35.3221]]** — Exudative AMD, left eye, active CNV *(converted from [[H35.3122]] — wet AMD now confirmed; retire H35.3122 for this eye)*
- [[H35.3112]] — Nonexudative AMD, right eye, intermediate dry stage *(right eye unchanged — separate active comorbidity)*
- [[H53.132]] — Sudden visual loss, left eye *(2-day acute onset — separately codeable)*

> [!important] Retire H35.3122 When Left Eye Converts to Wet AMD
> Once wet AMD is confirmed in the left eye, **stop coding [[H35.3122]]** (intermediate dry) for the left eye and replace with **[[H35.3221]]** (active CNV) going forward. The wet AMD code supersedes the prior dry AMD code for the converted eye. Only resume dry AMD coding for the left eye if the wet AMD resolves to scar ([[H35.3223]]) AND a separate GA lesion is also present ([[H35.3123]]/[[H35.3124]]).

### Scenario 2 — Reactivation After 8 Weeks — Code Shifts Back to H35.3221 (Outpatient)

> **Clinical Vignette:** A patient on Eylea T&E protocol — previously at 10-week intervals with resolved fluid. Returns at week 10. OCT left eye: **new subretinal fluid** at the fovea — approximately 150μm — new onset since last visit. VA unchanged from last visit (20/40 OS). Physician documents: "Reactivation of CNV, left eye — fluid recurrence — adjust interval back to 8 weeks — inject today." Eylea HD administered.

**CPT / HCPCS:**
- [[67028]]-LT — Intravitreal injection, left eye
- J0179-LT-JZ × 1 — Aflibercept 8mg (Eylea HD), no waste
- [[92134]] — OCT *(fluid detection at this visit)*
- [[92014]] — Comprehensive exam, established patient

**ICD-10-CM:**
-  **[[H35.3221]]** — Exudative AMD, left eye, **active CNV** *(NOT [[H35.3222]] — new OCT fluid confirmed today → this is active CNV, not inactive)*

> [!success] Code Reverts to H35.3221 on Reactivation — This Is Expected and Correct
> The code appropriately moves from [[H35.3222]] (at prior inactive visits) **back to [[H35.3221]]** today because the OCT shows recurrent fluid. This is not a coding error — it reflects the dynamic nature of wet AMD. Both codes will appear in the patient's encounter history, alternating with treatment response. This is normal and correct.

### Scenario 3 — Bilateral Active CNV — Same-Day Bilateral Injection (Outpatient)

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 79-year-old male with bilateral wet AMD — both eyes on monthly Lucentis loading phase. Today is loading dose #2 for both eyes. OCT both eyes: fluid present bilaterally. VA: 20/60 OD, 20/80 OS.

**CPT / HCPCS:**
- [[67028]]-RT — Intravitreal injection, right eye *(separate line)*
- [[67028]]-LT — Intravitreal injection, left eye *(separate line)*
- J2778-RT-JZ × 5 — Ranibizumab 0.5mg right eye, no waste
- J277]-LT-JZ × 5 — Ranibizumab 0.5mg left eye, no waste
- [[92134]] — OCT *(bilateral — one code, document both eyes in report)*
- [[92014]] — Comprehensive exam

**ICD-10-CM:**
- [[H35.3231]] — Exudative AMD, bilateral, active CNV *(both eyes active at same stage — bilateral code preferred)*
- *OR: [[H35.3211]] +  **[[H35.3221]]*** — Right active CNV + left active CNV *(separate codes also acceptable — confirm payer preference)*

> [!note] Bilateral Code vs. Two Separate Laterality Codes — Both Are Acceptable
> When both eyes have the **same wet AMD stage**, you may use either:
> - **[[H35.3231]]** (bilateral active CNV) — single code for both eyes; cleaner
> - **[[H35.3211]] + [[H35.3221]]** (right active + left active) — separate codes; preferred by some payers who want explicit laterality on each claim line matching the -RT/-LT injection modifiers
>
> Verify your payer's preference. Some commercial payers and MACs prefer the bilateral code; others want to see the laterality-specific codes matched to the injection claim lines. When in doubt, separate laterality codes provide the clearest audit trail linking diagnosis to treatment side.

### Scenario 4 — Wet AMD Left Eye Inpatient — Additional Diagnosis (Inpatient Cardiac)

> **Clinical Vignette:** An 82-year-old female is admitted for NSTEMI, left main CAD. H&P documents active wet AMD, left eye — currently receiving Vabysmo injections every 8 weeks — last injection was 6 weeks ago. Ophthalmology consulted during admission to assess timing of next injection. VA OS 20/40. OCT done: "Stable minimal SRF — continue current T&E schedule."

**Principal Diagnosis:**
- [[I21.02]] — ST elevation MI, left main coronary artery *(principal)*

**Additional Diagnoses:**
-  **[[H35.3221]]** — Exudative AMD, left eye, active CNV *(documented active condition, ophthalmology consulted, OCT performed — meets UHDDS criteria)*
- [[I25.10]] — Atherosclerotic CAD *(left main disease)*

> [!note] "Stable Minimal SRF" — Is This Still H35.3221?
> Yes. "Minimal SRF" on OCT still represents **active CNV with fluid** — the CNV is still leaking, even if the fluid volume is small and visually stable under T&E management. The code is  **[[H35.3221]]** (active) until OCT confirms **complete fluid resolution** ([[H35.3222]] inactive). A T&E patient who has "stable SRF" by their retina physician's treatment endpoint documentation is still technically  **[[H35.3221]]** at that visit. Some retina practices accept small stable SRF as an acceptable T&E endpoint — this can create ambiguity. When documentation says "stable, acceptable SRF — treat and extend" → query if active vs. inactive is intended, or use  **[[H35.3221]]** as clinically supported by the presence of fluid.

## ⚠️ Coding Pitfalls and Tips

| | Pitfall or Tip |
|---|---|
| ❌ | **Never carry forward [[H35.3222]] (inactive) when today's OCT shows new fluid** — reactivation =  **[[H35.3221]]**; check every visit |
| ❌ | **Never code the prior dry AMD stage alongside [[H35.3221]] unless GA co-exists** — once converted,  **[[H35.3221]]** replaces the H35.312x dry code for that eye |
| ❌ | **Never bill [[67028]] without -LT modifier for left eye** — modifier is required for laterality identification and bilateral claim adjudication |
| ❌ | **Never omit JZ or JW modifier on drug J-codes** — claim denial since July 1, 2023  |
| ❌ | **Never use [[H35.3221]] for non-AMD CNV** — myopia-related CNV, POHS, angioid streaks, or other CNV etiologies have their own codes |
| ✅ | **Read the OCT before assigning active vs. inactive** — the presence or absence of fluid is the single most reliable determinant |
| ✅ | **The injection decision is your fastest clue** — injected today = active CNV =  **[[H35.3221]]** in the vast majority of cases |
| ✅ | **Bilateral active CNV** — use [[H35.3231]] or separate laterality codes; know your payer's preference |
| ✅ | **Code vision impairment separately** — [[H54.8]], H54.12x when VA ≤20/200 left eye; don't let VA data sit undocumented |
| ✅ | **Update code dynamically** —  **[[H35.3221]]** ↔ [[H35.3222]] may alternate over the course of treatment; each visit's code reflects that visit's OCT |
| ✅ | **Watch for gene therapy approvals** — ADVM-022 and suprachoroidal platforms in late-phase trials; when approved, new CPT/HCPCS will be required for delivery procedures  |
| ✅ | **Document fellow eye AMD status separately** — right eye AMD (whatever stage) must be independently coded at each encounter |
