# 🧬 ICD-10 CM H57.02 — Anisocoria

> [!success] Billable Code Confirmed
>
> **ICD-10-CM H57.02** is a valid, billable **5-character ICD-10-CM code** for FY2026. Characters 1-3 (H57) define the category "**Other disorders of eye and adnexa**"; character 4 (0) narrows to anomalies of pupillary function; and character 5 (2) specifies anisocoria as the distinct pupillary anomaly. No additional characters are required.

> [!warning] Non-Billable Parent Codes — Never Submit These
>
> - ❌ `H57` — 3-character header — does not specify the type of ocular disorder
> - ❌ `H57.0` — 4-character header — does not specify which pupillary anomaly is present
>
> Always submit **H57.02** (all 5 characters) when anisocoria is documented.

> [!important] Clinical Context: Physiologic vs. Pathologic Anisocoria
>
> **ICD-10-CM H57.02** captures anisocoria regardless of etiology — physiologic (**benign, <2 mm asymmetry, no disease**) and pathologic (**caused by iris disease, CN III palsy, Horner syndrome, pharmacologic agents, or [[herniation]]**) are both coded here. The distinction in documentation drives workup and associated diagnoses, not code selection.

> [!danger] Code Classification
>
> **ICD-10-CM Diagnosis Code** — **wRVU**, assistant-at-surgery payable, and global period fields are not applicable to this **diagnosis** code. For procedural coding associated with this diagnosis, refer to the **CPT** Procedural Crosswalk and **ICD-10-PCS Crosswalk** sections below.

## 🔍 Code Description

**ICD-10 CM H57.02** classifies **[[anisocoria]]**, defined as unequal pupil size between the two eyes. The condition may represent a benign physiologic variant (**asymmetry <2 mm with no associated disease**) or a clinically significant manifestation of an underlying neurological or ophthalmologic process.^1^

Pathologic **[[anisocoria]]** reflects disruption of the autonomic pathways controlling the [[iris]] sphincter or dilator muscles — specifically the parasympathetic fibers of **[[cranial nerve III]]** (**oculomotor nerve**) or the sympathetic chain via the **cervical ganglia**. Etiologies include Horner syndrome, [[CN III palsy]], Adie tonic pupil, pharmacologic **[[mydriasis]]/[[miosis]]**, and acute cerebral [[herniation]].^2^

> [!tip] Don't Default to H57.00
> If the provider documents "**anisocoria**" by name — even without specifying physiologic vs. pathologic — **H57.02** is the correct code. Reserve **H57.00** (**Unspecified anomaly of pupillary function**) only when the documented finding is a vague pupillary abnormality with no further characterization.

## 📋 Clinical Overview

### Physiologic vs. Pathologic Anisocoria

Accurate documentation review is essential because both variants map to **H57.02**, but pathologic **anisocoria** requires additional etiologic coding and has very different clinical implications for workup, treatment, and payer scrutiny.

| Feature | **Physiologic Anisocoria** | **Pathologic — Parasympathetic Lesion** | **Pathologic — Sympathetic Lesion** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pupil Size Difference** | <2 mm | >2 mm (larger, abnormal pupil) | >1 mm (smaller, abnormal pupil) |
| **Light Reaction** | Normal bilaterally | Diminished or absent (affected side) | Normal bilaterally |
| **Associated Findings** | None | Ptosis, ophthalmoplegia (CN III) | **[[Ptosis]]**, **[[anhidrosis]]** (Horner) |
| **Common Etiology** | Normal variant | **CN III palsy**, Adie pupil, herniation | Horner syndrome, Pancoast tumor |
| **Additional ICD-10-CM** | None needed | **[[G52.2]], [[G83.81]]** | **[[G90.2]]** |
| **Urgency** | Routine | Emergent if acute onset | Urgent workup |

> [!important] CDI Query Trigger — Anisocoria Etiology Not Documented
> When **[[anisocoria]]** is charted as a finding but no underlying etiology is documented, and the clinical picture suggests a neurological cause (**acute onset, [[ptosis]], [[ophthalmoplegia]], or imaging findings**), send a CDI query asking the provider to clarify the etiology. Coding the underlying cause (**CN III palsy, Horner syndrome**) alongside **H57.02** more fully captures patient complexity and may affect DRG weight.

### Manifestations & Symptom Burden

Common presenting signs and associated **manifestations** that should be coded when separately documented:

- **[[Ptosis]] ([[H02.40]]-[[H02.43]]):** Drooping of the upper eyelid, present in both CN III palsy and Horner syndrome-associated anisocoria
- **[[Diplopia]] ([[H53.2]]):** Double vision occurring with CN III palsy causing anisocoria from parasympathetic disruption
- **[[Headache]] ([[R51.9]]):** May indicate aneurysmal compression of CN III — emergent workup trigger
- **[[Anhidrosis]] ([[L74.51]]):** Absence of sweating on the ipsilateral face in Horner syndrome

> [!tip] Coding Manifestations
> Always code documented manifestations to fully capture the patient's complexity. Examples:
> - **[[H02.401]]** — Unspecified ptosis of right eyelid
> - **[[H53.2]]** — Diplopia
> - **[[G90.2]]** — Horner syndrome (if documented as etiology)

> [!warning] Sequencing and Complications
> **H57.02** sequences under **MDC 02** when it is the principal inpatient diagnosis. Sequencing impacts **DRG** weight significantly — always align principal diagnosis with the condition chiefly responsible for the admission per **UHDDS** guidelines.

## 💊 Coding Scenarios and Examples

---

### Scenario 1 — Outpatient Ophthalmology: Incidental Physiologic Anisocoria on Routine Exam

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 34-year-old female presents for a comprehensive eye exam. Upon pupillary evaluation, the ophthalmologist notes the right pupil measures 3.5 mm and the left measures 4.0 mm in ambient light, with equal and brisk reactions to light and accommodation bilaterally. No ptosis, diplopia, or other neurological findings are present. The provider documents "physiologic anisocoria — benign variant, no workup indicated."

**CPT (Profee):**
- [[92014]] — Comprehensive ophthalmological service, established patient

**ICD-10-CM:**
- [[H57.02 1]] — Anisocoria *(principal/first-listed; physiologic variant, no additional etiologic code needed)*

---

### Scenario 2 — Emergency Department: New-Onset Anisocoria with CN III Palsy

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 58-year-old male presents to the ED with sudden-onset right-sided headache, right ptosis, and a blown right pupil (7 mm, unreactive). The left pupil is 3 mm and reactive. CT angiography reveals a posterior communicating artery aneurysm compressing the right CN III. Neurosurgery is consulted emergently and the patient is admitted.

**Principal Diagnosis:**
- [[I67.1]] — Cerebral aneurysm, nonruptured *(reason for admission)*

**Secondary Diagnoses:**
- [[H57.02 1]] — Anisocoria *(pupillary finding secondary to compression)*
- [[H49.01]] — Third (oculomotor) nerve palsy, right eye *(CN III palsy driving the anisocoria)*
- [[H02.401]] — Unspecified ptosis of right eyelid *(separately documented)*

**MS-DRG Assignment:** Sequences to MDC 01 (Nervous System) under the cerebral aneurysm principal diagnosis. H57.02 as secondary does not independently shift DRG but adds clinical specificity.

---

### Scenario 3 — CDI Query: Anisocoria with "Asymmetric Pupils" Only

> **Clinical Vignette:** An inpatient progress note reads: "Neuro exam notable for asymmetric pupils, right > left. Will monitor." No etiology is documented. The patient has a history of neck dissection for thyroid cancer. The coder notes the finding but cannot determine if this is physiologic, Horner-related, or traumatic.

**Action / Outcome:**
The documentation "asymmetric pupils" is sufficient to support H57.02. However, given the history of neck dissection (a known risk for Horner syndrome), a CDI query should be sent to determine if an etiologic diagnosis can be established.

**Query Response:** Provider updates documentation to confirm: "Anisocoria in the setting of prior neck dissection — consistent with iatrogenic left Horner syndrome."

**Corrected ICD-10-CM Coding:**
- [[H57.02 1]] — Anisocoria
- [[G90.2]] — Horner syndrome *(etiology now documented; code additionally)*

## ⚠️ Coding Pitfalls and Tips

|     | Pitfall or Tip                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                      |
| --- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ❌   | **Using H57.0 (the parent code).** H57.0- is a non-billable 4-character header. Submitting it will result in claim rejection or denial. Always use the full 5-character **H57.02**.                                                                                                                                 |
| ❌   | **Defaulting to H57.00 when anisocoria is documented.** If the word "**anisocoria**" appears anywhere in the documentation, **H57.02** is correct — do not assign the unspecified code **H57.00**.                                                                                                                  |
| ❌   | **Failing to code the underlying etiology.** Anisocoria is frequently a symptom of a separately codeable condition. Code also **Horner syndrome, CN III palsy**, or other documented etiologies per **ICD-10-CM convention**.                                                                                       |
| ✅   | **Always query when etiology is ambiguous but clinically suggested.** A focused **CDI** query asking the provider to confirm the cause of **anisocoria** (**especially in inpatient settings with relevant history**) can unlock additional **HCC**-bearing diagnoses.                                              |
| ✅   | **Code manifestations separately.** Document-supported ptosis, diplopia, or anhidrosis should be coded alongside **H57.02** to fully reflect clinical complexity in both profee and inpatient settings.                                                                                                             |
| ✅   | **Verify sequencing for inpatient admissions.** When **anisocoria** is the sign driving an admission that uncovers a neurological principal diagnosis (**e.g., [[aneurysm]], [[herniation]]**), ensure the principal diagnosis is the condition established after study per **UHDDS guidelines** — not the symptom. |
