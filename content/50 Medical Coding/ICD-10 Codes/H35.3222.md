# 🧬 ICD-10-CM H35.3222 — Exudative AMD, Left Eye, Inactive CNV

> [!success] Billable Code — No Warning Needed
> **ICD-10 CM [[H35.3222]]** is a valid, billable, fully specified 7-character ICD-10-CM code for FY2025.  All seven characters are present and clinically meaningful: `H35` (**category**) + `.3` (**degeneration of macula**) + `2` (**exudative**) + `2` (**left eye**) + `2` (**inactive CNV**). This is the **post-treatment success code** — it documents that anti-VEGF therapy has controlled CNV activity and the macula is currently fluid-free.

> [!warning] Non-Billable Parent Codes
> - ❌ `H35.322` — 6-character header — missing stage character
> - ❌ `H35.32` — 5-character header — missing laterality AND stage
> Always submit **[[H35.3222]]** (all 7 characters) for inactive CNV, left eye.

---

## 🔍 Code Description

**[[H35.3222]]** classifies **exudative (wet) age-related macular degeneration of the left eye with inactive choroidal neovascularization** — the stage where CNV vessels are present in the left eye but **no longer actively leaking**, as confirmed by the absence of subretinal fluid (SRF), intraretinal fluid (IRF), or sub-RPE fluid on OCT at the time of the encounter. [web:152]

**[[H35.3222]]** represents the **treatment success state** of wet AMD — the clinical goal of every anti-VEGF injection is to move the patient from [[H35.3221]] (active CNV with fluid) to **[[H35.3222]]** (inactive CNV, fluid resolved). However, unlike [[H35.3223]] (inactive scar), where CNV has permanently converted to fibrovascular scar, **inactive CNV in [[H35.3222]] is a dynamic, reversible state** — the CNV membrane remains viable and can reactivate at any time, causing recurrent fluid and requiring the code to revert to [[H35.3221]]. This perpetual vigilance — monitoring for reactivation while extending injection intervals — is the defining clinical reality of the **[[H35.3222]]** stage and the entire Treat-and-Extend (T&E) treatment paradigm. [web:160]

## 📊 Inactive CNV in Staging Context

| Code | Stage | OCT | Fluid? | Anti-VEGF? | Code Stability |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [[H35.3220]] | Unspecified ⚠️ | Unknown | ? | Cannot determine | N/A |
| [[H35.3221]] | Active CNV | SRF/IRF/PED/hemorrhage | ✅ YES | ✅ Inject | Changes with treatment |
| **[[H35.3222]]** | **Inactive CNV ← This Code** | **Flat, dry macula — CNV membrane visible** | ❌ **NO** | **T&E or PRN** | **Reverts to H35.3221 if fluid returns** |
| [[H35.3223]] | Inactive scar | Disciform fibrosis | ❌ No | Usually stopped | More stable — permanent scar |

> [!important] H35.3222 vs. H35.3223 — The Critical Distinction
> Both codes represent "inactive" wet AMD — but they are **fundamentally different end states**:
>
> | Feature | **[[H35.3222]]** Inactive CNV | [[H35.3223]] Inactive Scar |
> |---|---|---|
> | CNV vessels | Present — quiescent | Replaced by fibrovascular scar |
> | Fluid | None currently | None — scar cannot leak |
> | Reactivation risk | ✅ **HIGH** — can re-activate | ❌ Very low — scar is stable |
> | Anti-VEGF continued? | ✅ Usually yes — T&E/PRN | ❌ Usually stopped |
> | VA trajectory | Stable, possibly improving | Permanently reduced |
> | OCT appearance | Flat macula ± CNV membrane | Hyperreflective subretinal fibrosis |
>
> If the physician documents **"scar"** or **"disciform"** or **"fibrosis"** → that's [[H35.3223]], not **[[H35.3222]]**. If the record shows fluid-free macula with CNV membrane present but no scar → **[[H35.3222]]**. When the distinction is ambiguous, query.

## 📋 Clinical Overview — The Treat-and-Extend World of H35.3222

### What "Inactive CNV" Means Clinically

At the **[[H35.3222]]** stage, the patient's CNV has been **suppressed by anti-VEGF treatment** to the point where: 

- The macula is **"dry"** — no subretinal, intraretinal, or sub-RPE fluid on OCT
- The CNV membrane may still be **visible on OCT-A** as a flow signal or hyperreflective material — but it is not actively leaking
- **Visual acuity is stable** or has improved from the prior active stage
- The patient is **no longer in the urgent treatment phase** — instead, they are in long-term management aimed at maintaining the dry state while minimizing injection burden

The defining clinical challenge at **[[H35.3222]]** is answering the question: **How long can we safely extend the injection interval before fluid returns?** This question drives the entire Treat-and-Extend protocol — the dominant treatment paradigm for wet AMD in modern retina practice.  

### The Treat-and-Extend Protocol — How It Creates H35.3222 Encounters

Treat-and-Extend (T&E), first described by Spaide and Freund, is the most widely used anti-VEGF strategy in wet AMD management: [web:160]

```
LOADING PHASE (H35.3221 — Active CNV)
Monthly injections × 3 until OCT shows DRY MACULA
            │
            ▼ Macula dry = Fluid resolved = CODE SHIFTS TO H35.3222
EXTENSION PHASE (H35.3222 — Inactive CNV)
Inject at THIS visit (preventive) + EXTEND interval by 2 weeks
            │
     Next visit (extended interval):
            ├── Still dry → inject + extend again (H35.3222 continues)
            ├── Still dry → inject + extend to max interval (H35.3222 continues)
            │         (Maximum interval: typically 12-16 weeks per drug)
            │
            └── FLUID RETURNS → H35.3221 (Active CNV — reactivation)
                  Shorten interval → treat back to dry → H35.3222 again
```

> [!tip] The T&E Injection at an H35.3222 Visit — Fully Billable
> One of the most important billing nuances at this stage: under T&E protocol, the physician **injects at the H35.3222 visit even though the macula is currently dry** — the injection is preventive, to maintain the extended interval and prevent fluid recurrence. This injection is **fully billable** with **[[H35.3222]]** as the supporting diagnosis. 
>
> Common coder misconception: "If there's no fluid, there's no active disease, so the injection isn't medically necessary." This is incorrect. T&E injection at H35.3222 is medically necessary and payer-supported because:
> - Discontinuing anti-VEGF in inactive wet AMD leads to rapid reactivation in the majority of patients
> - Maintaining the extended T&E interval preserves vision and reduces reactivation risk
> - All major MACs and commercial payers recognize T&E as standard of care for wet AMD
>
> The medical necessity documentation should explicitly state the T&E protocol rationale — e.g., "Wet AMD, left eye, currently inactive on T&E — macula dry — injecting to maintain treatment interval — extending to 10 weeks."

### PRN (Pro Re Nata / As-Needed) Protocol — The Alternative

Under the PRN protocol (less common than T&E but still used): [web:109]

```
LOADING PHASE (H35.3221 — Active CNV)
Monthly injections × 3 until macula dry
            │
            ▼ CODE SHIFTS TO H35.3222
PRN MONITORING PHASE (H35.3222 — Inactive CNV)
Monthly OCT — NO INJECTION given if macula is dry
            │
     At each monthly monitoring visit:
            ├── Dry → H35.3222 — no injection — monitoring visit only
            │
            └── FLUID RETURNS → H35.3221 → inject → back to monitoring
```

> [!note] PRN vs. T&E — The Coding Is the Same, the Billing Is Different
> Both PRN and T&E result in **[[H35.3222]]** when the macula is dry. The coding is identical. The **billing difference** is:
> - **T&E dry visit** → injection IS given → bill [[67028]]-LT + J-code + [[92134]] + [[92014]]
> - **PRN dry visit** → injection NOT given → bill [[92134]] + [[92014]] only (no injection CPT or drug J-code)
>
> The claim volume for T&E is higher than PRN, even when both eyes are equally controlled, because T&E injects regardless of fluid status under the preventive protocol. Both are clinically legitimate and payer-supported.

## 🔍 OCT at H35.3222 — What You're Looking For

The OCT report at an **[[H35.3222]]** encounter should confirm a **dry macula** — the absence of all active fluid markers: 

| OCT Feature | H35.3222 Expected Finding | Alert: Consider H35.3221 If |
|---|---|---|
| Subretinal fluid (SRF) | ❌ Absent | Any SRF present |
| Intraretinal fluid (IRF) | ❌ Absent | Any cystoid spaces |
| Sub-RPE fluid / PED | ❌ Absent or stable chronic PED (non-leaking) | New or enlarging PED |
| SHRM (subretinal hyperreflective material) | May be present as stable treated membrane | New SHRM or growing SHRM |
| Subretinal hemorrhage | ❌ Absent | Any new hemorrhage |
| CNV membrane (OCT-A) | May be visible as stable flow signal | New enlargement of flow signal |
| Central retinal thickness (CRT) | Stable or improving from prior active phase | New CRT increase ≥ 15-25μm |

> [!caution] The Stable PED Problem — **[[H35.3222]]** or H35.3221?
> **Pigment epithelial detachments (PEDs)** are a common source of ambiguity in wet AMD activity coding. A patient may have a **stable, chronic fibrovascular PED** under the RPE that does not represent active CNV leakage — this is **[[H35.3222]]** territory. However, a **new or enlarging PED** with increasing sub-RPE fluid may represent active CNV → [[H35.3221]]. The key is the physician's documentation of whether the PED is stable and unchanged vs. new or growing. When the record says "stable PED, no active fluid" → **[[H35.3222]]**. When it says "enlarging PED with sub-RPE fluid" or "symptomatic PED" → query or [[H35.3221]] based on context.

### OCT Coding Updates for 2025 — What Changed

> [!info] 92134 OCT Code — 2025 Changes
> The OCT code family underwent revisions at the start of 2025. [web:155] Key points for **[[H35.3222]]** monitoring visits:
> - **[[92134]]** — Scanning computerized ophthalmic diagnostic imaging, posterior segment, with interpretation and report, unilateral or bilateral — remains the primary code for macular OCT in wet AMD monitoring
> - **New for 2025**: Additional OCT-A (optical coherence tomography angiography) code clarifications; confirm your payer's current LCD for OCT-A coverage under **[[H35.3222]]**
> - Bilateral OCT remains billable as a single [[92134]] code with documentation of both eyes — payer policies on billing one vs. two units for bilateral OCT have been updated; verify your MAC's current guidance
>
> Always check the current Retina Today OCT coding updates (published annually) for the most current [[92134]] billing guidance, as this is one of the most frequently revised outpatient retina billing areas.

Always reassess VA at each encounter. If VA remains ≤20/200, continue coding [[H54.8]] alongside **[[H35.3222]]**. Do not automatically drop vision impairment codes simply because the CNV is now inactive.

This is expected for PRN protocol and will not trigger a denial — it reflects appropriate clinical decision-making: observed, no fluid, no injection needed today.

## 💊 Coding Scenarios

---

### Scenario 1 — Established T&E Patient, Dry at 10-Week Visit (Outpatient)

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 77-year-old female with wet AMD left eye on Vabysmo T&E protocol. Last visit: 8 weeks ago — fluid resolved, extended to 10-week interval. Today at 10 weeks: OCT left eye — completely dry. No SRF, no IRF. CRT 235μm (stable). VA: 20/40 OS. Physician documents: "Wet AMD left eye, inactive — macula dry on OCT — excellent response — Vabysmo injection given today — extending to 12 weeks." Vabysmo 6mg administered.

**CPT / HCPCS:**
- [[92014]] — Comprehensive ophthalmological exam, established
- [[92134]] — OCT posterior segment *(dry macula confirmed — required at every injection visit)*
- [[67028]]-LT — Intravitreal injection, left eye
- J2777-LT-JZ × 60 units — Vabysmo 6mg, no waste

**ICD-10-CM:**
- **[[H35.3222]]** — Exudative AMD, left eye, inactive CNV *(OCT dry — CNV currently quiescent — T&E injection to maintain suppression)*

> [!success] This Is the Classic **[[H35.3222]]** T&E Scenario
> This scenario repeats every 8-16 weeks throughout the patient's lifetime of wet AMD management. The code is **[[H35.3222]]** at every dry-macula T&E visit, regardless of how many years the patient has been on treatment or how many injections they have received.

---

### Scenario 2 — PRN Protocol, Bilateral Wet AMD — Different Activity Status Each Eye (Outpatient)

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 74-year-old male with bilateral wet AMD on PRN monitoring. Today: OCT right eye — subretinal fluid present, new — physician injects right eye. OCT left eye — completely dry, unchanged from last visit — no injection left eye. VA: 20/80 OD (decreased), 20/35 OS.

**CPT / HCPCS:**
- [[67028]]-RT — Intravitreal injection, right eye only
- J0178-RT-JZ × 1 — Eylea 2mg, right eye, no waste
- [[92134]] — OCT, bilateral *(both eyes documented)*
- [[92014]] — Comprehensive exam, established

**ICD-10-CM:**
- [[H35.3211]] — Exudative AMD, right eye, active CNV *(new SRF today — active at this visit)*
- **[[H35.3222]]** — Exudative AMD, left eye, inactive CNV *(dry on OCT — no injection left eye today)*

> [!tip] Asymmetric Activity Status — Separate Codes, One Visit
> This is a perfect example of why bilateral wet AMD codes (H35.3231/H35.3232) are not always appropriate — the eyes are at **different activity stages on the same day**. Right eye is active ([[H35.3211]]) requiring injection; left eye is inactive (**[[H35.3222]]**) requiring only monitoring. Two separate laterality-specific codes correctly represent the clinical picture. The claim shows injection CPT + J-code for right eye only, with both AMD codes as diagnoses.

---

### Scenario 3 — H35.3222 Becomes H35.3223 — Scar Formation (Outpatient)

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 86-year-old male with longstanding wet AMD left eye — on quarterly Vabysmo T&E. Today: OCT left eye — no fluid — but new extensive **subretinal fibrosis** replacing the prior CNV membrane location — physician documents: "Disciform scar developing, left eye — inactive wet AMD progressing to scar formation — transitioning off anti-VEGF — low vision referral." No injection today.

**ICD-10-CM — Code Change:**
- [[H35.3223]] — Exudative AMD, left eye, **inactive scar** *(scar now documented → retire **[[H35.3222]]** for this eye; [[H35.3223]] is the new permanent code)*
- [[H54.8]] — Legal blindness *(if VA ≤20/200 — common at disciform scar stage)*

> [!important] **[[H35.3222]]** Retires When Scar Forms — Assign H35.3223 Going Forward
> Once the physician documents **disciform scar** or **subretinal fibrosis** replacing the CNV membrane, transition from **[[H35.3222]]** to [[H35.3223]] permanently for that eye. The two codes are not interchangeable — **[[H35.3222]]** implies active CNV that is controllable with anti-VEGF; [[H35.3223]] implies the CNV has burned out and been replaced by scar. Do NOT continue coding **[[H35.3222]]** after scar is documented, as this would misrepresent the clinical end-stage status of the eye.

---

### Scenario 4 — H35.3222 in the Inpatient Setting (Inpatient Admission)

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 79-year-old female admitted for hip fracture. H&P documents wet AMD, left eye, on monthly Eylea — "currently controlled, last injection 3 weeks ago — scheduled for injection in 1 week at outpatient retina practice." OCT not performed during admission. VA OS: 20/50.

**Additional Diagnosis:**
- **[[H35.3222]]** — Exudative AMD, left eye, inactive CNV *(physician documents "currently controlled" — inactive status at admission — meets UHDDS criteria as active chronic condition affecting discharge planning and ophthalmology follow-up coordination)*

> [!note] "Currently Controlled" = Inactive CNV = **[[H35.3222]]**
> When the H&P or attending note documents wet AMD as "currently controlled," "on maintenance injections," or "stable on anti-VEGF," this language supports **[[H35.3222]]** (inactive CNV) — not [[H35.3221]] (active CNV). The inpatient coding goal is to capture the current active status of the condition as documented at admission. "Controlled" and "stable" = inactive. If the note says "active" or OCT shows fluid → [[H35.3221]].

## ⚠️ Coding Pitfalls and Tips

| | Pitfall or Tip |
|---|---|
| ❌ | **Never carry [[H35.3222]] forward when current OCT shows fluid** — any fluid = [[H35.3221]] active; check every visit's OCT before assigning |
| ❌ | **Never assume [[H35.3222]] means no injection should be billed** — T&E injections at dry-macula visits are fully billable and medically necessary [web:109] |
| ❌ | **Never use [[H35.3222]] when scar is documented** — scar = [[H35.3223]]; they are not interchangeable |
| ❌ | **Never use bilateral code [[H35.3232]]** when the two eyes have different activity stages — separate laterality codes required |
| ❌ | **Never drop vision impairment codes** ([[H54.8]], [[H54.12x]]) just because CNV is now inactive — prior damage may persist |
| ✅ | **Read the OCT every visit** — dry macula = **[[H35.3222]]**; any fluid = [[H35.3221]]; scar = [[H35.3223]] |
| ✅ | **Document T&E rationale at every inactive injection visit** — "injecting to maintain T&E interval and prevent reactivation" is the medical necessity anchor |
| ✅ | **"Currently controlled" / "stable" / "quiescent" language in H&P** → **[[H35.3222]]** in inpatient setting |
