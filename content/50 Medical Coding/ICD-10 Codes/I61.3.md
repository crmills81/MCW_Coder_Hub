# 🧬 ICD-10 CM I61.3 — Nontraumatic Intracerebral Hemorrhage in Brain Stem

> [!success] Billable Code Confirmed  
> **ICD-10-CM [[I61.3]]** is a **4-character code** (**letter I, category 61, subclassification 3**) and is the **[[terminal]]**, fully specified code for a **nontraumatic bleed located in the midbrain, pons, or medulla**.<sup>1,2</sup> No additional characters are required, so it is valid for claim submission as a principal or secondary diagnosis.

> [!warning] Non-Billable Parent Codes  
> **I61** (**Nontraumatic intracerebral hemorrhage**) is a category header and cannot be reported because it lacks the site specificity required. **I60-I69** is the **cerebrovascular disease block** and is never reportable as a code.

> [!important] Clinical Context  
> Selection of **[[I61.3]]** is driven by documented **brainstem** location of a **nontraumatic parenchymal bleed**.<sup>5</sup> Provider documentation of "**pontine**," "**midbrain**," or "**medullary**" **[[hemorrhage]]** all index to this code.

> [!danger] Code Classification  
> This is an **ICD-10-CM diagnosis code** used for **inpatient facility** and professional fee reporting. It is not a **procedure code**, so procedures performed for the **hemorrhage** are reported with **CPT** (**professional**) or **ICD-10-PCS** (**facility**).

---

## 🔍 Code Description  

**ICD-10-CM [[I61.3]]** reports an **acute, spontaneous (nontraumatic) hemorrhage within the substance of the brainstem, most often the pons**.<sup>2,5</sup> It is distinguished from other site-specific **intracerebral hemorrhage codes** such as **[[I61.0]]** (**subcortical hemisphere**), **[[I61.1]]** (**cortical hemisphere**), and **[[I61.4]]** (**cerebellum**), and it should not be confused with **[[I63.9]]** (**[[cerebral infarction]]**), which is **[[-sche-|Ischemic]]** rather than **hemorrhagic**.

**Brainstem hemorrhage** carries high morbidity because the brainstem houses **consciousness, cranial nerve, [[cardiorespiratory]], and motor pathway centers**.<sup>5</sup> **Hypertensive small-vessel disease** is the leading cause, with **[[vascular]]** **malformations and anticoagulant-associated bleeding** as other recognized causes. When **ventricular extension** is documented, **[[I61.5]]** may be reported in addition to this code, which should be confirmed against current **Coding Clinic guidance.**

> [!example] Site Specificity Matters  
> Choosing **[[I61.3]]** over **[[I61.9]]** requires documentation of the **bleed location**, typically from **CT** or **MRI reports** cited by the treating **physician**. Unspecified codes weaken severity capture and invite payer or audit queries.

> [!tip] 
> **Coders** may not assign the site from imaging alone, so the attending or **neurosurgeon** must document the **brainstem** location (**pons, midbrain, medulla**) in the record.<sup>1</sup>

## ✅ Includes  
- **Pontine hemorrhage** — spontaneous bleed in the pons, indexing directly to this code.
- **Midbrain and medullary hemorrhage** — spontaneous bleeding in these brainstem segments, reported with **[[I61.3]]** when nontraumatic.
- **Primary brainstem hemorrhage due to hypertensive [[vasculopathy]]** — reported with **[[I61.3]]** plus the hypertension code (I10-I16 range) as documented.

---

## ❌ Excludes  

### Excludes 1  
- **I69.1-** (**Sequelae of nontraumatic intracerebral hemorrhage**) — reported after the acute episode for **residual deficits** such as **[[hemiplegia]]** or **[[dysphagia]]**, and never together with the acute code for the same encounter.

> [!danger] 
> The most common **Excludes 1 error** is continuing to report **[[I61.3]]** on **[[rehabilitation]]** or **outpatient follow-up claims** after acute discharge. Use the **I69.1-** sequela codes there instead.<sup>1</sup>

### Excludes 2  
- **S06.-** (**Traumatic intracranial injury**) — a separate traumatic event. **Both** can be reported **only when the record clearly documents two distinct causes**, which is clinically uncommon for the same site, so query when documentation is unclear.

## 📋 Clinical Overview  

### Brainstem versus Other Intracerebral Locations  
Site drives code **selection, clinical presentation, and prognosis**. The table below contrasts **[[I61.3]]** with two common siblings.<sup>5</sup>

| **Feature** | **[[I61.3]]** | **Related [[I61.4]]** | **Related [[I61.0]]** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Location** | Midbrain, pons, or medulla. | Cerebellar hemisphere or vermis. | Deep hemispheric white matter and basal ganglia region. |
| **Typical presentation** | Rapid decline in consciousness, cranial nerve and ocular motor findings, and quadriparesis. | Headache, vomiting, ataxia, and risk of rapid brainstem compression. | Contralateral weakness, sensory loss, and speech or visual field deficits. |
| **Key complications** | Respiratory failure, dysphagia, and obstructive hydrocephalus. | Obstructive hydrocephalus and brainstem compression requiring surgical decompression. | Mass effect, edema, and intraventricular extension. |

> [!important] 
> A **CDI query** is warranted when the record lists "**brainstem bleed**" without a documented **[[hemorrhage]]** etiology or when **[[edema]]**, **[[compression]]**, or **[[hydrocephalus]]** findings on imaging are not addressed in provider notes.

### Manifestations & Symptom Burden  
- **Depressed level of consciousness or [[coma]]** — often the dominant finding and may be quantified with the **[[Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS)]]**.
- **Cranial nerve and ocular motor deficits** — such as **gaze palsies** and pupillary abnormalities.
- **[[Quadriparesis]] or [[hemiparesis]]** — motor pathway involvement.
- **[[Dysphagia]] and airway compromise** — frequent drivers of [[intubation]] and feeding tube decisions.
- **Autonomic instability** — including temperature, blood pressure, and respiratory pattern abnormalities.

> [!tip] 
> Report documented deficits with their own codes (**for example G81.9- [[hemiplegia]], [[R13.10]] [[dysphagia]] unspecified, and [[G46.3]] brain stem stroke syndrome when documented**), and use the **Glasgow Coma Scale codes** (**R40.2-**) as guidelines allow, since they are s**eventh-character dependent.**<sup>1</sup>

## 💰 HCC Risk Adjustment  

| **Item** | **Detail** |
|---|---|
| **Model** | CMS-HCC V28 |
| **HCC** | HCC 248 — Intracranial Hemorrhage |
| **Mapping status** | **[[I61.3]]** is a payment HCC |
| **Capture setting** | Acute inpatient stay or qualifying face-to-face encounter during the acute episode |
| **Annual recapture** | Acute code should not be reported after discharge from acute care, so recapture relies on sequela or status coding as documented |
| **Verification** | Confirm against the current CMS mapping table for your payment year |

**Acute hemorrhage** is generally captured in the hospital setting, so the **main risk adjustment** **errors** are carrying the acute code forward in outpatient follow-up and under-documenting residual deficits that qualify for separate **HCC mapping**.<sup>4</sup> Payers expect **[[MEAT]]**-supported documentation, so coders should confirm that evaluation and treatment are clearly stated in provider notes.

Sequence **[[I61.3]]** first when the **[[hemorrhage]]** is the condition established after study as chiefly responsible for admission.<sup>1,3</sup> Surgical cases such as **[[craniotomy]]** for **[[hematoma]]** **[[evacuation]]** may group to **surgical MDC 01 DRGs** instead, so verify with your grouper. Related secondary conditions such as **[[G93.5]]** (**[[compression]] of brain**) and **[[G93.6]]** (**cerebral [[edema]]**) may raise severity but can be excluded by **CC Exclusion logic**, so confirm in the **CMS Definitions Manual**.

**NCD/LCD information:**
- No **NCD** or **LCD** is specific to the **[[I61.3]]** diagnosis itself.
- **NCD 220.1** (**Computed Tomography**) and **NCD 220.2** (**Magnetic Resonance Imaging**) govern imaging used to diagnose and monitor **[[hemorrhage]]**.<sup>6</sup>
- Check the **Noridian JE/JF LCDs** in the **CMS Medicare Coverage Database** for any **imaging**, **[[neurophysiology]]**, or **[[rehabilitation]] policy** that lists **[[I61.3]]** as a covered diagnosis.<sup>6</sup>

## 💊 Coding Scenarios and Examples  

<br>

### Example 1
> **Clinical Scenario:**  
> A 68-year-old with longstanding hypertension presents with acute decreased consciousness. CT shows a pontine hemorrhage and the neurointensivist documents hypertensive pontine hemorrhage. The intensivist provides 52 minutes of critical care on the day of admission.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[99291]] | Critical care time of 30-74 minutes is documented for a critically ill patient. |
| PDx | [[I61.3]] | Nontraumatic hemorrhage in the pons is the reason for care. |
| SDx | [[I10]] | Hypertension is coded separately because ICD-10-CM does not presume a link between hypertension and cerebrovascular disease. |

> [!tip] 
> Sequence [[I61.3]] first as the focus of care.
> A CDI query is appropriate if the provider suspects a hypertensive emergency but does not document it.

### Example 2
> **Clinical Scenario:**  
> A patient with a brainstem hemorrhage develops obstructive hydrocephalus. Neurosurgery places an external ventricular drain at the bedside via twist drill hole. A separately identifiable E/M is also documented on the same day.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[61107]] | Twist drill placement of a ventricular catheter for drainage. |
| CPT 2 | [[99223]] [[-25]] | Significant, separately identifiable E/M on the same day as a minor procedure. |
| PDx | [[I61.3]] | The hemorrhage is the underlying reason for the encounter. |
| SDx | [[G91.1]] | Obstructive hydrocephalus is documented as a complication of the bleed. |

> [!tip] 
> Verify the global period for [[61107]] in the CMS PFS before choosing between modifier [[-25]] and [[-57]].
> A CDI query is appropriate if hydrocephalus is seen on imaging but not named by the provider.

### Example 3
> **Clinical Scenario:**  
> A patient on warfarin arrives in the ED with sudden collapse. A non-contrast head CT shows an acute brainstem hemorrhage. The radiologist dictates the interpretation, and the attending documents a warfarin-associated brainstem hemorrhage with coagulopathy.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[70450]] [[-26]] | Professional interpretation of the non-contrast head CT. |
| PDx | [[I61.3]] | The hemorrhage is located in the brainstem. |
| SDx | [[D68.32]] | Coagulopathy due to the anticoagulant is documented by the attending. |
| SDx | [[T45.515A]] | Adverse effect of the anticoagulant, initial encounter. |

> [!tip] 
> Add [[Z79.01]] for long-term anticoagulant use when documented.
> Confirm that the provider documented the causal relationship before assigning adverse effect codes.

## ⚠️ Coding Pitfalls and Tips  

- **Pitfall 1**: Reporting **[[I61.3]]** after discharge from acute care for **[[rehabilitation]]** or follow-up; **Tips**: Switch to the **I69.1-** **[[sequela codes]]** for residual **deficits**, or **[[Z86.79]]** when there are no residuals.
- **Pitfall 2**: Assuming **[[hypertension]]** caused the bleed and omitting a separate **[[hypertension]]** code; **Tips**: Report **[[I10]]** (**or the appropriate I10-I16 code**) in addition to **[[I61.3]]**, since the guideline does not presume that link for **cerebrovascular disease**.
- **Pitfall 3**: Pairing **NIHSS codes** (**R29.7-**) with **[[I61.3]]**; **Tips**: The **Tabular** instructs that **R29.7-** is used with **[[cerebral infarction]]** (**[[I63.9]]** family), so for **[[hemorrhage]]** use the **Glasgow Coma Scale codes** (**R40.2-**) if documented.<sup>1,2</sup>
- **Pitfall 4**: Selecting **[[I61.9]]** when the **brainstem** location is documented; **Tips**: Read the provider note and imaging impression for site language and assign **[[I61.3]]**.
- **Pitfall 5**: Missing documented **intraventricular extension**; **Tips**: Review for **extension** and, when documented, consider adding **[[I61.5]]**, confirming current **Coding Clinic guidance**.
- **Pitfall 6**: Confusing **hemorrhagic transformation** of an **[[infarct]]** with a primary **hemorrhage**; **Tips**: Query the provider, since **hemorrhagic conversion** of an **ischemic stroke** is classified differently from a primary bleed such as **[[I61.3]]**.
