# 🧬 ICD-10 CM G35 — Multiple Sclerosis (FY2026 Expansion)

> [!warning] Non-Billable Parent Code (FY2026 Update)
>
> As of October 1, 2025 (FY2026), **G35** is a **non-billable category header**<sup></sup>. The ICD-10-CM code set was significantly expanded to capture the specific clinical course and activity status of Multiple Sclerosis. You must code to the highest level of specificity using the new subcodes to avoid claim denials.

> [!success] Billable Subcodes Confirmed
>
> Always submit a valid 4- or 5-character subcode, such as **[[G35.A]]** (Relapsing-remitting) or **[[G35.B1]]** (Active primary progressive), to ensure claim acceptance and proper HCC capture<sup></sup>.

> [!important] Clinical Context: Active vs. Non-Active Disease
>
> The new FY2026 subcodes for progressive forms of MS distinguish between **Active** and **Non-Active** disease<sup></sup>. "Active" indicates ongoing disease activity requiring monitoring or intervention, such as clinical relapses within the past year, new/enlarging lesions on MRI, or gadolinium-enhancing lesions.

## 🔍 Code Description

**ICD-10 CM [[G35]]** is the parent category for **Multiple Sclerosis (MS)**, an [[autoimmune]] demyelinating disease of the central nervous system (CNS)<sup></sup>. During the disease process, the immune system inappropriately attacks the [[myelin sheath]]—the protective lipid and protein layer surrounding nerve fibers in the brain, optic nerves, and spinal cord<sup></sup>. This [[demyelination]] disrupts electrical signal transmission, resulting in a wide array of physical, visual, and cognitive impairments.

Historically, all forms of MS were captured under the single code **G35**. However, the disease presents in several distinct clinical courses, which are now formally represented in the FY2026 code set<sup></sup>:

1. **Relapsing-Remitting MS (RRMS):** The most common initial presentation (~85% of cases), characterized by clear acute exacerbations (**relapses**) followed by periods of partial or complete recovery (**remissions**)<sup></sup>. 
2. **Primary Progressive MS (PPMS):** Gradual, continuous neurologic deterioration from symptom onset without distinct relapses<sup></sup>.
3. **Secondary Progressive MS (SPMS):** Begins initially as an RRMS course, but progressively shifts into a steady decline of neurologic function, with or without acute relapses<sup></sup>.

```text
 │
 ├── G35.C Secondary progressive multiple sclerosis ❌ Non-billable Header
 │    ├── G35.C0 …… unspecified ✅ Billable
 │    ├── G35.C1 Active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis ✅ Billable
 │    └── G35.C2 Non-active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis ✅ Billable
 │
 └── G35.D Multiple sclerosis, unspecified ✅ Billable
````

> [!tip] Specificity is Mandatory
> 
> **[[G35.D]]** (**Multiple sclerosis**, unspecified) should only be used when the provider has explicitly stated that the type of MS cannot be determined or is not documented in the medical record. Always query the neurologist or review consultation notes to upgrade to an A, B, or C subcode.

## ❌ Excludes

### Excludes 1 — Cannot Be Coded Simultaneously

The following conditions represent distinct pathological entities and cannot be coded alongside **G35** unless independently diagnosed as a separate condition:

|**Code**|**Description**|**Note**|
|---|---|---|
|[[G36.0]]|Neuromyelitis optica [Devic]|A separate autoimmune demyelinating disease primarily affecting the optic nerves and spinal cord (often associated with aquaporin-4 antibodies).|
|[[G36.1]]|Acute and subacute hemorrhagic [[leukoencephalitis]] [Hurst]|A rare, rapid, and severe demyelinating disease.|
|[[G37.0]]|Diffuse sclerosis of central nervous system|Schilder's disease; distinct from classic multiple sclerosis.|
|[[G37.3]]|Acute transverse [[Myelitis]]|When presenting as an isolated demyelinating event of the spinal cord, without fulfilling McDonald criteria for MS.|

## 📋 Clinical Overview

### Pathophysiology

**[[Multiple Sclerosis]]** is a T-cell mediated [[autoimmune]] disorder resulting in focal areas of myelin destruction (**plaques**) primarily in the white matter of the CNS. While remyelination can occur early in the disease, chronic inflammation leads to irreversible axonal damage and [[neurodegeneration]], causing progressive disability.

### Classic Symptoms & Manifestations

Because plaques can form anywhere in the CNS, MS is notoriously heterogeneous in presentation. Common symptoms include:

|**Symptom Category**|**Clinical Presentation**|**Common Associated Codes**|
|---|---|---|
|**Visual Disturbances**|Optic neuritis (often the first symptom, presenting as unilateral vision loss with pain on eye movement), diplopia, internuclear ophthalmoplegia.|[[H46.9]], [[H53.2]]|
|**Sensory Issues**|Numbness, paresthesias (tingling, "pins and needles"), Lhermitte's sign (electric shock sensation down the spine upon neck flexion).|[[R20.2]], [[R20.0]]|
|**Motor Dysfunction**|Muscle weakness, [[spasticity]], [[hyperreflexia]], gait abnormalities, [[monoplegia]], or [[paraplegia]].|[[R26.89]], [[G82.20]]|
|**Cerebellar / Brainstem**|[[Ataxia]], severe intention tremor, [[dysarthria]], [[vertigo]].|[[R27.0]], [[R47.1]]|
|**Autonomic / Sphincter**|Neurogenic bladder (retention or incontinence), bowel dysfunction, sexual dysfunction.|[[N31.9]], [[R32]]|

### Diagnostic Criteria (McDonald Criteria)

Diagnosis requires demonstrating **"dissemination in space" (DIS)** (lesions in multiple distinct areas of the CNS) and **"dissemination in time" (DIT)** (lesions occurring at different times), ruling out other causes.

- **MRI:** The gold standard. Shows hyperintense white matter lesions on T2/FLAIR and gadolinium-enhancing lesions on T1 (**indicating active inflammation**).
    
- **Lumbar Puncture (CSF analysis):** Often reveals oligoclonal bands (OCBs) indicating intrathecal IgG synthesis.
    
- **Optical Coherence Tomography (OCT):** Detects retinal nerve fiber layer thinning.

## 🛠️ CPT Procedural Crosswalk

While there are no direct surgical repair codes for MS, patients undergo extensive diagnostic testing and pharmacological management.

### Diagnostics & Evaluation

|**CPT Code**|**Description**|**wRVU (Est.)**|
|---|---|---|
|[[99214]]|E/M established patient, moderate complexity (typical for chronic MS management)|1.92|
|[[99285]]|Emergency Department visit, high complexity (e.g., severe acute exacerbation)|3.80|
|[[70553]]|MRI Brain with and without contrast (crucial for evaluating active lesions)|2.50 (PC)|
|[[62270]]|Spinal puncture, lumbar, diagnostic (CSF analysis for oligoclonal bands)|1.60|
|[[92134]]|Optical coherence tomography (OCT) of optic nerve|0.85|
|[[95904]]|Nerve conduction studies (rarely primary, but used for differentials)|Variable|

> [!note] Modifier Usage
> 
> When billing diagnostic imaging like an MRI ([[70553]]) in a facility setting, ensure the professional component is billed with modifier **[[-26]]** if the provider is only interpreting the results. When performing a distinct E/M alongside a minor procedure (**like a lumbar puncture**), append modifier **[[-25]]** to the E/M code.

## 💊 Coding Scenarios and Examples

### Scenario 1 — Outpatient Follow-up for Stable RRMS

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 32-year-old female presents to the neurology clinic for her bi-annual MS check-up. She was diagnosed 3 years ago and is currently on Ocrelizumab infusions. She reports no new symptoms, and her recent MRI shows no new or enhancing lesions. Her neurological exam is stable with mild residual right leg weakness.

**CPT:**

- [[99214]] — Level 4 established patient E/M
    

**ICD-10-CM:**

- [[G35.A]] — Relapsing-remitting multiple sclerosis _(FY2026 billable code for RRMS)_
    
- [[Z79.899]] — Other long term (current) drug therapy _(Capturing the disease-modifying therapy)_
    

---

### Scenario 2 — Inpatient Admission for SPMS with Acute Progression

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 55-year-old male with a 15-year history of MS is admitted after a rapid decline in mobility. He transitioned to a secondary progressive course two years ago. An inpatient MRI with contrast reveals two new gadolinium-enhancing lesions in the cervical spine. He is started on a 5-day course of high-dose IV methylprednisolone.

**CPT:**

- [[99223]] — Initial hospital care, high complexity
    

**ICD-10-CM:**

- [[G35.C1]] — Active secondary progressive multiple sclerosis _(FY2026 subcode representing SPMS with evidence of new inflammatory activity on MRI)_
    
- [[R26.2]] — Difficulty in walking, not elsewhere classified
    

---

### Scenario 3 — Emergency Department Visit for Optic Neuritis (Initial Presentation)

> **Clinical Vignette:** A 28-year-old female presents to the ED with acute right eye pain on movement and blurry "foggy" vision. An urgent neurology consult is called. Based on history, exam, and an urgent MRI brain showing a demyelinating lesion on the right optic nerve, the preliminary diagnosis is [[Multiple Sclerosis]], type unspecified pending further workup.

**CPT:**

- [[99284]] — Emergency department visit, moderate-high complexity
    
- [[70553]][[-26]] — MRI Brain w/ & w/o contrast, professional component
    

**ICD-10-CM:**

- [[G35.D]] — Multiple sclerosis, unspecified _(Used because the exact subtype [RRMS vs PPMS] cannot yet be definitively established at the initial acute presentation)_
    
- [[H46.9]] — Unspecified optic neuritis _(Appropriate to code the acute manifestation driving the ED visit)_

## ⚠️ Coding Pitfalls and Tips

||**Pitfall or Tip**|
|---|---|
|❌|**Do NOT use the parent code G35** for encounters on or after October 1, 2025. It will result in a claim denial. You must use a 4- or 5-character subcode (e.g., [[G35.A]], [[G35.B0]]).|
|❌|**Do NOT use an "Active" subcode (e.g., [[G35.C1]]) without clinical evidence.** Documentation must explicitly mention recent relapses, new MRI lesions, or active progression requiring intervention.|
|✅|**Review historical documentation.** If a patient's chart just says "MS," look back at previous neurology consults. Often, the neurologist has documented "RRMS" or "SPMS," allowing you to upgrade from [[G35.D]] to [[G35.A]] or [[G35.C0]].|
|✅|**Code Manifestations.** MS is systemic. Code the secondary conditions resulting from the disease, such as [[neurogenic bladder]], depression, or spasticity, to accurately reflect the patient's complexity and medical decision-making.|
|✅|**Documentation is Key.** Providers should be educated on the FY2026 changes to ensure they are dictating the specific course (**Relapsing-Remitting, Primary Progressive, Secondary Progressive**) and activity status (**Active, Non-Active**) in their assessment and plan.|
