# 🧬 ICD-10 CM I85.10 — Secondary Esophageal Varices Without Bleeding

> [!success] Billable Code Confirmed
> **ICD-10 CM [[I85.10]]** is a **5-character code**: category **I85** (**esophageal varices**), subcategory **I85.1** (**secondary**), and a fifth character **0** for "**without bleeding**." No further characters exist beneath it, so it is a valid, billable diagnosis code for **FY2026**.¹

> [!warning] Non-Billable Parent Codes
> **I85** (**Esophageal varices**) is a category header and cannot be reported on a claim. **I85.1** (**Secondary esophageal varices**) is a subcategory that still needs the fifth character for bleeding status.¹

> [!important] Clinical Context
> The "**secondary**" varices codes are used when the provider documents varices due to another disease, such as **[[cirrhosis]]**, **portal [[hypertension]]**, **alcoholic or toxic liver disease, or [[schistosomiasis]]**. The fifth character reflects whether there is current bleeding.²

> [!danger] Code Classification
> This is an **ICD-10-CM diagnosis code**. It is not a procedure code. It has a "**code first underlying disease**" instruction, so it is not reported as the principal or first-listed diagnosis.²

---

## 🔍 Code Description

**ICD-10 CM [[I85.10]]** describes **dilated esophageal veins that develop because of another condition, most often portal hypertension from liver disease**, and that are not bleeding at the time of the encounter. The **Alphabetic Index** directs **varix, esophagus**, "**in (due to)**" **[[cirrhosis]]** of liver, **portal hypertension**, **alcoholic liver disease**, **[[schistosomiasis]]**, and toxic liver disease to this code.² Underlying causes are coded first, for example **[[K74.60]]** (unspecified cirrhosis of liver), **[[K70.30]]** (**alcoholic cirrhosis of liver without ascites**), or **[[K76.6]]** (**portal hypertension**).

The code applies only when there is no current hemorrhage. If the provider documents active or recent variceal bleeding at the encounter, the correct code is **[[I85.11]]**. The category also carries a "**use additional code to identify alcohol abuse and dependence (F10.-)**" note, so alcohol use disorder documentation should be captured with a billable code such as **[[F10.20]]** when supported.²

> [!example] Bleeding Status Drives the Code
> The **fifth character** separates **[[I85.10]]** from **[[I85.11]]**. Bleeding status can change the **DRG family** and the **severity picture**, so the provider's documentation of current hemorrhage matters.³

> [!tip]
> **Esophageal varices** without a documented cause go to **[[I85.00]]**. Esophageal varices with a documented cause go to **[[I85.10]]**. Check the provider's wording before choosing.²

## ✅ Includes

- **Esophageal varices secondary to alcoholic liver disease**. Report the alcoholic liver disease first, for example **[[K70.30]]**.²
- **Esophageal varices secondary to cirrhosis of liver**. Report the cirrhosis first, for example **[[K74.60]]**.²
- **Esophageal varices secondary to [[schistosomiasis]]**. Report the schistosomiasis first, for example **[[B65.1]]** when documented as intestinal *S. mansoni*.²
- **Esophageal varices secondary to toxic liver disease.** Report the toxic liver disease first, for example **[[K71.7]]** when fibrosis and cirrhosis are documented.²

## ❌ Excludes

### Excludes 1
No **Excludes1 note** is listed at **I85.10, I85.1, or I85.** Even so, **[[I85.00]]** (**primary**) and **[[I85.10]]** (**secondary**) should not be assigned together for the same varices, because they describe different etiologies.²

> [!danger]
> The common **error** is reporting **[[I85.00]]** and **[[I85.10]]** together, or choosing **[[I85.00]]** when the provider documented **cirrhosis** or **portal hypertension** as the cause. Another error is sequencing **[[I85.10]]** first instead of the underlying disease.

### Excludes 2
The **I00-I99 chapter-level Excludes2 note lists**, among others, certain **infectious and parasitic diseases (A00-B99), neoplasms (C00-D49), endocrine and metabolic diseases (E00-E88)**, and **symptoms and signs (R00-R94)**.² An **Excludes2 note** means both conditions can be reported together when both are documented. For example, the varices and a documented **[[neoplasm]]** of the liver can both be coded.

## 📋 Clinical Overview

### Primary vs Secondary vs Bleeding Status
**Esophageal varices** are coded by etiology (**primary or secondary**) and by bleeding status. The table shows how **[[I85.10]]** compares with its closest neighbors.²

| **Feature** | **[[I85.10]]** | **Related [[I85.11]]** | **Related [[I85.00]]** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Etiology** | Secondary — due to another documented disease. | Secondary — due to another documented disease. | Primary/idiopathic or no cause documented. |
| **Bleeding** | No current bleeding. | Current bleeding documented. | No current bleeding. |
| **PDx eligibility** | Not reportable as PDx (code first underlying disease). | Listed as a PDx in DRG 368-370.³ | Listed as a PDx in DRG 368-370.³ |

> [!important]
> A **CDI trigger** is "**[[varices]]**" documented with **[[cirrhosis]]** or **portal [[hypertension]]** but with no linking statement. Query the provider to document the relationship so the secondary code and the underlying disease are both supported.²

### Manifestations & Symptom Burden
- **Asymptomatic varices** are often found on screening **EGD** in patients with **[[cirrhosis]]**.⁶
- Underlying **portal [[hypertension]]** may show as **[[ascites]]**, **[[splenomegaly]]**, or **[[thrombocytopenia]]**.
- Risk of future **[[hemorrhage]]** is the main clinical concern even when there is no current bleeding, which is why surveillance and **[[prophylaxis]]** are common.⁶
- **Hepatic [[encephalopathy]]** or **[[jaundice]]** from the underlying liver disease may be present.

> [!tip]
> **Code** the **underlying disease** and **any documented manifestations separately**. The varices code does not capture **[[ascites]]**, **[[encephalopathy]]**, or **alcohol use disorder**.²

| **Role**                                     | **MS-DRG**                                          | **Notes**                                                                             |
| ---------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **PDx family (does not include [[I85.10]])** | **368** — Major Esophageal Disorders with MCC       | PDx list includes [[I85.00]], [[I85.01]], [[I85.11]], [[B37.81]] among others.³                   |
| **PDx family (does not include [[I85.10]])** | **369** — Major Esophageal Disorders with CC        | Same PDx list.³                                                                   |
| **PDx family (does not include [[I85.10]])** | **370** — Major Esophageal Disorders without CC/MCC | Same PDx list.³                                                                   |
| **[[I85.10]] as secondary diagnosis**        | Depends on the principal diagnosis              | Confirm CC/MCC status in CMS Appendix C (v43, FY2026) before assuming an impact.³ |

**Sequence** the underlying disease first, then **[[I85.10]]**. A secondary diagnosis cannot act as a **CC/MCC** if it falls under a **CC exclusion** for the principal diagnosis, so check the **PDX exclusion list** for the case.³

**NCD/LCD information:**
- **NCD 100.10** (**Injection Sclerotherapy for Esophageal Variceal Bleeding**) addresses sclerotherapy and banding for variceal bleeding.⁵ A without-bleeding code alone may not satisfy that policy, so read the NCD criteria before billing.
- **LCD L33583** (**Diagnostic and Therapeutic [[Esophagogastroduodenoscopy]]**) describes EGD indications and says esophageal varices may be injected with sclerosing solutions.⁴ **LCD L35350** (**Upper Gastrointestinal [[Endoscopy]]**) covers EGD indications and cross-references the sclerotherapy NCD.⁵ Confirm which LCD your MAC uses.
- **Commercial example**: **Anthem CG-MED-59** (2026) covers endoscopic variceal ligation for esophageal varices and says ligation may be repeated every 1 to 8 weeks until varices are eradicated.⁶

## 🛠️ Commonly Associated CPT Codes

- **[[43235]]** — Diagnostic EGD, including collection of specimens by brushing or washing when performed. It is bundled into therapeutic EGD codes in the same session.
- **[[43243]]** — EGD with injection **[[sclerosis]]** of esophageal and/or gastric varices. Check the NCD and LCD criteria before billing for non-bleeding varices.⁴⁵
- **[[43244]]** — EGD with band ligation of esophageal and/or gastric varices. This is the usual prophylactic banding code and fits repeated sessions.⁶
- **[[43204]]** — Esophagoscopy with injection sclerosis of esophageal varices. Use it only when a standalone esophagoscopy is performed, not with an EGD.
- **[[43205]]** — Esophagoscopy with band ligation of esophageal varices. The same standalone rule applies.
- **[[37182]]** — Insertion of transvenous intrahepatic portosystemic shunt(s) (TIPS). It is used for refractory portal hypertension complications and includes extensive associated imaging and access work.

### NCCI Bundling Considerations
Diagnostic EGD **[[43235]]** is included in **therapeutic EGD codes** such as **[[43243]]** and **[[43244]]** when done in the same session. Injection sclerosis and band ligation in the same session are usually edit pairs, so check the **current NCCI PTP table** before using **[[-59]]** and document separate varices or sites. Standalone **[[esophagoscopy]]** codes (**[[43204]]**, **[[43205]]**) are not reported with **EGD**.

## 💊 Coding Scenarios and Examples

<br>

### Example 1
> **Clinical Scenario:**
> A patient with alcoholic cirrhosis without ascites and documented alcohol dependence is admitted for surveillance. The gastroenterologist performs EGD with prophylactic band ligation of non-bleeding esophageal varices secondary to the cirrhosis.

| Field        | Code           | Rationale                                                              |
| ------------ | -------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| CPT          | [[43244]]  | EGD with band ligation of esophageal varices.                          |
| PDx          | [[K70.30]] | The underlying disease is listed first per the "code first" note.      |
| Secondary Dx | [[I85.10]] | Secondary varices without bleeding, linked to the alcoholic cirrhosis. |
| Secondary Dx | [[F10.20]] | Use additional code for alcohol dependence when documented.            |

> [!tip]
> Do not list [[I85.10]] first. If the provider documents bleeding at the time of banding, switch to [[I85.11]].

### Example 2
> **Clinical Scenario:**
> A hepatologist sees an inpatient with cirrhosis and portal hypertension for an initial consult. The decision to band known non-bleeding secondary varices is made and the banding is done the same day.

| Field        | Code              | Rationale                                                                         |
| ------------ | ----------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| CPT          | [[99223]] [[-25]] | Initial hospital E/M, significant and separately identifiable from the procedure. |
| CPT 2        | [[43244]]         | EGD with band ligation.                                                           |
| PDx          | [[K74.60]]        | Underlying cirrhosis first.                                                       |
| Secondary Dx | [[K76.6]]         | Portal hypertension, documented.                                                  |
| Secondary Dx | [[I85.10]]        | Secondary varices without bleeding.                                               |

> [!tip]
> The E/M with [[-25]] needs documentation of work beyond the usual pre-procedure evaluation. A "bundled" E/M is not billable if the note only covers the procedure decision.

### Example 3
> **Clinical Scenario:**
> A patient with unspecified cirrhosis undergoes diagnostic EGD. The scope is stopped early because of oxygen desaturation after non-bleeding secondary esophageal varices are seen.

| Field | Code | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[43235]] [[-53]] | Diagnostic EGD discontinued for patient safety. |
| PDx | [[K74.60]] | Underlying cirrhosis first. |
| Secondary Dx | [[I85.10]] | Varices seen and documented as secondary to cirrhosis, without bleeding. |

> [!tip]
> [[-53]] needs the reason for stopping documented, and some payers want a modifier [[-52]] versus [[-53]] rationale. Query the provider if the note lacks a clear reason.

## ⚠️ Coding Pitfalls and Tips

- **Pitfall 1**: Sequencing **[[I85.10]]** as the principal diagnosis. **Tips**: Follow the "**code first underlying disease**" note and put the **[[cirrhosis]]**, **portal [[hypertension]]**, or other cause first.²
- **Pitfall 2**: Using **[[I85.00]]** when the provider links **varices** to **[[cirrhosis]]**. **Tips**: **Query** for the relationship if the note lists both but does not connect them.²
- **Pitfall 3**: Reporting **[[I85.10]]** when bleeding is described as past history. **Tips**: Use **[[I85.11]]** only for current **hemorrhage** and read the date and context.
- **Pitfall 4**: Forgetting the "**use additional code**" for **alcohol abuse** and **dependence**. **Tips**: Add **[[F10.20]]** or the matching **F10 code** only when the provider documents it.²
- **Pitfall 5**: Reporting **[[43235]]** with **[[43243]]** or **[[43244]]** in the same session. **Tips**: Check the **NCCI edit** and bill the **therapeutic EGD** only.
- **Pitfall 6**: Assuming **Medicare** covers **[[sclerotherapy]]** or **banding for non-bleeding varices** under **NCD 100.10**. **Tips**: Read the **NCD** and your **MAC's LCD** before billing.⁴⁵
