# 👩🏾‍⚕️CPT Code 55866 - Laparoscopy, Surgical Prostatectomy, Radical, With or Without Nerve Sparing

> **Full CPT Descriptor:**
> **Laparoscopy**, surgical [[prostatectomy]], radical, with or without nerve sparing

---

## 📋 Procedure Overview

**CPT [[55866]]** describes a **minimally invasive radical prostatectomy** performed via a laparoscopic or — far more commonly in contemporary urologic practice — a **robotic-assisted laparoscopic approach** using a surgical robotic platform (**most widely, the Intuitive Surgical da Vinci system**). This code captures the complete surgical extirpation of the prostate gland, prostatic capsule, seminal vesicles, and a cuff of bladder neck through multiple small abdominal port incisions rather than the open infraumbilical incision used in [[55840]].

[[55866]] is the **dominant radical prostatectomy code** in current urologic practice. As of recent national data, more than **85-90% of all radical prostatectomies** performed in the United States are completed via robotic-assisted laparoscopic technique, making [[55866]] the most frequently reported radical prostatectomy CPT code by a significant margin. The open retropubic approach ([[55840]]) and open perineal approach ([[55810]]) have been largely supplanted at most academic and high-volume community centers.

> **Critical Note for Coders:** There is **no separate CPT code** for robotic-assisted surgery. The CPT system does not distinguish between **standard laparoscopic** (**hand-held instruments, surgeon at bedside**) and **robotic-assisted laparoscopic** (**surgeon at a remote console, robotic arms at the bedside**). Both are captured under [[55866]]. The presence of the da Vinci system, Versius, or any other robotic platform does not change code selection — [[55866]] is the correct code regardless of the robotic platform used, provided the approach is laparoscopic/robotic rather than open.

The phrase **"with or without nerve sparing"** means that whether the neurovascular bundles (NVBs) are **preserved** (**unilateral or bilateral nerve-sparing technique**) or **sacrificed** (**wide excision, non-nerve-sparing**), the code remains [[55866]]. Nerve-sparing decisions are driven by oncologic risk, Gleason score, clinical stage, MRI findings, preoperative erectile function, and surgeon/patient preference — but they do **not** alter CPT code selection.

Like its open counterpart [[55840]], [[55866]] **does not include** pelvic lymph node dissection. If any degree of PLND is performed concurrently, the appropriate code changes. However — critically — unlike the open [[prostatectomy]] family (**which has three distinct codes: [[55840]], [[55842]], [[55845]]**), **[[55866]] is the sole laparoscopic/robotic prostatectomy code** in the CPT system, and there is currently **no separate laparoscopic PLND code** that replaces [[55866]] the way [[55842]] and [[55845]] replace [[55840]]. When laparoscopic PLND is performed concurrently with [[55866]], it is reported as a **separate add-on or companion code** (*see Excludes/Separately Reportable section below*).

## ⚙️ Procedural Mechanics

### Patient Positioning & Setup

1. Patient is placed in **steep Trendelenburg position** (typically 25-30°) to allow bowel to fall cephalad and expose the pelvis.
2. A urethral Foley [[catheter]] is placed for bladder decompression and intraoperative landmark identification.
3. The abdomen is insufflated with CO₂ to achieve pneumoperitoneum (typically 12-15 mmHg).
4. **Port placement** — typically 5-6 ports:
   - One 12mm camera/assistant port ([[infraumbilical]] or [[supraumbilical]])
   - Three or four 8mm robotic trocar ports (in a curved arc across the lower abdomen)
   - One or two 5-12mm assistant ports for the bedside assistant

### Transperitoneal vs. Extraperitoneal Approach

- **Transperitoneal (most common):** Peritoneal cavity is entered; bladder is dropped posteriorly; space of Retzius developed intraperitoneally. Standard for most robotic cases.
- **Extraperitoneal (less common):** Balloon dissector develops the space of Retzius without entering the [[peritoneal]] cavity; similar anatomic planes to open retropubic approach. May be preferred in patients with prior abdominal surgery.

Both approaches use [[55866]] — the approach variation does not affect code selection.

### Robotic Docking & Surgical Steps

5. The **robotic system is docked** to the ports; the surgeon moves to the console.
6. **Bladder mobilization:** The space of Retzius is developed; the bladder is mobilized off the anterior abdominal wall.
7. **Endopelvic fascia:** Bilateral endopelvic fascia is incised at the levator ani reflection; the prostate apex is exposed.
8. **Dorsal vein complex (DVC):** The DVC (**Santorini's plexus**) is ligated or controlled with bipolar energy/suture.
9. **Bladder neck dissection:** The anterior bladder neck is transected; the posterior bladder neck is incised and the ureteral orifices are confirmed to be away from the dissection plane.
10. **Seminal vesicle and vas deferens dissection:** The bilateral seminal vesicles and ampullae of the vasa deferentia are mobilized and transected. The posterior plane between Denonvilliers' fascia and the rectum is developed.
11. **Neurovascular bundle management (if nerve-sparing):**
    - *Bilateral nerve sparing:* Robotic precision dissection sharply releases the NVBs bilaterally off the posterolateral prostate using cold scissors (athermal technique); clips or Hem-o-lok clips for [[hemostasis]].
    - *Unilateral nerve sparing:* One bundle preserved, contralateral bundle taken with the specimen.
    - *Non-nerve sparing (wide excision):* Both bundles incorporated into the specimen for oncologic margin clearance.
12. **Apical dissection and urethral transection:** The urethra is transected at the apex under direct robotic vision; maximal urethral length is preserved for continence.
13. **Specimen entrapment:** The prostate and seminal vesicles are placed in an endoscopic specimen retrieval bag.
14. **Lymph node dissection (if performed):** Robotic-assisted bilateral pelvic lymphadenectomy may be performed at this point (see coding notes below).
15. **Urethrovesical anastomosis (UVA):** The bladder neck is tailored and anastomosed to the divided urethra using running absorbable suture (van Velthoven technique or barbed suture variant). The Foley bridges the anastomosis.
16. **Specimen extraction:** Laparoscopic bag is extracted through an enlarged port site (typically 3-4 cm enlargement at one port).
17. **Closure:** Ports are removed; CO₂ is evacuated; fascia closed at 12mm or larger ports; skin closed.

> 📌 **wRVU Parity with Open Approach:** [[55866]] carries the **same wRVU (27.72) as [[55840]]** (**open radical retropubic prostatectomy**), reflecting CMS's valuation of equivalent physician work despite the different approach. This is a frequently debated point in urologic surgery economics — robotic [[prostatectomy]] involves significant console time, cognitive complexity, and technical precision that some argue is undervalued relative to open surgery, while others argue the reduced physical demand of the robotic console justifies parity rather than a premium.

> ⚠️ **Annual Update:** wRVU and total RVU values are adjusted annually through CMS MPFS rulemaking. Values above reflect recently published schedules — always verify using the [CMS Physician Fee Schedule Look-Up Tool](https://www.cms.gov/medicare/physician-fee-schedule/search) for the applicable payment year.[^1]

> 📌 **Facility Fee Considerations:** When [[55866]] is performed in a hospital outpatient or ambulatory surgery center (ASC) setting, the **facility** bills under APC (Ambulatory Payment Classification) coding, not CPT-driven DRGs. The majority of radical prostatectomies are still performed as inpatient procedures due to length of stay, anastomosis monitoring, and catheter management, though same-day and 23-hour stay robotic prostatectomy is increasingly common at high-volume centers.

## ✅ Includes (Bundled — Do Not Bill Separately)

The following services are **inherently included** within [[55866]] and are **not separately reportable** in the same operative session:

- **Pneumoperitoneum establishment** (CO₂ insufflation, Veress needle or Hasson technique) — port placement and abdominal access are integral
- **Robotic system docking** — the act of docking the robot is not a separately billable service; there is no CPT code for robotic platform use
- **Urethrovesical anastomosis** — integral to completion of prostatectomy
- **Bladder neck reconstruction/tailoring** — required for anastomosis
- **Dorsal vein complex ligation** — standard hemostatic step
- **Seminal vesicle and vas deferens dissection and excision** — included in the procedure (though separately coded in ICD-10-PCS for inpatient billing)
- **Posterior dissection (Denonvilliers' fascia)** — integral to prostate mobilization
- **Diagnostic laparoscopy** ([[49320]]) — the laparoscopic visualization of the abdomen and pelvis is inherent to the approach; do not separately bill [[49320]]
- **Foley catheter placement** — standard perioperative management; not separately billable
- **Intraoperative fluoroscopy** — if used (uncommon in robotic prostatectomy); bundled
- **Surgical drain placement** — not separately billable
- **Nerve monitoring** — cavernous nerve electrostimulation if used; bundled
- **Cystoscopy** ([[52000]]) — if performed intraoperatively to confirm anastomotic integrity or assess ureteral orifices; bundled by most payers in this context unless separately documented with distinct indication
- **Intraoperative frozen section** — if pelvic lymph nodes are sent for intraoperative pathologic evaluation; pathology codes separately billed by pathologist only

### Separately Reportable with [[55866]] — Pelvic Lymph Node Dissection

> **This is the most important coding nuance for [[55866]]** and differs significantly from the open prostatectomy code family.

Unlike open radical prostatectomy — where PLND changes the **base code** from [[55840]] → [[55842]] → [[55845]] — the laparoscopic prostatectomy code [[55866]] **does not have companion codes** built into the CPT family that incorporate PLND into the descriptor. Therefore, when a **laparoscopic/robotic PLND** is performed concurrently with [[55866]], it is reported as a **separate, additional CPT code** alongside [[55866]]:

| Separately Reportable Code | Description | Modifier Needed? |
|---|---|---|
| [[38571]] | Laparoscopic bilateral pelvic [[lymphadenectomy]] (**separate procedure**) | [[-51]] (**multiple procedures**) — apply to [[38571]] as the lower-valued code; some payers bundle and require [[-59]] |
| [[38562]] | Limited lymphadenectomy for staging — pelvic and para-aortic (**open**) | Not applicable for laparoscopic PLND |
| [[38564]] | Limited lymphadenectomy for staging — retroperitoneal (**open**) | Not applicable for laparoscopic PLND |

> ✅ **Correct Billing When Robotic PLND is Performed:**
> - [[55866]] (robotic radical prostatectomy)
> - [[38571]]-[[-51]] (laparoscopic bilateral pelvic lymphadenectomy)
>
> Both codes are reported. [[38571]] is subject to the multiple procedure reduction (typically 50% on the lower-valued code under Medicare). Always verify payer-specific NCCI edits — some payers bundle [[38571]] into [[55866]] and require [[-59]] to unbundle; others do not reimburse separately regardless of modifier.

> ⚠️ **Audit Risk:** Failing to report [[38571]] when PLND was performed is an undercoding risk. Reporting [[38571]] without clear operative documentation of lymph node dissection is an overcoding risk. The operative note must explicitly describe the PLND — lymphatic packet names, anatomic boundaries, node counts sent to pathology.

## 🏥 ICD-10-CM Diagnosis Codes Commonly Paired with [[55866]]

### Primary Malignant Diagnosis

| **ICD-10-CM Code** | Description                    | HCC v24    | HCC v28    |
| -------------- | ------------------------------ | ---------- | ---------- |
| **[[C61]]**        | Malignant neoplasm of prostate | **HCC 11** | **HCC 17** |

> **[[C61]]** is the **sole ICD-10-CM code for primary prostate malignancy** — there is no laterality, sub-site, or histologic subtype distinction at the code level in ICD-10-CM for prostate cancer. This single code covers all primary prostate malignancies regardless of Gleason score, clinical stage (cT1-cT4), PSA level, or risk stratification tier. [[C61]] is the **principal diagnosis** for the overwhelming majority of [[55866]] claims with curative intent.

> **HCC 11 / HCC 17:** Active prostate malignancy maps to the cancer HCC category under both CMS-**HCC v24** (**HCC 11** — Colorectal, Bladder, and Other Cancers) and the restructured v28 model (**HCC 17**). This carries significant RAF weight and must be captured and reported at every qualifying encounter where the malignancy is being actively managed or treated. Under v28, the cancer groupings were reorganized — verify the current-year mapping file for applicable RAF coefficients.[^5]

| **709** | Major Male Pelvic Procedures w/o CC/MCC | **~1.30**                       |

> **DRGs 707-709** are the standard MS-DRG grouping for robotic radical [[prostatectomy]] admissions when the principal diagnosis is prostate cancer ([[C61]]) and ICD-10-PCS codes reflect prostate resection (**0VT04ZZ** — [[percutaneous]] endoscopic approach). The **CC/MCC tier split** has dramatic financial impact on facility reimbursement — **DRG 707** reimburses approximately **2.7x more** than DRG 709.

> **Key DRG Drivers for Tier Elevation:**
> The following conditions, when documented and coded, can elevate a case from **DRG 709** (w/o CC/MCC) to DRG 708 (w/ CC) or DRG 707 (w/ MCC):

### ICD-10-PCS Equivalents (Inpatient Facility Billing)

**Root Operation: Resection (T)**
*"Cutting out or off, without replacement, all of a body part"*
*(Complete prostatectomy = Resection, not Excision — the entire prostate is removed)*

| ICD-10-PCS Code | Description |
|---|---|
| **0VT04ZZ** | Resection of prostate, percutaneous endoscopic approach |

> For robotic-assisted laparoscopic radical prostatectomy ([[55866]]), the ICD-10-PCS approach character is **4 — Percutaneous Endoscopic** (laparoscopic/robotic), yielding **0VT04ZZ**. This is the defining code that separates robotic prostatectomy from open (0VT00ZZ) in the inpatient facility claim and may affect grouper-specific DRG logic at some institutions.

**Seminal Vesicle Resection (Code Separately per ICD-10-PCS Guidelines):**

| ICD-10-PCS Code | Description                                                               |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **0VTL4ZZ**     | Resection of bilateral seminal vesicles, percutaneous endoscopic approach |
| **0VTJ4ZZ**     | Resection of right seminal vesicle, percutaneous endoscopic approach      |
| **0VTK4ZZ**     | Resection of left seminal vesicle, percutaneous endoscopic approach       |

> Per ICD-10-PCS guidelines and supported by AHA Coding Clinic guidance, when seminal vesicles are removed as part of radical prostatectomy, the **seminal vesicle resection should be coded separately** as the seminal vesicles constitute a distinct ICD-10-PCS body part value. For bilateral excision (standard), **0VTL4ZZ** (bilateral) is the appropriate code.[^3]

### Example 1 - Standard Robotic Radical Prostatectomy, No PLND

> A 59-year-old male with cT2b Gleason 3+4=7 (Grade Group 2) prostate adenocarcinoma, PSA 7.6 ng/mL, NCCN intermediate-risk, undergoes robotic-assisted laparoscopic radical prostatectomy with bilateral nerve sparing. The operative report documents: steep Trendelenburg, transperitoneal approach, five robotic ports placed, da Vinci Xi docked, bilateral endopelvic fascia incised, DVC controlled with running 2-0 Vicryl, bilateral nerve-sparing dissection using cold scissors technique, bladder neck transected, bilateral seminal vesicles and vasa deferentia excised, prostate delivered, van Velthoven urethrovesical anastomosis completed, specimen retrieved via extraction bag through enlarged port site. **No pelvic lymph node dissection performed.** Estimated blood loss 150 mL.

**CPT:** [[55866]]
**ICD-10-CM:** [[C61]]

> ✅ No PLND = [[55866]] alone. Bilateral nerve sparing does not alter code. da Vinci platform does not create a separate code. This is the most commonly encountered [[55866]] scenario.

---

### Example 2 - Robotic Radical Prostatectomy WITH Bilateral Pelvic Lymphadenectomy

> A 65-year-old male with cT2c Gleason 4+4=8 (Grade Group 4) prostate cancer, PSA 18.4 ng/mL, NCCN high-risk, undergoes robotic-assisted radical prostatectomy. The operative note states: "Following completion of the prostatectomy and prior to closure, a bilateral pelvic lymphadenectomy was performed. The right and left external iliac lymphatic packets were dissected from the bifurcation of the common iliac artery to the circumflex iliac vein distally, and the obturator fossae were cleared bilaterally. A total of 14 lymph nodes were retrieved and sent to pathology in separate labeled containers."

**CPT:**
- [[55866]] (robotic radical prostatectomy)
- [[38571]]-[[-51]] (laparoscopic bilateral pelvic lymphadenectomy — modifier [[-51]] for multiple procedures, applied to the lower-valued code; verify payer NCCI edit — some require [[-59]])

**ICD-10-CM:** [[C61]]

> ✅ Unlike the open code family where PLND changes the base code ([[55840]] → [[55842]]/[[55845]]), for [[55866]] the PLND is always reported **separately** as [[38571]].

---

### Example 3 - Robotic Prostatectomy + PLND, Positive Nodes (HCC Impact)

> Same scenario as Example 2. Final pathology returns: Gleason 4+4=8 prostate adenocarcinoma, pT3a, **2 of 14 lymph nodes positive for metastatic adenocarcinoma** (1 right obturator, 1 left external iliac). Surgical margins negative.

**CPT:**
- [[55866]]
- [[38571]]-[[-51]]

**ICD-10-CM:**
- [[C61]] (primary prostate malignancy — still active/principal)
- [[C77.5]] (secondary malignant neoplasm of intrapelvic lymph nodes — confirmed by pathology) — **HCC 8**

> ⚠️ **Critical HCC Capture:** Adding [[C77.5]] for confirmed nodal metastasis elevates the HCC from **HCC 11** to **HCC 8 (Metastatic Cancer and Acute Leukemia)**, which carries a substantially higher RAF coefficient. This must be coded when documented by the pathologist and acknowledged by the treating physician. This is a high-value, frequently missed diagnosis code that significantly impacts risk adjustment accuracy.

### Example 4 - Inpatient Robotic Prostatectomy with CC/MCC Comorbidities

> A 72-year-old male with prostate cancer ([[C61]]), CKD stage 3b ([[N18.32]]), and T2DM with hyperglycemia ([[E11.65]]) undergoes robotic radical prostatectomy with bilateral PLND. On post-op day 1, he develops acute urinary retention requiring prolonged Foley catheterization. Hemoglobin drops from 13.2 to 10.1 g/dL postoperatively — physician documents acute posthemorrhagic anemia.

**Principal Diagnosis:** [[C61]]
**Secondary Diagnoses:**
- [[N18.32]] (CKD stage 3b) — **HCC 137; CC for MS-DRG**
- [[E11.65]] (T2DM with [[hyperglycemia]]) — **HCC 19; CC for MS-DRG**
- [[D62]] (acute posthemorrhagic anemia) — **CC for MS-DRG**
- [[N99.89]] (postprocedural complication, genitourinary — urinary retention)

**ICD-10-PCS Procedures:**
- **0VT04ZZ** (Resection of prostate, percutaneous endoscopic)
- **0VTL4ZZ** (Resection of bilateral seminal vesicles, percutaneous endoscopic)
- **07TC4ZZ** (Resection of pelvic lymphatic, percutaneous endoscopic — bilateral)

**MS-DRG Analysis:**
- With MCC: DRG 707 (~3.50 relative weight)
- With CC only: DRG 708 (~1.90 relative weight)
- Without CC/MCC: DRG 709 (~1.30 relative weight)
- **Likely DRG 708 or 707** depending on grouper evaluation of D62 and N18.32 CC/MCC designation — verify with current-year grouper. Multiple CCs may combine under some grouper versions to trigger an MCC tier. CDI query for malnutrition/hypoalbuminemia if documented by clinical indicators.

### Example 5 - Robotic Prostatectomy Miscoded as Open (Audit Scenario)

> An auditor reviews a claim submitted as [[55840]] (open radical retropubic prostatectomy). The operative report begins: *"The patient was positioned in steep Trendelenburg. CO₂ insufflation was established via Hasson technique. The da Vinci Xi robotic system was subsequently docked to four robotic ports and one assistant port placed in a standard configuration across the lower abdomen…"*

**Submitted Code:** [[55840]] — **INCORRECT**
**Correct Code:** [[55866]] — laparoscopic/robotic approach

> ❌ This is one of the highest-frequency audit findings in urologic surgery. The presence of **Trendelenburg positioning, CO₂ insufflation, trocar/port placement, robotic docking language** in the operative report immediately identifies a laparoscopic/robotic case. [[55840]] is an open retropubic procedure only. The wRVU difference between [[55840]] and [[55866]] is zero (both 27.72) — so the financial impact of this specific error is neutral — but it remains a documentation integrity and compliance issue that triggers audit scrutiny.

### Example 6 - Salvage Robotic Prostatectomy After Prior Radiation (Modifier -22)

> A 68-year-old male with biochemical recurrence ([[R97.21]]) and biopsy-proven local recurrence of prostate cancer after definitive external beam radiation therapy (EBRT) 4 years prior undergoes salvage robotic-assisted radical prostatectomy. The operative report documents: "Severe periprostatic fibrosis and obliteration of tissue planes secondary to prior radiation. Dissection was significantly complicated by dense adherence of the rectum to the posterior prostate requiring careful sharp dissection to avoid rectal entry. Operative time 5 hours 22 minutes. Estimated blood loss 620 mL. Bilateral seminal vesicles densely adherent and requiring piece-meal dissection."

**CPT:** [[55866]]-[[-22]]
**ICD-10-CM:**
- [[C61]] (recurrent/active prostate malignancy — salvage prostatectomy for recurrence)
- [[Z85.46]] — *do NOT use here; cancer is active, not historical*
- [[R97.21]] — may be listed as additional context but [[C61]] is primary
- Relevant radiation history: [[Z92.3]] (personal history of irradiation)

> ✅ **Modifier [[-22]] is strongly supported here.** Salvage prostatectomy after radiation is one of the most technically demanding procedures in urologic oncology, with significantly increased operative time, blood loss, and risk of rectal injury compared to primary prostatectomy. The operative note clearly documents the specific circumstances of increased complexity. A written attestation from the surgeon within the operative note or a separate addendum strengthens the [[-22]] claim. Expect payer requests for operative documentation — have it ready.

### Example 7 - 23-Hour Stay Outpatient Robotic Prostatectomy (Emerging Practice)

> A 57-year-old healthy male (ASA I) with low-risk prostate cancer undergoes robotic radical prostatectomy at a hospital outpatient department. He is admitted for 23-hour observation and discharged the following morning with Foley catheter in place. No complications.

**Surgeon CPT:** [[55866]]
**ICD-10-CM:** [[C61]]
**Facility Billing:** APC-based (not MS-DRG — this is outpatient, not inpatient); the hospital bills under the applicable **Outpatient Prospective Payment System (OPPS)** APC for radical prostatectomy.

> 📌 **Outpatient vs. Inpatient Distinction:** When robotic prostatectomy is performed as an outpatient (observation or same-day surgery), the facility does NOT generate an inpatient UB-04 claim and MS-DRG assignment does not apply. The **physician's CPT code ([[55866]]) and ICD-10-CM codes are the same regardless** of inpatient vs. outpatient status. The 90-day global period applies regardless of facility setting.

### Example 8 - Post-Prostatectomy Anastomotic Stricture (Within Global Period)

> Six weeks after robotic radical prostatectomy, the patient returns to the OR with difficulty voiding and a rising PSA. Cystoscopy reveals a tight vesicourethral anastomotic stricture. The urologist performs direct vision internal urethrotomy (DVIU) of the anastomotic stricture.

**Surgeon CPT (Return to OR):** [[52276]]-[[-78]] (cystourethroscopy with direct vision internal urethrotomy; modifier [[-78]] = unplanned return to OR, related procedure, within global period)
**Do NOT re-bill [[55866]]** — the global period is active.
**ICD-10-CM:** [[N99.112]] (postprocedural urethral stricture, male, membranous urethra)

> ✅ The 90-day global period covers complications of [[55866]] managed by the same surgeon. The [[-78]] modifier allows separate billing for a return to the operating room during the global period for a **related** complication. If the stricture repair requires **anesthesia**, it is billable with [[-78]]. Office-based dilation or catheter exchange during the global period is typically **not separately billable** and is considered included in the global surgical fee.

## 📝 Clinical & Documentation Tips for Coders

- **Robotic platform name does not change the code** — da Vinci, Versius, Hugo, or any other robotic surgical platform are all coded as [[55866]] when used for radical prostatectomy. There is no CPT premium code for robotic surgery; the platform is bundled into the facility's technology costs, not reflected in a higher physician CPT code.
- **The single most common [[55866]] coding error** is failing to identify and separately report concurrent PLND ([[38571]]). Unlike the open code family, [[55866]] has no "with PLND" variant — PLND is always a separate add-on. Systematically reviewing the operative note for any description of lymph node dissection is essential.
- **Conversion from robotic to open** is resolved simply: bill the **completed** approach. If the procedure was completed open, bill [[55840]] (± [[55842]] or [[55845]] per PLND extent). If completed robotic, bill [[55866]]. The aborted robotic attempt is not separately billable. Consider [[-22]] on the completed code if conversion dramatically increased complexity.
- **The 90-day global period** is one of the most consequential coding compliance elements for [[55866]]. All routine post-op visits, catheter management, incontinence counseling, and minor complication management within 90 days are bundled in the surgeon's global fee. Accurate calendar tracking of the global period start and end date is essential for compliance teams.
- **Seminal vesicle resection (0VTL4ZZ)** should be coded as a separate ICD-10-PCS code on inpatient claims per AHA Coding Clinic guidance — it is a distinct body part value and its separate coding accurately represents the full extent of the procedure for DRG grouping and quality reporting.[^3]
- **CDI query targets for DRG 709 → 708/707 elevation:** The most impactful clinical documentation queries for the inpatient robotic prostatectomy population are CKD staging (N18.3x/N18.4/N18.5), anemia etiology (posthemorrhagic [[D62]] vs. anemia of chronic disease [[D63.0]] vs. anemia of malignancy), malnutrition (hypoalbuminemia + weight loss + clinical context = [[E44.0]] or [[E41]]), diabetes with hyperglycemia/complications, and cardiopulmonary comorbidities. A single confirmed MCC can add over **$5,000-8,000** in DRG-based facility reimbursement at standard Medicare IPPS rates.
- **Modifier [[-22]] for robotic prostatectomy** is clinically supported in: prior pelvic radiation (salvage prostatectomy — highest complexity), prior TURP with obliterated vesicoprostatic junction, prior pelvic surgery with dense adhesions, extreme morbid obesity (BMI >45), intraoperative hemorrhage requiring complex hemostasis or transfusion, and significant anatomic anomalies. The operative report must contain specific language quantifying the increased difficulty — not just "difficult dissection" but explicit description of what made it atypical.
- **Biochemical recurrence coding** post-[[55866]]: After the global period, rising PSA is coded as [[R97.21]] (biochemical recurrence). Do not re-code [[C61]] unless clinical or radiographic recurrence is confirmed. If PSMA PET or biopsy confirms recurrent disease, reactivate [[C61]] with appropriate metastatic codes as applicable.
- **ICD-10-PCS approach character** for robotic prostatectomy is always **4 (Percutaneous Endoscopic)** — representing laparoscopic/robotic access. This distinguishes it from open (0) in the PCS table and is critical for accurate DRG grouping and procedure representation in the inpatient claim.
