# 👩🏾‍⚕️CPT Code 55840 - Prostatectomy, Retropubic Radical, With or Without Nerve Sparing

> **Full CPT Descriptor:**
> **Prostatectomy**, [[retropubic]] radical, with or without nerve sparing

---

## 📋 Procedure Overview

**CPT [[55840]]** describes an **open radical retropubic prostatectomy (RRP)** — the complete surgical extirpation of the prostate gland, prostatic capsule, seminal vesicles, and a portion of the bladder neck through a **lower midline retropubic (anterior, extraperitoneal) abdominal incision**, performed as definitive surgical treatment for **clinically localized or locally advanced prostate cancer**. This is one of the most technically demanding and oncologically significant procedures in urologic surgery and carries a **90-day global period**, the longest global window in the CPT system.

The phrase **"with or without nerve sparing"** within the descriptor means that whether the neurovascular bundles (NVBs) — the paired cavernous nerve bundles responsible for erectile function, running posterolaterally along the prostate — are **preserved (nerve-sparing technique)** or **sacrificed (non-nerve-sparing, wide excision)**, the CPT code is the same. The surgical decision regarding nerve sparing is driven by oncologic risk, clinical stage, Gleason score, preoperative erectile function, and tumor proximity to the neurovascular bundles. This decision must be clearly documented in the operative report but does **not** alter code selection.

The retropubic approach provides excellent visualization of the prostate apex, urethra, and neurovascular bundles and has historically been the predominant open surgical approach, though it has been largely supplanted by robotic-assisted laparoscopic radical prostatectomy ([[55866]]) at most academic and high-volume centers. [[55840]] remains an important code for open surgical cases, conversions from robotic/laparoscopic approaches, and high-complexity cases where open access is required.

This code does **not include** pelvic lymph node dissection. If a limited lymph node biopsy or pelvic lymphadenectomy is performed concurrently, use [[55842]] (with limited lymph node biopsy) or [[55845]] (with bilateral pelvic lymphadenectomy) **instead of** [[55840]].

## ⚙️ Procedural Mechanics

The procedure proceeds as follows:

1. **Patient positioning:** Supine with mild Trendelenburg; lower abdominal prep and draping; Foley catheter placed.
2. **Incision:** Lower midline infraumbilical vertical incision from umbilicus to pubic symphysis; rectus fascia is incised; space of Retzius (retropubic space) is developed extraperitoneally.
3. **Endopelvic fascia:** Bilateral endopelvic fascia is incised at the levator ani reflection, exposing the prostate apex laterally.
4. **Dorsal vein complex (DVC):** The dorsal venous complex (Santorini's plexus) is ligated — a critical hemostatic step at the prostate apex overlying the urethra. Various ligation techniques are used (absorbable suture, clip, energy device).
5. **Lymphadenectomy:** In [[55840]], limited or extended pelvic lymph node dissection is **NOT** performed; if dissection occurs, the code changes (see Code Tree). Some surgeons perform a limited staging PLND regardless — this changes code selection.
6. **Bladder neck dissection:** The anterior bladder neck is identified and incised, separating the prostate from the base of the bladder; posterior bladder neck dissection follows.
7. **Seminal vesicle and vas deferens dissection:** The seminal vesicles and ampullae of the vasa deferentia are mobilized and transected.
8. **Neurovascular bundle management:**
   - *Nerve-sparing:* The NVBs are carefully dissected off the posterolateral prostate using antegrade or retrograde technique; cold scissors preferred to avoid thermal injury.
   - *Non-nerve-sparing (wide excision):* The NVBs are sacrificed with the specimen for wider oncologic margins.
9. **Apical dissection and urethral transection:** The urethra is carefully transected at the prostate apex, preserving maximal urethral length for continence outcomes.
10. **Specimen removal:** The prostate, bilateral seminal vesicles, and vas deferens are removed en bloc.
11. **Urethrovesical anastomosis (UVA):** The bladder neck is reconstructed and anastomosed to the divided urethra using interrupted or running absorbable sutures (Van Velthoven technique or modified variants); Foley catheter bridges the anastomosis.
12. **Closure:** A surgical drain is placed in the pelvis; fascia, subcutaneous tissue, and skin are closed in layers.

## ✅ Includes (Bundled - Do Not Bill Separately)

The following services are **inherently included** in [[55840]] and are **not separately reportable** in the same operative session:

- **Urethrovesical anastomosis** - integral to completing the prostatectomy
- **Bladder neck reconstruction** - required as part of the anastomosis
- **Dorsal vein complex ligation** - standard hemostatic step
- **Dissection and mobilization of seminal vesicles and vasa deferentia** - included in the prostatectomy
- **Foley urethral catheter placement** - part of routine operative management
- **Surgical drain placement** - not separately billable
- **Intraoperative fluoroscopy or imaging** - if used (uncommon in open RRP; typically bundled)
- **Cystoscopy** - if performed during the same session to assess anastomosis or confirm ureteral orifice position (bundled by most payers; [[52000]] not separately billable with [[55840]] without strong documentation of separate medical necessity)
- **Nerve monitoring** - intraoperative cavernous nerve electrostimulation or monitoring is bundled

**Clinical Decision: Which Prostatectomy Code?**
```
Radical Prostatectomy Performed
│
├── What approach was used?
│   ├── Open retropubic (lower midline, space of Retzius) → [[55840]] family
│   ├── Open perineal (lithotomy, perineal incision) → [[55810]] family
│   └── Laparoscopic or robotic-assisted → [[55866]]
│
└── Was pelvic lymph node dissection performed? (Review entire operative note)
    ├── No PLND performed → [[55840]] (retropubic open)
    ├── Limited PLND / lymph node sampling / sentinel node biopsy → [[55842]]
    └── Full bilateral PLND (external iliac + internal iliac/hypogastric + obturator) → [[55845]]
```

> 🔑 **Robotic Note for Coders:** The vast majority of contemporary radical prostatectomies in the United States are performed using the **da Vinci robotic system** and are reported as [[55866]] — NOT [[55840]]. When you encounter [[55840]] in current practice, verify the operative note confirms a true **open retropubic approach** (infraumbilical incision, extraperitoneal dissection). [[55866]] includes an "assistant port" surgeon role that may be separately reportable; [[55840]] follows standard assistant surgeon rules.

## 🏥 ICD-10-CM Diagnosis Codes Commonly Paired with [[55840]]

### Primary Malignant Diagnoses

| ICD-10-CM Code | Description | HCC v24 | HCC v28 |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[C61]] | Malignant neoplasm of prostate | **HCC 11** | **HCC 17** |

> **[[C61]]** is the singular ICD-10-CM code for primary prostate cancer — there is no laterality distinction at this level in ICD-10-CM, as the prostate is a single, midline gland. This is the **principal diagnosis** for virtually all radical prostatectomy admissions with curative intent.

> **HCC 11 (CMS-HCC v24) / HCC 17 (v28):** Malignant neoplasm of the prostate maps to the cancer HCC category. This carries significant risk-adjustment weight and must be captured and reported at every qualifying encounter. Under v28 restructuring, urologic malignancies may shift to different HCC groupings — always verify against the current year's mapping file.[^5]

### Stage & Risk Stratification - ICD-10-CM Does Not Capture TNM Staging Directly

ICD-10-CM does not contain T/N/M staging codes for prostate cancer. However, the following codes help characterize disease extent and are critical for inpatient coding accuracy:

| ICD-10-CM Code | Description | HCC v24 | HCC v28 |
|---|---|---|---|
| [[C61]] | Prostate malignancy (all stages — localized to metastatic) | HCC 11 | HCC 17 |
| [[C79.82]] | Secondary malignant neoplasm of genital organs (metastatic to prostate area) | HCC 8 | HCC 8 |
| [[C79.51]] | Secondary malignant neoplasm of bone (prostate cancer with bone mets) | **HCC 8** | **HCC 8** |
| [[C79.52]] | Secondary malignant neoplasm of bone marrow | HCC 8 | HCC 8 |
| [[C77.5]] | Secondary malignant neoplasm of intrapelvic lymph nodes | HCC 8 | HCC 8 |
| [[C77.2]] | Secondary malignant neoplasm of intra-abdominal lymph nodes | HCC 8 | HCC 8 |

> **HCC 8 (Metastatic Cancer and Acute Leukemia):** Carries a dramatically higher risk coefficient than HCC 11/17. Patients with metastatic prostate cancer undergoing palliative or salvage procedures should have metastatic sites coded when documented. **Radical prostatectomy is typically NOT performed in the setting of distant metastatic disease** — if both [[C61]] and metastatic codes are present, ensure clinical context justifies the coding combination (e.g., oligometastatic disease, cytoreductive intent, clinical trial).

## 🏨 MS-DRG Assignment

> **Inpatient Coding Note:** CPT codes drive **physician/professional fee billing** and outpatient facility claims. For **inpatient hospital billing (UB-04)**, ICD-10-PCS procedure codes drive MS-DRG assignment. Radical prostatectomy falls under **MDC 12 (Diseases & Disorders of the Male Reproductive System)**.

### Relevant MS-DRGs - MDC 12

| MS-DRG | Description | Approx. Relative Weight[^4] |
|---|---|---|
| **707** | Major Male Pelvic Procedures w/ MCC | ~3.50 |
| **708** | Major Male Pelvic Procedures w/ CC | ~1.90 |
| **709** | Major Male Pelvic Procedures w/o CC/MCC | ~1.30 |

> **DRGs 707-709** are triggered when the principal diagnosis is a qualifying male pelvic condition (most commonly prostate cancer — [[C61]]) AND an ICD-10-PCS OR procedure qualifying as a "major male pelvic procedure" (prostatectomy) is coded. The CC/MCC split has a dramatic impact on reimbursement: DRG 707 (w/ MCC) reimburses approximately 2.7x more than DRG 709 (w/o CC/MCC).

> **CDI Opportunity:** Prostatectomy patients — particularly elderly males with prostate cancer — frequently have significant comorbidities (CKD, diabetes, cardiovascular disease, obesity, anemia of malignancy) that may qualify as **CC or MCC**. Clinical documentation improvement (CDI) queries on:
> - **CKD stage** (N18.x — CC/MCC-eligible depending on stage)
> - **Anemia due to malignancy** ([[D63.0]] — CC)
> - **Malnutrition** ([[E44.0]], [[E44.1]] — MCC/CC)
> - **Diabetes with complications** (CC/MCC depending on manifestation)
> - **Hypoalbuminemia / protein deficiency** ([[E46]] — MCC)
> - **DVT/PE** (if occurring postoperatively — [[I82.401]] — MCC)
> …can significantly affect DRG tier and facility reimbursement.

## 📖 Coding Examples

---

### Example 1 - Straightforward Open Radical Retropubic Prostatectomy, No PLND

> A 58-year-old male with clinical T2a Gleason 3+4=7 prostate adenocarcinoma, PSA 8.2 ng/mL, undergoes open radical retropubic prostatectomy with bilateral nerve-sparing technique. The operative report documents: infraumbilical incision, development of space of Retzius, DVC ligation, bilateral nerve-sparing dissection, bladder neck division, seminal vesicle excision, urethral transection, and tension-free urethrovesical anastomosis. **No lymph node dissection was performed.**

**CPT:** [[55840]]
**ICD-10-CM:** [[C61]]

> ✅ No PLND = [[55840]]. Nerve-sparing technique does not alter code selection.

---

### Example 2 - Open Radical Retropubic Prostatectomy WITH Limited Lymph Node Biopsy

> A 62-year-old male with cT2c Gleason 4+3=7 prostate cancer, PSA 12.5 ng/mL, Decipher score intermediate-high, undergoes open radical retropubic prostatectomy. The operative note states: "A limited pelvic lymph node dissection was performed including the right and left obturator fossae and distal external iliac regions. Six lymph nodes were retrieved bilaterally. Frozen sections negative for malignancy. Prostatectomy then proceeded…"

**CPT:** [[55842]] *(NOT [[55840]] — limited lymph node biopsy was performed)*
**ICD-10-CM:** [[C61]]

> ⚠️ This is one of the most common **down-coding errors** in radical prostatectomy — reporting [[55840]] when [[55842]] or [[55845]] is correct. Always read the full operative report; lymph node dissection language is often in the first paragraphs and easy to miss if only the prostatectomy description is reviewed.

---

### Example 3 - Open RRP with Full Bilateral Pelvic Lymphadenectomy

> A 65-year-old male with high-risk cT3a Gleason 4+5=9 prostate cancer, PSA 32 ng/mL, and suspicious pelvic nodes on MRI, undergoes open radical retropubic prostatectomy with extended bilateral pelvic lymphadenectomy. The operative note documents resection of bilateral external iliac, obturator, and hypogastric (internal iliac) nodal packets. Seventeen lymph nodes retrieved; three positive for metastatic adenocarcinoma.

**CPT:** [[55845]] *(bilateral PLND including external iliac, hypogastric, obturator = full bilateral lymphadenectomy)*
**ICD-10-CM Primary:** [[C61]]
**Secondary:** [[C77.5]] (secondary malignant neoplasm of intrapelvic lymph nodes — positive nodes confirmed) — **HCC 8 (Metastatic Cancer)**

> ⚠️ **HCC Impact:** The addition of [[C77.5]] (positive pelvic nodes = regional metastasis) significantly elevates the HCC risk score from HCC 11 to **HCC 8**, which carries a substantially higher RAF (Risk Adjustment Factor). This must be coded when documented by the pathologist and acknowledged by the physician.

### Example 4 - Inpatient Admission for Open RRP with Postoperative Complication

> A 70-year-old male with prostate cancer ([[C61]]) undergoes open radical retropubic prostatectomy ([[55840]]). On postoperative day 2, he develops a symptomatic pelvic hematoma with hemoglobin drop requiring transfusion. He is taken back to the OR for evacuation of the pelvic hematoma. He is also noted to have CKD stage 3a (documented and managed during the admission) and T2DM with hyperglycemia.

**Principal Diagnosis:** [[C61]] (prostate malignancy — reason for admission/surgery)
**Secondary Diagnoses:**
- [[N18.31]] (CKD stage 3a) — **HCC 137**
- [[E11.65]] (T2DM with hyperglycemia) — **HCC 19**
- [[N99.89]] (postprocedural complication, genitourinary — pelvic hematoma)
- [[D62]] (acute posthemorrhagic anemia) — **CC**
- [[Z79.4]] (long-term insulin use, if applicable)

**ICD-10-PCS Procedures (Primary Admission):**
- 0VT00ZZ (Resection of prostate, open)
- 0VTL0ZZ (Resection of bilateral seminal vesicles, open)

**ICD-10-PCS Procedures (Return to OR):**
- 0W9H0ZZ (Drainage of pelvic cavity, open — hematoma evacuation)

**CPT (Surgeon Billing — Return to OR):**
- [[55840]] with modifier [[-78]] (unplanned return to OR, related procedure, within global period)
— plus a procedure code for hematoma evacuation (e.g., [[49322]] or appropriate code based on operative note)

**MS-DRG:** Likely **DRG 707** (Major Male Pelvic Procedures w/ MCC) — acute posthemorrhagic anemia ([[D62]]) may qualify as MCC; CKD 3a and T2DM with hyperglycemia as CC. Verify with current year grouper logic.

### Example 5 - Robotic Radical Prostatectomy Miscoded as 55840 (Audit Scenario)

> An auditor is reviewing a claim. The CPT code submitted is [[55840]]. The operative report begins: "The patient was positioned in steep Trendelenburg. The da Vinci Xi robotic system was docked. Four 8mm robotic trocars and one assistant port were placed…"

**Correct CPT:** [[55866]] *(laparoscopic/robotic radical prostatectomy — NOT [[55840]])*
**Submitted CPT:** [[55840]] *(INCORRECT — open retropubic approach was not performed)*

> ❌ **Common Audit Finding:** [[55840]] specifies the **retropubic open approach**. Robotic-assisted and standard laparoscopic radical prostatectomies are **[[55866]]**, regardless of whether nerve sparing was performed, regardless of the robotic platform used, and regardless of whether lymph node dissection was performed concurrently. The presence of trocar/port placement language in the operative note is the red flag that the procedure was minimally invasive, not open.

### Example 6 - Post-Prostatectomy Biochemical Recurrence Follow-Up (Global Period Expired)

> A 64-year-old male, 18 months post open radical retropubic prostatectomy for [[C61]], presents for urology follow-up. PSA is 0.35 ng/mL on two successive measurements (up from undetectable), consistent with biochemical recurrence. The urologist orders pelvic MRI/PSMA PET and discusses salvage radiation therapy options. No new surgical procedure is planned today.

**E/M Visit Code:** 99214 or 99215 (outpatient established patient visit — appropriate complexity)
**ICD-10-CM:**
- [[R97.21]] (rising PSA following treatment for malignant neoplasm of prostate)
- [[Z85.46]] (personal history of malignant neoplasm of prostate)

> ✅ The 90-day global period has long expired (18 months post-op); E/M visit is independently billable. [[C61]] is **not** re-coded unless there is confirmed recurrent/metastatic disease. [[R97.21]] captures biochemical recurrence without confirmed clinical recurrence. If PSMA PET confirms metastatic disease, appropriate metastatic codes (e.g., [[C79.51]] for bone mets, [[C77.5]] for nodal disease) would then be added.

### Example 7 - Conversion from Robotic to Open Approach (Mid-Case)

> A 67-year-old male with significant prior pelvic radiation (for rectal cancer) and a hostile pelvis undergoes planned robotic radical prostatectomy ([[55866]]) for salvage prostatectomy after radiation failure. After robotic port placement and initial dissection, severe pelvic adhesions and inability to achieve adequate visualization necessitate conversion to open retropubic approach. The case is completed open.

**CPT:** [[55840]] (open retropubic radical prostatectomy — the completed approach)
**Modifier:** [[-22]] recommended — salvage prostatectomy after radiation in a hostile pelvis with intraoperative conversion is significantly more complex than primary open RRP; document increased operative time, complexity of dissection, and blood loss.
**Do NOT bill:** [[55866]] — the laparoscopic attempt was not completed as a therapeutic procedure; only the completed open procedure is billed.
**ICD-10-CM:**
- [[C61]] (malignant neoplasm of prostate — recurrent/persistent after radiation)
- [[Z85.46]] — consider if this is true recurrence vs. persistent disease; [[C61]] is typically retained as active cancer code in radiation failure cases
- [[Z96.641]] or relevant history codes if prior pelvic surgery is relevant

## 📝 Clinical & Documentation Tips for Coders

- **The most critical documentation element** for [[55840]] vs. [[55842]] vs. [[55845]] is the operative note's description of whether **pelvic lymph node dissection was performed** and to what extent. Read the entire operative note — PLND descriptions often appear near the beginning and may be missed if only the prostatectomy portion is reviewed.
- **Nerve-sparing status does not change the CPT code** — [[55840]] covers all variants (bilateral nerve-sparing, unilateral nerve-sparing, non-nerve-sparing). Document it clinically for outcomes purposes, but it is not a coding decision point.
- **Open vs. robotic/laparoscopic** is the most consequential approach distinction. [[55840]] = open retropubic only. Any port/trocar placement = [[55866]]. A conversion from robotic to open = bill [[55840]] for the completed procedure.
- **The 90-day global period** requires vigilance. All post-op visits for prostatectomy complications, catheter management, and routine follow-up within 90 days are **bundled** for the operating surgeon. Only unrelated conditions or separately identifiable new problems may be billed separately during this window.
- **Seminal vesicle resection** should be coded separately in ICD-10-PCS (0VTL0ZZ) as it is a distinct body part value — this is consistently supported by AHA Coding Clinic guidance and can affect DRG CC/MCC capture indirectly through procedure complexity.[^3]
- **CDI query targets for DRG optimization:** CKD stage, anemia of malignancy, malnutrition/hypoalbuminemia, obesity, diabetes with complications, cardiovascular comorbidities, and any postoperative complications (ileus, DVT, hematoma, urinary leak). These can move a case from DRG 709 to DRG 707, representing a substantial difference in reimbursement.
- **Pathology coding:** The prostatectomy specimen is a **Level VI** surgical pathology service ([[88309]]) — the most complex level — billed by the pathologist. The surgeon's CPT code is entirely separate and independent. The Gleason score from the final pathology report is a clinical result, not a separate billable service for the surgeon.
- **PSA surveillance billing post-prostatectomy:** After the 90-day global expires, routine PSA follow-up encounters are billed as standard E/M visits. [[R97.21]] is the appropriate diagnosis for biochemical recurrence; [[Z85.46]] for disease-free surveillance. Do not re-code [[C61]] unless active disease is confirmed.
- **Modifier [[-22]] documentation:** For [[55840]], [[-22]] is frequently supported in cases of prior pelvic surgery, pelvic irradiation, inflammatory adhesions, extreme obesity (BMI >40), or intraoperative hemorrhage requiring complex hemostasis. The operative report must specifically describe the unusual circumstances, estimated blood loss, operative time, and the surgeon's statement of increased complexity. Without this documentation, payers will routinely deny or reduce the [[-22]] claim.
