# CPT 66180 - Aqueous shunt to extraocular equatorial plate reservoir

### Primary Procedure
- **66180 - Aqueous shunt to extraocular equatorial plate reservoir (e.g., Ahmed, Baerveldt, Molteno, Schocket type implant):**

  **Detailed Explanation:** This procedure is utilized for refractory, severe, or complex glaucoma when standard filtration surgery (like a trabeculectomy) has failed or is at high risk of failing. The surgeon implants a medical device consisting of a tiny silicone tube attached to a plastic end-plate. The plate is sutured far back on the white of the eye (the sclera, at the "equator" of the globe), tucked under the eye muscles. The tube is then tunneled under the conjunctiva and inserted into the anterior chamber. Excess aqueous fluid drains through the tube and pools over the plate, where it is absorbed by surrounding blood vessels.

### ICD-10-CM Diagnosis Code(s) (Top 6 Options)
*Note: Tube shunts are heavily associated with secondary glaucomas and advanced disease states.*

1. **H40.89 - Other specified glaucoma:** Often used as the base code for **Neovascular Glaucoma**, which is a primary indication for a tube shunt over a trabeculectomy because new blood vessels will rapidly scar over a standard tissue flap. *(Note: Code the underlying condition first, such as diabetic retinopathy).*
2. **H40.11X3 - Primary open-angle glaucoma, severe stage:** Used when maximum medical therapy and previous trabeculectomies (66170/66172) have failed.
3. **H40.22X3 - Chronic angle-closure glaucoma, severe stage:** Used for chronic anatomical blockage requiring a physical tube to bypass the angle.
4. **H40.41X3 - Glaucoma secondary to eye inflammation, right eye, severe stage:** Uveitic glaucoma strongly predisposes the eye to scarring, making tube shunts the preferred surgical option.
5. **H40.51X3 - Glaucoma secondary to other eye disorders, right eye, severe stage:** Often utilized for traumatic glaucoma or ICE syndrome.
6. **H40.61X3 - Glaucoma secondary to drugs, right eye, severe stage:** Steroid-induced refractory glaucoma.

# CPT/HCPCS Code(s) & Alternatives

It is vital to differentiate devices *with* a reservoir plate (66180) from micro-stents *without* a plate, as well as revisions.

- **66180 - Aqueous shunt to extraocular equatorial plate reservoir.** (Base code for this note).
- **Alternative (No Plate) - 66183:** Insertion of anterior segment aqueous drainage device, *without* extraocular reservoir (e.g., XEN gel stent, minimally invasive glaucoma shunts).
- **Alternative (Revision with Graft) - 66184:** Revision of aqueous shunt to extraocular equatorial plate reservoir; *with* graft. (Used when an existing tube is eroding through the conjunctiva and needs to be recovered).
- **Alternative (Revision without Graft) - 66185:** Revision of aqueous shunt; *without* graft. (Used to clear a blocked tube or reposition it).
- **Alternative (Removal) - 66187:** Removal of implanted aqueous shunt.

# Exclusives/Inclusives (Bundling & NCCI Edits)

### Inclusives (Commonly Bundled - Do Not Bill Separately)
- **66625 (Iridectomy):** Performing a peripheral iridectomy to prevent the iris from plugging the silicone tube is strictly bundled.
- **65800 (Paracentesis):** Tapping the anterior chamber to inject viscoelastic or adjust pressure is included.
- **Viscoelastic/Healon:** The use of viscoelastics to maintain the anterior chamber during tube insertion is bundled.
- **Tube Ligation:** Surgeons often tie off (ligate) a Baerveldt tube with a Vicryl suture to prevent the eye from draining too fast in the first few weeks. This tying and subsequent laser lysis of that suture is bundled into the global package.

### The "Patch Graft" Controversy (CPT 67255)
To prevent the silicone tube from rubbing through the conjunctiva (erosion), surgeons usually cover the tube with a patch of donor tissue (e.g., Tutoplast, processed pericardium, sclera, or cornea).
- **Billing 67255 (Scleral reinforcement):** Historically, this was billed separately. However, **NCCI edits now heavily bundle 67255 into 66180.** - **Exception/Modifier:** You *can* bypass this edit with **Modifier -59 or -XU** *only if* the patch graft is placed at a completely distinct anatomic site from the shunt insertion, or if specific payer policy explicitly allows it for a donor graft. *Audit Warning:* Many Medicare Administrative Contractors (MACs) will automatically deny 67255 when billed with 66180, considering the graft an integral part of modern tube shunt insertion. Check your specific payer's Local Coverage Determination (LCD).

### Assistant Payable
- **No:** An assistant surgeon (Modifier -80, -82) is not permitted/payable by Medicare for this procedure.

# Detailed Clinical Context & Documentation Tips (Audit Safeguards)

- **Device Supply Billing (HCPCS L8612):** In an Ambulatory Surgery Center (ASC) or hospital outpatient department, the facility bills for the physical device itself using **L8612** (Aqueous shunt). The physician *cannot* bill for the device unless the surgery was somehow performed in an office setting (POS 11), which is exceedingly rare for this code.
- **Tissue Supply Billing (HCPCS V2790):** If a donor patch graft (pericardium/sclera) is used, the facility should bill **V2790** (Amniotic membrane or donor tissue for surgical purposes) to capture the cost of the biological implant, even if the surgeon cannot separately bill the 67255 CPT code.
- **Valved vs. Non-Valved:** The operative note should specify the type of implant. **Ahmed** valves restrict flow automatically to prevent hypotony (dangerously low eye pressure). **Baerveldt and Molteno** shunts are non-valved, requiring the surgeon to manually tie off the tube until the plate encapsulates in scar tissue (about 4-6 weeks). Both types map to 66180.
- **Laterality is Mandatory:** Always append Modifier **-RT** (Right side) or **-LT** (Left side).
