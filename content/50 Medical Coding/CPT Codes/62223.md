# 🧠 CPT 62223 — Creation of Shunt; Ventriculo-Peritoneal, -Pleural, Other Terminus

<br>

> [!info] Quick Reference  
> **wRVU**: 13.70 (2026; verify 2027)<sup>2</sup><sup>3</sup> | **Global Period**: **<font color="#ff0000">090</font>** | **Assistant Payable**: 0 | **Bilateral Indicator:** 0 |  
> **Rule**: **CPT [[62223]]** is a complete-system code that covers the **burr hole or twist drill access, ventricular catheter, valve, distal catheter, subcutaneous tunneling, CSF sampling, imaging of distal placement, and initial valve programming**.<sup>4</sup> Code selection is driven by where the shunt starts (**ventricle vs. subarachnoid/subdural space**) and where it ends (**vascular vs. non-vascular terminus**).<sup>8</sup> Its **90-day global** means revisions in the postoperative period need **[[-78]]** to be paid.<sup>7</sup>

---

## 📋 Clinical Description
**CPT [[62223]]** describes creation of a **CSF shunt from a cerebral ventricle to the peritoneal cavity, pleural cavity, or another non-vascular terminus**, most commonly the **ventriculoperitoneal (VP) shunt.**<sup>1</sup> The **[[surgeon]]** places the proximal catheter into the ventricle through a **burr hole or twist drill hole**, attaches a valve (**fixed or programmable**), and tunnels the **distal [[catheter]]** subcutaneously to the drainage site. The procedure treats **[[hydrocephalus]]** by diverting excess **CSF** to a body cavity where it can be absorbed.<sup>1</sup> All components of the new system are included in this single code, so the burr hole and tunneling are not reported separately.<sup>4</sup>

Its closest sibling is **[[62220]]**, which is the same ventricular-origin shunt but ending in a **vascular terminus** (**atrium, jugular vein, or auricle**).<sup>8</sup> If the shunt instead originates in the **[[subarachnoid]]** or **[[subdural]]** space and drains to the peritoneum or pleura, **[[62192]]** is the correct code.<sup>8</sup> Revisions of an existing system are reported with **[[62225]]** (**ventricular catheter**) or **[[62230]]** (**valve or distal catheter**), not **[[62223]]**.<sup>4</sup> When a neuroendoscope is used to place the ventricular catheter, add-on **[[62160]]** may be reported with **[[62223]]**.<sup>5</sup>

**This procedure may be performed in the following clinical contexts:**  

- **Idiopathic normal pressure [[hydrocephalus]]** — **an older adult with gait disturbance, cognitive decline, and urinary incontinence** who improved after a high-volume lumbar tap or lumbar drain trial undergoes elective VP shunt placement with a programmable valve.  
- **Obstructive [[hydrocephalus]]** — a patient with aqueductal **[[stenosis]],** a posterior fossa **[[tumor]]**, or a ventricular **[[cyst]]** has **CSF** flow blocked and needs permanent diversion, sometimes alongside an endoscopic procedure.<sup>5</sup>  
- **Pediatric congenital hydrocephalus** — an infant with **[[myelomeningocele]]** and **[[hydrocephalus]]**, **Chiari II malformation**, or **aqueductal [[stenosis]]** receives a **VP shunt** for progressive **[[ventriculomegaly]].**<sup>12</sup>  
- **Ventriculopleural or other terminus shunt** — when the **peritoneum** is unusable because of **adhesions, [[peritonitis]], or prior abdominal surgery**, the **distal [[catheter]]** is placed in the pleural cavity or another non-vascular site under the same code.<sup>1</sup>

## 🔬 Anatomical & Procedural Considerations

| **Variant**     | **Mechanism**       | **Key Notes**       |
| :-----------: | --------------- | --------------- |
| **Ventriculoperitoneal (VP)** | The ventricular catheter is placed through a frontal or parieto-occipital burr hole, connected to a valve, and the distal catheter is tunneled to the abdomen and placed in the peritoneal cavity through a small incision or laparoscopically. | This is the most common configuration. If a general surgeon places the peritoneal catheter laparoscopically, check payer policy for co-surgeon or separate reporting, and check that the co-surgeon indicator allows [[-62]] before using it. |
| **Ventriculopleural** | The distal catheter is tunneled to the chest and placed in the pleural space, where CSF is absorbed by the pleura. | This is reported with the same code, [[62223]]. It is usually reserved for older children and adults because of the risk of pleural effusion, and the op note should document the pleural terminus. |
| **Other non-vascular terminus (e.g., gallbladder, subgaleal)** | The distal catheter drains to another non-vascular site when the peritoneum and pleura are not options. | This is rare. Document the exact terminus, because vascular termini (atrium, jugular) move the service to [[62220]].<sup>8</sup> |

> [!tip] Clinical Pearl  
> Read the op note for two things before choosing a code: where the **proximal catheter** sits and where the distal catheter ends.<sup>7</sup> Ventricle to **peritoneum** or **pleura** is **[[62223]]**, ventricle to atrium or jugular is **[[62220]]**, and **subarachnoid/subdural** to **peritoneum** is **[[62192]]**.<sup>8</sup> Make sure this is a new system and not a revision, because replacement of a single component is **[[62225]]** or **[[62230]]** and complete removal with replacement is **[[62258]]**.<sup>4</sup>

## ✅ Procedure Includes
- **Entry into the skull by twist drill or burr hole** for ventricular access.<sup>4</sup>  
- **Placement and attachment** of the ventricular catheter, valve, and distal catheter.<sup>4</sup>  
- **CSF sampling** at the time of catheter placement.<sup>4</sup>  
- **Subcutaneous tunneling of the shunt tubing** from the head to the distal terminus.<sup>4</sup>  
- Imaging to confirm **distal shunt placement.**<sup>4</sup>  
- Initial programming of a **programmable valve** during the operative session.<sup>4</sup>  
- **Routine postoperative care** during the **90-day** global period, including wound checks and uncomplicated follow-up visits.

> [!warning] Bundling Alert  
> **CPT [[62223]]** has a **90-day global**, so related postoperative visits are bundled, the **decision-for-surgery E/M** needs **[[-57]]**, and a related return to the OR (**such as [[62225]] for proximal obstruction**) needs **[[-78]]** or it may be denied as bundled.<sup>7</sup> When **[[62223]]** is performed with an endoscopic procedure that also includes **ventricular [[catheter]] placement**, such as **[[62161]]**, **AAPC guidance** is to report **[[62223]]** with **[[-52]]** and **[[-51]]** to account for the overlap.<sup>6</sup> **Medicare** has treated +**[[61781]]** (**navigation**) as informational on remittances for some shunt claims, and **[[69990]]** is often denied with **[[62223]]**, so check **NCCI edits** and payer policy before billing either.<sup>1</sup> **Audit** risk is highest when the op note does not clearly separate a new creation from a revision or does not document the distal terminus.

## 🏥 MS‑DRG Considerations
**CPT [[62223]]** is reported on the professional claim and does not group to an **MS-DRG** by itself. The facility claim for an inpatient shunt creation typically groups to **MS-DRG 031** (**Ventricular Shunt Procedures with MCC**), **032** (with CC), or **033** (**without CC/MCC**) based on the **ICD-10-PCS bypass code**, so capturing **CC/MCC** secondary diagnoses on the facility side affects payment.<sup>4</sup> For your profee claim, sequence the diagnosis that supports the medical necessity of **CSF diversion** first and keep it consistent with the facility's principal diagnosis when possible. **NCD/LCD**: I found no **NCD** or **LCD** specific to **C**PT **[[62223]]** in the **Medicare Coverage Database**, so **Medicare** coverage relies on general reasonable-and-necessary rules, NCCI edits, and your **MAC**'s billing guidance (**Palmetto GBA for North Carolina**).<sup>9</sup> **PFS** comparison: The fee schedule lists **[[62223]]** as status **A** (**active, separately payable**) with a **090** global.<sup>2</sup> Because no **LCD** restricts the code, the fee schedule status and **NCCI edits** are the main payment controls for Medicare. Commercial and **Medicare Advantage plans** may still require prior authorization, especially for **[[G91.2]]**.

> - **Bypass** (**1**) is used for shunt creation because **CSF** is rerouted to a new destination; **Drainage** (**9**) is not used for **shunt** placement.<sup>4</sup>

## 📝 Coding Examples

### Example 1
> **Clinical Scenario:**  
> A 74-year-old with progressive gait apraxia, memory decline, and urinary incontinence has [[ventriculomegaly]] on MRI and marked gait improvement after a 3-day lumbar drain trial. The neurosurgeon places a right frontal burr hole VP shunt with a programmable valve and sets the initial pressure in the OR. No known cause of the hydrocephalus is documented.

| **Field** | **Code**      | **Rationale**                                                                                                            |
| ----- | --------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| CPT   | [[62223]] | New ventricle-to-peritoneum shunt system. Initial programming is included, so [[62252]] is not reported.<sup>4</sup> |
| PDx   | [[G91.2]] | Idiopathic normal pressure hydrocephalus with no documented cause.                                                   |

> [!note] 
> Document the tap test or lumbar drain trial result to support medical necessity, since payers often ask for it for idiopathic NPH.

### Example 2
> **Clinical Scenario:**  
> A patient with obstructive hydrocephalus undergoes VP shunt placement. The neurosurgeon uses a neuroendoscope to guide and confirm placement of the ventricular catheter, and the op note describes the endoscopic portion in detail.

| **Field** | **Code**      | **Rationale**                                                                                                           |
| ----- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| CPT 1 | [[62223]] | Creation of the VP shunt system.<sup>5</sup>                                                                        |
| CPT 2 | [[62160]] | Add-on for intracranial neuroendoscopy used to place the ventricular catheter, reported with [[62223]].<sup>5</sup> |
| PDx   | [[G91.1]] | Obstructive hydrocephalus is the documented indication.<sup>5</sup>                                                 |

> [!warning] 
> If the neuroendoscopic service is a separate primary procedure that already includes catheter placement, such as [[62161]], report [[62223]] with [[-52]] and [[-51]] rather than +[[62160]].<sup>6</sup>

### Example 3
> Clinical Scenario:  
> Three weeks after VP shunt creation, the patient returns with headache and enlarging ventricles. The same neurosurgeon takes the patient back to the OR and replaces the obstructed ventricular catheter.

| **Field** | **Code**                      | **Rationale**                                                                                                                                        |
| ----- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| CPT   | [[62225]]-[[-78]] | Proximal catheter replacement during the 90-day global of [[62223]] is reported with [[-78]].<sup>7</sup>                                        |
| PDx   | [[T85.01XA]]          | Mechanical breakdown of the ventricular shunt during active treatment. Check documentation for a more specific obstruction or displacement code. |

> [!note] Global period reminder
> With [[-78]], the revision does not start a new 90-day global; the original [[62223]] global continues. Without [[-78]], the payer may deny the revision as bundled.<sup>7</sup>

## ⚠️ Common Coding Pitfalls

- **Pitfall 1**: Choosing the code based on the **shunt brand or valve type instead of the anatomy**. Code selection depends on the proximal location (**ventricle vs. subarachnoid/subdural**) and the distal terminus (**vascular vs. non-vascular**).<sup>8</sup>  
- **Pitfall 2**: Reporting **[[62223]]** for a revision. If an existing system is being repaired or partly replaced, use **[[62225]]** or **[[62230]]**, and use **[[62258]]** when the complete system is removed and replaced in the same session.<sup>4</sup>  
- **Pitfall 3**: Reporting **[[62252]]** for the initial valve setting. Initial programming in the OR is included in **[[62223]]**.<sup>4</sup>  
- **Pitfall 4**: Missing **+[[62160]]** when a **[[neuroendoscope]]** is documented for **[[catheter]]** placement. It is a valid **add-on** with **[[62223]]** when the op note supports it.<sup>5</sup>  
- **Pitfall 5**: Forgetting **[[-78]]** on a return to the **OR** during the **90**-day global. Revisions without **[[-78]]** are commonly denied as part of the global package.<sup>7</sup>  
- **Pitfall 6**: Adding a **G91 code** to a congenital combination code. Codes such as **[[Q03.0]]**, **[[Q05.2]]**, and **[[Q07.02]]** already describe **[[hydrocephalus]]**, so a separate **G91 code** is redundant.
