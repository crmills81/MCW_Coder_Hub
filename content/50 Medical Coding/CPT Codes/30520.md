## 😷 CPT Code 30520: Documentation & Billing Guide

## Short Definition

Septoplasty** or **submucous resection, with or without cartilage scoring, contouring or replacement with graft.

### Long Definition

**CPT code 30520** describes the surgical correction of a deviated nasal septum through septoplasty or submucous resection. The nasal septum is the midline structure dividing the nasal cavity into right and left sides, composed of cartilage anteriorly (**quadrangular cartilage**) and bone posteriorly (**perpendicular plate of ethmoid and vomer**). A [[deviated septum]] occurs when this structure is displaced from the midline, causing nasal obstruction, difficulty breathing, chronic [[sinusitis]], [[epistaxis]], or other functional problems. 

The surgical procedure involves making an incision in the nasal mucosa, elevating [[mucoperichondrial]] and [[mucoperiosteal]] flaps, removing or repositioning the deviated portions of septal cartilage and bone, and then reapproximating the mucosal flaps. The code includes various techniques: cartilage scoring (**making controlled cuts to weaken and straighten cartilage**), contouring (**reshaping cartilage), and replacement with graft (using cartilage from elsewhere to reconstruct the septum**). The procedure may be performed alone or in conjunction with turbinate procedures when both structures contribute to nasal obstruction. 

This is a functional surgery intended to improve nasal airway patency and breathing, not primarily for cosmetic purposes. The global surgical period is 90 days, during which routine postoperative care including nasal packing removal, debridement, and follow-up visits are included in the surgical fee.

### Area of Body

**Nasal septum and nasal cavity, specifically:**

**Primary Surgical Site - Nasal Septum:**

- **Anterior septum (cartilaginous):**
	- Quadrangular (septal) cartilage
	- Upper lateral cartilage attachments
	- Anterior septal angle
	- Caudal septum (columellar region)
- **Posterior septum (bony):**
	- Perpendicular plate of ethmoid bone
	- Vomer bone
- **Superior septum:**
	- Cribriform plate junction (high risk area)
- **Inferior septum:**
	- Maxillary crest attachment
	- Floor of nose junction

**Related Structures:**

- Nasal mucosa (mucoperichondrium over cartilage, mucoperiosteum over bone)
- Septal blood supply (anterior and posterior ethmoid arteries, sphenopalatine artery)
- Nasal valve area (internal and external)
- Septopremaxillary ligament (Pitanguy's ligament)
- Nasal floor and turbinates (may be addressed if contributing to obstruction)

**Structures Exposed But Not Primarily Operated Upon:**

- Inferior turbinates (separate procedure codes if reduced)
- Middle turbinates (visualization only unless separate pathology)
- Nasal passages bilaterally
- Anterior skull base (must avoid)

### Service Components

**Included Services in CPT 30520:**

**Pre-operative (Day of Surgery):**

- Pre-operative assessment and marking
- Anesthesia induction (general or local with sedation - anesthesia separately billable by anesthesiologist)
- Patient positioning
- Intranasal topical decongestants and anesthesia
- Injection of local anesthetic with [[epinephrine]] for hemostasis

**Intraoperative Procedure:**

- Incision through nasal mucosa (hemitransfixion or Killian incision typically)
- Elevation of mucoperichondrial flaps bilaterally
- Elevation of mucoperiosteal flaps over bony septum
- Identification and exposure of deviated septal cartilage and bone
- Resection of deviated portions of septal cartilage
- Removal of deviated bony septum (perpendicular plate, vomer)
- Preservation of adequate dorsal and caudal septal support (L-strut)
- **Optional techniques included in 30520:**
	- Cartilage scoring: Making controlled cuts to straighten cartilage without removing
	- Cartilage contouring: Reshaping and thinning cartilage
	- Cartilage replacement with graft: Harvesting cartilage from removed septum and replacing as spreader grafts, batten grafts, or reconstructive grafts
- Repositioning of straightened septum to midline
- Reapproximation and quilting of mucosal flaps
- Placement of internal splints or quilting sutures
- Nasal packing placement (if used)
- [[Hemostasis]]

**Immediate Post-operative:**

- Patient recovery and monitoring (separately billable by facility)
- Post-operative instructions
- Prescriptions (pain medication, antibiotics if indicated)

**Post-operative Care Included in 90-Day Global Period:**

- All routine post-operative visits (typically day 1, week 1, weeks 2-4, months 2-3)
- Nasal packing removal (typically 24-48 hours post-op)
- Nasal splint removal (typically 1 week post-op)
- Suture removal if external sutures used
- Nasal debridement and cleaning (routine)
- Assessment of healing and septum position
- Treatment of routine complications:
	- Minor epistaxis (not requiring return to OR)
	- Crusting management
	- Synechia lysis (minor adhesions)
	- Routine infections treated medically
- Post-operative education and counseling

**Excludes:**

**Other Nasal Procedures (Separately Billable When Appropriate):**

- **Rhinoplasty codes ([[30400]]-[[30462]]):** Cosmetic or reconstructive nose reshaping; different from functional septoplasty
- **Turbinate procedures:**
	- [[30130]] - Excision inferior turbinate, partial or complete
	- [[30140]] - Submucous resection inferior turbinate, partial or complete
	- [[30801]] - Ablation, soft tissue of inferior turbinates, superficial
	- [[30802]] - Ablation, inferior turbinates, intramural
	- [[30930]] - Fracture nasal turbinate(s), therapeutic
	- Can be billed with 30520 using modifier 59 when separate medical necessity documented
- **Sinus procedures ([[31231]]-[[31298]]):** Functional endoscopic sinus surgery; may be performed with septoplasty
- **Nasal polyp removal ([[30110]]-[[30115]]):** May be performed with septoplasty
- **Repair of nasal vestibular stenosis ([[30465]]):** Different procedure
- **Septorhinoplasty (30520 + rhinoplasty codes):** When both functional and cosmetic components

**Procedures Bundled into Septoplasty:**

- Simple nasal cautery for hemostasis during procedure
- Routine nasal examination and endoscopy as part of procedure
- Irrigation and debridement during global period (routine)
- Minor revisions during global period
- Packing/unpacking of nose

**Separately Billable (Not Included):**

- Anesthesia services by anesthesiologist/CRNA (00160-00164)
- Pre-operative consultations and evaluations (with modifier 57 if decision for surgery)
- Unrelated services during global period (modifier 24)
- Significant complications requiring return to OR (modifier 78)
- Unrelated procedures during global period (modifier 79)
- CT scan of sinuses pre-operatively (70486-70488)
- Nasal endoscopy (31231) - typically bundled if same session, may be separately billable at different encounter

## Assistant Surgeon Status

**Assistant Payable:** Yes, with medical necessity

**Medicare Assistant Surgeon Policy:**

- CPT 30520 allows for assistant surgeon payment
- Medicare indicator: "Assistant surgeon may be paid" (typically indicator 0 or 1)
- Payment: 16% of surgeon's allowed amount for physician assistant
- Must document medical necessity in operative report

**When Assistant Surgeon May Be Medically Necessary:**

- Complex revision septoplasty with extensive scarring
- Severe deviation requiring extensive reconstruction
- Combined with multiple other procedures (sinus surgery, turbinate reduction)
- Significant bleeding requiring additional hemostasis assistance
- Teaching hospitals with resident unavailable (modifier 82)
- Patient factors: Bleeding disorders, difficult anatomy, obesity
- Surgeon factors: Additional retraction, suction, or assistance required

**When Assistant Typically NOT Necessary:**

- Routine primary septoplasty
- Straightforward deviation
- Experienced surgeon with standard case
- No unusual complexity

**Applicable Assistant Surgeon Modifiers:**

- **Modifier 80:** Assistant surgeon (physician) - 16% payment
- **Modifier 81:** Minimum assistant surgeon (rarely used) - 16% payment
- **Modifier 82:** Assistant surgeon when qualified resident unavailable (teaching hospitals) - 16% payment
- **Modifier AS:** Physician assistant, nurse practitioner, or CNS as assistant - 13.6% payment (85% of 16%)

**Documentation Requirements for Assistant:**

- Operative report must document assistant's participation
- Specific tasks performed: Retraction, suctioning, hemostasis, tissue handling
- Medical necessity statement justifying need for assistant
- Primary surgeon attestation
- For modifier 82: Documentation that qualified resident not available

**Payer Variations:**

- Medicare: May allow with documentation
- Medicare Advantage: Often requires prior authorization
- Commercial payers: Variable; many allow for complex cases
- Some payers deny routinely; others approve based on documentation
- Pre-authorization recommended if planning to bill assistant

**Critical Modifier Notes:**

**Do NOT Use Modifier 50 (Bilateral):**

- Nasal septum is a single midline structure
- Procedure not performed bilaterally
- Using modifier 50 incorrect and may result in denial or overpayment

**Do NOT Use RT/LT:**

- Septoplasty addresses midline septum, not unilateral structure
- Not appropriate for laterality modifiers

**Modifier 51 (Multiple Procedures) - VERY COMMON:**

- Used when septoplasty performed with other procedures same session
- Common combinations:
	- 30520 (septoplasty) + 30140-51 (turbinate resection)
	- 30520 (septoplasty) + 31256-51 (maxillary antrostomy)
	- 30520 (septoplasty) + 31267-51 (ethmoidectomy)
- Highest RVU procedure paid 100%, additional procedures reduced 50%
- Many payers auto-apply modifier 51; may not need to append manually

**Modifier 59 (Distinct Procedural Service):**

- Critical for billing turbinate procedures with septoplasty
- NCCI may bundle certain turbinate codes with 30520
- Modifier 59 bypasses edit when procedures are distinct
- Must document separate medical necessity:
	- Septal deviation AND inferior turbinate hypertrophy
	- Different anatomic structures
	- Different diagnoses/indications
	- Not for hemostasis or access
- Example: 30520, 30801-59 (ablation of turbinates with modifier 59)

**Modifier 22 (Increased Procedural Services):**

- Use when significantly more complex than typical septoplasty
- Justifications:
	- Extensive revision surgery with scarring
	- Severe S-shaped or multiple deviations
	- Saddle nose requiring extensive reconstruction
	- Operative time 50%+ longer than typical
	- Significant unexpected complexity
- Requires detailed documentation:
	- Operative report explicitly stating increased difficulty
	- Cover letter explaining complexity
	- Request specific percentage increase (typically 20-50%)
	- Comparison to standard procedure

**Modifier 57 (Decision for Surgery):**

- Used on E/M visit resulting in decision for major surgery
- Required by Medicare when decision made day before or day of surgery with 90-day global
- Example: Patient seen in office, exam reveals severe septal deviation, surgery scheduled
- Bill: 99213-57 or 99214-57 (depending on complexity)

**Modifier 78 (Return to OR for Complication):**

- Used when patient returns to OR during 90-day global period for complication
- Payment: Approximately 70% of surgical fee
- New global period does NOT start
- Examples:
	- Severe epistaxis requiring surgical control
	- Septal hematoma requiring drainage
	- Septal perforation requiring repair
- Complications managed in office (not OR) are included in global

## Coding Examples

**Example 1: Primary Septoplasty for Deviated Septum**

Patient Presentation: 35-year-old male with chronic nasal obstruction, worse on right side, present for several years and progressively worsening.

**History**:

- Constant nasal congestion, right > left
- Mouth breathing at night
- Loud snoring reported by spouse
- Failed conservative management (nasal steroids, antihistamines)
- No improvement with 3 months medical therapy
- History of nasal trauma in adolescence (sports injury)

**Physical Examination:**

- Anterior rhinoscopy: Severe S-shaped septal deviation with convexity to right, obstructing right nasal passage 80-90%
- Nasal endoscopy: Septal deviation confirmed, right middle turbinate medialized due to septal pressure, left nasal passage relatively patent
- No purulent drainage, no polyps

**Imaging**:

- CT sinus: Deviated septum confirmed, otherwise clear sinuses

Assessment: Severe deviated nasal septum with significant nasal airway obstruction, failed medical management

**Plan**: Septoplasty recommended

**Surgery: Septoplasty (30520)**

- General anesthesia
- Hemitransfixion incision made on left side
- Mucoperichondrial and mucoperiosteal flaps elevated bilaterally
- Severe S-shaped deviation of cartilaginous and bony septum identified
- Deviated portions of quadrangular cartilage carefully resected, preserving adequate 1.5cm L-strut for dorsal and caudal support
- Perpendicular plate of ethmoid and portions of vomer removed
- Remaining cartilage scored and contoured to straighten
- Septum repositioned to midline
- Mucosal flaps reapproximated with quilting sutures
- Silastic splints placed bilaterally
- No complications

**Coding**:

- **30520** - Septoplasty

**Diagnosis**:

- **J34.2** - Deviated nasal septum

**Post-operative Course:**

- Day 1: Routine post-op check (included in global)
- Week 1: Splint removal (included in global)
- Week 2: Nasal debridement and assessment (included in global)
- Month 2: Final assessment, excellent result, 90% improvement in breathing

**Medical Necessity:** Septoplasty medically necessary for severe nasal obstruction due to deviated septum, failed conservative therapy, significantly impacting quality of life.

**Example 2: Septoplasty with Bilateral Inferior Turbinate Reduction**

**Patient**: 42-year-old female with chronic nasal obstruction and congestion despite medical management.

**Pre-operative Findings:**

- Septal deviation to left with spur
- Bilateral inferior turbinate hypertrophy significantly contributing to obstruction
- Failed medical therapy (nasal steroids, antihistamines) for 6 months
- CT scan: Septal deviation and bilateral turbinate hypertrophy, clear sinuses

**Assessment**:

- Deviated nasal septum
- Bilateral hypertrophic inferior turbinates
- Both contributing to nasal obstruction

**Surgery Performed:**

1. Septoplasty (30520)
	- Septal deviation corrected as above
2. Bilateral submucous resection of inferior turbinates (30140)
	- Separate procedure addressing turbinate hypertrophy
	- Incisions made along inferior turbinates
	- Submucosal tissue resected bilaterally
	- Mucosa preserved
	- Hemostasis achieved

**Operative Time**: 105 minutes (longer than septoplasty alone due to turbinate component)

**Coding**:

- **30520** - Septoplasty (primary code, 100% payment)
- **30140-51** - Submucous resection inferior turbinate, bilateral (modifier 51 for multiple procedure, 50% payment)

**Alternative Coding with Modifier 59:**

- **30520** - Septoplasty
- **30140-59** - Submucous resection with modifier 59 (distinct procedural service)

**Diagnoses**:

- **[[J34.2]]** - Deviated nasal septum (primary diagnosis for 30520)
- **[[J34.3]]** - Hypertrophy of nasal turbinates (separate diagnosis for 30140)

**Critical Documentation:**

- Must document that BOTH conditions present
- Both contributing to obstruction
- Turbinate reduction NOT performed for hemostasis or surgical access
- Separate pathologies requiring separate procedures

**Medical Necessity**: [[Septoplasty]] addresses septal deviation; turbinate reduction addresses separate inferior turbinate [[hypertrophy]]. Both procedures medically necessary to adequately treat patient's nasal obstruction.

**Example 3: Septoplasty with Functional Endoscopic Sinus Surgery**

**Patient**: 48-year-old with chronic rhinosinusitis and nasal obstruction.

**History**:

- Chronic sinus infections, multiple courses of antibiotics over 2 years
- Constant nasal congestion
- Facial pressure and headaches
- Failed medical management (antibiotics, steroids, saline irrigation)

**Pre-operative:**

- CT sinus: Deviated septum, bilateral maxillary and ethmoid sinusitis with polyposis, blocked osteomeatal complexes

**Diagnosis**:

- Chronic rhinosinusitis with nasal polyposis
- Deviated nasal septum

**Surgery:**

1. Septoplasty (30520) - Corrected septal deviation for surgical access and improved breathing
2. Bilateral maxillary antrostomy (31256) - Opened maxillary sinuses, removed polyps
3. Bilateral total ethmoidectomy (31255) - Removed all ethmoid air cells and polyps
4. Bilateral polypectomy extensive (31051) - Removed extensive nasal polyps

**Coding**:

- **31255** - Bilateral total ethmoidectomy (likely highest RVU, primary code)
- **31256-51** - Bilateral maxillary antrostomy (modifier 51)
- **30520-51** - Septoplasty (modifier 51)
- **31051-51** - Polypectomy extensive (modifier 51)

**Note**: Some payers automatically apply multiple procedure reduction; others require modifier 51 appended.

**Diagnoses**:

- **J32.9** - Chronic sinusitis, unspecified (or specify maxillary/ethmoid)
- **J33.9** - Nasal polyp, unspecified (or specify bilateral)
- **J34.2** - Deviated nasal septum

**Documentation Note**: When septoplasty performed as part of approach for FESS, must document separate medical necessity for septoplasty beyond surgical access. Document that septal deviation itself contributes to nasal obstruction and requires correction.

**Example 4: Revision Septoplasty with Increased Complexity**

Patient: 55-year-old with recurrent nasal obstruction 5 years after primary septoplasty.

**History**:

- Prior septoplasty performed elsewhere 5 years ago
- Good initial result but gradual return of obstruction
- CT shows residual/recurrent deviation with scar tissue

**Pre-operative Assessment:**

- Anterior rhinoscopy: Scarring at prior surgical site, synechiae between septum and turbinate
- Persistent septal deviation despite prior surgery
- Internal nasal valve collapse noted

**Surgery: Revision Septoplasty**

- Significant scarring encountered during flap elevation
- Difficult tissue planes due to prior surgery
- Limited cartilage available due to prior resection
- Cartilage graft harvested from left ear (conchal cartilage) for spreader graft to support internal valve
- Extensive reconstruction required
- Operative time: 140 minutes (typical septoplasty 60-90 minutes)

**Documentation in Operative Report:**  
"Revision septoplasty significantly more complex than primary procedure due to extensive scar tissue from prior surgery. Tissue planes obliterated requiring meticulous sharp dissection. Limited septal cartilage available due to over-resection at prior surgery. Required harvest of conchal cartilage from left ear for spreader grafts to reconstruct internal nasal valve and provide support. Operative time increased 75% compared to typical septoplasty due to complexity. Blood loss 150cc (vs typical 50cc)."

**Coding**:

- **30520-22** - Septoplasty with modifier 22 for increased procedural services

**Modifier 22 Documentation Submitted to Payer:**

- Cover letter explaining:
	- Revision surgery with extensive scarring
	- Increased operative time (140 min vs typical 60-90 min)
	- Need for cartilage graft harvest from ear
	- Significantly increased complexity
	- Request 40% increase in reimbursement
- Copy of operative report highlighting complexity
- Copy of prior operative report showing previous surgery

**Diagnoses**:

- **J34.2** - Deviated nasal septum, recurrent
- **J34.89** - Other specified disorders of nose and nasal sinuses (can document post-surgical changes)

**Medical Necessity**: Revision septoplasty medically necessary for recurrent nasal obstruction following failed prior surgery; increased complexity warrants modifier 22 consideration.

**Example 5: Cannot Bill Septoplasty During Rhinoplasty for Access**

**Patient**: 32-year-old desires cosmetic rhinoplasty for dorsal hump and bulbous tip. Also has mild septal deviation causing minimal nasal obstruction.

**Examination**:

- External: Dorsal hump, bulbous tip, wide nasal bones
- Internal: Mild septal deviation, 30% obstruction on left
- Patient primarily concerned with cosmetic appearance

**Pre-operative Discussion:**

- Patient informed that rhinoplasty is cosmetic and not covered by insurance
- Septoplasty component may be covered if functional indication present
- Clear documentation of functional vs cosmetic components

**Surgery:**

1. Septoplasty performed to straighten septum and provide access for rhinoplasty
2. Rhinoplasty: Dorsal hump reduction, tip refinement, osteotomies

**Billing Challenge:**

- If septoplasty performed primarily for ACCESS to perform rhinoplasty (not standalone functional indication), cannot bill 30520 separately
- If septoplasty has independent functional indication (significant obstruction), can bill separately

**Correct Approach - Scenario A (Septoplasty Mainly for Access):**

- **30420** - Rhinoplasty, primary (patient pays out-of-pocket)
- Do NOT bill 30520 separately (included in rhinoplasty as access)

**Correct Approach - Scenario B (Septoplasty Has Functional Indication):**

- **30520** - Septoplasty (bill insurance with functional diagnosis)
- **30420-51** - Rhinoplasty (patient pays out-of-pocket)
- Must document functional necessity for septoplasty independent of rhinoplasty

**Diagnosis for Septoplasty:**

- **J34.2** - Deviated nasal septum (functional obstruction documented)

**Financial**:

- Insurance covers 30520 if functional indication documented
- Patient pays for 30420 (cosmetic rhinoplasty)

**Compliance Issue**: Do NOT bill functional septoplasty to insurance when performed solely for access to perform cosmetic rhinoplasty. This is insurance fraud.

**Example 6: Septoplasty with Assistant Surgeon**

**Patient**: 60-year-old with severe S-shaped septal deviation, history of multiple nasal surgeries, on anticoagulation for atrial fibrillation.

**Pre-operative Complexity:**

- Severe deviation requiring extensive reconstruction
- Prior surgeries with expected scarring
- On aspirin and Plavix (cardiology cleared to hold Plavix 7 days pre-op, continue aspirin)
- Expected increased bleeding risk

**Surgery: Revision Septoplasty with Assistant Surgeon**

- Primary surgeon and physician assistant (PA) present
- PA provided retraction, suctioning, assistance with hemostasis
- Significant oozing due to anticoagulation
- PA assistance essential for visualization and hemostasis
- Operative time: 120 minutes

**Operative Report Documentation:**  
"Physician assistant [name] provided essential assistance during this complex revision septoplasty. Assistant provided continuous suction and retraction allowing adequate visualization in presence of significant oozing due to patient's anticoagulation therapy. Assistant also provided hemostasis with bipolar cautery at multiple bleeding points. Assistance was medically necessary due to complexity of revision surgery, extensive scarring, and increased bleeding risk."

**Coding**:  
**Primary Surgeon Bills:**

- **30520-22** - Septoplasty with modifier 22 (if justified by complexity)

**Physician Assistant Bills:**

- **30520-AS** - Septoplasty with modifier AS (PA as assistant)

**Diagnoses**:

- **J34.2** - Deviated nasal septum
- **I48.91** - Atrial fibrillation (supporting diagnosis for increased bleeding risk)
- **Z79.02** - Long-term use of antiplatelet therapy

**Reimbursement**:

- **Primary surgeon**: 100% of allowable (or increased if modifier 22 approved)
- **PA as assistan**t: 13.6% of allowable (85% of 16% assistant surgeon rate)

**Medical Necessity for Assistant:** Complex revision with bleeding disorder and anticoagulation requiring additional assistance for safe surgery.

## Documentation Requirements

**Pre-operative Documentation for Medical Necessity:**

**Clinical History Required:**

- Duration and severity of nasal obstruction
- Laterality: Unilateral vs bilateral
- Impact on quality of life:
	- Difficulty breathing through nose
	- Mouth breathing
	- Sleep disturbance
	- Snoring
	- Chronic fatigue
	- Exercise intolerance
	- Chronic sinus infections
- Failed conservative therapy:
	- Nasal steroids (document medication, duration, compliance)
	- Antihistamines
	- Decongestants
	- Nasal saline irrigation
	- Duration of medical therapy (typically 3-6 months minimum)
	- Response or lack thereof
- Prior nasal trauma or surgery
- Associated symptoms: Epistaxis, facial pain, sinus infections

**Physical Examination Documentation:**

- Anterior rhinoscopy findings:
	- Septal deviation description (C-shaped, S-shaped, spur)
	- Degree of obstruction (quantify as percentage or descriptive: mild/moderate/severe)
	- Location of deviation (anterior, posterior, superior, inferior)
	- Which side more obstructed
- Nasal endoscopy findings:
	- Detailed septum assessment
	- Turbinate size and appearance
	- Nasal valve assessment
	- Sinus ostia visualization
	- Presence/absence of polyps, masses, purulent drainage
- External nasal examination (document if external deformity present)

**Imaging (When Performed):**

- CT sinus (not always required for septoplasty alone):
	- Document septal deviation
	- Rule out concurrent sinus disease
	- Surgical planning for complex cases
	- Not mandatory for straightforward primary septoplasty

**Medical Necessity Criteria (Insurance Requirements):**

**Typical Coverage Criteria:**

- Documented nasal obstruction significantly impacting daily activities
- Objective findings of significant septal deviation on exam
- Failed conservative medical management (3-6 months minimum)
- Procedure will improve airway patency and breathing

**Medicare Coverage:**

- Covered when medically necessary for functional impairment
- Not covered for purely cosmetic indications
- Documentation must support functional limitation

**Commercial Payers:**

- Variable criteria; check specific policy
- Many require:
	- Failed medical therapy 3-6 months
	- Specific exam findings
	- Prior authorization

**Operative Report Must Contain:**

**Standard Elements:**

- Patient identification, date, location
- Pre-operative diagnosis: Deviated nasal septum, specify severity
- Post-operative diagnosis: Same
- Procedure name: Septoplasty or submucous resection of nasal septum
- Surgeon, assistants (if any), anesthesia type
- Indication for surgery

**Detailed Procedure Description:**

**Approach and Incision:**

- Type of incision (hemitransfixion, Killian, complete transfixion)
- Location and extent

**Dissection:**

- Elevation of mucoperichondrial flaps
- Elevation of mucoperiosteal flaps
- Extent of dissection

**Septal Work:**

- Description of deviation found (location, severity, type)
- Cartilaginous components addressed:
	- Quadrangular cartilage resection or repositioning
	- Amount of cartilage removed or preserved
	- Documentation of adequate L-strut preservation (minimum 1-1.5cm dorsal and caudal support)
- Bony components addressed:
	- Perpendicular plate of ethmoid
	- Vomer
	- Maxillary crest
- Techniques used:
	- Cartilage scoring (if performed): Location, technique
	- Cartilage contouring (if performed): How cartilage reshaped
	- Cartilage graft (if performed): Source of graft, location placed, fixation method
- Repositioning of septum to midline

**Closure:**

- Reapproximation of mucosal flaps
- Suturing technique (quilting sutures, mattress sutures)
- Splint placement (Silastic, Doyle, other)
- Packing placement (if used)

**Hemostasis:**

- Method of achieving hemostasis
- Estimated blood loss

**Complications:**

- Any intraoperative complications (bleeding, perforation, etc.)
- How managed

**Condition:**

- Patient condition at end of procedure
- Disposition (recovery room, home same day, admitted)

**Key Documentation Points:**

- Extent of deviation
- Amount of tissue removed
- Preservation of structural support (L-strut)
- Any grafting performed
- If combined with other procedures, clearly document each separately

**For Modifier 22 (Increased Complexity):**

- Explicitly state why more complex than standard
- Increased operative time (document time, compare to typical)
- Technical difficulties encountered
- Scarring, adhesions, unusual anatomy
- Need for grafting from separate site

**For Assistant Surgeon:**

- Document assistant's presence and participation
- Specific tasks performed by assistant
- Medical necessity for assistant

**Post-operative Documentation:**

**All Visits During 90-Day Global Period:**

- Date and reason for visit
- Nasal examination findings
- Septal position assessment
- Healing progress
- Any complications
- Treatment provided:
	- Packing/splint removal (document)
	- Debridement (describe)
	- Medications adjusted
- Patient education and counseling

**Post-operative Instructions Documented:**

- Activity restrictions
- Nasal care (saline irrigation, avoid blowing)
- Medications prescribed
- Signs/symptoms warranting urgent call
- Follow-up appointment schedule

## Billing Guidelines and Best Practices

**Global Surgical Package (90-Day Global Period):**

**Included in 30520 Payment:**

- Pre-operative visit day of/day before surgery (unless decision visit with modifier 57)
- Intraoperative services
- All post-operative visits for 90 days:
	- Day 1 check
	- Week 1: Splint removal, nasal examination
	- Week 2-4: Debridement, assessment
	- Month 2-3: Final assessment
- Nasal packing removal
- Splint removal
- Routine debridement and cleaning
- Suture removal if used
- Treatment of routine complications:
	- Minor epistaxis managed in office
	- Synechia lysis (minor adhesions)
	- Crusting management
	- Routine infections treated medically
- Standard post-operative supplies and materials

**NOT Included (Separately Billable):**

- E/M visit where decision for surgery made (modifier 57 if within 1 day of surgery)
- Pre-operative CT scan (70486-70488) if performed
- Pre-operative nasal endoscopy at separate encounter (31231)
- Anesthesia services by anesthesiologist/CRNA (00160-00164)
- Unrelated E/M services during global period (modifier 24, different diagnosis)
- Unrelated procedures during global period (modifier 79)
- Significant complications requiring return to OR (modifier 78, reduced payment ~70%)
- Major revisions (new procedure after global period)

**Billing with Turbinate Procedures - Critical NCCI Rules:**

**NCCI Bundling:**

- Some turbinate codes may have NCCI edits with 30520
- **30930 (fracture nasal turbinates): CANNOT bill with 30520** - Bundled per NCCI
- **30801, 30802, 30130, 30140: CAN bill with 30520 using modifier 59**

**Correct Billing for Septoplasty + Turbinates:**

- **30520** - Septoplasty (primary code)
- **30140-59** or **30140-51** - Turbinate procedure with modifier
	- Modifier 59: Indicates distinct procedural service, bypasses NCCI edit
	- Modifier 51: Indicates multiple procedures, may be auto-applied by payer

**Documentation Required for Turbinate Billing:**  
Must document:

- TWO separate diagnoses:
	- J34.2 (deviated septum) - links to 30520
	- J34.3 (turbinate hypertrophy) - links to turbinate code
- Both conditions contributing to obstruction
- Turbinate procedure NOT performed for:
	- Hemostasis alone
	- Surgical access
	- Part of septoplasty technique
- Medical necessity for BOTH procedures

**Which Turbinate Code to Bill:**

- Do NOT bill multiple turbinate codes together
- Choose ONE turbinate procedure:
	- Bill higher RVU code if multiple techniques used
	- 30130 or 30140: RVU 4.78 (highest turbinate RVU)
	- 30801: RVU 2.53
	- 30802: RVU 3.17

**Multiple Procedure Payment Reduction:**

- Highest RVU code: 100% payment
- Additional codes: 50% payment (some payers have different formulas)
- Example:
	- 30520 (7.77 wRVU): 100% = $371
	- 30140-51 (4.78 wRVU): 50% = $116
	- Total payment: $487

**Billing with Sinus Surgery:**

- Septoplasty commonly performed with FESS
- All procedures billable with appropriate modifiers
- Must document separate medical necessity for septoplasty beyond surgical access
- Document that septal deviation contributes to nasal obstruction independent of sinus disease

**Functional vs Cosmetic - Critical Compliance:**

**Functional Septoplasty (Covered):**

- Primary indication: Nasal obstruction from septal deviation
- Failed medical therapy
- Impacts breathing and quality of life
- Diagnosis: J34.2 (deviated septum)
- Medical records support functional impairment

**Cosmetic Rhinoplasty (NOT Covered):**

- Primary indication: Appearance
- Patient desires cosmetic improvement
- Insurance does NOT cover
- Patient financial responsibility

**Septorhinoplasty (Combined):**

- Functional component (30520): Billable to insurance if meets criteria
- Cosmetic component (30420, etc.): Patient pays out-of-pocket
- MUST clearly separate and document:
	- Functional indication for septoplasty independent of cosmetic goals
	- Cosmetic indications for rhinoplasty
	- Separate consent forms
	- Clear financial agreements with patient
- Cannot bill functional septoplasty to insurance if performed solely for access to rhinoplasty

**Compliance Red Flags:**

- Billing functional septoplasty when primarily cosmetic
- Billing septoplasty for rhinoplasty access without functional indication
- Upcoding routine septoplasty with modifier 22 without justification
- Billing multiple turbinate procedures together
- Billing turbinates when performed only for hemostasis
- Inadequate documentation of failed medical therapy

**Prior Authorization:**

- Medicare: Generally not required for septoplasty
- Medicare Advantage: Often DOES require prior authorization
- Commercial payers: Variable; many require prior auth
- Submit with authorization:
	- Clinical notes documenting obstruction
	- Exam findings
	- Failed medical therapy (document medications, duration)
	- Functional impact
	- CT scan if obtained

**Advance Beneficiary Notice (ABN):**

**When to Provide ABN:**

- Medicare beneficiary and coverage uncertain
- Indication may be considered cosmetic
- Insufficient documentation of failed medical therapy
- Patient requesting surgery without clear functional impairment
- Revision septoplasty (may be scrutinized)

**Modifier Use with ABN:**

- **GA modifier:** ABN obtained, patient agrees to pay if denied
- **GZ modifier:** Expected denial, ABN not obtained (high audit risk)

**Common Billing Errors to Avoid:**

1. **Using modifier 50 (bilateral)** - Septum is midline; not bilateral procedure
2. **Using RT or LT modifiers** - Not appropriate for midline structure
3. **Billing with 30930 (fracture turbinates)** - Bundled per NCCI, cannot bill together
4. **Billing multiple turbinate codes** - Choose highest RVU code only
5. **Billing turbinates without modifier 59** - May deny due to NCCI edit
6. **Inadequate medical necessity documentation** - Must document failed medical therapy
7. **Billing cosmetic as functional** - Insurance fraud
8. **Missing failed medical therapy documentation** - Common denial reason
9. **Not documenting separate turbinate medical necessity** - Denials likely
10. **Billing septoplasty during rhinoplasty global for access only** - Not separately billable

**Best Practices:**

**Documentation Excellence:**

- Thorough pre-operative H&P documenting functional impairment
- Failed medical therapy clearly documented (medications, duration, lack of response)
- Detailed operative report describing deviation and surgical correction
- Clear post-operative notes documenting healing and outcome
- Separate diagnoses for septoplasty and turbinates when both performed

**Medical Necessity:**

- Document significant functional impairment
- Quantify obstruction severity
- Failed conservative therapy minimum 3-6 months
- Impact on quality of life documented
- Appropriate diagnosis codes

**Coding Accuracy:**

- Do not use bilateral or laterality modifiers
- Correct use of modifier 59 with turbinates
- Choose highest RVU turbinate code only
- Modifier 22 only when truly justified with extensive documentation
- Modifier 57 on decision visit E/M

**Compliance:**

- Separate functional from cosmetic clearly
- Do not upcode without justification
- Follow NCCI bundling rules
- Prior authorization when required
- ABN when coverage uncertain
- Accurate diagnosis coding

**Financial Transparency:**

- Clear patient communication about coverage
- Written estimates for patient responsibility portions
- Separate consent and financial agreements for cosmetic components
- No surprise billing

## Clinical Indications and Diagnosis Codes

**Primary Diagnosis for Septoplasty:**

**Deviated Nasal Septum (Most Common):**

- **J34.2** - Deviated nasal septum
	- Primary diagnosis code for septoplasty
	- Must be documented on physical exam
	- Should correlate with imaging if CT obtained

**Related Nasal Obstruction Diagnoses:**

- **J34.89** - Other specified disorders of nose and nasal sinuses
	- Can use for post-surgical changes, chronic nasal obstruction
- **J34.81** - Nasal mucositis (ulcerative)
- **J34.3** - Hypertrophy of nasal turbinates
	- Use when turbinate procedure also performed
	- Separate diagnosis linking to turbinate code

**Chronic Sinusitis (When Concurrent FESS Performed):**

- **J32.0** - Chronic maxillary sinusitis
- **J32.1** - Chronic frontal sinusitis
- **J32.2** - Chronic ethmoidal sinusitis
- **J32.3** - Chronic sphenoidal sinusitis
- **J32.4** - Chronic pansinusitis
- **J32.8** - Other chronic sinusitis
- **J32.9** - Chronic sinusitis, unspecified

**Nasal Polyps (When Concurrent Polypectomy):**

- **[[J33.0]]** - Polyp of nasal cavity
- **[[J33.1]]** - Polypoid sinus degeneration
- **[[J33.8]]** - Other polyp of sinus
- **[[J33.9]]** - Nasal polyp, unspecified

**Trauma-Related:**

- **[[S02.2XXA]]** - Fracture of nasal bones, initial encounter (if acute trauma)
- **[[S02.2XXD]]** - Fracture of nasal bones, subsequent encounter (follow-up after healing)
- **[[S02.2XXS]]** - Fracture of nasal bones, sequela (late effects, post-traumatic deviation)
- **[[M95.0]]** - Acquired deformity of nose (post-traumatic)

**Sleep-Related Breathing Disorders:**

- **G47.30** - Sleep apnea, unspecified
- **G47.33** - Obstructive sleep apnea (adult)(pediatric)
- Use as supporting diagnosis when nasal obstruction contributes to sleep apnea
- Primary diagnosis should still be J34.2

**Epistaxis (Supporting):**

- **[[R04.0]]** - Epistaxis
- Can use as secondary diagnosis if chronic epistaxis related to septal deviation

**Congenital Anomalies:**

- **[[Q67.4]]** - Other congenital deformities of skull, face and jaw
- Rare; typically J34.2 more appropriate

**Post-Surgical Complications (Revision Cases):**

- **J34.2** - Deviated nasal septum (still primary)
- **J34.89** - Other specified disorders (can note post-surgical changes)
- **[[T85.9XXA]]** - Complication of prosthetic device, implant and graft if applicable
- Document revision surgery in operative report

**Supporting/Secondary Diagnoses:**

- **[[Z79.01]]** - Long-term use of anticoagulants (bleeding risk)
- **I48.91** - Atrial fibrillation (if on anticoagulation)
- **Z87.891** - Personal history of nicotine dependence (healing concerns)
- **E11.9** - Type 2 diabetes (healing concerns)

**Diagnoses That Do NOT Support Medical Necessity:**

- Cosmetic concerns alone
- Normal examination findings
- Screening codes
- Purely aesthetic indications

**Incorrect or Insufficient Diagnosis Coding:**

- Using only J34.89 without J34.2 (be specific)
- Missing turbinate diagnosis when billing turbinate code
- Using only sleep apnea code without documenting septal deviation
- Cosmetic rhinoplasty codes for functional septoplasty

**Diagnosis Coding Best Practices:**

- J34.2 should be primary diagnosis for septoplasty
- Add J34.3 when turbinate procedure performed (link to turbinate code)
- Include chronic sinusitis codes when FESS performed
- Supporting diagnoses help tell full clinical story
- Document all diagnoses affecting surgical risk (anticoagulation, diabetes, etc.)
- Link each CPT code to appropriate diagnosis code on claim

**ICD-10 Documentation Tips:**

- J34.2 requires clinical exam documentation showing deviation
- Document severity (mild, moderate, severe)
- Document which side more obstructed
- If trauma-related, use appropriate history codes
- For revision, document recurrent or persistent deviation

This completes the comprehensive documentation for CPT 30520.
