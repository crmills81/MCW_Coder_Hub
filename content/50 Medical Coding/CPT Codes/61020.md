# 🧠 CPT 61020 — Ventricular Puncture Through Previous Burr Hole, Fontanelle, Suture, or Implanted Ventricular Catheter/Reservoir; Without Injection

<br>

> [!info] Quick Reference  
> **wRVU**: 1.47 (2026)<sup>5</sup> | **Global Period**: **<font color="#ff0000">000</font>** | **Assistant Payable**: 1 | **Bilateral Indicator:** 0 |  
> **Rule**: **CPT [[61020]]** is a needle-only service through an access route that already exists. A new **burr hole** or a new **[[catheter]]** is not part of this code. The **000** global means the pre-procedure work and same-day care are bundled, so a **same-day E/M** needs documentation of a significant, separately identifiable service with modifier **[[-25]]**.<sup>4</sup>

---

## 📋 Clinical Description
**CPT [[61020]]** describes **a ventricular puncture done by passing a needle through a prior burr hole, open fontanelle, suture, or an implanted ventricular catheter/reservoir**, with **CSF withdrawn and nothing injected**.<sup>1</sup><sup>6</sup> The goal is usually diagnostic (**CSF cell count, culture, cytology**) or temporizing (**relief of pressure**). The access route has to already exist, which separates this code from creating a **burr hole** or placing a shunt.

Its closest sibling is **[[61026]]**, which is the same puncture but with injection of medication or another substance for diagnosis or treatment.<sup>6</sup> Because **[[61020]]** is without **injection**, the documentation has to show that nothing was **instilled, such as contrast, antibiotics, or [[chemotherapy]]**. If the note supports an injection, **[[61026]]** is the more accurate choice. Reservoir or shunt tubing taps that are not ventricular punctures belong under **[[61070]]**.<sup>6</sup>

**This procedure may be performed in the following clinical contexts:**  

- **Suspected shunt infection** — CSF is sampled from the reservoir to look for ventriculitis or shunt infection before antibiotics are changed.  
- **Shunt malfunction work-up** — CSF is withdrawn to measure pressure and judge whether a proximal obstruction is present.  
- **Infant with open fontanelle and [[hydrocephalus]]** — a needle is passed through the fontanelle or suture for temporary decompression or sampling.  
- **Intraventricular reservoir sampling** — an Ommaya-type or similar reservoir is accessed for CSF analysis only, with no drug instilled.

## 🔬 Anatomical & Procedural Considerations

| **Variant**     | **Mechanism**       | **Key Notes**       |
| ----------- | --------------- | --------------- |
| **Through implanted reservoir/catheter** | The needle enters the subcutaneous reservoir, which connects to the ventricular catheter, and CSF is withdrawn. | This is the most common real-world use. Document the reservoir type and the volume of CSF removed. |
| **Through previous burr hole** | The needle passes through the scalp and the existing burr hole into the ventricle. | A new burr hole is reported with a different code. Document that the hole was already present. |
| **Through fontanelle or suture (infant)** | The needle passes through the anterior fontanelle or an open suture into the lateral ventricle. | This is done in neonates and infants. Document sterile technique and the patient's tolerance. |

> [!tip] Clinical Pearl  
> The choice between **[[61020]]** and **[[61026]]** rests on one thing: was anything injected? A **diagnostic or therapeutic instillation** moves you to **[[61026]]**. The op or procedure note should state what was done, which ventricle or reservoir was accessed, and the **CSF volume** and appearance. Missing this detail is the most common reason the code gets questioned on review.

---

## ✅ Procedure Includes
- **Sterile prep, local anesthesia, and positioning** of the patient.  
- **Needle puncture** through the existing access route.  
- **Withdrawal of CSF**, including opening pressure if measured at the time.  
- **Routine post-procedure** monitoring on the day of service, which is part of the **000** global.<sup>4</sup>  
- **Dressing** or **pressure** at the puncture site.

## ❌ Excludes / Do Not Report Together

| **Code**              | **Description**   | **Relationship**   |
| :-----------------: | ------------- | -------------- |
| **[[61026]]**     | Ventricular puncture through previous burr hole, fontanelle, suture, or implanted ventricular [[catheter]]/reservoir; with injection of medication or other substance for diagnosis or treatment | This is the with-injection sibling. Report one or the other for the same puncture, not both. If the service included an injection, report [[61026]] only. |
| **[[61070]]**     | Puncture of shunt tubing or reservoir for aspiration or injection procedure | Report this when the shunt tubing or reservoir is punctured and the ventricle itself is not the target. Choose based on documentation of the target. |

> [!warning] Bundling Alert  
> **CPT [[61020]]** has a **000** global period, so **same-day E/M services** are generally not separately payable unless they are significant and separately identifiable, in which case append modifier **[[-25]]**.<sup>4</sup> If a second puncture is needed for a separate reason, modifier **[[-59]]** or **[[-76]]** may apply depending on payer rules. Audits tend to target a **missing procedure note** or a **billed injection** that is not documented.

## 🏥 MS‑DRG Considerations
The professional service is reported on the **CMS-1500**, and the facility is paid under the **MS-DRG** for the stay or the **OPPS** for outpatient services, so **[[61020]]** does not drive an **MS-DRG** on its own. For an **inpatient profee claim**, report **[[61020]]** under the performing physician with the diagnosis that supports medical necessity. **NCD/LCD**: I found no **NCD** or **LCD** specific to **CPT [[61020]]** in the **Medicare Coverage Database**. **NCD 20.9.1** (**Ventricular Assist Devices**) came up in my search but does not apply to this service.<sup>7</sup> Because **PFS** status "**A**" does not mean there is an **NCD** or that the service is covered, the **MAC** makes the coverage decision. Check your **MAC's LCD and billing articles** for the code before billing.<sup>4</sup>

## 📝 Coding Examples

### Example 1
> **Clinical Scenario:**  
> A 6-month-old with a ventricular reservoir and fever has CSF withdrawn from the reservoir by needle. Nothing is injected, and the sample is sent for culture. The neurosurgeon documents the volume and appearance of the fluid.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[61020]] | Needle puncture of an implanted reservoir without injection. |
| PDx | [[T85.730A]] | The sample was taken to evaluate suspected shunt infection. |

> [!note] 
> Document the clinical reason for the sample so medical necessity is clear.

### Example 2
> **Clinical Scenario:**  
> An adult with an Ommaya reservoir has CSF removed and then intrathecal medication instilled through the same puncture. The note documents both steps.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT 1 | [[61026]] | The injection makes this the with-injection code. |
| CPT 2 | Not reported | [[61020]] is not reported with [[61026]] for the same puncture. |
| PDx | [[G91.1]] | Use the documented hydrocephalus type. |

> [!warning] 
> Do not report [[61020]] and [[61026]] together for the same puncture.

### Example 3
> **Clinical Scenario:**  
> A neurosurgeon aspirates CSF through an existing burr hole in a patient with documented obstructive hydrocephalus. No injection is given, and the patient is seen in the office.

| **Field** | **Code** | **Rationale** |
|---|---|---|
| CPT | [[61020]] | Puncture through a previous burr hole without injection. |
| PDx | [[G91.1]] | The hydrocephalus type is documented as obstructive. |

> [!note] Global period reminder
> The 000 global means a same-day E/M requires [[-25]] and separate documentation.

## ⚠️ Common Coding Pitfalls

- **Pitfall 1**: Reporting **[[61020]]** when an injection was given. The injection moves the service to **[[61026]]**, so read the note for any instilled substance.  
- **Pitfall 2**: Reporting **[[61020]]** for a new burr hole. The code requires an existing access route.  
- **Pitfall 3**: Billing **[[61020]]** and **[[61026]]** together. Both describe the same puncture, so only one is reported.  
- **Pitfall 4**: Using **[[61020]]** for a shunt tubing tap. Puncture of shunt tubing or a reservoir for aspiration or injection can belong to **[[61070]]**, depending on the target documented.  
- **Pitfall 5**: Missing **[[-25]]** on a same-day E/M. The **000** global bundles routine same-day care.  
- **Pitfall 6**: Using unspecified or parent diagnosis codes. Report the most specific **[[hydrocephalus]]** or **shunt** complication code supported by the note.
