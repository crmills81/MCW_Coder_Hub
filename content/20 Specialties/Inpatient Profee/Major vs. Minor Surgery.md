**Major vs. minor surgery** is determined by the complexity, risk, and recovery time of the procedure. **Minor surgeries** are generally less invasive, have lower complication risks, and often allow for same-day recovery, while **major surgeries** are more complex, often involve vital organs or large body cavities, require general anesthesia, and have longer recovery periods and higher risks.


#### Minor surgery

- **Risk**: Low
- **Anesthesia**: Often local, sometimes regional or general
- **Complexity**: Superficial, does not require opening a major body cavity
- **Recovery**: Short, often same-day discharge is possible
- **Examples**: Mole removal, simple tooth extraction, or sewing up a cut

#### Major surgery
- **Risk**: High, with a greater risk of complications
- **Anesthesia**: Typically general
- **Complexity**: Involves major organs or body cavities
- **Recovery**: Extended, often requires an inpatient hospital stay
- **Examples**: Open-heart surgery, organ transplants, or cancer operations
