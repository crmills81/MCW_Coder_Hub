The MAR stands for Medication Administration Record.
In the inpatient setting, the MAR is the document (often electronic) where nurses record every medication that is given to the patient.

It shows:
• What medication was given
• When it was given (date and time)
• How it was given (route, like oral or IV)
• How much was given (dose)
• Who gave it (nurse initials or electronic signature)

The MAR helps ensure that patients receive the right medication, at the right time, in the right dose, and prevents medication errors.

Correct answer: Condition Code 44 can be used only if the physician changes the patient’s status before discharge, and it is reported on the outpatient claim.

Condition Code 44 is a Medicare specific rule used when a hospital decides that a patient who was initially admitted as an inpatient should actually be billed as an outpatient instead. For Condition Code 44 to be applied correctly, two things must happen before the patient leaves the hospital:

  1. The utilization review (UR) committee must agree that the inpatient admission was not medically necessary, and
  2. The physician responsible for the case must formally change the patient’s status to outpatient.
    Because Medicare requires these steps to occur before discharge, Condition Code 44 cannot be used after the patient has gone home. Once the status is changed, the hospital must bill the stay as outpatient and report Condition Code 44 on the outpatient claim.

Feedback

Correct Answer: Deliberate ignorance of whether the information on a claim is true or false
Feedback/Rationale
Under the False Claims Act (31 U.S.C. §3729), the term “knowingly” includes three types of conduct:

  1. Actual knowledge of falsity
  2. Deliberate ignorance of the truth or falsity
  3. Reckless disregard of the truth or falsity
    This means the FCA does not require intent to defraud—the government only needs to show one of the three levels of knowledge.
    • Deliberate ignorance is explicitly included in the FCA’s definition of “knowingly.”
    • It is considered a form of willful blindness and is actionable under the statute.
